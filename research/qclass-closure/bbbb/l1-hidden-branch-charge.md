**Result of the charging attempt.** I do not obtain an unconditional upper bound on H in terms of r. **What must be charged.** Let a be the marks' parity. A candidate is a regular coincidence on the marked ordered-pair cover U above a pole x≠a. A witness travels from a compatible lift of the ordered marks to that coincidence with both projections legal. Seeing the physical midpoint independently from each mark does not give matching developed velocities and travel times. For a dark pair write Hₓ for the number of these candidates over x and H=ΣHₓ. **A necessary test for a per-corner proof.** Give the approach its most permissive choice of charge. For each hidden branch b form a set B(b) of physical reflexes: C belongs if there exists a route from the marks through that *specified midpoint germ*, continuing straight across earlier reflex contacts if necessary, with all other ordinary billiard rules enforced, and meeting C. Count contacts on either half. This definition permits a choice among infinitely many routes; B(b) itself is finite. A capacity-c charge of every branch exists precisely if, for every subset F of the hidden branches,

    |F| ≤ c · |union of B(b) over b in F|.

This is Hall's theorem after making c copies of each physical reflex. A singleton F already fails if B(b) is empty. Algebraic membership supplies continuation along the cover, but supplies no relaxed straight route. Even a first-hit construction on one half of an unsuccessful shot does not identify the requested midpoint on its continuation. Convex contacts cannot silently be treated as reflex charges.

For a route to an off-class midpoint x the primitive integer direction has parity x−a and the midpoint occurs after an odd number of primitive steps. Hence every lattice hit has class a or x, since integer-wall reflections preserve parity. This gives a further necessary test for *every* nonempty subset X of the three off-classes:

    Σ(x in X) Hₓ ≤ c · (rₐ + Σ(x in X) rₓ).

The source-class reflexes are shared across X and may only be counted once. These inequalities are obstructions to a charging proof. There is no deduction of them from darkness. Even if they hold, Hall still has to hold for the actual supports of individual branches within each class.
