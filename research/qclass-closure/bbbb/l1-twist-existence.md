### (a) Two-pole orbits, including all triples

Fix a peripheral basis adapted to the test arc from a to x. Throughout, permutations act on sheet labels on the left, (XY)(i)=X(Y(i)), and the based peripheral tuple has product one in that convention. All incidences are labeled in the same fiber. Replacing XY by YX while keeping the old incidence labels can change the test. Write X=gₓ, Y=gᵧ and k=XY, with x,y adjacent away from a. Make a bipartite multigraph with black vertices the cycles of X, white vertices the cycles of Y, and one edge labeled i for each sheet i, incident to the two cycles containing i. Remember the cyclic order at every vertex given by its permutation. Components are exactly the orbits of ⟨X,Y⟩: changing edges at a vertex is applying a power of the corresponding generator. Conversely this graph with its rotations reconstructs X and Y up to simultaneous sheet relabeling.

**Topology restricts the classification.** Take the preimage in Q of a closed disk containing x,y and no other pole. Each component thickens the corresponding graph (lift an interval joining the two poles). It is a subsurface of a sphere, hence has genus zero. Its boundary circuits are the cycles of k up to orientation. Explicitly, from edge i turn once at white to Yi and once at black to XYi. Every label occurs once among the resulting cycles. A black bivalent vertex records one transposition, and its two incident labels give its two exact positions on these cycles. Thus a triple is a three-way turn in cyclic order. Replacing it by three bounces would change the boundary cycles and the test.

This is a classification by planar bipartite maps with rotations, rather than by permutation-group names. More explicitly, on one component let E be its number of sheets, L its number of leaves, B its number of trivalent vertices and F its number of k-cycles. Euler and the degree sum give

    F = E − V + 2 = 2 + (B−L)/2,       Σ(face cycle lengths) = E.

With B=0 one gets a path (including one edge) or an alternating cycle (including two parallel edges). On an E-edge path k is one E-cycle; on a 2h-edge alternating cycle k consists of two h-cycles, since two successive turns advance by two edges around the ring. For B>0, suppress bivalent vertices. The connected planar core is a multigraph with leaves and trivalent vertices; loops after suppression are allowed. Retain the rotation at every triple, then restore each chain with its length and alternating colors. This construction recovers all two-pole orbit types occurring here. Conversely every such bipartite planar map gives a two-permutation orbit, without asserting it completes to the required four-pole room. At n=22, B≤8, E≤22 and F≤6 per component. There can be arbitrarily many sheet positions relative to a vertex if n varies; the bound on triples does not make a trivalent component dihedral.

### (b) Which transpositions a twist can actually use

**Why the full twist is a geometric state.** Put the basepoint and the a-tail outside a disk enclosing the adjacent poles x,y. Fix the Hurwitz half-twist convention H(X,Y)=(Y,Y⁻¹XY). Then H²(X,Y)=(k⁻¹Xk,k⁻¹Yk) and the product remains k=XY. Topologically, H² is the boundary Dehn twist: rotate the two-pole disk through one full turn relative to its exterior, interpolating in a collar of its boundary. Its two inside tails pick up the boundary loop; all outside tails, including the marked a-incidences, stay fixed. Choosing the opposite twist changes j to −j and preserves the family with j∈ℤ.

There is also a direct flat realization on the pillowcase. In unit torus coordinates modulo ±1, the horizontal band 0<y<1/2 projects injectively in the transverse coordinate to an annulus of circumference one. Its two limiting rims carry the two pairs of poles. The integral shear (x,y)↦(x+2y,y) fixes all four poles, is the identity on both rims, and winds exactly once between them. Hence it represents a Dehn twist about the annulus core, up to choice of sign. For a different two-pole curve use its pillowcase slope to change integral basis; the conjugate shear gives the same mapping-class construction. Equivalently, carrying the reference disk and tails by a geometric marking conjugates the boundary twist. The collar-supported representative tracks the based incidences, and the affine representative identifies the straight arc in the resulting isotopy class. This changes the marked origami state. It need not be an affine self-map of the original room. No convention for a composite code word is used. Index each k-cycle starting at an arbitrary origin, increasing by one under k. For ordered face cycles I,J of lengths m,h, make a set D(I,J) as follows. For each transposition of X take both orders (u,v) of its labels. If u is in I and v in J, insert

    pos_J(v) − pos_I(u) mod gcd(m,h)

in D(I,J). Allow I=J; then the modulus is m. These are exactly the simultaneous-shift orbits of ordered pairs hit by the transpositions. For incident sheets s∈I at S and t∈J at T a twist works iff their own offset belongs to D(I,J). Indeed the twist conjugates X by a power of k. Reaching (u,v) requires the same exponent to advance s to u and t to v. The two resulting congruences are consistent precisely when the offsets agree modulo the gcd. This also proves independence of the chosen face origins. It tests existence over every integer exponent without exploring any braid words. [Geometric twist formula](l1-cylinder-levels.md)

**A family where existence does follow.** On a path component with both generators involutions, index the k-cycle by i mod E. The identity XkX=k⁻¹ gives X(i)=c−i for a constant c. For distinct incident labels s,t on this path the matching equation is 2j≡c−pos(s)−pos(t) mod E. Whenever it is soluble, the two transported labels are distinct, so their X-cycle is a transposition rather than a fixed point. Thus one odd-length path containing any compatible s,t suffices. On an even-length path the displayed parity condition is exact. On an alternating cycle X exchanges its two k-cycles and reverses the cyclic index; with origins chosen so X sends index zero to zero on the opposite cycle, the equation for s,t on opposite faces is 2j≡−pos(s)−pos(t) mod h. No transposition of X joins labels on the same face of this alternating cycle.

**A small-modulus sufficient condition for physical marks.** For a face pair I,J supporting source and target incidences, reduce their allowed positions modulo g=gcd(m,h) to subsets Sᵢ,Tⱼ of ℤ/g. Take the allowed incidences for one compatible lift pair, or take their unions when all cross-choices are allowed. The exact test is (Tⱼ−Sᵢ)∩D(I,J) nonempty. If D contains δ and |Sᵢ|+|Tⱼ|>g, the translates Sᵢ+δ and Tⱼ intersect by pigeonhole. In particular, on two coprime boundary lengths a single black transposition incident to both faces suffices whenever the marks have the required incidences. At modulus two, both residues available at either endpoint suffice if the other endpoint and D are nonempty. Consequently a failing family with D nonempty must satisfy |Sᵢ|+|Tⱼ|≤g at each occupied face pair. Counting the 15 or more transpositions across all faces does not establish an intersection on an occupied pair.

**What the product relation adds.** In a coherent basis gₐXYg_w=1, the complementary disk has the same boundary lengths, with reverse boundary orientation. Take one vertex for each connected preimage of either disk and one edge for each matched boundary circle. The resulting graph is a tree: each boundary circle separates the sphere, hence its edge is a bridge, and Q is connected. For a given lift over a, both incident sheets are in the same complementary-disk component. If the chosen lifts lie in different complementary components, a twist can work only if these vertices have distance two in the tree. In that event there is a unique intervening x,y-component and one boundary edge toward it from each vertex. At least one sheet from each lift must lie on those two boundary cycles, and their offset must pass the D test there. If the lifts lie in the same complementary component and can use the same x,y-component, there is only one tree edge between these components: the two sheets must use the same face. This distance and incidence filter uses the global product relation before any congruence test.

D220 gives A₂₂ or S₂₂ on the primitive branch, and every regular off-class transposition is on the marked ordered-pair curve. Riemann–Hurwitz gives Σtₚ=26, so the three off-classes supply 26−tₐ≥15 targets. Neither transitivity of the full group nor that total gives a lower bound in the **particular face pair and residue** occupied by s,t. Different residues on a single face are already separate orbits for ⟨k⟩. An empty entry is invariant under the chosen twist, but need not be preserved by gₐ, g_w or a different twist. I obtain no monodromy block, corner-only class, or contradiction from emptiness in the tested families. ### (c) Exact residual for specified families

Fix the adapted bases for the proposed ordered pole-pair families and transport the marks to each. For one fixed marked room there is a finite graph test. Its vertices are the regular physical interior marks over a. In each family's table put an edge between two marks whenever the offset for one of their incident sheet pairs belongs to the entry for its ordered pair of faces. Take the union of these edges over the families. **The failing marked pairs are exactly the missing edges**; this follows from the twist equivalence above. In particular, for mₐ such marks the failure count for this tuple and these bases is binom(mₐ,2) minus the number of distinct edges in the union. Each interior mark uses two distinct a-transpositions on Q, and tₐ≤11, so mₐ≤5 and there are at most ten physical pairs to classify for a given tuple and class.

This description decides membership from rotations, boundary positions and mark incidences. It is not an enumerated list or a count of failing degree-22 room tuples. No small list follows here. An unmarked cycle passport does not specify the offsets. Also an ordered pair of pole *names* alone does not fix an arc system on a four-punctured sphere. Testing the six ordered choices in prescribed bases is a different claim from testing their full pure-braid orbits. Failure of these two-traversal families would leave longer legal walks available.
