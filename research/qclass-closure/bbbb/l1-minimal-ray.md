Advances: L1 — a singleton-support dark ray is excluded for one separating rectangle with fixed ends, and for a removable terminal cap whenever the collapsed room lights the retained pair. A six-reflex end lock has an unequal growth ratio 1:2. An eight-reflex strip with two equal-period separating components admits exact cancellation, yet in the example a short representative is lit. These statements do not exclude all singleton or two-gap dark rays.

### Calibration first: what free length is actually known?

The smallest dark control on file here is the nine-reflex R22. Independently stretching the horizontal axis by d>0 gives a genuine dark family with fixed transverse ordinates: the diagonal map commutes with every axial reflection. It is **rank one in horizontal shape coordinates**. I have no certified fold family on file with a free *relative* horizontal length after dividing out this stretch. In particular the independently lengthened R22 arm loses darkness. D154 gives a reason for caution: for normalized lattice rooms with its proper clean quotient the physical wall gaps are bounded by 4r−2. At fixed r this excludes a shape-changing family of primitive wall geometries of this certified kind with unbounded gaps. [R22 geometry](../../illumination-publication/repository/preprints/twenty-two-wall/manuscript.tex) · [D154 review](../../illumination-generalization-2026-11-06/fable/directions-2026-11-09/d154-review-r22arm/review.md)

Here are two shortest horizontal-progress tests with distinct length patterns, counting all reflected axial travel. In unstretched coordinates S=(0,0), T=(2,2). In (0,1) the relevant transverse interval is (−2,2), in (1,2) it is (1,3), and the intervening gate is (1,2). The shortest travel is 2d. The moduli are (d/8,d/4), so the full persistent integer dual is ℤ(2,−1). Although the raw entries differ, the pushed coefficients on the two gaps are (+1/4,−1/4). The two **transverse periods are 8 and 4**, already unequal in the known fold. Each developed endpoint ordinate is even, so the midpoint gate ordinate is integral and misses (1,2).

For the shortest itinerary with a backward start, turn at x=−d, return through 0, transmit at d, and finish at 2d. Its travel is 4d, minimal for a backward start since the first possible turning level is −d. The four widths are (2,2,4,2), all travels d. The full persistent dual is the set 2n₀+2n₁+n₂+2n₃=0. It contains (1,−1,0,0), which pushes to zero on the twice-visited gap, and (0,0,2,−1), which gives the same equal-and-opposite tie as above. These are relations, not individually asserted to exclude every oriented box. Darkness of this family is certified by the fold. Neither calculation tests a dimension-two horizontal dark flat.

### Singleton support: three different geometric obligations

Cut each component of the open strip at its middle by a transverse crosscut, with the marks off the cut. The disjoint crosscuts have a tree as their component adjacency graph: each proper crosscut in a disk splits one piece in two. The separating cuts are precisely the edges on the unique path between the marked pieces. Thus k counts those edges, **not** all the rectangles lengthened by the gap. On a singleton-support flat the gap length can be varied to any positive value, including arbitrarily small ones, holding the others fixed (subtract or add the coordinate ray while positive). One regular chord at any such value contradicts darkness on the flat.

**D232 conventions for the ray tests.** A smooth continuation of a side wall at a level admits the corresponding gate endpoint with the ordinary reflection sector. A closure hit with just one endpoint active and every other inequality strict is safe: the connected component through the hit either enters the open box or lies in the endpoint hyperplane, where the coset is dense in that component. A false closure hit requires at least two active closed endpoints; on such a face impose the simultaneous equalities on the integer windings before testing the relative-open face. A partial first or last visit uses its actual travel, so cancellation with a full traversal of the same strip requires checking the moduli. [D232 review](../../illumination-generalization-2026-11-06/fable/directions-2026-11-09/d232-review-dark-flat-exactness/review.md)

* **k=1.** For one separating rectangle with sealable doors and fixed opposite ends, the long-corridor construction supplies two finite first-door legs at a **common absolute slope**. For the open-box tie lemma, “regular” requires every event of both legs, including both mouth hits, strictly inside its interval. Choose the middle visit's side-reflection parity so that its developed slope matches the reversed target leg's signed slope. Under these hypotheses a gap traversed only there has a nonzero pushed coefficient on a dark flat. With additional growing nonseparating components one must produce such legs avoiding those components; the fixed-end conclusion does not automatically extend to that geometry. The reviewed coefficient recursion for a prescribed itinerary can use g/(2r_E) at a section ball of radius r_E with g gate events: a missed ball gives covering radius at least r_E and Euclidean transference gives a dual vector of length at most g/(2r_E). The constants are explicit once the itinerary data are fixed. [Common first-hit construction](README.md) · [Tie and coefficient argument](l1-dark-flat-exactness.md)
  
  For a source on an open mouth, launch directly into the empty strip. Choose a closed slope interval [s₀,s₁] of positive width strictly inside a regular window of the opposite fixed end. In signed transverse phase the matching function is (L+D)s+C, with L the middle length and D the absolute axial travel of the reverse leg: unfolding makes its signed transverse displacement affine with derivative of magnitude D, adding to L in the matching equation. C includes the fixed mouth-start ordinate. When (L+D)(s₁−s₀)>2w the phase range contains a multiple of the full period 2w, matching position and velocity. The end leg stays regular throughout that interval. This gives the mouth-start claim for sufficiently large L. With two marks on opposite open mouths, the direct segment already stays inside the rectangle. [Signed-phase proof](README.md)

* **k=0.** The two marks lie in one retained piece. A regular witness missing the cut-off components persists directly. A witness at any positive integral representative also gives the reviewed growth transfer for anchored marks, but that result assumes the seed. There is a further small-length reduction for **flush terminal caps**: suppose a rectangular appendage attaches along its full transverse interval and ends at a solid parallel wall. Suppose shrinking it to zero gives a strictly simple core retaining both interior marks. A regular witness in the core persists for sufficiently small positive cap length. Indeed, in the core each transverse reflection strictly inside the former mouth can be replaced by reflection on the translated cap wall. In the fixed unfolded word the target image and all intersection points converge to those of the core chord. Finitely many strict wall-interior and corner-clearance inequalities persist. Inside the new thin appendage the two short legs remain strictly between its sides. This argument requires mouth endpoints to be avoided even when they become smooth subdivision points in the core. It works for finitely many such caps at once. It gives a contradiction if the core has such a seed, for example by a direct chord. A general branch behind a crosscut need not collapse to a parallel mirror. Core lighting and regularity at the artificial endpoints also need proof. [Growth transfer and its seed hypothesis](l1-growth-modes.md)
* **k≥2.** Two separating crosscuts of one x-gap can be visited successively in opposite axial directions. Equal transverse periods occur exactly when the two component intervals have equal lengths; symmetry of the ends is unnecessary. If the two periods are rational but unequal, changing the periods alone does **not** remove cancellation: for periods P₁,P₂ in common integral units, the integer entries P₁/g and −P₂/g, g=gcd(P₁,P₂), have zero push on their common gap. This is an exact relation for every gap length. Whether it blocks depends on its affine level and the open box.

For an explicit eight-reflex instance of the last case take these transverse intervals on successive x bands:

| x range | intervals |
|---|---|
| (−2,−1) | (2,3), (8,9) |
| (−1,0) | (1,3), (7,9) |
| (0,1) | (1,4), (7,10) |
| (1,1+L) | (0,4), (6,10) |
| (1+L,9+L) | (0,10) |

Take S=(0,2), T=(0,8). Both are pinned and interior. The first three interfaces each contribute two reflexes; the join to the right bar contributes two more. The room is a disk with two arms, and both growing components separate S from T. Both periods are 8, giving the (+1,−1) cancellation on one visit per arm. But at L=1 the chord reflected once at the right wall joins the pair. Its rising ordinate is 2+3x/10 on the outward leg, and the return leg has ordinate 8−3x/10 at the same abscissa. At x=1 and x=2 these are strictly in their respective gates; the wall hit is (10,5). This discharges that entire candidate dark line by a single symbolic specialization. It does not establish a seed for arbitrary far bars, mouth placements, or attached branches.

### Two gaps: a rational tie does not identify a symmetry

A tie supported on γ,η gives eη/eγ=−dγ/dη. If other coordinates of the pushed vector are nonzero but fixed along the ray, the slope equation is unchanged. Nothing in this identity identifies boundary intervals, copy gluings, or an involution. R22 above even has unequal transverse periods on the shortest tied visits.

A six-reflex stress test shows that a persistent *blocking* tie can have unequal pushed coefficients. Rotate the earlier two-gate family so its axial variable is the x-coordinate; retain unit transverse scale. Write the successive transverse intervals as (0,22),(0,8),(7,17),(16,24),(16,25), with respective axial lengths 1,a,B,2a,1. Put the source at transverse ordinate 7 on the first internal interface and the target at 22 on the last. Both are pinned by supports elsewhere and interior. All parameters are positive. The two end-length coordinates satisfy η=2γ. Varying a alone is an extreme ray on the parameter cone with the other lengths held proportional to one fixed positive template. This is a test flat of geometries, not a known dark flat.

For an unreflected axial departure and arrival the first opening (7,8) requires the absolute transverse travel z=as to be within 2 of 0 modulo 16, allowing either transverse launch sign. Viewed backwards from 22 the end travel is 2z, within 4 of 0 modulo 16. Both resulting physical ordinates fold strictly above 17 in the width-eight end: the unfolded range is (18,26), folding to (18,24]. Hence they miss the second gate (16,17). This excludes **all middle itineraries** with those end legs, including reversals and arbitrary side windings. The end coefficients n=(2,−1) push to (1/8,−1/16), ratio 1:2. The equal transverse periods therefore coexist with unequal axial lengths and the two full end strips cannot be exchanged isometrically. The test leaves changed end legs open; it is not a counterexample to an implication whose antecedent is actual darkness on the whole flat. No example with a two-gap nonsymmetric lock on *all* itineraries is established here. [Equal-travel precursor](l1-two-gate-boundary.md)

### Induction and first open case

There are at most r+2 vertical walls, hence at most r+1≤9 physical x-gaps at r≤8. Refining by two interior marks adds at most two gaps (≤11); pinned marks add none. For a homogeneous flat of dimension d in m gap coordinates, an extreme ray has at least d−1 independent active coordinate constraints, so its support has size at most m−d+1. Iteration would need either a lit specialization, or independent pushed constraints that reduce the possible flat dimension; repeated visits with net push zero do neither. Even exclusion of dark flats of dimension ≥2 would only give qualitative finiteness of primitive possibilities under the finite-flat hypotheses, not a numerical census threshold without effective data.

**First open case for the tie lemma.** Every candidate itinerary traverses the same growing gap at least twice. With two equal transverse periods P, entries +1 and −1 give pushed coefficient 1/P−1/P=0. Detecting either visit separately therefore cannot force a tie to a different growing gap. The eight-reflex example above is lit at L=1; the unresolved case is the general two-component geometry without a known lit specialization or a blocking relation with nonzero push. The same-end seed problem at k=0 and common-slope avoidance at k=1 are separate geometric obligations. No numerical N₀ follows.
