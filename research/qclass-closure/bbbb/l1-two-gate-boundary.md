Advances: L1 — two displaced gates can be locked for every winding and every intervening itinerary by the same exposed wall in a six-reflex room. An outer roof reflection unlocks them. A middle-width winding then gives an explicit lighting tail for the specified pinned pair. The boundary-order obstruction occurs before any count of nine distinct reflexes.

### Two gate tests that do not depend on the middle itinerary

Suppose the source-to-first-gate leg and last-gate-to-target leg have equal absolute axial travel a, each in an end rectangle of width w. Trace the second leg backwards. Both legs spend the same transverse travel p a, where p is the absolute horizontal-to-vertical velocity ratio. Side reflections fold this travel modulo 2w. For each end form the open set of phases in the circle of length 2w that put its mark in its gate when traced outwards from the mark; take the union over both horizontal launch signs. A common phase is necessary. This follows independently at the two ends and applies to every trajectory with the stated end legs, even one that crosses many slabs or reverses between them. Compatibility of the two signs through the middle is a further condition. Disjoint end-phase sets exclude the whole class.

### A pinned family where all middle repairs fail at one wall

Let a,t,B be positive integers. Put b=a+B and D=b+a. Use the interior of the union of the closed tier rectangles below.

| Height | Horizontal interval |
|---|---|
| (−a,0) | (0,20t) |
| (0,a) | (0,8t) |
| (a,b) | (7t,17t) |
| (b,D) | (16t,24t) |
| (D,D+3a) | (16t,25t) |

All successive overlaps are open and nonempty. The changes of slab endpoints contribute 1+2+2+1=6 reflexes (16 walls). S=(7t,0) and T=(20t,D) lie strictly inside the overlaps at their heights. Their abscissas are wall supports in the middle and bottom tiers respectively. The gates are G₁=(7t,8t) at a and G₂=(16t,17t) at b. Each is individually accessible from its end.

Consider any witness that starts upward, goes straight in height to its first passage through G₁, and, after its last passage through G₂, goes straight in height up to T. Allow arbitrary side reflections on both legs. In units of t put z=pa/t. In the first end rectangle, to fold 7±z into (7,8) one needs z modulo 16 in (0,2) or (14,16). Indeed the two unfolded copies of the opening on either side of the right wall form (7,9) with the wall point 8 excluded; using this larger set is sufficient for the exclusion. In either range the transverse displacement is congruent to some d with |d|<2. Traced back from T, both candidate abscissas then fold to (20±d)t in (18t,22t), strictly inside the **same** exposed floor (17t,24t) at b. Neither reaches G₂. Conversely the phase z=7/2, for instance, reaches G₂ from T with the leftward sign. Thus the failure is genuinely simultaneous. The direct chord is among the excluded paths. Middle windings, reflections, and even downward legs leaving the middle slab do not alter this first/last-visit contradiction.

The failed backwards approaches all hit the interior of that one floor segment. On its left is the reflex at (17t,b); on its right is the convex corner at (24t,b). Varying the middle itinerary supplies no further distinct reflex. This refutes charging a fresh reflex to each failed middle repair. It leaves open a charge for defeating genuinely different **end** itineraries.

### A roof reflection and a winding close the long-middle tail

Use no lower end bounce. In the upper end, go to the roof at D+3a and return to T. The vertical travels in the two ends are now a and 7a. Keep the horizontal velocity rightward on both end legs. The two required physical crossing abscissas, in units of t, are 7+z and 20−7z. Both lie strictly inside their gates whenever

    3/7 < z < 4/7.

For such z the upper leg stays strictly within the upper tiers: it starts in (16t,17t), moves right throughout, hits the roof at (20−3z)t, and ends at 20t. At both passages through height D it lies in the overlap (16t,24t). The lower leg stays inside its rectangle. In the middle rectangle of width 10t insert k full transverse periods, preserving the exit position and velocity sign in the unfolding. Matching horizontal travel exactly is equivalent to

    z = (13+20k)/(B/a+8),    k ≥ 0 integral.

Indeed total absolute vertical travel is B+8a and the developed transverse displacement is (20−7)t+20kt. The first gate lies strictly inside the middle interval, as does the required second gate on its increasing unfolded copy. Every intervening side reflection hits a vertical side strictly between heights a and b. This verifies both openings, the physical roof, the target image and every other interface.

At B=18a, k=0 gives z=1/2 for every t. For **every B>132a**, the allowed open interval for k has length (B/a+8)/140>1 and positive lower endpoint. Hence a compatible integer exists for every such B, uniformly in t. This threshold is sufficient. The exact test for this template at smaller B is the displayed strict interval and integer equation. This is a tail for the specified pair; no claim about all pairs or all short middles is required for the boundary test.

**Reusable form.** In a rectangular middle of width M and axial length B, suppose prescribed end itineraries have total absolute axial travel E and leave a common open interval (p₋,p₊) of positive slopes after checking *all* end faces. Suppose the selected middle entry and exit are on increasing copies, so the developed target displacements are K₀+2Mk, with compatible endpoint and k≥0. Then the exact test is whether ((B+E)p₋−K₀,(B+E)p₊−K₀) contains 2Mk. In particular width greater than 2M suffices when the lower endpoint is nonnegative. This explains why length helps only after the end itineraries supply an interval of positive width.

**First open L1 case.** The locked equal-travel class is excluded with six reflexes, and one alternative end itinerary lights the pair throughout the stated tail. The remaining general step is to produce a pair of end itineraries with a common open slope interval and compatible middle copies in an arbitrary growing chart. The argument by distinct wall endpoints cannot count failures that reach the same exposed floor. There is no proof here that defeating all changes of end itinerary costs nine reflexes.
