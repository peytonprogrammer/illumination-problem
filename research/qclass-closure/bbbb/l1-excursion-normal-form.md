Advances: L1 — a finite tier graph captures all legal nonaxial itineraries, including returns across several slabs. The step from that graph to boundedly many repeat counters remains open. Even one fixed itinerary of a pinned pair in a five-reflex chart has a non-Presburger length domain. For a fixed winding, failure of every sign-compatible count can also have strictly negative clearance for geometric reasons.

### A. What can be put in normal form

Cut at all wall and mark levels on both axes. The open grid rectangles inside the room give at most (r+3)² cells for an orthogonal room with r reflexes: each axis has at most r+2 parallel walls, plus at most two new mark levels, hence at most r+3 gaps. Use states (cell, signs of the two nonzero velocity components). At an open side transmit to the neighbor, or reflect back into the incident cell if the side is an exposed wall. Add a simultaneous crossing through an artificial grid vertex when it is interior, and a single reflection at a smooth wall point on a subdivision line. Forbid every physical corner. Use the incident sectors at the two marks for the initial and final states. Axial paths are handled separately along a single coordinate line.

This graph has at most 4(r+3)² cell states plus start and finish. Every finite regular billiard path traces a walk in it. Conversely develop the cells of any proposed walk and join the source to the selected developed target. The walk is realized precisely when this line meets the specified faces in order and in their relative interiors (with exact incidence for the allowed subdivision-vertex events). Between events this follows from convexity of each rectangle; refolding gives the reflection law. This gives a complete finite *description of words*. For example, eliminating graph states gives finitely many nested repetitions using only r to bound the expression size. A repetition of a choice of two different excursions permits an independently chosen word on every repetition. Its total counts do not retain the order of the visits or their clearances. Completeness of a finite union of bounded counter templates would require proving that some witness can always be selected from those templates. The graph argument supplies no such selection. The earlier slab normal form is the subfamily in which a repeated downward leg stays in its slab. [Slab excursion theorem](l1-interior-bounce.md)

**The claimed arithmetic consequence fails before completeness.** The visit inequality is (l−X₀)H < Ks < (u−X₀)H. For fixed geometry **and fixed transverse image K** it is linear in the accumulated delays for a specified visit. Varying K together with the delays introduces K times a delay. Varying the room also introduces products of gap lengths on opposite axes and the restriction hⱼ=2mⱼLⱼ. A family of inequalities linear in the counters after specialization need not define a joint polyhedron. This is the obstruction recorded in PROGRAM after d204. [PROGRAM, per-pattern correction](../../illumination-generalization-2026-11-06/fable/directions-2026-11-09/PROGRAM.md)

Here is a pinned example with **zero reflections**. Take integers m≥3 and u>m+1. At successive heights −1,0,m,u−1,u,u+1 use the five open horizontal tier intervals

    (−2,1), (−1,1), (0,m), (0,m+2), (0,m+3).

As usual take the interior of the union of the closed rectangles. All overlaps are positive. The side changes contribute 1+2+1+1=5 reflexes. Both (0,0) and (m,u) are interior and pinned by the displayed levels. Their direct chord crosses height m at x=m²/u. It must pass through (0,1); the later crossing at u−1 automatically belongs to (0,m). Slab convexity proves that this fixed itinerary is legal exactly for u>m². All chart coordinates are affine in (m,u). If this itinerary domain were Presburger, taking the least legal u in each fiber would give a Presburger graph u=m²+1. A semilinear graph contained in this parabola has no nonzero period vector: any such vector generates an infinite arithmetic progression, whereas a line meets the parabola in at most two points. A finite union of singleton linear sets cannot cover the graph. Thus even allowing auxiliary integer variables and projection cannot produce the proposed joint polyhedral encoding for this itinerary. Other itineraries could still make the *union* semilinear or even universal.

### B. Arithmetic versus missing a wall

In R22 the direct winding with one double bounce reaches (1,2) at its proposed roof turn. This particular equality is the vanishing of a single visit determinant. R22's global identity uses additional data: the three-copy fold, truncated paths between distinct even sites, and that an intermediate odd-coordinate lattice site folds to a forbidden vertex. Those data imply darkness for every winding. Vanishing of one determinant alone establishes collinearity. [R22 fold proof](l1-periodic-obstruction.md)

**Strict failure for every count of a sign-compatible excursion.** Take tiers with levels −1,0,4,8,9,10 and intervals

    (−2,4), (−1,4), (0,10), (2,11), (2,8).

This is a strictly simple six-reflex room. S=(0,0) and T=(8,8) are pinned. For the direct winding the transmission at y=4 needs a minus shift into (0,4). Make m≥1 double excursions in the slab (4,8). Its ceiling is exposed on (0,2), its floor on (4,10). Every count gives the required sign, crossing first at x=4/(1+m). The first roof and floor turns would be at x=8/(1+m) and x=12/(1+m), respectively. For m=1,2 the roof exceeds 2 by at least 2/3. For m≥3 the floor is at most 3, missing its wall by at least 1. Thus the minimum signed clearance is strictly negative for **every** positive count, with a uniform margin. This failure persists under sufficiently small perturbations preserving the initial graze (the displayed bounds use only m=2 and m=3 and monotonicity).

The pair is lit by a different path: unfold T over the outer roof y=10 to (8,12). The crossing abscissas at y=4,8,9 on ascent are 8/3,16/3,6. The roof hit is 20/3 in (2,8), and the return through y=9 is at 22/3 in (2,8). The ray then reaches T. Thus even quantification over **all counts in a fixed excursion template** forces neither a zero clearance nor a parity factor separating the pair. The stronger assertion about failure across all windings and all itineraries remains open; this example addresses the fixed-chain conjecture.

### Ten-wall follow-on

For LLLRLLLRLR take turns after the successive edges, starting east. With xᵢ the vertical and yᵢ the horizontal supports the successive vertices are (x₀,y₀),(x₁,y₀),(x₁,y₁),(x₂,y₁),(x₂,y₂),(x₃,y₂),(x₃,y₃),(x₄,y₃),(x₄,y₄),(x₀,y₄). For any admissible ordering these vertices give the grid test above: in a horizontal tier pair consecutive sorted intersections with the vertical edges to form its component intervals. Here is the explicit clearance system in the original stress chart, with order 3,4,2,0,1 on both axes. [Ten-wall chart](l1-crossing-reduction.md)

Write A<B<C<F<J for the five x levels and b₀<b₁<b₂<b₃<b₄ for the y levels. The four slab intervals are (A,B),(A,F),(C,F),(C,J). On an upward transmission the three gate intervals are respectively (A,B),(C,F),(C,F). The exposed horizontal faces at b₁,b₂,b₃ are respectively (B,F) on the upper slab's floor, (A,C) on the lower slab's ceiling, and (F,J) on the upper slab's floor. The outer floor and roof are (A,B) and (C,J). For every visit use the incident gate or turn interval (a,b) from this list. In a reflected transverse copy replace it by its ordered developed endpoints (a*,b*). With start abscissa X₀ the constraints are

    (a*−X₀) H < K sᵥ < (b*−X₀) H,    H = Σ over legs |Δy|,

where sᵥ is the prefix of the same sum to the visit. For m excursions in tier i each adds 2m(bᵢ₊₁−bᵢ) to H; intermediate turn visits use their individual prefixes. For fixed room, fixed winding and a specified chain these are the requested specialized linear clearance tests in the delays. For the variable word they are polynomial gate tests. A joint polyhedral description does not follow.

An additional scope issue is visible without solving the inequalities: **this particular stress chart has no interior pinned mark.** At an internal horizontal support the overlap is one of (A,B),(C,F),(C,F), and each is between consecutive x supports. The exterior levels contain no interior mark either. Thus a pinned-pair theorem in this chart is vacuous, while the old stopping rule asks about *all* interior lattice marks and all length assignments of the word.

**No numerical N₀.** Neither the finite walk description nor the clearance tests bound the area of a least dark realization. The first open geometric step is witness selection: if a pinned pair is joined by a walk whose downward excursions cross several interfaces, must some joining walk have a bounded sequence of repeated excursion types depending only on r? Even a positive answer would still need a separate theorem about the union of the parameter domains to recover Presburger decidability.

### B, sharpened after d228. An even-height gap in a lit pinned pair

**Progression test.** Suppose a specified development has a nonempty open clearance interval (a,b) for H, and the attainable heights under consideration are exactly H₀+qj for integers j≥0, q>0. The first attainable height strictly above a is

    h* = H₀ + q max(0, floor((a−H₀)/q)+1).

There is a witness to these scalar tests exactly when h*<b. In particular, above the progression's starting point, a missed interval lies between two consecutive progression points, with equality allowed at its excluded endpoints. This is an obstruction for that development. A geometric witness also needs every other visit and the compatible endpoint. With several independent delays, the attainable heights are H₀+Σλⱼmⱼ, mⱼ≥0. Their gcd supplies a necessary congruence; it need not describe the initial attainable heights (delays 4 and 6 miss 2). Even for the same H the visit prefixes can depend on which delays produced it. [D228, claims 2 and 6](../../illumination-generalization-2026-11-06/fable/directions-2026-11-09/d228-review-interior-bounce/review.md)

**Counterexample to the proposed factor inference, with exactly R22's interval.** Fix any positive integer c. At levels −1,0,1,2,3,4 use, from bottom to top, the open tier intervals

    c(−2,4), c(−1,4), c(0,10), c(4,11), c(2,8).

Take the interior of the union of the closed rectangles. All overlaps are positive. The side changes contribute 1+2+2+2=7 reflexes. The distinct same-parity marks S=(0,0) and T=(8c,2) are interior to overlaps and pinned: the middle tier supplies the x=0 support, and the last tier the x=8c support. The direct chord grazes (4c,1). In the direct transverse development K=8c, put the double excursion in 1<y<2. Every positive count gives the required minus sign at the y=1 transmission. For the first roof and floor hits the vertical travel prefixes are 2 and 3. Their exposed faces impose

    0 < 16c/H < 4c,       4c < 24c/H < 10c.

Together these inequalities are exactly 4<H<6. The first gate also passes throughout this interval. For a single excursion even the last arrival at level 2 is in the open endpoint component: at the relaxed value H=5 its abscissa is 32c/5 in (4c,10c). This illustrates the distinction between reaching the component and aiming at T. One excursion fixes H=4; allowing any positive repeat count gives H=2+2m, m≥1. The real window contains 5 and misses every attainable height. At H=4 the first roof hits its endpoint. At H=6 the first floor hits its endpoint. Larger counts already fail the floor condition. These two necessary visit tests thus exclude the entire count family without any assumption about its later turns.

Yet **one ordinary reflection joins S to T for every c**. Launch with velocity (−5c,1). It hits the exposed side x=−c at y=1/5, crosses y=1 at x=3c in (0,4c), and reaches (8c,2) inside (4c,10c). Slab convexity checks both legs. This supplies a nonaxial legal ray for the very pair with the R22-type even-height gap. It also shows why parity across *all* windings is insufficient on its own: the successful ray has even vertical travel 2, but its transverse target image has changed and the interval (4,6) no longer applies.

For R22 the same calculation with K=2 and its own face endpoints gives precisely (4,6); the independent three-copy fold rules out other developments. The seven-reflex example has the same missed interval, the same parity of both marks and the same endpoint failures. Hence these hypotheses do not force a factor separating S and T in the sense needed for case (ii). They do not decide whether an individual direction class admits some factor. A factor proof would need a relation on sheets respected across state changes and strong enough to obstruct every compatible development. The local height calculation gives no such relation. [R22 fold proof](l1-periodic-obstruction.md)

**First open implication.** The seven-reflex example has a witness in the monotone tier form once the transverse winding changes. It settles the proposed inference from one missed progression. For (A), bounded-template witness selection remains open when a known joining ray returns across multiple interfaces. For (B), the open case is simultaneous failure over all compatible windings and all relevant itineraries in a room with at most eight reflexes. D228's fixed-chain membership criterion gives no transfer from one missed interval to the others.
