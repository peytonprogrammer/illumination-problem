# Two-axis decision at bounded local thickness

**Theorem for review.** Given a wall bound W and an integer τ₀, it is decidable whether any strictly simple orthogonal integral room of at most W walls, with largest inscribed axis-aligned square of side at most τ₀, has a dark pair of distinct same-parity interior lattice marks. The two bounding dimensions can both be unbounded. Independent wall-gcd-one conditions may be imposed. This is a decision algorithm, with no claim about the answer or feasible runtime. It applies at W=20. It uses neither finite flatness nor an assumed dimension of a dark flat.

### Disjoint insertion regions

Use the wall-and-mark gap grid. Enumerate its finitely many order cells. Record which x and y gaps exceed τ₀ and fix every remaining gap to one of the integers from 1 through τ₀. A rectangle made of a consecutive block of columns and rows, with all its open cells occupied, has shorter side ≤τ₀ precisely when at least one of the two sums of gaps is ≤τ₀. The truth is determined by the pattern: a sum containing a long gap exceeds τ₀. Check this for every full block. This exactly enforces the thickness condition, since a square with positive interior can be extended to the grid lines bounding the open cells its interior meets. Strict simplicity makes the closure of every occupied block part of the closed polygon.

In a retained pattern no occupied cell is long on both axes. Inside an open long x-gap every occupied component is a horizontal rectangle with physical horizontal sides. Its transverse height is fixed and at most τ₀. The transposed fact holds for a long y-gap. The remaining pieces consist of fixed-size cells. They translate as lengths vary. Put K=2 lcm(1,…,τ₀). It is a transverse phase period for every variable strip in either orientation. Marks occur at grid intersections, hence outside strip interiors. In any positive finite route, entry and exit for one such strip are at its normal ends. Artificial cut coincidences on a continuing smooth wall use the single doubled transverse chart. Distinct orthogonal variable strips cannot be joined by a legal passage through their common tip: two diagonally occupied quadrants with the long-long quadrant absent give either a reflex corner or a nonsimple point. Consecutive parallel strips can meet normally and their end windows are fixed up to translation.

### Transferring one witness with two kinds of insertion

Choose a positive integral witness at a node, with long-gap coordinates X₀,Y₀. Use the finite marked origami decision in primitive-dark-flats.md to extract a legal path if lit. An axial path supplies an order-cell chord. Otherwise orient the developed axes so its straight direction has slope s₀>0. Write its developed displacement as (D₀,s₀D₀), D₀>0. Retain all traversals of variable strips, and the finite legal portions in the remaining rigid pieces. N is the number of variable traversals counted with multiplicity. A route using none transfers immediately.

In a target room put ΔX=X−X₀, ΔY=Y−Y₀. Initially assume these are nonnegative. For s in a sufficiently short fixed closed interval I⊂(0,∞) around s₀, require the following to hold to tolerance η, on every variable gap:

    |s ΔXᵢ − kᵢK| < η,       |ΔYⱼ/s − lⱼK| < η.

Here kᵢ,lⱼ are nonnegative integers, with η<K/2. In the unfolded witness replace each horizontal-strip traversal by a traversal with additional developed vector (ΔXᵢ,kᵢK), and each vertical traversal by one with additional vector (lⱼK,ΔYⱼ). The developed signs in these formulas are positive in the chosen travel axes, even on a physically returning trip. Integer transverse periods preserve the signed local exit state. Consequently the translated reference events define a *formal* chain in the new unfolded charts, with their original local entrance and exit points. This chain need not be straight. Its terminal vector (D*,E*) is the original vector plus all the displayed additions. Aim the physical shot at slope s*=E*/D*.

For any recorded event the absolute intercept error |s x−y| of the translated reference event relative to the line from the source is ≤B, where

    B=|s−s₀|D₀ + N max(1,s)η.

Indeed the old developed prefix lies exactly on the original line and each completed insertion contributes ≤max(1,s)η. This applies throughout an old rigid leg; inside a variable strip only its two normal end sections need comparison. The terminal error gives |s*−s|D*≤B. All reference events have developed abscissa between 0 and D*. Their intercept errors at s* are therefore ≤2B, with terminal error zero. At a vertical test section this is a height error ≤2B; at a horizontal section it is an abscissa error ≤2B/s*. Choose I and η so s* stays bounded away from zero (D*≥D₀), and so both errors stay within the original regular charts.

For precision about clearance, take open windows on the normal end sections and open reflecting edges of the recorded rigid portions. For those fixed unfolded portions, lines sufficiently close in intercept and slope cross the prescribed open edges in order and miss every unused physical edge: remove small event neighborhoods and use the positive separation of the remaining compact segments. One may merge adjacent rigid cells, so simultaneous artificial grid crossings need no order prescription. Coincident endpoints of artificial cuts on a continued smooth wall can instead be tested in its reflected chart. For the variable strips, between the chosen normal sections both long sides are physical walls, with no corner in their open lengths. In the unfolded strip the reference exit is translated by whole transverse periods, so the *same* windows test the actual next signed state. These facts prove legality by induction. At a horizontal section the intersection error in the abscissa uses the lower bound on s*, as above. All margins are effective for a rational certificate, by strict rational inequalities in a sufficiently small chart (or by shrinking rational I and η until the finite inequalities hold). This proves transfer simultaneously in both axes even with very different inserted lengths.

### Finite rank descent

Maintain separate rational affine spaces Aₓ,Aᵧ for the long gaps, with the fixed gaps, positive length and any integer promises retained. At a node select one eligible lattice sample. A dark sample answers yes. A lit sample gives I,η as above. Candidates with a coordinate smaller than the corresponding sample are handled by fixing that coordinate to each eligible smaller integer and recursing. A coordinate constant at this node needs no such branch. This costs finitely many proper affine slices.

For the remaining candidates average a product of cosines over s∈I with normalized Lebesgue measure. Its arguments are πsΔXᵢ/K and πΔYⱼ/(sK), each cosine raised to 2M. Put a equal to the total number of variable coordinates. Choose M so that exp(−4M(η/K)²) < c/2, where c=(2M+1)^(−a), shrinking η below K/4 if needed. The Fourier coefficients before substitution are nonnegative, sum to 1, and the constant coefficient is ≥c. Each Fourier term has phase 2π(As+B/s) with A=n·ΔX/K, B=m·ΔY/K and integer rows of sup norm ≤M.

For this fixed I and c one can compute R such that the absolute normalized integral of any term with max(|A|,|B|)>R is <c/4. Here is an elementary bound. Write I=[u,v], u>0 and L=v−u. If |A|≥2|B|/u², the derivative of As+B/s is monotone with magnitude ≥|A|/2. Integration by parts on I gives integral magnitude ≤4/(π |A|) before normalization. Otherwise |B| dominates max(|A|,|B|) up to a constant depending only on u, and the second derivative has constant sign and magnitude ≥λ=2|B|/v³. Remove the subinterval where the first derivative has magnitude ≤√λ; its length is ≤2/√λ. On the at most two remaining intervals integration by parts with monotone derivative gives a further bound ≤4/(π√λ). Divide by L. These bounds tend effectively to zero in the indicated regimes.

Any frequency whose rows are constant on their respective affine spaces has A=B=0 and contributes positively. If all other frequencies are above R, the mean is at least 3c/4, while everywhere outside the simultaneous η-box it is <c/2. Some s thus transfers the witness. Consequently a dark candidate must have a frequency below or at R with at least one row nonconstant on its space. In particular that row takes an **integer** value at the candidate differing by at most KR from its value at the sample. Enumerate all nonconstant bounded rows on either axis, and all such bounded integer offsets, including zero. Each branch is a proper rational affine slice. This may admit surplus children but misses no dark candidate. Dimensions strictly decrease and the tree is finitely branching, hence finite. A zero-dimensional eligible node is the sample itself. This argument makes no identifiability assumption about route equations and does not equate a linear dependence with darkness.

### Choosing a primitive sample in affine slices

Without wall-gcd promises this is integer linear feasibility. With primitivity, solve the separate linear inequalities, equalities and parity condition per axis in all gap coordinates, including constants. Write the resulting Presburger set effectively as a finite union of linear sets b+Σℕvᵢ (nonnegative integer gap coordinates). Let the wall projection sum fine gaps between consecutive wall levels. Write a for the projection of b and gᵢ for the projections of its generators. The wall vector a is positive. If span(a,gᵢ) has rank one, the only possible positive primitive wall vector is the primitive generator of its ray; test that vector by integer linear feasibility.

In larger rank, reject this linear set if a prime divides all coordinates of a and every gᵢ. Otherwise for each prime dividing the content of a select nonnegative coefficient residues so b'=Σtᵢgᵢ is nonzero modulo that prime. Combine across primes by CRT, and add a suitable multiple of their product to one coefficient so b' is not proportional to a. Some two-row minor of (a,b') has nonzero absolute value J. Every common prime divisor of a+kb' divides J. For each prime dividing J, at most one k residue is forbidden if b' is nonzero mod that prime; if b' is zero mod that prime, a is nonzero there. CRT gives a nonnegative k avoiding all forbidden residues. The resulting positive primitive vector lifts via the chosen coefficients to the original linear set. This handles the affine offsets introduced by averaging. It never combines one axis's gcd with the other's.

### Implication for the rigidity task

Both normalized spans can now diverge while this decision still terminates, as long as τ₀ is supplied. The proof bounds the *rows in a recursion node* from a lit witness and handles every positive-dimensional surviving arithmetic slice there. It proves neither that actual dark flats are axis rays nor that the answer on the thin class is no. In the fat case a varying x-gap and varying y-gap can occupy the same cell. Inserting normal lengths then changes the transverse period of the other insertion, breaking the two additive error formulas. This is precisely the use of thickness in the transfer lemma. An effective bound on the local thickness of at least one counterexample, conditional on existence, would now suffice; it would be strictly less restrictive than supplying an upper bound on a bounding dimension. No room, ray or direction enumeration was executed.
