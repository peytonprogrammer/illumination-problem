# Local thickness, rectangle trees and free crossing positions

Take τ to be the side of the largest **axis-aligned** square contained in the closed room. This convention is needed for the coordinate-gap argument. The optical goal continues to concern distinct interior marks and excludes every contact with a physical corner.

## What can be classified without knowing the lengths

Use maximal horizontal runs, cut locally at reflexes as in [the parallel decomposition](whole-room-decomposition.md). A vertex of the resulting tree is a rectangle. On each of its top and bottom sides record the incident openings in left-to-right order. At either lip of an opening exactly one of the following occurs: the two runs end together, the lower run extends farther, or the upper run extends farther. In the first case that lip is a smooth physical wall point. In each of the other cases it is a reflex vertex. Strict simplicity excludes contacts between disconnected runs just at a lip. Two coincident ends at the same opening would make the runs identical and the seam removable. Each reflex occurs at precisely one such lip by inspecting the three occupied local quadrants. Consequently, if k is the number of rectangles and t is the number of doors having **two** reflex lips, then

    r = k−1+t,       0 ≤ t ≤ k−1.

Thus the nine-piece trees are especially restricted: every edge has one flush lip. At eight pieces at most one door has two reflex lips. At seven pieces at most two do. On any one side of a rectangle at most two openings can be flush: each must use one of its two ends, and distinct open intervals on that side are disjoint. Thus every vertex in a nine-piece tree has degree at most four, with at most two neighbors above and two below. If there are two on a side they attach at opposite ends with solid wall between them.

Here is a finite combinatorial parametrization, without enumerating rooms. Take a tree on k≤9 vertices, give each vertex a bottom and top level in weak order among at most ten horizontal wall levels, and a left and right endpoint in weak order among at most ten vertical wall coordinates. Interpret the resulting rank rectangles literally. Require positive dimensions, disjoint interiors, no point-only self-contact on the union boundary, a simple boundary cycle, and precisely the indicated positive-length horizontal adjacencies. Require the vertical sides to be physical and merge identical consecutive runs. Retain k−1+t≤8. These finite predicates give exactly the possible decorated trees up to coordinate orders and relabelling. Every actual room supplies its orders, and realizing each coordinate by its rank gives a lattice representative. A separate bound τ≤τ₀ is imposed on metric realizations. The parametrization does not assign a universal optical verdict to its entries.

## A crossing theorem using both sheets of a flush door

Suppose the successive horizontal runs on the two sides of a seam are (0,U) and (0,V), 0<U≤V, after translation or reflection. For any two marks in their interiors, one on each side, a legal path crosses the seam with **no vertical reversal** (the physical y coordinate advances monotonically). Other doors on their horizontal sides do not affect this claim. It applies in every metric realization of that edge. It also proves whole-room illumination for the one-rectangle and two-rectangle flush types; marks on the open seam in the latter case see marks in either closed rectangle by chords.

Write the mark abscissas X,Y and their distances from the seam a,b, with X on the U side. In developed coordinates join (X+2jU,−a) to (Y,b). At the seam the abscissas form the progression

    c_j = [b(X+2jU)+aY]/(a+b).

Choose j nearest to the one giving zero. Then |c_j|≤bU/(a+b)<U. Both rectangles fold c_j to the same physical position |c_j| on the same horizontal sheet. Up to the seam use the ordinary width-U folding. Beyond it the developed line lies between c_j and Y, entirely in (−V,V), so width-V folding gives the prescribed target. At c_j=0 the ray reflects on the continuous vertical wall through the seam; this is a legal smooth reflection, and the two one-sided folded legs have exactly the required velocity change. Its transverse velocity there cannot vanish as X is interior. There are no other intermediate horizontal interfaces. The same proof works after reversing the order of the two marks.

This proves every pair in adjacent runs across a one-reflex door is lit, irrespective of width, denominator or parity. In particular all adjacent-run interior pairs in every nine-piece tree are settled. It would be invalid to concatenate these witnesses at a freely chosen point in an intervening run: the two choices of j can give different velocities. For a concrete example of this underlying difficulty, the four-band cov279 proof needs a bottom reflection for certain end-to-end pairs, even when all three windows are allowed to vary. Its successful construction uses the width-(2q+3) transit and a simultaneous placement, as established in [thin-room.md](thin-room.md).

## What thickness bounds along the tree

In the rectangular grid of consecutive physical wall abscissas and ordinates, every occupied open grid box is wholly inside the room. An occupied box cannot have both gaps greater than τ₀ if τ≤τ₀. More strongly, inside any x gap longer than τ₀ every connected vertical run has height ≤τ₀, since the entire run extends across that gap. Interchange axes for the horizontal version. The boxes having *both* gaps ≤τ₀ have total area at most 81τ₀², since there are at most nine gaps per axis. A long-x box cannot share an edge with a long-y box: one common coordinate gap would then have to be both short and long. Consequently any path switching between these two kinds of boxes through edge adjacencies must traverse the bounded-area part. This is a geometric reduction to long straight strips and bounded junction regions. It provides no outgoing velocity at a junction.

Even allowing every crossing position in every open door, the named DB55 pair (1,1)–(9,1) excludes *all* horizontally monotone routes by the midpoint obstruction at x=5, and equal endpoint heights exclude all vertically monotone ones except the blocked chord. The saved turning path lights the pair. Thus even the union of the two tree-path tests without reversals is incomplete at τ=5. [Exact path](reversal.md) There is no established type on which **all** routes and placements fail. The missing result is simultaneous slope selection through separated junctions, allowing reversals; the finite parametrization alone does not limit the number of such visits.

## Fat follow-on

An inscribed τ-square really does force a large *decomposition piece*. Intersect it with the k≤r+1 rectangles of either parallel decomposition. One intersection has area at least τ²/k. Each side of that intersection is at most τ, so **both** sides are at least τ/k. This transfers a lower bound on thickness to a piece with known physical reflecting sides. A square drawn arbitrarily inside the room cannot itself serve as a reflecting chamber.

The remaining failure of inference is quantitative. The reviewed d124 depth test depends on the areas of the exterior attachments. D125 additionally needs the common legal access interval, its signed exits and clearance from other openings. The τ/k estimate supplies none of these data. In particular a proof using just a contained square has no license to reflect on an internal side of it. No polynomial access threshold in τ and r or counterexample to illumination follows here. A general thin theorem for arbitrary fixed τ₀ and a complementary fat theorem would already cover every area; an area census would be necessary only if the theorems left a quantitatively bounded exception range.
