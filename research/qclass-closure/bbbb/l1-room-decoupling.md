Advances: L1 — for two compatible regular end legs at one slope, an empty gate box forces a quantitatively bounded dual relation involving the single middle visit. The bound uses the actual clearances of the end legs and has no lcm. Counting away all cross-end relations fails even in the aggregated model. I do not obtain a bound in the full aperture widths alone.

### 1. Independent excursion counts

For a fixed box of dimension g, flatness-shortness gives |nⱼ|≤2wⱼ Flt(g)/d_min, by the mean-zero potential argument. If no such relation involves M, every such relation has n_M=0. In a model with aggregated end travels h₀+A k₀ and h₂+C k₂, A,C>0, a cross relation with n₀n₂≠0 then gives an affine line in the count plane, after fixing the coefficients and the other visits. It does **not** give finitely many pairs. Equal end widths, initial travels and delay quanta already give the relation (1,0,−1) at every k₀=k₂. More generally if an integral solution exists and the two count coefficients have opposite signs and rational ratio, an infinite progression of nonnegative solutions passes through it.

There is a useful weaker count. Put bⱼ=floor(2wⱼ Flt(g)/d_min) and P=∏_{j≠M}(2bⱼ+1). In {0,…,N}² at most P(N+1) pairs lie on these cross lines, since each coefficient vector fixes at most one k₂ per k₀. For N≥P some pair avoids them. This requires a *fixed* coefficient budget, genuine independent choices and nonzero count coefficients. In the actual gate graph every extra round trip introduces additional wall events, visit coefficients and clearance tests. A vector need not be constant on those visits. Both g and the coefficient budget change. Avoiding the lines of the aggregated model proves no geometric existence statement. [Potential bound and six-reflex lock](l1-total-resonance.md)

### 2. What the smaller-room result excludes

Seal a positive open door on a fixed orthogonal end. From an interior mark some finite regular first-door itinerary exists on an open interval of slopes. For two such ends one can choose a common slope first, then the two itineraries. The sealed-room minimal-direction proof supplies this statement even if multiple side or level reflections precede the first hit. Thus no internal obstruction excludes *all* end itineraries to the door. It can exclude every repetition of a particular motif whose visits are forced onto the wrong faces. For a rectangular end of width w and axial height L, for instance, the kth top contact from an upward start at depth a has transverse travel (a+2kL)s. It is the first exit exactly when it folds into the open door, all earlier top visits miss the door, and all preceding roof/floor hits avoid vertices and door endpoints. If 2Ls/(2w) is irrational, the top phases are dense, so a hit occurs; one chooses s outside the countable corner and endpoint directions. For an unrestricted end the same conclusion is an existence theorem for an itinerary, not permission to prescribe k. [Sealed-end theorem](README.md)

For specified oriented end itineraries, a dual vector supported on one end projects to a congruence satisfied by every actual realization of that end, even before attaching T. In particular it cannot certify emptiness by a missed projected interval if the end already has a regular witness. More strongly, two legs that work at the same slope satisfy *all* dual congruences with n_M=0 simultaneously. The following argument exploits that fact without enumerating counts.

### 3. A relation involving M from common end clearance

Use the provisional d230 lattice model for integral data, with the endpoint orientations fixed. Write the event box as J, the lattice as Λ, the base coset as v+Λ, and the generator for the middle winding as b. Assume the slab M is traversed once and that some slope supplies regular compatible legs at both ends. Compatibility here permits a real transverse winding across M; the integer winding is exactly the matching condition still to be imposed. Let x be the combined vector of end events, including both mouth crossings. Then

    x ∈ J,       x = v + λ + τ b,       λ∈Λ, τ∈ℝ.

Indeed the actual end legs give the integer side windings in every other visit. Keeping their slope fixed, choose the middle winding as a real variable to make the total developed endpoint displacement equal slope times total travel. This also makes each prefix event agree: the source leg determines the events before M and the backward target leg determines the events after M. In the dual formula, pairing with b gives −n_M up to the choice of orientation. [Gate-box formulas](../../illumination-generalization-2026-11-06/fable/directions-2026-11-09/d230-fresh-angles/README.md)

**Clearance-weighted lemma.** Set ρᵢ=dist(xᵢ,∂Jᵢ)>0, and F*=max_{1≤d≤g} Flt(d). If the coset misses J, then some dual vector ℓ with n_M≠0 satisfies

    (Σ ρᵢ² ℓᵢ²)^(1/2) ≤ C_g = (F*/2) √(1+(g−1)/4).

Here is the dimension argument. Subtract v+λ and scale coordinates by 1/ρᵢ. In the new coordinates the open unit ball centered at τb (with b also scaled) contains no lattice point. In dimension d, flatness of this ball gives a nonzero primitive dual of norm at most Flt(d)/2. If it detects b, finish. Otherwise cut by its kernel through the origin. This is a rational lattice subspace containing b and the center, so the section is again a full unit ball in its dimension and still misses its section lattice. Continue. For b≠0 the process detects b no later than dimension one; if b=0 the center was already a lattice point.

For the norm on lifting back, each successive primitive dual on the section extends to the original lattice dual because the section lattice is primitive (the quotient is torsion-free). Its orthogonal projection to that section is the chosen vector. Starting with the detecting lift, subtract integer multiples of the earlier lifted vectors in reverse order, rounding the component along each earlier orthogonal normal to within half its projected length. These operations preserve the pairing with b. The final section component has length at most F*/2 and the at most g−1 perpendicular components have length at most F*/4 each. Pythagoras gives C_g, and undoing the coordinate scaling gives the stated weights. This only needs flatness in the successive dimensions and the integer gate-box model.

In the potential notation, with ρ_min=min ρᵢ, Cauchy–Schwarz gives Σ|cᵢ₋₁−cᵢ|≤√g C_g/ρ_min. The mean-zero relation bounds every |cⱼ| by the same number. Since |n_M|≥1,

    μ_M ≤ (2√g C_g/ρ_min) Σ_{j≠M} wⱼ μⱼ .

Thus a dark cross-end pair obeys this bound on **each itinerary admitting such common end legs**. In particular the sealed-end theorem supplies at least one for an actual separating rectangle with fixed ends. This bound replaces the lcm in MQ/δ by the number of events and the clearance of a common realization; there is no demonstrated numerical improvement. If one could choose the legs with ρᵢ≥η dᵢ, the relation would have full-aperture width Σdᵢ|ℓᵢ|≤√g C_g/η. The entrance-window theorem establishes positivity, not a chart-uniform η or g. The lemma neither yields the original Flt(g) budget nor applies to arbitrary prescribed end legs.

**First stall in iteration.** The six-reflex two-gate chart already has a monotone itinerary with a single middle visit and disjoint end-slope sets. There is no x for that choice. Its roof alternative supplies x, but that operation changes the other visits and the coefficient bound; it does not constrain every slab on the original itinerary. Even bounds of one modulus by a sum of others on different itineraries do not bound all pairwise ratios without an independent control on the other terms. The room-level target still needs a bounded-complexity choice of end legs with a fixed fraction of clearance, or a substitute for that choice. No such general iteration, dark counterexample at eight reflexes, or aperture-only bound follows here. [Explicit chart and alternative](l1-two-gate-boundary.md)
