# Ends of a wholly dark deformation line

**Verdict.** A separating point pinch transfers darkness to a smaller chamber when both limiting marks belong to that chamber and its legal paths avoid the contact. For opposite-side marks, even a primitive same-parity BBBB sequence with six reflex corners can require an unbounded number of reflections to cross a single shrinking overlap. The named cov279 control below proves this for *every* path to its marked target. It gives an obstruction to the proposed endpoint argument. For opposite convex lips, the final theorem excludes a wholly dark component when the two marks can reach the contact in their sealed chambers and the two access paths share no proportional run width. It allows turns on both sides. The general case remains open.

## Which ends have billiard content?

Use the rational one-axis pencil of [accumulation.md](accumulation.md), with labeled walls, fixed ordinates and affine abscissas for both vertices and marks. Lighting is open relative to strictly simple rooms with distinct interior marks. Its proof gives a more useful boundary statement: any limiting witness with finitely many transverse bounces in surviving open edges, and all other parts clear of changing boundary, persists. Unfold its word and join the perturbed endpoints by the straight chord. Its ordered intersections stay in the same open edges by strict inequalities. A singular endpoint in the closure of the dark set only excludes witnesses transferable in this way. It need not define a billiard at all.

At finite parameter, partition the line at equalities of two nonidentical affine abscissas, including the marks. Between consecutive equalities the rectangular cell incidence, all signed lengths, and membership of the marks are constant combinatorially. Thus a finite end is rational and has one or more of the following events. Persistent coordinate ties are allowed. Harmless changes of order can lie inside the physical interval.

* **Unbounded parameter.** After reversing the parameter if needed, divide horizontal coordinates by t as t tends to positive infinity, keeping ordinates fixed. The limiting abscissas are the rational velocities. This is a billiard equivalence at each finite t, since independent axis stretches commute with both wall reflections. If the velocity polygon and marks are strictly physical with the original type, the endpoint is a regular rational parameter in the chart u+e/t. A lit limit excludes darkness by openness. Otherwise one has a degeneration of the kind below in that chart (a translation is removed first). Nonradial wall motion ensures positive limiting horizontal span when the original span diverges. An unbounded run alone gives no separating rectangular passage, and both chamber depths can grow at the same rate as its length. Indeed, starting at any strictly physical labeled configuration e, take a sufficiently close strictly physical rational horizontal vector u of the same order type, nonproportional to e, with the same vertical data. The pencil e+tu is physical for t≥0, since the defining order inequalities are linear. In coordinates divided by 1+t it stays between two nearby regular shapes. This also works by a small perturbation of a named control. Use d110 or d112 only when the actual chamber and threshold hypotheses hold. [Length tail](length-periodicity.md) · [Rectangular chambers and the meaning of d](resonant-gluing.md)
* **A wall vanishes.** If simplification produces a strictly simple lower-wall room, both limiting marks are distinct interior points, and a legal limiting path avoids every scar (the points or segments where boundary was erased, identified or split), that path persists. This proves induction at an ordinary corner collapse when all scars are genuine remaining corners. A scar on an open wall can be the only reflection point of the available witness. A surviving slit can cut across an otherwise legal chord. Lower-wall illumination by itself supplies neither avoidance condition. Apply an existing closure only to rooms and marks within its hypotheses, including any lattice or class restriction after normalization. In particular d100 replays two residual rooms and d102 verifies the Q-class closure. A rational limiting tuple need not have the required primitive lattice marks. [D100 scope](../../illumination-generalization-2026-11-06/fable/directions-2026-11-09/d100-check-residual/README.md) · [Q-class theorem](../twenty/README.md)
* **Nonincident walls meet.** Separate the incident sectors before asking which chamber contains a mark. Point contacts, positive-length contacts and simultaneous collapses are possible. At a horizontal level two neighboring runs can lose their last open overlap, while both run widths have positive limits. No long corridor is involved. The transfer lemma and control below treat a single point contact. They leave positive-length slits and simultaneous contacts open.
* **A mark leaves the admissible set.** At a stable interior collision a single convex disk gives a direct chord for all nearby parameters. The same argument works at an isolated stable smooth boundary point or convex corner when *both* marks approach it, using a local half-disk or quadrant in the varying room. At a stable reflex corner the local chord exists for angular separation strictly below π. For separation at least π the doubled sector of angle 3π forces a nonlocal excursion. With one mark reaching a smooth wall, a limiting path departing transversely into the room, followed by stable contacts, persists by the same unfolding test. A corner endpoint needs a departure with positive angular clearance and control of the perturbed initial segment. Neither follows from illumination of interior pairs. With fixed ordinates a first isolated contact in the relative interior of a stable *horizontal* edge is impossible: its ordinate was already the mark's and its abscissa would already have been between the edge endpoints. Vertical walls and corners remain possible. [Reflex-corner calculation](resonant-gluing.md)

## Pinch transfer for marks in the same chamber

Suppose the limiting closed pieces A and B are strictly simple, intersect just at p, and off p their interiors and open edges persist locally with their labels (merging collinear edges is harmless away from the merge point). At an isolated contact of two closed simple orthogonal chambers whose intersection is exactly p, their occupied local quadrants must be opposite single quadrants: adjacent occupied quadrants across the two pieces would share a boundary ray. Thus both lips are convex vertices. If S and T limit to distinct interior points of A, any legal A-path avoiding p transfers. Indeed its compact trace has positive distance from p. Take small disjoint neighborhoods along each contact and positive clearance from unused boundary on the intervening closed subsegments. The unfolding argument above now applies entirely in this stable region. The contrapositive says that a dark sequence forces *every* legal A-witness to visit p. If p is a vertex of A, these visits are forbidden, so the limiting pair is dark in A. The same proof works for finitely many scars if the witness avoids all of them.

In the elementary pinch of two stacked chambers with a single horizontal overlap ending at opposite lips, cutting the open overlap before passage to the limit splits each reflex lip into a right angle and a straight angle. Thus the two sealed pieces together lose two reflex corners at the cut. If the sealed pieces retain their individual types up to the limit, both have fewer walls than the original. Extra collapses require a separate check of the pieces and scars. This transfers a dark pair to a smaller room in the same-side case with vertex contact. To conclude by induction, the smaller-room theorem must cover the limiting marks. Rational positions can fail to be lattice marks, or to share the required parity, after primitive normalization; a theorem for all distinct real interior marks would suffice. For marks in opposite components neither chamber contains the pair. Darkness of an approaching sequence gives no smaller pair by this proof. In particular independent paths to the contact would still need compatible developed directions.

## Opposite-side control in the named cov279 staircase

Use the rows of [d69's cov279](../../illumination-generalization-2026-11-06/fable/directions-2026-11-09/d69-bbbb/decomp.py). Move just the left wall of its third row. The open horizontal runs, in order of height, are

| Height band | Horizontal run |
|---|---|
| (0,2) | (0,2) |
| (2,3) | (2−t,4) |
| (3,4) | (2,5) |
| (4,6) | (4,6) |

At the interfaces glue the open overlaps. At t=1 these are exactly the original cells. For 0<t<1 the room is strictly simple. At each of the three interfaces the left and right ends both step right, giving six reflex corners, hence 16 walls. Fix S=(1/2,1), T=(3,3). T is interior on an artificial band cut. At t=0 both marks remain interior in their respective chambers, which meet only at p=(2,2), a convex corner of each. The separating seam has width t and zero longitudinal length.

**Primitive BBBB arithmetic.** Choose q=12j+4 and t=3/q with j≥1 and stretch horizontally by q. The horizontal wall lengths include q and 2q−3, so their gcd is one. The vertical gcd is one. The marks become (q/2,1) and (3q,3), both even-odd. The area is 13q+3, exceeding 4r+4, so every parity class has a regular site by [d69 §1](../../illumination-generalization-2026-11-06/fable/directions-2026-11-09/d69-bbbb/README.md). The pinch imposes no failure of BBBB on the approaching rooms. At the nodal endpoint the strictly simple room classification ceases to apply.

**These pairs are lit.** The marks lie one unit of height below and above the seam. On the positive horizontal sheets their unfolded seam abscissas are the translate
     
    (1/2+3)/2 + 2 Z + (2+t) Z.

Indeed a crossing at c from the lower rectangle uses a copy c+4i; in the upper rectangle write the crossing as c+2(2+t)j and the target on its positive sheet. Equating the two unit-height horizontal displacements yields exactly the displayed translate, up to generator signs. An abscissa in (2−t,2) gives one legal upward shot, reflecting only in vertical walls in the two open bands. For the chosen q the spacing is gcd(2q,2q+3)/q=1/q, less than the overlap 3/q. No search is needed.

**Every lighting word diverges in length.** In the coordinates (2x,y), at t=0 every wall reflection preserves both coordinate parities on integer points. All images of p, S and T respectively have parities (0,0), (1,1) and (0,1). The vectors from S to any developed copy of p and any developed copy of T therefore have determinant odd. No developed line can pass through all three. In particular this obstruction includes trajectories which visit the opening several times.

Quantitatively, take any legal shot with m reflections at t>0. Develop its target and its first crossing of y=2 into the upper chamber using respectively the full word and the corresponding prefix. These points are collinear with S. Evaluate the same formal reflections at t=0, replacing the crossing by p. This comparison does not require the word to be legal at zero. Ordinates stay fixed. A moving reflected wall in the scaled x coordinate moves at most 2t, so each reflection contributes at most 4t to the error in developed abscissa. Replacing the seam point costs at most another 2t. Each developed ordinate relative to S is bounded by 6(m+1), by summing the vertical travels of the physical segments. The nonzero integer determinant at zero thus satisfies

    1 ≤ 6(m+1)(8m+2)t ≤ 48(m+1)²t.

Consequently the asymptotic bound shows that even the shortest lighting word has m+1 ≥ 1/√(48t). The named control has distinct interior limiting marks, strictly simple lower-wall chambers, positive chamber widths, and no surviving bounded-word witness. The parity obstruction concerns passage through the *node* in the limit. It proves no dark pair at positive t. In fact the two-band formula also lights every irrational t here by density.

## What remains

A wholly dark line is excluded if either end has a transferable witness. Outstanding before the opposite-lip test below are:

- Matching the limiting mark coordinates to the hypotheses of smaller-room induction.
- Opposite-side access with a hidden limiting mark, or a width class shared by every pair of access legs. The [one-class calculation](shared-width-and-stops.md) resolves the reference-phase congruence, while alternative actual paths can remain.
- Positive-length slits and simultaneous degenerations.
- A wall collapse whose witnesses all meet a scar.
- Boundary marks without a transverse departure, including one mark reaching a corner and two marks approaching a reflex with angular separation at least π.

The point-pinch control explains why a bounded-word argument fails even with two interior limiting marks. There is no resulting bound on area. The later [finite point-scar result](point-scars.md) narrows several of these cases under fixed-chamber hypotheses.

## Opposite-side transfer with turns before the opening

There is a way to use an interior parameter even when *every* bounded word fails at the endpoint. The two limiting legs may have different slopes, may reverse vertical direction, and may revisit a band. The test below concerns the widths of the bands they actually use.

**Theorem.** Consider a rational fixed-height pencil as above, with rational fixed mark ordinates. At one finite end suppose two strictly simple chambers meet at opposite convex quadrants of a single point p. At nearby physical parameters they join through one open horizontal overlap shrinking to p. Assume the rest of each sealed chamber is stable along the legs below. In each limiting chamber suppose the interior mark can reach p without an intervening corner. Cut the chambers horizontally at every wall and mark ordinate. Take the positive widths of the open runs traversed by the two legs, continued on the interior side of the parameter endpoint. If no width used on one leg is a constant multiple of a width used on the other, the physical line has a lit member. Consequently it cannot be wholly dark. Proportional widths *within* either leg are allowed. Since all constant widths are mutually proportional, at most one leg may use any constant-width run.

More precisely, use the runs visited by the continued legs to an interior mouth point. If a limiting contact occurs on an artificial cut, include all runs actually visited on continuation. If there are other simultaneous losses of boundary, require the chosen legs to avoid their scars. The argument applies at any transcendental parameter sufficiently close to this end with the stated width separation. By d116 it then excludes darkness at every irrational parameter in the component. The direct argument does not use d89 or a bound on r.

**1. Two first arrivals with independent slopes.** Choose the first visit to p on each limiting leg. Its earlier compact portion avoids p, and its final segment enters the corner transversely from inside its quadrant. An axial approach along the boundary cannot be a final segment from the interior of a convex quadrant. For parameters close enough to the end that the mouth lies within both final chords’ clearance cones, take either leg to the midpoint of the open mouth, moving its terminal point slightly within that mouth if necessary. Unfold the fixed finite word. Its earlier contacts persist on surviving edges and avoid the new opening. Its last chord remains inside the sealed chamber up to the selected mouth point: locally the corner is a quadrant and the chord approaches from strictly inside both supporting half-planes. Both legs are first arrivals. The two horizontal signs at the mouth are opposite when both rays are directed *toward* it, because the limiting quadrants are opposite. Their vertical signs are also opposite. The magnitudes s_A,s_B of horizontal travel per unit vertical travel can differ. At fixed physical parameter choose a common mouth abscissa in this open interval avoiding contacts on artificial cuts at vertical walls. For a fixed word these extra coincidences occur at isolated abscissas: the developed position at a positive intermediate vertical travel varies by a nonzero fraction of the terminal displacement. Initial and final marked levels use one-sided states.

**2. Synchronize the two histories.** Fix a nearby transcendental parameter t. For each run traversal j on either chosen leg record its positive vertical travel d_j and doubled width P_j(t). The cuts make d_j rational. Repetitions may be kept as separate coordinates. Launch both original rays with their own initial signs and a common positive slope magnitude s. The horizontal advance on traversal j, in turns around its doubled run, is s v_j modulo one, where v_j=d_j/P_j(t). Positions and signs at successive cuts follow by folding from the previous state. In a neighborhood of the original vector of phases for each leg, the sequence of horizontal levels, wall turns there and run choices persists, including first arrival at the mouth. Extra vertical-wall bounces inside an open run meet smooth edges. This is continuity of a finite sequence of unfolded band crossings with strict endpoint inequalities.

The desired torus vector has coordinates s_A v_j on A and s_B v_j on B. It lies in the closure of the positive real flow s v modulo one. Indeed, by the character criterion this requires every integer vector k with Σ k_j v_j=0 to annihilate the desired vector modulo one. At transcendental t such a relation is a rational-function identity. Group the terms by proportional affine polynomials P_j. Distinct nonconstant classes have distinct poles, so their contributions vanish separately by taking residues. The possible constant class then also sums to zero. Width separation places each class on one side only. It follows that both partial sums Σ_A k_j v_j and Σ_B k_j v_j vanish separately, proving the claim. This argument allows all the internal phase relations created by returns to the same run.

**3. Turn approximate arrivals into an exact billiard path.** Choose positive s with the combined phases arbitrarily close to the desired vector. Let c_A(s), c_B(s) be the two first-arrival abscissas. Their mismatch can be made arbitrarily small. Throughout a fixed sufficiently small increment interval about each such s, both arrivals remain in the opening with the same terminal signs. The interval radius can be chosen uniformly over sufficiently close approximations at this fixed t: take a smaller neighborhood inside the phase chart and use the fixed vector v for increments. On that interval the derivatives of the two arrival abscissas are σ_A D_A and σ_B D_B, where D_i is the total *absolute* vertical travel along leg i and σ_i its terminal horizontal sign. To see the formula, unfold each traversal using its initial horizontal sign. Every vertical-wall fold acts by a sign on the accumulated displacement; after all traversals the common terminal sign multiplies their sum. Horizontal-wall reflections preserve horizontal velocity. Since σ_A=−σ_B, the absolute mismatch derivative is D_A+D_B. A correction of magnitude |c_A−c_B|/(D_A+D_B) gives equality while preserving first arrival. Reversing the target leg now matches both velocity components at the same interior point. This supplies a legal shot.

**Which marks can fail the access assumption?** For each fixed sealed limiting chamber, only finitely many interior positions can be hidden from its convex contact vertex. On the four-copy translation unfolding leave the regular lift of p available as a source and mark every other convex-corner lift as forbidden of order zero. Apply Lelièvre–Monteil–Weiss Theorem 2 at this regular source. A segment may return to the source lift; retaining the part after the last such return gives a leg without an earlier visit to the physical p, which has just one lift. Folding reaches the interior mark and never crosses another corner. Thus when *all* run-width classes in the two chambers are disjoint, a wholly dark line with stable pieces would require at least one limiting mark to belong to this finite exceptional set in its own chamber. This chamber-level corollary requires at least one chamber with all its run widths varying. It does not apply to cov279: each chamber has a constant-width run. The narrower itinerary theorem applies there because the upper leg uses only the varying run. Finiteness does not decide membership for a prescribed lattice mark. [LMW, Theorem 2 and §2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1407.2975)

**Rational illustration of the matching mechanism in cov279.** The theorem treats transcendental parameters; the following rational shots follow by direct arithmetic. Take the mouth abscissa c=2−t/3. The two direct legs have s_A=3/2−t/3 and s_B=1+t/3, and admit slope increments 4 and 4+2t per full horizontal winding. Adding m full windings on each leg makes the slopes identical exactly at t=3/(12m+4). Both arrivals retain their original signs and positions. These are the primitive same-parity parameters proved above to require diverging word length. Thus the matching argument can supply an exact path even where the determinant excludes *every* bounded list near the end.

**Residual for a single separating pinch.** With interior marks and stable convex lips, a wholly dark component requires a hidden mark at a lip or a proportional width shared across every pair of admissible access histories. Shared width is a necessary escape from this test, not evidence of darkness. When there is a shared class, the character test in step 2 still applies: it asks that (s_A−s_B)Σ_A k_j v_j be integral for every relation k. Whether varying the common mouth point or the legs always satisfies these conditions for r≤8 remains open.
