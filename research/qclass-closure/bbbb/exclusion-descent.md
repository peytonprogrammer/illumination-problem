# Cov4181: the exclusion descends, but the blocked site stays regular

**Verdict.** On the standard pillowcase base, cov4181 refutes the assertion that an excluded primitive class must become corner-only *at its relevant site* on a nontrivial factor. Nevertheless its exclusion has an explicit four-sheet quotient proof. That quotient has a corner-only fiber over a *different* site. Thus cov4181 does not refute the weaker “some site” assertion. The proof below identifies exactly which property of the other site obstructs the developed path.

## The unique proper quotient

Use the room's half-translation double Q and write B for the unit pillowcase. Relative to S=(2,3) use the pole labels a (source), b (horizontal first step), c (diagonal), d (vertical). Both S and T=(4,3) are over a. The primitive parity (0,1) sees only a and d at lattice hits.

Replaying d66's sheet calculation from the 28 named cells gives intermediate degrees over B of 1, 4 and 28. The only proper intermediate sphere N has four blocks of seven sheets. In one marking its branch permutations, with fixed letters omitted, are

    A=(01),   B₁=(01)(23),   C=(123),   D=(12).

Both lifts of S project to the singleton letter 3 at a and both lifts of T to the singleton letter 2. Over d the two-cycle of D is a **regular** point of N. The identity quotient Q also has regular points over d. Equivalently, merging every upstairs d-transposition and closing under monodromy identifies all 28 sheets. Only the degree-one pillowcase remains, and it identifies both marked images. Over c, however, N has one zero and one pole and no regular point. ## A four-sheet exclusion theorem

Suppose a four-sheet pillowcase quotient has local cycle types at a,b,c,d as displayed, and the two upstairs endpoint orbits project to the two *different singleton poles* of A. Then no trajectory joining those orbits can have first primitive step from a to d. This statement allows all corner continuations in the projected arc graph, so it does not rely on treating a quotient pole as a physical stop.

**Proof.** Choose any simple pillowcase arc of the specified class. Take labeled tails adapted to this arc, with the peripheral loops in standard order. Their permutations have product identity and retain the indicated conjugacy classes. The two marked poles remain the two singleton letters of A. Pass to the action on the three partitions of four letters into two unordered pairs. Its kernel is the Klein four group. B₁ acts trivially there, C acts by a 3-cycle, and A and D act by transpositions. The product relation forces those last two transpositions to be different: if they agreed, the relation would force the 3-cycle to be identity. In S₄, identical or disjoint transpositions have the same action on the three partitions. Hence the supports of A and D meet in **exactly one letter**, in every adapted basis.

The preimage of the arc has one edge per sheet, with endpoints identified according to the cycles of A and D. One singleton of A is fixed by D as well, so its edge is a component by itself; the other singleton is in a different component. A developed trajectory would project to a connected walk along this one arc, since repeated primitive steps traverse the arc alternately forwards and backwards. Such a walk cannot connect the two singleton poles. This proves the exclusion at all slopes in the class. The group action on the three pairings is used to prove an identity; it is not a three-sheet intermediate cover. [Arc dictionary](per-class-converse.md)

Thus in cov4181 the obstruction is already present on N, even before one asks which quotient walks lift legally. The corner-only site c is never visited by a class-d trajectory. Its 3-cycle constrains the two branch permutations at the sites the trajectory *does* visit. The regular point over d survives, and the two marks always sit in different components of the lifted arc. This also explains why zero reflex-stop cost and descent to a proper quotient can coexist. [Independent physical exclusion](dyadic-classes.md) · [Stop-cost replay](orbit-exclusion-cost.md)

## Exact structural charge and what BBBB excludes

For a factor of inner degree k with the four cycle types above, n=4k. Each of the k points above the c-zero is unramified over that zero, because its composite local degree is already three and physical local degrees over the pillowcase are at most three. Consequently the room spends at least k **distinct physical reflexes over c**, giving r≥k and W≥2k+4=n/2+4. They are an indirect cost, even though the class-d relaxed route family in cov4181 is empty. For cov4181 this charges seven of the eight reflexes. These are the same seven already counted for its empty physical class c; adding the two charges would be wrong. At r≤8 this mechanism requires n≤32. It cannot occur in BBBB, since its proper quotient is clean over c.

Here BBBB means that all four canonical equivalence relations B_x are universal. Merely finding a physical regular point over each site does not establish this hypothesis. The distinct-singleton condition in the four-sheet argument was checked for cov4181 only. The charge gives n≤4r for this cycle portrait, sharpening n≤4r+4 by four cells. The zero charge and isogeny argument also appear in [canonical transfer](canonical-transfer.md).

More generally, a normalized orthogonal BBBB room cannot have any proper pillowcase factor of outer degree 2, 3 or 4. Indeed every class on such a factor must contain a regular point: otherwise the partition merges all the relevant upstairs two-cycles, contrary to universality of the canonical equivalence. At degree two, four required transpositions already exceed the sphere ramification budget of two. A degree at most four cannot contain a 3-cycle and a 2-cycle at the same site. Therefore this factor has no zeros. It is a quadratic sphere with four simple poles, whose orienting double is a torus. That torus would factor the absolute period map of the room unfolding through an unramified isogeny of degree equal to the outer degree. The normalized room has absolute periods exactly 2ℤ², the lattice of the base torus, ruling out such a proper isogeny. The degree comparison follows by taking the orienting double on both sides; both double covers have degree two. This excludes small factors, not exclusions arising on a primitive room itself. ## What a path invariant proves

For a normalized room, pure endpoint holonomy modulo 2ʲ cannot by itself certify this exclusion. Differences between abscissas of vertical mirror lines generate ℤ: they include the horizontal edge lengths, of gcd one. Products of their reflections generate translations by 2ℤ horizontally; the analogous statement holds vertically. For any primitive v and same-parity S,T, S+2v is therefore a *formal* developed image of T. This statement concerns the reflection group, without an itinerary legality assertion. A parity test on formal endpoint displacements cannot distinguish the missing arc component. The four-sheet proof retains its sheet labels. Invariance along legal straight continuations does not imply invariance under arbitrary monodromy, which can concatenate different arcs.

**Scope of the reduction.** In the reviewed physical corner-only class, the all-area theorem covers arbitrary real, distinct interior endpoints in strictly simple orthogonal lattice rooms below 22 walls. The separate d64 result and the checkout's replay cover the proper-canonical-factor branch for interior lattice pairs, including different parities. The README does not extend that latter statement to arbitrary real marks or to mixed 45° walls. [Q-class closure](../README.md) · [All-real theorem](../twenty/README.md)

**Open case.** The relevant-site version fails. The different-site version survives this test, and the four-sheet mechanism above is excluded from BBBB. Neither the example nor the proof classifies primitive exclusions or exclusions supported on a factor of outer degree at least five. In particular, absence of this mechanism gives no conclusion about all BBBB pairs. The next structural question is whether in a BBBB factor the two marked images can stay in different connected components of the full preimage of *every* simple a-to-d arc, even when all quotient vertex continuations are allowed. This is a sheet-action question strictly separated from legality of lifts; it does not address failures that occur only on lifting.
