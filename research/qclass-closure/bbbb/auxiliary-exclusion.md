# When does removing regular-site swaps give an actual fold?

**Result.** Killing regular-site swaps in an auxiliary permutation representation gives a corner-only sheet factor exactly when the kernel has more than one orbit on physical sheets. In BBBB an auxiliary quotient that kills those swaps is invisible on single-sheet labels. In the large primitive case even an arbitrary nonabelian auxiliary group of this kind is impossible. This specifies which part of the cov4181 argument survives passage to a general room. It does not convert all path invariants into group representations.

## Kernel test, including auxiliary groups that do not act on the sheets

Let G be the transitive monodromy on the n sheets of the pillowcase double Q. For a site x let g be its local permutation, whose cycles have lengths 1, 2 or 3. Set K to the normal closure of g³ in G. Then the K-orbits are the finest G-invariant partition merging all regular transpositions at x. Indeed g³ flips each regular pair and fixes every other sheet. Its conjugates generate precisely the equivalence relation obtained by transporting all these identifications. On the resulting quotient no two-cycle at x remains. This recovers the canonical partition Bₓ.

Now let ρ:G→H be any surjection on which ρ(g) has odd order. Since g⁶=1, this is equivalent to killing g³. The orbits of ker ρ give an actual quotient of the sheet action, possibly just one block. They carry odd-order inertia at x, hence no regular two-cycle. For a chosen sheet i their number is exactly [H:ρ(Gᵢ)]: identify the sheets with G/Gᵢ, and send hGᵢ to ρ(h)ρ(Gᵢ). Its fibers are precisely the kernel orbits. In particular a nontrivial auxiliary group can carry no sheet information whatsoever. In BBBB, K and hence ker ρ are transitive, so ρ(Gᵢ)=H at every sheet. A stabilizer of an *ordered pair* of sheets is smaller; the identical coset formula applies to its orbit but would give a factor of the ordered-pair cover. That is the distinction a descent proof must bridge.

In the primitive range n≥49, r≤8, d99 gives A_n≤G. A BBBB site has a two-cycle, so K is nonidentity. The normal subgroups of A_n and S_n then give A_n≤K. Consequently any auxiliary image in which even one site has odd-order inertia has order at most two. In particular there is no S₄ quotient, nor a nonabelian substitute that works by making some site odd. The conclusion concerns representations of *sheet monodromy*. It does not classify invariants of the direction-marked affine action or prove that all three primitive classes light a pair. **Cov4181 checks both sides.** Its four-sheet representation is onto S₄: the swaps (01),(12) and the conjugates of (12) by (123) generate all transpositions along a connected graph. At d its inertia is a transposition, so this representation does not kill g_d³. The canonical quotient at d is the one-block base. At c the auxiliary inertia is (123), so the representation does kill g_c³. Its kernel has four orbits (the actual blocks): the four-sheet action is faithful, and any finer kernel-orbit partition would be discrete or another proper partition. The latter is ruled out by the named block computation; the former would embed the transitive 28-sheet group in S₄, impossible since 28 does not divide 24. At c upstairs there are no regular swaps at all, so K_c itself is trivial. The four-sheet factor explains why the two projected marks remain in different arc components at d even though there is a regular point there. ## What the first possible clean-free proper quotient must look like

Let Q→N have degree k>1 and let N→B have degree five in a normalized orthogonal BBBB room. Every site downstairs contains a two-cycle. The only cycle types are U=(2,1,1,1), V=(2,2,1) and Z=(3,2). They contribute 1, 2 and 3 respectively to the ramification of N→B. The sphere formula gives a sum of eight over four sites, hence the numbers of U and Z sites agree. There must be a Z site: otherwise the orienting double of N is a torus and gives a proper isogeny factor of the primitive absolute torus map. Thus the only multisets are U,V,V,Z and U,U,Z,Z. The sheet group of N is S₅: prime degree gives primitivity; at Z, cubing the permutation gives a transposition, and its conjugates form a connected graph by primitivity.

**Cost and uniqueness.** At a Z site x there are only a degree-three point and a degree-two point downstairs. Neither has any room for further ramification above it because the composite local degrees are at most three. Thus x upstairs has exactly k reflexes, k regular points on Q, and no convex corners. One Z site costs k distinct reflexes; two cost 2k. Since n=5k, the respective area ceilings at r≤8 are **40 and 20**. These are ceilings for this factor type, independent of darkness.

Moreover for a specified Z site there is **at most one** such five-block system. The support of gₓ³ is exactly the union of the two blocks over the regular two-cycle, and its complement is the union of the other three. Two proposed partitions with that same Z site both refine this identical two-set partition. Their join is therefore a proper G-invariant coarsening of each. A transitive action on five blocks is primitive, so each must equal the join. In particular a proposed five-sheet positional obstruction cannot choose different five-sheet quotients at will while keeping this mixed site. At Z all regular upstairs marks project to the unique regular point; two marks of that parity cannot be separated merely by putting their images in different arc components.

**Remaining gap.** The Z fiber in degree five contains both a zero and a regular point. Cov4181's proof used a different site whose inertia was purely odd. The arguments above neither rule out arc-component separation in the two five-sheet signatures nor show it happens; even proving it impossible would leave higher degrees and exclusions arising only on lifting. The next concrete test at the theorem level is whether, in U,V,V,Z or U,U,Z,Z, every pair of *distinct projected endpoint images* over a U or V site shares a component of some adapted arc in a prescribed class. This is a finite permutation question without any assumption about corners hit upstairs. No room, ray, direction or word discovery search was run.
