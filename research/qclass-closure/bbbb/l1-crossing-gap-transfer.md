# What an occupied crossing changes

Keep distinct interior lattice marks anchored on their respective pieces of a wall-and-mark order chart. A vertical cut inserts horizontal length t and a horizontal cut inserts vertical length u. In d184 disjoint strips kept their transverse folding periods. At an occupied crossing a horizontal run can have width D₀+t at the same time as its vertical travel is a₀+u. ## Exact transfer through an occupied crossing

Use the first two horizontal runs of the named [st279, m=5](stabilizer-orbits.md), leaving its upper runs fixed. Write the bottom run as (5−D,5) at heights (4−a,5), with D≥5 and a≥4. The next run is (4,7) at heights (5,6), followed by (5,8). These are independent insertions into the lower square of st279, to the left of x=4 and above the original source level y=1, in a frame translating the lower-left material while holding the third and fourth bands fixed. The cuts intersect inside that square. Set S=(4,5−a), T=(6,6). T is on an artificial section inside the room. S is one unit above the bottom. Odd a gives same-parity marks. The walls remain strictly simple, with the unit steps upstairs preserving axial normalization (the bottom-edge changes do not remove the unit horizontal offsets or unit-height bands).

Prescribe upward travel through these two runs, allowing *every* horizontal winding, either initial and terminal horizontal sense, and every open point on their common seam. This chart has an exact test. Write z=5−x for the seam coordinate, 0<z<1. The lower z interval is (0,D); the upper is (−2,1). Lower source images are c=±1 mod 2D. Upper target images are e=−1 or −3 mod 6. Choose the chart physically at the seam and allow either sign of the slope v=dz/dy there. Unfolding gives z≡c+av mod 2D and z≡e−v mod 6. Consequently the route works **if and only if**

    (0,a+1) meets c + a e + gcd(2D,6a)ℤ
    for at least one c∈{1,−1}, e∈{−1,−3}.

Bézout recovers the common v. The seam ends are physical corners. All lateral reflections during the open runs are on smooth walls. There is no fixed artificial cut sheet excluding alternative lifts in this test. At the original D=5, a=4 it succeeds, since the spacing is 2 and the window has length 5.

**Failure is not semilinear in the two insertions.** Take any prime a>5. If a does not divide D, the spacing is at most 6, strictly less than a+1, so the route succeeds. At every D divisible by 3a the spacing is 6a. The four residues ±1−a and ±1−3a mod 6a all miss (0,a+1). Suppose the failure set were Presburger. After quantifier elimination the finitely many congruences would have a common positive period P in D. For each fixed a, the truth of every linear inequality stabilizes at sufficiently large D, so P would be an eventual period on *every* row. Choose a prime a>max(5,P) and a sufficiently large multiple D of 3a. There is failure at D and success at D+P. This contradicts uniform eventual period. The proof uses no enumeration. It is in a single marked order chart after restricting away from the initial ties, and works on the same-parity subdomain.

This excludes a route-by-route semilinearity theorem even for one seam and all its windings. It also gives failures outside any finite union of affine lines: each prime row contains infinitely many failed D, and finitely many lines cannot contain infinitely many points on each such row. It does not exhibit a dark pair. A transfer permitted to change vertical itinerary can leave this chart.

## An effective transfer on separated scales

The crossing still permits a *sequential* use of d184. First insert the horizontal travel t by a vertical cut of the marked base. Let M>0 be its crossing count, P₁ the lcm of all crossed doubled heights, X,Y the developed base travels and q the reduced slope denominator. For integer t>XP₁q−X/M, use the d182 rounding construction. Its new travels are exactly X′=X+Mt and Y′=Y+MkP₁, with k nearest to tY/(XP₁). In particular Y′≤Y+MtY/X+MP₁/2. If the cut is missed retain the original shot. Pure axial shots are handled directly.

Now choose the second cut in the required marked y-gap of the intermediate room, using the transposed margin construction. Of the horizontal runs at this level, just the one containing the first cut has increased width, from w to w+t. Let P₀ be an integer common multiple of the doubled widths of *all other* runs (1 for an empty list). A safe second period is 2(w+t)P₀. This covers every interval even if the intermediate shot visits many more of them than the base shot. Its transposed denominator is at most Y′, since its new endpoints are integral. Thus

    u > 2(w+t)P₀ (Y+MtY/X+MP₁/2)²

suffices after the first threshold. If the second cut is missed the shot already persists. The room obtained is the intended insertion cross: each insertion acts separately on its own coordinate. The intermediate cut placement does not change the marked room if it stays in the designated gap. This gives an explicit cubic threshold in t. Interchanging axes gives the other extreme. These two estimates give genuine effective transfers but leave the diagonal and a much wider intermediate range uncovered.
