# Can corner alignments bound the area of a dark room?

**Outcome.** Even three exact corner hits that determine every horizontal proportion once the vertical proportions are fixed need not bound a single denominator. Here is a family inside the true BBBB core and the additional arithmetic hypothesis that would suffice.

Take an integer m ≥ 3 and four consecutive columns on the same floor. Give them widths m, m+1, m, m+1, and let the ceiling increase by these same increments, starting from height zero before the first column. Write x_j for the cumulative widths and L=x_4. The room is a strictly simple ten-wall staircase. The horizontal wall gcd and vertical wall gcd are both one. It has three reflex corners, exactly (x_j,x_j) for j=1,2,3. Its area exceeds 4r+4=16, so it is true BBBB by the project's normalized-room reduction.

Set S=(1,1) and T=(L−1,L−1). They are distinct interior lattice points of the same parity. The direct segment meets all three reflex corners. In unit bounding-box coordinates the three incidences say X_j=Y_j, with X_j the normalized horizontal break and Y_j the normalized ceiling on its left. These are three independent equations on the three horizontal breaks for fixed vertical data, and conversely. Yet X_1=m/(4m+2) has reduced denominator (4m+2)/gcd(m,2), unbounded with m. Even imposing full separate rank on the two axes leaves the three common proportions free.

This pair is lit. Reflect T across the floor to (L−1,1−L). The segment from S to this image hits the floor once at x=1+(L−2)/L. Before the bounce it stays in the first column. After folding, its height is strictly below y=x up to T: the difference is affine and positive at the bounce and zero at T. Each column ceiling is at least x throughout that column, so no seam corner or ceiling intervenes. Thus the alignments record a failed shot in an illuminated pair. They cannot be promoted to constraints imposed by darkness.

**One sufficient arithmetic condition.** Subdivide each coordinate axis at wall and mark coordinates. Normalize each vector of positive gaps by its total. In these variables every specified unfolded corner collinearity is a bilinear equation in the horizontal and vertical gaps: each developed coordinate is an integer linear form in the gaps of its own axis, and collinearity is a two-by-two determinant. With at most W walls, two marks and a prescribed bound K on the reflection length, only finitely many such polynomials occur, effectively listable. If a rational dark configuration were an isolated real solution *in its room proportions* of some such system (project away the marks first), its normalized room denominators would have a computable bound depending only on W,K. Indeed eliminate the marks by real quantifier elimination, compute isolated points of each projected semialgebraic set, retain rational coordinates, and take a common denominator per axis. Clearing denominators gives a lattice realization; dividing the axis gcds gives the primitive room. Finite choices of coordinate order and coincident coordinates cause no problem. This is a sufficient condition, with no usable numerical bound asserted.

**Missing implication.** Darkness excludes every legal itinerary, but a failed itinerary can miss a doorway by a strict inequality. It supplies no corner equality in that case. Even equality certificates must isolate the room after *both* axes vary. There is no proof that a minimal counterexample supplies such a system of effective complexity. Failure of one-shot compression and this example rule out two shortcuts, not the existence of a small-counterexample theorem. No computational discovery search was run.
