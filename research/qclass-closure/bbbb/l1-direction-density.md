Advances: L1 — straight-line reachability of an off-class regular midpoint on the marked pair component.

**Result.** For a fixed compatible starting lift and off-class candidate on the same component, the holonomy directions of legal-to-traverse *broken* paths are dense in RP¹. The directions of *straight* legal segments to that candidate are either empty or dense. Both statements hold at every wall count. The first gives no existence test for the second. ### Two direction sets with fixed endpoints

Pass to a connected translation double cover of the normalized marked component U (or one component of the pullback of the pillowcase's torus cover). It is a finite translation cover of the square torus with lattice periods after rescaling. Choose compatible lifts s of the marked starting branch and b of the candidate. Mark all preimages of physical forbidden corners in either projection; there are finitely many. The marked lattice endpoints, singularities and forbidden points lie over torsion points. Relative holonomy now has a well-defined vector, up to simultaneous sign downstairs. In the first set allow piecewise straight paths, with repetitions allowed, avoiding forbidden points in their interiors. In the second require a single straight segment throughout. A segment in the second set projects to the two synchronized legal half-paths and gives the stated midpoint.

**Broken paths.** Join the endpoints by a broken path as in the previous note. At a regular interior point of that path, use the torus punctured at the finitely many exceptional images, including branch values. Two based loops avoiding those points have independent horizontal and vertical periods. Each has a positive power that lifts closed at the chosen sheet, because it permutes a finite fiber. Inserting arbitrary signed powers of the lifted loops yields holonomies v+iP+jQ, with i,j integers and P,Q independent. Directions of this translated lattice are dense: approximate points tending to infinity on any prescribed ray to within a bounded fundamental parallelogram. These loops are closed upstairs but have nonzero developed displacement. Nothing makes the resulting path straight.

**Straight segments.** Suppose one legal segment exists with direction u. The subgroup Γ of SL(2,ℤ) preserving the translation cover and its decorations has finite index: one may first fix the finite torsion grid of base marks, then stabilize the finite gluing and marking data for covers of this degree. Passing to finite index also fixes s,b individually, their local sectors if needed, and the forbidden set. Equivalently subdivide to a square tiling with all these points at vertices and stabilize the labeled tiling type under change of basis. An affine lift then sends the segment to another legal segment between the same endpoints. This is the seed hypothesis in d151; its positive lower density is an arithmetic counting statement. For angular density there is a short independent argument. For any rational direction q distinct from u take an integral parabolic fixing q (a conjugate of the unit shear). Some positive power lies in Γ. Its iterates send u towards q. Rational directions are dense, proving the claim without inferring angular density from positive lower density alone. Taking the finite union over compatible lifts and candidates gives the same empty-or-dense dichotomy.

**Why angular openness does not supply the seed.** For a fixed lifted path class the developed endpoint fixes one displacement v. The chord reaches b only at direction [v]. Varying the slope slightly can preserve a finite sequence of regular wall crossings while moving the final point off b. In fact the set of all endpoint-matching directions is countable here (relative periods lie in a lattice translate), so the straight set has empty interior even when dense. To make an open-window argument work one needs, for the *same* class, its displacement direction inside its own legal corridor of directions. Density of directions obtained by varying the class does not imply that diagonal match. Conversely, for the actual straight set, hitting any prescribed nonempty open interval is equivalent to having a seed at all.
