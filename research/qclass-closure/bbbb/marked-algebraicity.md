# Algebraicity with regular marked points

**Theorem (reduction to genuine zeros, for review).** In a complex stratum of nonzero holomorphic Abelian differentials with ordered distinct regular marks, any relatively closed GL⁺(2,ℝ)-invariant set is a finite union of relatively closed algebraic subvarieties. Algebraic here means over ℂ. No effective equations or bounds on their coefficients follow.

**Inputs checked.** Eskin–Mirzakhani–Mohammadi Theorems 2.1 and 2.2 give orbit closures and the finite-union conclusion on the area-one slice. Lelièvre–Monteil–Weiss §2.2, Theorem 4 and the sentence immediately following it explicitly allow regular marks in this dynamical input. Their exact remark is “Note that the results of [EMM] work for strata with marked points, i.e. they allow αᵢ = 0.” This is asserted there without a separate proof. Each EMM piece supports a full-support ergodic affine measure, so almost every point has dense orbit in that piece. Restore positive scaling to obtain the complex affine loci. Filip Theorem 5.4 supplies algebraicity for genuine-zero strata upstairs. His Remark 1.7 discusses the locally finite immersed branches. The marked extension below is our deduction. [EMM](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1305.3015) · [LMW](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1407.2975) · [Filip](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1311.2350)

**The two finite maps.** Write A for the marked stratum. For at least two regular marks take connected cyclic triple covers branched exactly at those marks. Assign local monodromies in ℤ/3 of sum zero: use pairs (1,−1) for even cardinality, and one triple (1,1,1) plus pairs for odd cardinality. Each assignment defines covers: the punctured-surface relation holds with zero handle monodromies and the image generates ℤ/3. For one mark in genus ≥2 include a chosen genuine zero as a second branch point (choosing its label is finite). With no marks one uses Filip directly. A genus-one differential with a single regular mark up to translation is an elliptic curve with origin and nonzero differential; GL⁺ acts transitively.

Let T remember the cover, deck generator and downstairs labels. The forgetful map p:T→A is algebraic, finite and surjective on coarse spaces. Explicitly, choose the cubic root of the line bundle of the weighted branch divisor (weights 1 or 2), form the cyclic cover and normalize. Roots are a torsor under the Jacobian's 3-torsion, finite étale locally on the base. Finite label choices and the scalar deck ambiguity do not affect the assertion on coarse spaces. For descent, first work on a finite level chart of the marked moduli stack admitting a universal curve. The root classes form the pullback of multiplication by three on the relative Picard torsor. Étale-locally choose a representative line bundle and a cubing isomorphism, build the normalized cover, and take the resulting classifying map to upstairs coarse moduli. On the double overlap these maps agree: representatives differ locally by a base line bundle and a choice of scalar trivialization, and changing a cube-root trivialization gives an isomorphic cover after étale refinement. Morphisms to the coarse space descend as an étale sheaf. The construction is invariant under level changes and isomorphisms of the marked base; equivalently the forgetful stack morphisms induce the two morphisms of coarse spaces by the coarse universal property. Finiteness downstairs can be checked on the level chart then passed to its finite quotient; upstairs the finite inertia description below gives the same coarse correspondence. There are 3^(2g) choices per assignment before quotienting by automorphisms. Unbranched zeros lift to three zeros of the original order, and every fixed point has order congruent to two modulo three. Every designated branch point has ramification index 3. A point of order a below has order 3(a+1)−1 at total ramification. Every regular mark thus becomes a genuine zero. Forget the generator and labels upstairs to get q:T→B, where B is an ordinary genuine-zero stratum. Both maps commute with the real-linear action in flat charts.

Here q is also finite **onto its closed image**. Over a smooth upstairs curve of genus at least two, its automorphisms form finite inertia, and preserving its differential and satisfying σ³=1 are closed conditions. The nonidentity condition is also closed in this inertia locus: its identity section is open by unramifiedness and closed by separatedness. Given a generator, the quotient and the differential on it are determined. In a smooth family with this generator, its fixed points are finite étale locally over the base: the derivative at an isolated fixed point is a nontrivial cube root, so the fixed-point equation is transverse, and the curve is proper. The action on the finite set of distinct upstairs zeros is locally constant after labeling them; their orders are fixed in B. Thus the required fixed-point profile, local characters, quotient genus and cycle data select open-and-closed parts, with finite label choices. These facts also show directly that a convergent sequence in B with these data has a subsequential limit carrying them. The quotient in families and descent of an invariant form are algebraic (locally at a branch point use z↦z³). This gives the stated finite map; it does not require the surface to have a unique such quotient. By 0E9C the stable-curve stack is proper and Deligne–Mumford. Its diagonal is proper and unramified, hence quasi-finite and proper, hence finite. Base change along the diagonal gives finite inertia. [Stacks Project, Theorem 109.25.3](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0E9C)

**Recover the correct marked component.** By the marked dynamical input it suffices to treat one orbit closure M, a closed pure-dimensional complex analytic set of dimension d with finitely many local branches. It is analytically irreducible: the connected group preserves each irreducible component (near a smooth point off the others this holds for all elements close to the identity, hence for the connected group); the component containing a point of dense orbit must contain its closure. Put Y=p⁻¹(M). It is closed analytic, pure of dimension d by the local covering description up to finite orbifold groups. The finite proper map q has closed analytic image q(Y) of dimension d, by the proper mapping theorem and finiteness of fibers. This image is invariant, so ordinary EMM 2.2 and Filip 5.4 make it algebraic. Set Z=q⁻¹(q(Y)); it is algebraic, of dimension at most d by finiteness. Then p(Z) is closed algebraic of dimension at most d and contains M. An irreducible closed analytic subset of that same dimension is a whole irreducible component: a proper analytic subset of the connected smooth locus of an irreducible complex variety has smaller dimension. Here the connectedness of the smooth locus of an irreducible complex algebraic variety also follows from algebraic/analytic connected-component comparison applied to that smooth locus (SGA 1, Exposé XII, §2). Hence M is algebraic. This dimension step recovers the correct component even when forgetting the deck data identifies several quotient structures. It avoids assuming that the unbranched forgetful map from a configuration space is proper.

**Application.** D134 accepts the algebraicity input required by d86 and d132. The Schwarz–Christoffel parameter map in d132 is a separate assertion. D116's main local-linearity proof does not use Filip. When algebraicity is required for a marked quadratic stratum with at most simple poles, first use the holonomy double of Filip Remark 1.6, retaining labels on all lifts of the marks and simple poles (which are regular upstairs), then apply the marked Abelian result.
