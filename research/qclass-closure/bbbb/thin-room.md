# A modular lighting theorem at a thin separating cut

**Theorem.** For every member of the normalized d117 cov279 stretch, q=12j+4, j≥1, every distinct same-parity pair of interior lattice points is lit. More generally the proof works for every integer q≥4 coprime to 3 in the same coordinates. The developed vertical travel is at most six. For marks at different heights a nonhorizontal certificate can be chosen with primitive vertical component at most three and absolute horizontal travel O(q²). The room has height six, six reflexes, area 13q+3 and a separating opening of width three. This includes pairs on the artificial band boundaries. The global proof uses neither a first-access slope interval nor an exhaustive direction test.

## Matching the two sides with one freely adjustable band

Write s for signed developed horizontal travel per unit of developed vertical travel. A horizontal band with left end l and width u has a phase of period 2u. At a physical coordinate x and sheet e=±1 use absolute phase E(l,e,x)=l+e(x−l). Propagation adds ds for vertical travel d, including when the physical vertical direction is downward. At a horizontal mirror only the physical vertical velocity reverses. At an interface transfer x and e together. The two phase representatives differ by an even integer if the walls are integral. Paths must cross the open overlaps and all prescribed horizontal reflections must occur in the interior of solid walls.

Suppose the outer legs have been placed legally from both marks, leaving one transit of an intervening band of width u and positive integral depth d. Measure the target leg in reverse, recovering the *forward* sheet for matching. Let P be an integer such that adding P to s preserves both placed outer legs' event positions and sheets. For integral vertical travels one may take the lcm of twice their run widths. The proof is by folding each run separately. Newly inserted vertical-wall bounces inside an open band are smooth. In the intervening band let F be required exit phase minus entry phase minus ds.

**Exact criterion.** A shift s+kP joins those legs iff F belongs to gcd(dP,2u) times the integers. Indeed it must solve dPk≡F modulo 2u. In particular if the gcd is two, integral endpoints of the same horizontal parity need only satisfy Ds∈2ℤ, where D is the total developed vertical travel. The outer folds and all changes of origins contribute even integers and sign changes on integral endpoints preserve parity. Thus this parity condition gives F∈2ℤ on the *actual sheets*. The criterion does require legal outer legs. Large common divisors can lock their phases. This identifies the obstruction for the specified placements and shear, without implying darkness.

## The four-band crossing

The horizontal runs, bottom to top, are (0,2q) for 0<y<2, (2q−3,4q) for 2<y<3, (2q,5q) for 3<y<4, and (4q,6q) for 4<y<6. At integral heights 2, 3 and 4 retain just the open overlap. Consider S=(X,1) and T=(Y,5) with X≡Y modulo two. Launch downward, reflect at the bottom, then increase y to T. Thus the four vertical distances are 3,1,1,1. The second band will be adjusted last.

1. Choose c from 2q−1, 2q+1 so that 3Y+2X−2c is nonzero modulo 2q. The two values differ by four, so at least one works. Put s=(c−X+4qi)/3. After the bottom reflection the first seam coordinate is 2q−1 on the sheet given by folding c. The bottom contact cannot be a corner since 3(X+s)=2X+c+4qi is odd.
2. Trace backward from T on the positive sheet. At y=4 its phase relative to 4q is Y−4q−s modulo 4q. Increasing i by one shifts it by −4q/3. Choose the nearest of these phases to zero, giving signed distance at most 2q/3. It cannot equal zero, since 3Y+X−c is odd. Its physical x₄ therefore lies strictly between 4q and 5q. Keep its forward sheet e₄.
3. Now change s by multiples of 4q. This preserves the bottom leg and x₄ with its sheet. In the third band trace back to y=3. The phase relative to 2q is e₄(x₄−2q)−s modulo 6q. Its three possible values are spaced by 2q. Select one within q of zero. It cannot be zero: modulo 2q this phase equals Y−2s, since every change of sheet representation between these two runs differs by a multiple of 2q. Vanishing would force 3Y+2X−2c≡0 modulo 2q. Thus 2q<x₃≤3q<4q, strictly inside the required overlap. Keep the forward sheet e₃.
4. The remaining shift P=12q preserves these placed legs. In the intervening band the period is 4q+6 and gcd(12q,4q+6)=2, since q is coprime to three. The total vertical travel D=6 satisfies 6s∈2ℤ. Explicitly F≡±Y−X−6s≡0 modulo two. The matching criterion supplies k, including the correct horizontal velocity at both mouths. The entire construction is periodic in s with period 12q(2q+3), so one may choose 0≤s<12q(2q+3). The primitive vertical component divides three.

The only intermediate horizontal wall used is the bottom. Every other change of band has just been placed strictly inside an overlap. This is why matching developed endpoints also gives a legal physical path. The wide first and last bands need not be replaced by sealed chambers with a common first-hit time.

## The other pairs exhaust the room

Marks at the same height have a direct chord within the single open interval at that height. The remaining possible height pairs are (1,3), (2,4) and (3,5). Each has one unit of vertical travel on each side of one seam. If the run widths are u,v, choose endpoint images U,V on either sheet. The crossings on the positive seam sheet are exactly (U+V)/2+gcd(u,v)ℤ. This follows by averaging independent endpoint images of periods 2u and 2v. An open overlap strictly wider than the spacing always works. Bézout gives the two image indices and hence a prescribed slope.

For (1,3) the gcd of 2q and 2q+3 is one and the opening has width three. For (2,4) the gcd of 2q+3 and 3q divides nine and is coprime to three, hence is one; the opening has width 2q. For (3,5) the gcd is q and the overlap is (4q,5q). A failure means the crossing translate coincides with the endpoints modulo q. Flipping the image of either mark changes the translate by its distance from the respective left wall, up to sign. Both flips can fail only when both abscissas are multiples of q. Their open ranges then force X=3q and Y=5q. For this pair take s=3q/2 from (3q,3), cross y=4 at 9q/2, reflect at the top y=6, and return to y=5. At the cap the unwrapped abscissa is 15q/2, strictly between successive mirror lines; after total vertical travel four the final abscissa folds from 9q to 5q. This completes the proof. The two-band Bézout solution can be reduced modulo the lcm of twice the widths, O(q²).

## Exact controls and limits of the height conjecture

The [replay](thin-room-replay.py) chooses residues by nearest-integer formulas and a modular inverse, then folds every constructed path from its source, checking each intermediate event and the target. No direction or word discovery is involved. For q=16,28,40,52,64 the respective totals are 2,182, 6,922, 14,326, 24,394 and 37,126 same-parity pairs, including respectively 481, 1,513, 3,121, 5,305 and 8,065 four-band pairs. These finite checks supplement the proof for all q. DBas at its native length has the independent one-cylinder all-real result; the saved direction (7,−2) for (1,3) to (11,3) replays with opening heights 13/7 and 9/7. DB55 at its native length has 64 opposite-chamber same-class pairs, all replayed by the existing free-sheet construction; same-chamber pairs have direct chords. D127 did not independently check either control. Neither control needs the four-band arithmetic. [DBas proof](convex-cylinder.md) · [DB55 certificate](twist-overlap.md)

**Why height alone does not give the suggested modulus.** Already on the bottom leg in this very family, take an interior lattice mark (X,1), initially upward, with direction (a,1) and integer a. Its first crossing at y=2 passes the tiny opening exactly when X+a is congruent to one of 2q−2, 2q−1, 2q+1, 2q+2 modulo 4q. The cyclic gaps in this set are 1,2,1,4q−4. The unique largest gap implies least period 4q in a. Keeping height, turn sequence, number of corners and primitive vertical component fixed does not bound even this first-door period. The interval geometry inside a fixed strip uses twice its *own* transverse width. A different successful route could have a different modulus. In the four-band proof it is the final gcd that stays small, while the preserving shift grows with q.

For a general height-h room the surviving requirements are to place the two outer legs on legal sheets and to control gcd(dP,2u) in at least one intervening band. Multiple separated narrow apertures, or widths with a large common divisor, can defeat these particular tests. Even a monotone upward passage can fail here: take X=2q−1,Y=6q−1 at heights 1,5. A single unit step from X into the lower opening forces s within four of 0 modulo 4q (either initial sheet). One backward unit step from Y then has folded position within five of 6q, outside (4q,5q) for q≥16. The bottom reflection above cures exactly this positional obstruction. No corresponding universal choice of reflections or intervening run has been proved.

## Fat rooms and effectivity

The named normalized odd crosses have r=4, bounding width and height both 2b, and every contained axis-aligned rectangle has shorter side at most four. There is no two-mouth separating corridor. In the canonical parallel decompositions a long horizontal arm has a remainder extending a distance of order b in the same axis, so the all-squares first-hit travel T* is at least of order b, while its depth is at most 2b. It cannot meet the quadratic depth requirement for large b. The narrow central vertical piece of the vertical decomposition has traversal depth 4; rotating interchanges the two cases. The d125 compact-aspect large-rectangle hypothesis likewise cannot follow from both bounding dimensions growing. The crosses themselves have a separate lighting theorem. Thus a bounding-box threshold alone cannot force the proposed piece geometry. [Cross geometry](general-gluing.md) · [Lighting proof](cross-placement.md)

There is no global N(r) from this argument. The constructive question left by the proof is whether an arbitrary bounded-height room admits a choice of outer legs for which at least one band has enough phase freedom to absorb their mismatch. A width-independent period of the first-door acceptance set would already contradict the exact family above.
