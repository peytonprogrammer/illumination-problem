### Where 49 enters

Write zₓ for the number of reflex corners of physical class x. In the primitive case d81 §2 proves either G contains Aₙ or n≤6zₓ for **each** positive zₓ. The nonexceptional fixed-point-ratio estimate is applied to gₓ², which moves exactly 3zₓ sheets. D81 treats the exceptional product actions separately before drawing that dichotomy. At 49 the latter alternative is impossible because zₓ≤8. If two classes contain reflex corners, the smaller has at most four, so 25 suffices in that case.

In the imprimitive case choose a maximal block system of block size k and degree m=n/k on blocks. Normalization gives kZ≤8 for the total number Z of quotient zeros. D81 §3 makes the block action alternating-containing using Jordan's theorem or Theorem G, then proves transitivity on ordered external sheet pairs via the matching or spare-block lemma. An external marked pair gives d≥n−k≥2n/3; if all eight distinct sheets are in one block, d79 gives direct visibility. The cutoff is the first integer beyond several different obstacles. In particular at 48, (k,m)=(2,24) with four quotient zeros at one pole and (4,12) with two at one pole are equality cases of the block-action test m>6z. At (8,6) the spare-block proof requires m≥7. Divisibility matters: 49 is odd (so the k=2 threshold of 50 is harmless), and no multiple of four between 49 and 51 or of eight between 49 and 55 needs its row's larger threshold. At 22, k≤8 and m≥3 leave only k=2,m=11 in the imprimitive case. The residual quotient multiplicities before using the degree-11 group list are z=2 or 3 at the least positive pole multiplicity; four 3-cycles do not fit on eleven blocks. ### A prime-degree improvement

Use Burnside's classical prime-degree theorem: a transitive permutation group of prime degree p is doubly transitive or is permutation-isomorphic to a subgroup of AGL(1,p). A normalized rectangle has area one, so a room here has a reflex class. In the affine alternative its nonidentity element gₓ² of order three fixes at most one sheet. For p=23 there is no such element at all, since 3 does not divide 23·22. For p≥29 it fixes p−3zₓ≥p−24>1. Thus G is doubly transitive. Every ordered pair of distinct sheets lies in one orbit. The marked union contains such a pair and excludes the diagonal, proving d=p−1.

Write cₐ=vₐ+3rₐ for the corner contribution at the source pole. For a dark same-class pair the hidden off-class count H satisfies H≥max(0,2d−2r−(n−cₐ)/2). At these primes it therefore satisfies **H≥2p−18−⌊p/2⌋**, already 17 at p=23. An algebraic count does not ensure a legal midpoint.

### What still holds at composite degrees

Each of the four source incident sheets is paired with all four target incident sheets in the marked union. Both projection fibers have size d on generic sheets, so d≥4. If d=4, those sixteen pairs exhaust both the incoming and outgoing degrees there. They form an isolated complete bipartite component on four sheets per side. Transitivity sends it to components covering the entire first coordinate, so 4 divides n. In particular d≥5 when 4 does not divide n. This argument uses occurrence of all local marked branches, without assuming an external orbit. Substitution in the preceding H inequality can give only H≥0 in the unclassified cases.

**Area 22 (d208).** In the primitive case the degree-22 list gives double transitivity and d=21. Otherwise k=2 and the primitive block action has degree 11. The affine case cannot contain a 3-element. Among the remaining possibilities outside the alternating-containing case, the only genus-zero type multiset from M11 (also covering the PSL(2,11) possibility) needs six quotient zeros. The budget 2Z≤8 allows only four. Thus H contains A11; the matching lemma and R1 give a single orbit on ordered external pairs. A marked external pair exists, hence d≥20. For darkness this gives H_hidden≥40−16−11=13; it does not supply a realized midpoint. ### Transferring to the twisted curve

Over a common regular base point, let L≤G₁×G₂ be the joint monodromy of f and τ∘f, where τ translates by the nonzero parity difference. Both projections of L are surjective; the two permutation groups are conjugate copies of the original monodromy after choosing sheet labels. The marked union contains a four-by-four set here too: transport toward the source pole along one short regular arc, with its translated arc at the target pole. Each of the two physical lifts per mark contributes two nearby sheets. Taking every branch through every pair of lifts includes all sixteen combinations. Thus the unconditional 4 bound, improved to 5 when 4 does not divide n, transfers verbatim. On a dark pair **Hₑ=2dₑ** by the twisted intersection count, so at least 8 candidates are hidden (at least 10 in the latter case). All these candidates are regular. The same degree bound gives different hidden counts in the two constructions. [Midpoint budget](midpoint-necessity.md) · [Twisted intersection count, reviewed in d207](l1-bbbb-area-bound.md)

Two sufficient conditions give a much stronger transfer.

1. **Primitive action and unpaired reflex support.** Suppose some pole x has zₓ>0 but z at x+e is zero. The square of the peripheral loop then acts as (u,1) with u≠1. The kernel on the second coordinate projects to a nontrivial normal subgroup of the first primitive group. Its orbits form a block system, hence it is transitive there. Together with surjectivity in the second coordinate this makes L transitive on all n² ordered pairs. Thus dₑ=n.
2. **Natural alternating or symmetric action, n≥7.** If either projection kernel is nontrivial the same argument applies. Otherwise L is the graph of an isomorphism. For these natural actions every automorphism is induced by relabeling the n letters (the exceptional degree six is outside the interval). The graph then has two orbits on pairs, a matching and its complement. A four-by-four marked set cannot fit in a matching. Hence dₑ≥n−1.

For each prime p≥29 either d81 already gives G⊇Aₚ or it forces every reflex into a single class (two positive classes would give p≤24). In the second case every nonzero e moves that class to a reflex-free class. This proves the claimed transfer at all six primes, with Hₑ≥2p−2 under darkness. At p=23, d208 uses the degree-23 group list. In the M23 case each order-three element has at least six 3-cycles, leaving room for only one reflex class. Hence dₑ=23 there. In the natural alternating or symmetric case dₑ≥22. The high twisted bound at area 22 does not follow from the untwisted matching lemma.
