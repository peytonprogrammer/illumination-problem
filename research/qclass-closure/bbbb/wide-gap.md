# What a gap of 31 guarantees

A gap of 31 excludes a clean physical fiber and, at r≤8, a proper clean quotient. This implication alone gives no lighting theorem in BBBB, where those exclusions already hold at every gap. I cannot prove that a wide-gap room has no totally dark lattice pair, even for same-parity interior marks. Two more precise conclusions are available.

## Slab thickness and the clean-quotient bound

In an open vertical slab between consecutive wall lines, the cross-section is a union of disjoint open intervals. Let h be the sum of their integral heights and G the gap. At each intermediate integer abscissa, one interval of height H contributes **exactly H regular lifts of each ordinate parity** to the room double. Indeed its lattice endpoints have weight one, its H−1 interior levels weight two, and the alternating sum of these weights is zero. There is no vertical wall at this abscissa. Different components have distinct endpoints by strict simplicity. Summing, for each pillowcase site x there are at least h floor((G−1)/2) regular lifts in the slab. The horizontal version exchanges the axes.

For a normalized proper clean factor of inner degree k with a downstairs zero and J nonempty clean sites, the reviewed sphere ramification budget gives

    h |J| floor((G−1)/2) ≤ 2k−2,     k ≤ r.
    G ≤ 2 floor((2k−2)/(h |J|)) + 2.

Thus 30 is the worst upper bound from this count at r=8; it requires h=1, |J|=1 and k=8 to attain 30. Height-sum at least two already gives G≤16, and h|J|>2k−2 gives G≤2. These are bounds for a specified obstruction, not thresholds for illumination. For a physical clean site the right-hand ramification allowance is zero and G≤2. **Two is false for proper clean quotients.** Named d64 room 1843 has horizontal wall lines y=0,1,5,6, hence gap 4. Its degree-five quotient has cycle type (3,1,1) at d, so that site is clean. The inner degree is four, W=12, and the physical pair (1,2)–(3,2) is lit by a horizontal chord. Thus even a normalized room with a verified class exclusion and clean proper quotient can exceed two. This gives a lower bound of four on a universal clean-quotient gap cap without a total-darkness hypothesis. ## Where the proposed illumination proof stops

Take the reviewed cov279 stretch q=12j+4 with q≥28. The four horizontal runs, in order of increasing y, are (0,2q), (2q−3,4q), (2q,5q), (4q,6q), with seam heights 2,3,4. There is a supporting-line gap (0,2q−3) of width 2q−3. In this slab the room is precisely the bottom rectangle of height two. Take S=(1,1), T=(4q+1,5). The rooms are normalized BBBB by d69 and their area 13q+3. The two interior marks have the same parity.

**T has no direct sightline into this strip.** Such a segment would cross y=2 at some u in (2q−3,2q). At y=4 its abscissa would be (u+2(4q+1))/3 < (10q+2)/3 < 4q. The actual open seam at y=4 begins at 4q. This obstruction persists for arbitrarily large gap. These marks *are* lit by the reviewed reflected modular construction. The example only refutes the direct-access premise. ## Size of the proposed residual

Count orthogonal rooms up to translation with axes and orientation fixed. Let S_ab be the number of reduced strictly simple d73 skeletons with W≤20, a horizontal gaps and b vertical gaps, 1≤a,b≤9. Positive assignments preserve simplicity. The **exact skeleton-times-length formula** before filtering BBBB is Σ S_ab P_a P_b, where P_j=Σ_(d=1)^30 μ(d) floor(30/d)^j counts gcd-one gap vectors. The axis wall gcd equals the gap gcd: each wall difference is a sum of gaps, and traversing the boundary expresses every difference of corner coordinates as a sum of signed wall lengths. Area≤64 exceptions to BBBB must subsequently be filtered. The published d73 skeleton counts were area-pruned, so substituting them here would give an undercount. [d73 representation and enumeration scope](../../illumination-generalization-2026-11-06/fable/directions-2026-11-09/d73-bbbb-21-36/README.md)

Two numerical bounds quantify this without a skeleton run.

* **Lower bound for true normalized BBBB rooms.** One fixed 20-wall skeleton with ten distinct supporting lines on each axis suffices. Such a skeleton exists by independently separating coincident supporting lines in the named d153 three-chamber polygon by sufficiently small rational perturbations, then taking coordinate ranks. Each wall is moved parallel to itself and each vertex is the intersection of its two moved walls. Positive edge lengths and positive separation of nonincident edges persist under sufficiently small perturbations; thus the skeleton is strictly simple. Assign its nine gaps on each axis values from 9 through 30 with gcd one. There are Σ μ(d)(floor(30/d)−floor(8/d))⁹ = 1,204,775,361,390 choices per axis. An occupied grid rectangle alone has area ≥81, so every assignment is BBBB by d69. Unique gap vectors give distinct rooms up to translation. **This one skeleton contributes 1,451,483,671,412,405,102,732,100 rooms.** Modulo rotations and reflections the lower bound loses at most a factor of eight. All have interior same-parity pairs. [Named skeleton seed](parity-shortening.md) · [d69 §1](../../illumination-generalization-2026-11-06/fable/directions-2026-11-09/d69-bbbb/README.md)
* **Upper bound for all normalized rooms.** With W=2m, root the counterclockwise boundary at a vertical edge. Encode in cyclic order the m vertical wall coordinates, followed separately by the m horizontal coordinates in the corresponding order. These lists determine the polygon. With a distinct lines there are onto(m,a)=Σ_i (−1)^(a−i) binom(a,i)i^m rank assignments to a list and P_(a−1) normalized gap assignments. Each valid polygon has m different rooted encodings because a strictly simple boundary repeats no oriented edge. Thus Σ_(m=2)^10 floor((Σ_(a=2)^m onto(m,a) P_(a−1))²/m) = **682,785,601,599,904,908,850,120,054,910,338,316,419** bounds the count from above. The upper bound includes invalid rank lists. The raw maximum number of length choices per skeleton is 30¹⁸, since there can be nine independent gaps on *each* axis.

These are bounds, not an exact numerical BBBB census. Even the one-skeleton lower bound rules out a room-by-room check on the existing scale. A viable finite check would need one certificate to cover many length assignments. No rooms, directions or words were enumerated.

**Decisive missing step.** Produce a legal common phase match, allowing route changes, for arbitrary attachments to at least one wide slab, or show total failure forces a clean factor. The smallest universal wide-gap illumination threshold is unknown. The geometric count sharpens the clean-quotient bound in thick slabs and room 1843 rules out the proposed uniform improvement to two for that mechanism.
