# Gluing through a long corridor

Take strictly simple orthogonal lattice end chambers A,B with aligned vertical mouths of the same positive integral width w. The intervening rectangle has length L and solid long sides. Its attachment must produce a strictly simple room. All marks below are distinct real interior points, including points on open mouths. Let D be the larger Euclidean diameter of the sealed chambers, in the original lattice units. Set

    m = 4⌈D²⌉,    H = 1 + 2F_(3m),    Z = 36wH².

Both chamber areas are at most D². Since w≤D, replacing w by D gives a diameter-only threshold. For L≥Z, d124 lights every opposite-chamber pair. It also lights a chamber mark to any corridor mark at depth z≥Z from that chamber. The latter follows from the one-leg interval in d124: its width for one leg is at least 1/(12H²), and the derivative of the phase is z+a with first-door travel a>0. This covers all real marks, however close to a critical level. [Reviewed arbitrary-chamber estimate](effective-chamber.md)

Rectangularity in d112 gave an arithmetic progression of portal-wall visits at spacing 2d. Increasing the slope past a lip selects the preceding visit, making every first-entry jump downward. The contracting bay in d113 changes the itinerary before the portal wall and gives an upward jump. D124 replaces the jump argument by a single interval of slopes on which both first-hit itineraries persist. A corridor longer than the stated threshold sweeps through a full phase period on that interval. This replacement proves the opposite-end assertion only. D112 used direct visibility separately inside each rectangular end. [D112](resonant-gluing.md) · [D113](general-gluing.md)

## A finite collar suffices

For each end A form the chamber with an outward strip of width w and depth Z. Require both of the following in that test domain.

1. Every pair inside A has a legal shot avoiding the distal cap.
2. From every interior mark of A to every strip target of depth 0≤z<Z, including its open mouth, there is a legal shot avoiding the distal cap.

The same two requirements apply at B. **If both ends pass, every pair of the assembled room is lit for L≥Z.** A cap-avoiding witness in the test domain embeds in the actual room. More strongly, after its first entry into an empty strip the sign of horizontal velocity cannot change without a distal reflection. Thus a witness for (1) stays in A and avoids reflecting on the deleted mouth. A witness for (2) enters once and goes no deeper than z. A tangential approach running along the interface can be retained if it is physically interior. All other pairs are covered by d124 or by a chord in the closed corridor between physical interior points. Two points on one open mouth can be joined along that mouth. This proves the assertion, including L=Z.

This test is sufficient. A pair failing it could use the remote chamber and return. Merely proving both *sealed* chambers fully lit leaves both requirements unverified. Nor is this a finite census: each collar condition quantifies over real marks.

**A class where both requirements follow.** Suppose extending the open mouth to arbitrary depth gives a rectangular hall with up to four rectangular arms, each occupying a full side as in d161, and the distal cut is the terminal cap of such an arm. The remainder of that arm can include part of the original sealed chamber. For two marks lying in a single bar the straight chord avoids the cap. For marks in adjacent arms the d161 shot reflects only from parallel side walls between an arm's mouth and the depth of its mark. It avoids every terminal cap. Seam points are covered by the same bar or seam chords of d161. This proves (1) and (2). A rectangular end with an arbitrary partial mouth also passes, by the chamber-to-strip proof of d112 at every depth and the interior chord. These two sufficient end conditions may be mixed independently. Hence the whole-room diameter tail applies, for example, to a rectangular end opposite an L-shaped end whose mouth extends a full terminal arm. The d112 bound L>4d remains much sharper when both ends are rectangles. This argument uses neither a room search nor a wall-count hypothesis. [Cap-avoiding arm proof](adjacent-arm-illumination.md)

## Area and the first remaining case

The bound W≤20 limits r to eight and a parallel rectangle decomposition to at most nine pieces. It does not force one piece to be a separating corridor with small sealed chambers. In the named odd crosses, gcd(4,b−2)=1 for odd b, the area is 16b−16 and the short scale stays 4 as b grows. All four arms terminate in caps. A transverse cut through an arm separates a rectangle ending in that cap from the rest, so if that rectangle is called a chamber, the other chamber still contains long arms of comparable diameter. Thus neither excluding exact dilations by normalization nor bounding piece count produces the required scale separation. For cov279 the cut-independent lower bound on the largest attached chamber likewise grows with the stretch. A normalized compact shape after similarity would itself give no bound on lattice area without an effective denominator bound. [Cross geometry](every-direction.md) · [Decomposition obstruction](whole-room-decomposition.md)

A dichotomy sufficient for L1 would have to certify every pair in an unbounded-area room via some gluing construction (including its same-end and collar hypotheses), or bound every surviving room's *lattice area* by an explicit N₀. At present the W≤20 input proves the piece count only. In fact even W=12 has unbounded sealed chamber diameter in the two-ended layout: the named DB55 top-line stretch has chamber widths 5, odd heights unbounded, corridor length 3 and separate wall gcds one. This precludes bounding the input D or this Fibonacci estimate by W alone. It leaves open the existence of a better, wall-dependent illumination theorem. R22 restricts extension to higher wall counts, as reviewed in d152. [DB55 normalization](stretch-stability.md) · [Dilation and R22](uniform-tail.md)

**First open gluing case.** A same-end pair in a nonrectangular chamber for which every sealed shot meets the deleted mirror, or a mark there paired to a near-collar target without a one-entry witness, needs a controlled return from the remote end. Arbitrary-chamber first access alone does not supply that return. Its existence for all admissible W≤20 attachments and an unconditional N₀ remain open.
