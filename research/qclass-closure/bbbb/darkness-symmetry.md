# Can a single half-turn explain darkness of complete physical lift orbits?

**Verdict for review.** Under the no-common-open-cylinder meaning of separation, cov446 refutes the proposed midpoint converse inside the stated range. Under actual all-lift darkness the r≤8 conjecture remains open. On every orthogonal room, at *any* wall count, there is a classification obstruction: no affine involution of derivative −I on its unfolding or any finite translation quotient with fixed points confined to projected physical corners can exchange two distinct projected physical orbit sets. R22's parity-and-seam proof illustrates the difference. This does not give an example of an actually dark room at W≤20.

## Which midpoint statement is necessary?

Write A,B for the complete sets of lifts of two distinct physical interior points, F for all physical corner lifts, and f:X→Y for a finite translation covering (identity allowed). A half-turn η blocks a specific ordered pair (a,b) by reversing a putative segment only when η f(a)=f(b). Reversal then starts at the correct point with the correct velocity. The set equality η f(A)=f(B) by itself supplies partners, not this equality for every element of A×B. For the one-map reversal proof to apply to *every* independent lift choice on the same quotient, f(A) and f(B) must each be singletons. Physical reflections act simultaneously on the endpoints of a path and cannot independently change its relative lift choice.

Furthermore, any midpoint obstruction transferred from downstairs needs a reason that a legal upstairs segment cannot run through the proposed forbidden locus downstairs. For a plain translation cover, a sufficient condition is f⁻¹(Fix η)⊆F. Containment Fix η⊆f(F) is weaker: an image of a corner could have other, regular, preimages. We prove the obstruction even using this weaker containment. An intermediate half-translation quotient, on which derivative signs are identified, does not alone justify the reversal calculation. A proposed use there must specify the orientation cover, lifted endpoints, and signs, or supply a separate argument.

## Classification on orthogonal unfoldings and all translation quotients

Let j_X be the physical half-turn of the four-copy unfolding. It fixes all the corner lifts, preserves every complete physical lift orbit and has sphere quotient (the two-copy room double). The zeros of the unfolding have order two. The rectangle gives genus one; otherwise j_X is the hyperelliptic involution. Let f:X→Y be as above, with f*ω_Y=ω_X and Y compact and connected.

1. **The physical involution descends.** The divisors x+j_X x are fibers of a degree-two map to the sphere. Their pushforwards f(x)+f(j_X x) on Y are linearly equivalent, because pushforward of a principal divisor is principal by the norm. They vary and have no common point: a common point would force every x into the union of two finite fibers or their images under j_X. More explicitly, membership of y₀ forces x∈f⁻¹(y₀)∪j_X f⁻¹(y₀). Pick two distinct members; they are disjoint, since two different effective linearly equivalent degree-one divisors would give a degree-one map from Y to the sphere (impossible with a nonzero holomorphic differential). The resulting degree-two pencil is complete: a degree-two line bundle on a positive-genus curve has at most two independent sections, by subtracting a point and using the degree-one observation. Its fiber involution j_Y satisfies f j_X=j_Y f, and j_Y*ω_Y=−ω_Y by pullback. In particular f(F)⊆Fix j_Y, and j_Y preserves f(A) and f(B) separately. For genus ≥2, j_Y is central by uniqueness of the hyperelliptic involution. [Hyperelliptic uniqueness, §2](https://msp.org/obs/2013/1-1/obs-v1-n1-p23-s.pdf)
2. **All zeros on Y have even order.** At a point of local degree e, ordₓ(ω_X)+1=e(ordᵧ(ω_Y)+1). The left side is 1 or 3, so the downstairs order is 0 or 2. Each downstairs zero has a zero above and hence belongs to f(F).
3. **Every other involution η with derivative −I has four fixed points outside f(F).** In genus one, write j_Y and η as z↦a−z and z↦b−z. If different, their four-point fixed sets are disjoint. In higher genus put δ=j_Yη. By centrality δ is a nonidentity involution with derivative I. It is fixed-point-free: at a point of order k choose the local coordinate u for which a flat primitive is u^(k+1). A translation fixing that point acts by u↦λu with λ^(k+1)=1. Since k is even, an involution has λ=1, hence is the identity. Now δ induces a nonidentity involution on the j_Y-quotient sphere (otherwise it would be a deck map, either 1 or j_Y). Its two fixed sphere points have unbranched two-point fibers, since δ cannot fix a singleton. On each such fiber both δ and j_Y swap the two points, so η fixes both. No other sphere fiber can contain an η-fixed point. These four points are outside Fix j_Y and hence outside f(F); they are regular by step 2.

Consequently Fix η⊆f(F) forces η=j_Y, including if one had hoped η were fixed-point-free. This classifies the only possible candidate under that containment; j_Y itself meets the containment when all its fixed points are projected forbidden sites. Since j_Y preserves the two projected orbit sets separately, it cannot exchange distinct ones. If f(A)=f(B), merely calling this an exchange gives no all-pairs conclusion. In the singleton case common image p has j_Y p=p; the stronger transfer condition fails since p has the regular source and target preimages. A separate proof of transfer for particular paths would already require geometry beyond this midpoint argument.

For a genuine mixed 45° unfolding, the following part still holds without any hyperelliptic assumption. The physical half-turn fixes every corner lift. Any other −I involution fixing even one of those points agrees with it: in the same local coordinate, λ^(k+1)=−1 and λ²=1 force λ=−1. Agreement as germs gives agreement globally. Thus on X a candidate with a nonempty fixed set confined to physical corners cannot exchange the distinct physical orbits. This proof leaves free involutions and arbitrary mixed translation quotients open. Even a free involution only pairs a given lift with its own image.

## Named controls and the scope of the converse

**cov446 (W=18, r=7).** The saved endpoints (1,2),(3,2) are directly joined by their horizontal segment. D72's complete coloured orbit of 9,072 states proves that no pair of their lifts lies in a common open corner-cut cylinder in *any* primitive direction. This proves that cyclic separation in the stated open-cylinder sense does not even imply darkness. The orthogonal quotient theorem above also rules out the claimed exchange of distinct orbit images by a qualifying involution. No new direction was tested. [D72 saved result](../../illumination-generalization-2026-11-06/fable/directions-2026-11-09/d72-cylinder-test/README.md)

**R22 (W=22, r=9).** Here all physical lift pairs really are dark. On the staircase the two projected physical points are the distinct smooth-wall points (0,0) and (2,−2). The physical half-turn on its translation unfolding preserves the lifts of each of these points separately. The proof takes a nonzero even developed endpoint displacement, divides out its maximal power of two, and finds an intermediate lattice site with an odd coordinate. Every such staircase site is a vertex. Folding reverses the path at smooth seam endpoints; a last-source/first-target truncation handles those visits. The forced intermediate point can vary with the path and need not even be its midpoint. This is an obstruction by parity and seam reversal. The quotient classification shows why looking for one mark-exchanging midpoint involution with only forbidden fixed points is the wrong model for that folding proof. R22 tests the mechanism outside the conjectured small-wall range. [Manuscript, folding and staircase parity proof](../../illumination-publication/repository/preprints/twenty-two-wall/manuscript.tex)

**Effect on the goal.** For actual darkness at r≤8 in orthogonal rooms, proving the proposed converse in a form that really blocks all pairs would already prove nonexistence, because its required exchange cannot occur. It is not a reduction to a remaining low-wall family of such involutions. In mixed rooms the positive-fixed-point case on X is likewise excluded. The live structural question is whether a *collective* obstruction to all relative lift choices exists in the small-wall range. A cycle separation or a bijection on two finite label sets alone does not establish one.
