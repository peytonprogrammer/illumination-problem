Advances: L1 — Coupled neck and middle-chamber phase tests for a pair across disjoint unit mouths.

Take the middle chamber to occupy 0<x<w. Its left opening is (0,1) in height and its right opening is (1+δ,2+δ), with integral δ>0. The middle floor is strictly below 0, so the left neck is strictly inset. The middle roof is h>0 above the upper opening. Let S be in the left outer chamber at height 0 and T in the right outer chamber at height δ+2, the residual row placement. Their horizontal distances to the outer neck mouths are e and f; the neck lengths are L and R. Write A=e+L, K=f+R. All these lengths are positive. Use r=|dy/dx|. The tests below prescribe straight stubs only inside the outer chambers; both necks may have arbitrarily many reflections.

**Neck map.** Launch S up/right, and trace backwards from T down/left. Require er<1 and fr<1 for these particular outer stubs. Measured up from the lower left-neck wall and down from the upper right-neck wall, the two unfolded phases at the middle chamber are Ar and Kr modulo 2. Folding sends a residue z in (0,1) to depth z with the launch sign, and a residue z in (1,2) to depth 2−z with the opposite sign. Integer phases hit mouth endpoints and are excluded. Inside the necks all intervening horizontal-wall bounces are smooth. The same r must satisfy both phase conditions.

**Opposite sides, displacement strip.** Take residues α=Ar−2i and β=Kr−2j both in (0,1), so the forward path points up on both sides of the middle chamber. The left entry height is α and the right exit height is δ+2−β. On reaching height 1 the horizontal position is a=(1−α)/r. Tracing back from the right to height 1+δ gives b=w−(1−β)/r. Require a,b∈(0,w); this makes both short end pieces inside the middle chamber free of side contacts. Between those levels the full vertical sides are solid. The middle translation a+δ/r ≡ b (mod 2w) joins the two pieces with matching velocity. Equivalently, for nonnegative integers i,j,m the candidate is

    r=(δ+2+2i+2j)/(A+K+(2m+1)w).

It works precisely on this branch when er,fr<1, α,β∈(0,1), and 1−α,1−β<wr. Indeed the unfolded horizontal travel across the entire middle rectangle is (2m+1)w; 2m side reversals leave the exit rightward. The tests keep all those reversals in the solid-sided strip.

**Opposite sides, upper roof pocket.** Keep 0<α=Ar−2i<1 but take 1<Kr−2j<2 and put β=2j+2−Kr. The backward ray from T now leaves its neck pointing up/left, giving a forward exit down/right. At height δ+2 the left and right legs require a=(δ+2−α)/r and b=w−β/r. For 0<a,b<w these legs cross no middle vertical wall before reaching the pocket base. The roof excursion translates a to a+2h/r modulo 2w. Matching b on the rightward sheet gives

    r=(δ+4+2i+2j+2h)/(A+K+(2m+1)w).

The exact tests for this itinerary are er,fr<1, the two strict residue conditions above, and δ+2−α<wr, β<wr. Roof clearance follows from 2(a+h/r) ≡ a+b (mod 2w) with 0<a+b<2w. Thus the congruence handles the velocity and both potential corner events. These two formulas are existence criteria for specified itineraries; the following parameter choices establish nonemptiness symbolically.

**Reflected-neck connections in the previous residual.** Set e=f=1 and prescribe r=1/2.

* Take w=2, δ any positive even integer, and L,R independently positive multiples of four. Both phases are 1/2, so a=b=1. The displacement strip adds 2δ, a multiple of 2w. This connects the marks even for |A−K| arbitrarily large, for which the earlier direct-leg interval is empty.
* For a nonzero pocket shift, take any integral δ≥1, w=2δ+8, h=δ+5, L a positive multiple of four and R≡2 (mod 4). The neck residues are 1/2 and 3/2. Here a=2δ+3 and b=2δ+7, both strictly interior. The roof shift 4h=2w+4 gives exactly the required four-unit displacement. These lengths can also be arbitrarily mismatched.

**First simultaneous obstruction.** For integral data with K=2A and max(e,f)≥3w, both tests above fail for every admissible straight-stub slope, regardless of how many bounces occur in either neck. Indeed wr<1/3. The strip test requires α>2/3, but then Kr modulo 2 equals 2α∈(4/3,2), on the wrong velocity sheet. The roof test requires wr>δ+2−α>1. These conditions occur with arbitrarily long necks, for example e=f=3w and R=2L+3w. The neck maps remove the earlier long-neck clearance restriction, yet their two outputs are correlated. Resolving this obstruction requires another history, such as a reflected outer-chamber stub or a middle path touching a side wall before reaching the separating strip. It is not evidence that the marks are dark.

### Adjacent mouths (j=1)

For a left entrance (l,l+1) and a top exit (t,t+1) in a middle rectangle [0,w]×[0,H], the two phases instead advance by Lr and R/r. As one exact branch, use a middle leg with no wall hit. If the forward left-neck exit is (0,y) moving up/right and the backward top-neck exit is (x,H) moving down/left, the necessary and sufficient matching equation is x=(H−y)/r. Both mouth coordinates must be strictly interior; convexity then guarantees that the middle leg stays inside. This also shows a difference for the two straight outer stubs: if both start at transverse edge heights and travel inward from the edges of unit-width apertures, the strict requirements er<1 and f/r<1 are incompatible for integral e,f≥1. General outer marks can supply different phases.

For an explicit positive family prescribe r=2/3. Put S one unit to the left of the outer left-neck mouth at height l, choose L a positive multiple of 3, and launch up/right. It reaches the middle entrance at y=l+2/3 moving up/right. Choose H−l an odd integer ≥3, t=3(H−l−1)/2, and w>t+1. The upper intersection has x=t+1/2. Put T one unit beyond the far end of the top neck at transverse coordinate t+2, inside a sufficiently wide end chamber, and take R a positive multiple of four. Backwards, T travels down/left to x=t+1/2 at the outer neck mouth; traversing the neck adds 3R/2, an even integer, to the leftward phase. It reaches the same x=t+1/2 at the middle chamber moving down/left. This gives a legal connection with unbounded lengths on both axes. Neither this construction nor the opposite-side pocket condition covers arbitrary adjacent-side pairs.

**Verdict.** L1 gains reflected-neck lighting families and an explicit joint phase obstruction for two natural middle itineraries. The first unresolved opposite-side family above has K=2A, long stubs and disjoint mouths. The full word LLLLRRLLRRLLLLRRLLRR remains open, as does adjacent-side LLLLRRLLLRRLLLLRRLRR. No denominator cutoff or enumeration was used.

### Arbitrary chamber dimensions and marks

In a rectangular outer chamber of floor F, height H₀ and width W₀, unfold its horizontal walls before folding the neck. For a source at height y, moving horizontally toward its first neck at distance e, launch with vertical sign σ. The chamber phase at the neck is y−F+σer modulo 2H₀. Fold into [0,H₀], add F, and multiply σ by the derivative (+1 or −1) of that fold for the arrival sign. Require the resulting height strictly inside the aperture (l,l+g). If its height above l is z, start the neck at phase z for an upward arrival or −z for a downward arrival. Here g is the aperture height. The neck of length L advances this phase by Lr modulo 2g. A return at phase 0 or g hits a mouth endpoint. Interior phases give both the height and the vertical sign for the middle test. The same rule backwards from T handles independent outer dimensions and a different second neck; reverse the final velocity for gluing. If the outer wall opposite the mouth is solid one may also use a single initial horizontal reversal, replacing e by 2W₀−e and checking the wall contact misses a corner. These operations give exact entrance data for the preceding matching tests without the straight-stub restrictions er,fr<1.

For clarity, for unit displaced necks let y_L,y_R denote the two physical entrance heights at the middle chamber, and retain only right/up forward velocity on the left. For a right/up continuation on the right, the strip test reads (2m+1)wr=y_R−y_L, with 0<1−y_L<wr and 0<y_R−(δ+1)<wr. For a right/down continuation after the roof it reads (2m+1)wr=2Y−y_L−y_R where Y=δ+2+h, with 0<δ+2−y_L<wr and 0<δ+2−y_R<wr. Here m≥0, and δ+2 is the pocket base. These follow by unfolding the middle side walls and, for the second equation, reflecting the target-side height about the actual roof. The earlier corner-clearance check still applies.

**A reflected-stub connection in the former K=2A residual.** Take r=1/4 and any independent integral outer heights H₀,H₁ large enough for their respective strictly inset apertures. Put the marks on the transverse rows of the original residual. Choose e=8H₀i+1 and f=8H₁j+1 with positive integers i,j, in sufficiently wide end chambers. The folded outer stubs therefore end 1/4 of a unit inside their apertures with the original velocity signs. Take L a positive multiple of eight large enough that 2L>f−2e; set A=e+L and R=2A−f, now strictly positive. Then K=2A and the two middle entrance phases are 1/4 and 1/2. Choose w≡1 modulo 4, w≥5, and δ=((4n+1)w−5)/4>0 for an integer n≥1. The middle strip test holds with m=2n: the unfolded rise is δ+5/4=(4n+1)w/4, and each end piece has horizontal travel at most 3<w. This still works with e,f≥3w, where the earlier straight-stub argument failed for every slope. The widths of the outer chambers and the middle roof height can be increased without changing this path.

### All lattice pairs with a 4-tall middle chamber

Keep both end chambers 3×3 and the common neck band (1,2); allow any positive integer neck lengths independently. Give the middle chamber width 3 and height 4, with floor 0. Every interior lattice pair in this infinite family is lit. The area-32 middle-height case follows by taking both lengths one.

**Marks in rows 1 and 2.** The different-row direct segments and same-row adjacent corridor bounces of d201 are unchanged. The adjacent length-one exception uses the pocket of the 3×3 end chamber (view the path from that end). For same-row outer marks the smooth outer-wall reflection tests depend only on horizontal coordinates. In the remaining cases d201 gives M=2,3,4 and an open entry interval of length at least 2. For row 1 the upper middle pocket now has h=2, w=3 and g=1, so the progression spacing is 1. For row 2 use the lower pocket beneath height 1, of depth 1, by reversing the vertical coordinate in the pocket formula; its spacing is 3/2. Both fit strictly. This part works for any integral middle height ≥3.

**Middle marks above the band, approached from row 1.** More generally put the pocket base at height 2 and give it arbitrary width w and height h. Place the middle target at local abscissa v∈(0,w) and height 2+t, 0<t<h. Orient x from the source end, so its local abscissa U is negative. From S at height 1 go right/up to the base at a=U+c, with c=|dx/dy|. The leg lies in the open neck band. In the upper part, stop at the target before reaching the roof. Unfolding just the sides gives the exact condition

    0<a<w,       (1+t)a ≡ ±v+tU  (mod 2w).

For integral t≥1 each sign has spacing 2w/(1+t)≤w. When equality holds the two residues are distinct since 0<v<w; they cannot both land only at endpoints. Thus one legal a exists. All side contacts before T are strictly above the base and below the roof. This construction works for arbitrary horizontal lengths and for every integral target above the band.

**Middle marks above the band, approached from row 2.** For the 4-tall chamber the only new row is 3. Orient x from the outer source, and let v=1 or 2 be the target's distance from the middle entrance; let B be the entire horizontal separation. Choose m=2/v and use unfolded ordinate 2+6mx−x/B at horizontal travel x from S. At every integral seam before the middle chamber, 6mx is a multiple of the end chamber's period 6 and of the neck period 2. The slow ordinate 2−x/B is strictly inside (1,2), so both folds agree in height and have positive derivative. At the middle entrance continue with the same slope, unfolding about 0 and 4. The unfolded final height measured from that entrance copy is 1+6mv=13, which folds to 3 modulo the period 8. There is no later seam and the target is interior. Two marks in the middle chamber itself see one another directly. This exhausts the new row, in both directions.

For an end chamber of arbitrary integral height H₀ containing the same unit band and a middle chamber with floor zero and arbitrary integral height H, take a source at integral abscissa and a positive integer m in the row-2 slope 2H₀m−1/B. Solvable congruences admit a positive representative. It reaches target height y if 2H₀vm ≡ ±y−1 (mod 2H), equivalently if gcd(2H₀v,2H) divides at least one right-hand side. Here the outer floor may be any integer: the slow ordinate lies inside that chamber throughout the initial leg and a full-period increment preserves its fold there. This is a sufficient condition for arbitrary widths and heights. It is not automatic for all rows.
