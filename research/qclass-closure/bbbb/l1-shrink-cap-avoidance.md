Advances: L1 — Test of cap avoidance in the normalized ten-wall chart, with the designated right mouth forbidden.

Let E be the eight-wall exterior shared by P and P_s. Strictly left-of-mouth marks are interior lattice points with x<x0. A legal cap-avoiding path for such marks is the same in both rooms, since the entire path lies in their common geometry. At a regular point of the open mouth a transverse ray enters the cap; the artificial wall reflection of the closed exterior is unavailable. At its lower endpoint there is a reflex corner of P_s, and the upper endpoint is smooth on the horizontal roof: a nonvertical ray arriving there from the left crosses to the right after reflection. Axis-parallel paths between marks strictly to the left cause no endpoint exception.

**A positive result without a slope cutoff.** The three open columns of E from left to right have vertical intervals (y3,y2), (y4,y2), (y4,y1). Each adjacent pair is nested. Any two interior marks strictly inside a single column see each other directly. Marks strictly inside adjacent columns have a cap-avoiding path monotone in x. This holds for real as well as lattice marks and requires no illumination premise.

Here is the adjacent-column construction. Orient horizontal travel from the narrower vertical interval to the wider one, reversing x if necessary. Take the shared endpoint as height zero and orient the vertical coordinate inward, so the smaller interval is (0,B). Let u ∈ (0,B) be the mark height in it, and let v be the other mark height in these coordinates. Let the positive horizontal distances from the marks to the seam be D and F respectively, and set β=F/(D+F). Unfold the horizontal walls on each side. The smaller mark has images ±u+2iB for i integral. Choose any image v′ of the larger mark, for instance v itself. The line joining the images crosses the seam at height β(±u+2iB)+(1−β)v′. For each sign these values form a progression with spacing 2βB. At least one sign gives a nonzero residue, since their difference 2βu is strictly between zero and the spacing. Pick the representative z of that residue nearest zero. Then 0<|z|≤βB<B. If z is negative, reflect the entire unfolded diagram in height zero. Zero is a horizontal reflecting wall on both sides, so negating both image heights preserves the two image sets. Now 0<z<B. On both sides the segment lies in the physical copy at the seam, so the two folded pieces meet with matching velocities. Every earlier and later horizontal-wall bounce is strictly inside its column's x-range. The path misses the corners and its x-coordinate remains between the marks. In particular it stays left of the mouth.

**First symbolic obstruction for separated columns.** A bound on the number of vertical reversals of an avoiding path cannot depend only on the wall count, even in the stated parity class. Take b₂=2, b₃=1, any n≥3 and k≥1. Put S=(x0−1,y1−1) in the right exterior column and T=(x3+1,y2−1) in the leftmost column. These are interior lattice marks with odd vertical difference 4k+1. Write a=x0−x2=n+2. For any cap-avoiding ray between them, let c=|dx/dy|. Both velocity components must be nonzero.

* Before descending from S to level y0, the ray is confined horizontally between x2 and the forbidden mouth line x0. The only available vertical reflecting wall there is x2, which it can strike at most once. The horizontal roof does not change its horizontal velocity. Thus horizontal travel is at most 2a, while total vertical travel is at least 4k−1. Hence c≤2a/(4k−1).
* From its last crossing of x2 until T, the ray stays in the first two columns. Their combined vertical range is y2−y3=3. Horizontal travel on that part is at least x2−(x3+1)=2n. Each leg between changes of vertical sign contributes at most 3 to vertical travel. If there are J such legs, 2n≤3Jc, so J≥ceil(n(4k−1)/(3(n+2))).

Thus for fixed n, any prescribed leg budget is exceeded for sufficiently large k by **every** cap-avoiding connection, if one exists. This defeats a monotone-in-y construction and any uniformly bounded number of vertical reversals. It does not defeat the monotone-in-x construction by itself: such a path can have many vertical reversals. Its remaining geometric obstacle is the second seam. The adjacent-column argument chooses one image to hit one aperture; in three columns the same straight line must meet the first aperture and, after folding across the middle column, arrive at the second with the matching height and velocity.

**What full illumination supplies and where this test stops.** The hypothesis gives a path in the room with its cap present. It supplies neither of those two seam conditions for a cap-avoiding path. The estimates above do not prove that all connecting paths must enter the cap, or that these P_s are fully lit. A second possible test is to use the closed exterior at a fixed slope p/q: determine whether one of the four rays from S reaches T before the forbidden mouth. Failure for that slope leaves every other slope available. The finite phase argument in the preceding note has no denominator-independent witness selection, and finding a cutoff on q for this test is the specified stopping point. The leg lower bound is not a denominator lower bound. No cutoff or search was attempted.

**Verdict.** Cap avoidance is proved for the single- and adjacent-column placements above. For separated columns the first obstruction is the need to clear two seams on one path, with unboundedly many vertical legs required in the displayed family. Whether a fully lit successor forces even one such avoiding path for every pair remains open. No counterexample to illumination or to cap avoidance is claimed.
