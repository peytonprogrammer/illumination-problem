# What a shrinking door does to the slope interval

**Verdict.** In the D58 straight viewer the supremum of usable slope widths on one fixed incoming itinerary is exactly w/D. This settles the width estimate there. Even this optimal interval is too short for the phase-sweep proof on D58's designed double resonances. A two-ended variant does survive shrinking when the two limiting door centers can be reached at the same slope.

## Aperture derivative and a sharp bound

Use s = transverse velocity divided by the absolute longitudinal velocity, with a fixed choice of initial signs. For a fixed source and reflection sequence ending at a straight door perpendicular to the longitudinal axis, let a be total absolute longitudinal travel. In orthogonal unfolding the door lies on a fixed line and its transverse crossing coordinate has derivative +a or −a with respect to s. A connected interval of slopes realizing the itinerary must therefore have width at most w/a. If an additional wall or vertex clips the accessible part of the door to a subinterval of length u, its slope width is exactly u/a. Closed subintervals strictly inside the admissible open interval suffice for the long-corridor theorem.

In D58 the viewer spans the entire aperture and has continuous side walls for the distance D to the source. In its transverse unfolding take any copy (p*,p*+w) of the aperture entirely to one side of the source coordinate x*. The slopes reaching it form ((p*−x*)/D,(p*+w−x*)/D), up to sign. Every such ray has the same sequence of side reflections: the source is interior and the image aperture lies within one channel copy. Thus every width strictly below w/D is attained on a closed interval. All incoming routes including those with turns have a≥D by net longitudinal displacement. This proves the upper bound w/D even when other chamber itineraries are allowed. Holding w and the combinatorial type fixed while increasing D rules out any positive universal bound on slope width in terms of reflex count and w alone.

## Why improving this estimate cannot settle D58

For a target at depth z the one-door phase advances by (a+z)Δs on one branch. Its total span is at most w(1+z/a) ≤ w(1+z/D). The entire period is 2w. If both reflected target images are allowed, the largest gap between their residues is 2w max(β,1−β), where the target lies at fraction β of the strip width. To force a hit from span alone one must span that gap (strict excess is sufficient). A shorter interval can succeed by its placement.

In D58's constructed cases β=1/20 and D=16h. The direct depth h gives a span at most 17w/16; the cap depth 3h gives at most 19w/16. Both fall below the largest target gap 19w/10. These inequalities apply even to viewer routes with extra turns **on each fixed branch**, since those increase a. For the original direct and cap families D58 also supplies the actual positions of the intervals: all four phases lie in the bad arcs. Other branches can shift an interval into a good position. The missing statement is about those positions modulo 2w. Increasing the incoming reflection count cannot repair the worst-case span estimate.

## A two-ended result independent of shrinking width

Fix two sealed end chambers and a source and target. Center the two shrinking portals at smooth boundary points. Suppose each mark has a finite corner-free leg ending transversely at its portal center, with the same positive absolute slope s* at both ends. Require no earlier visit to that portal center. The chamber legs may turn. Let a,b>0 be their respective absolute longitudinal travels. Keep the centers fixed and shrink both widths to w. For all sufficiently small w, every joining corridor length ℓ satisfying **ℓ > |a−b|** lights this pair, whenever the joined room is strictly simple.

Indeed, each finite reference itinerary has a neighborhood in the sealed chamber in which its collisions remain transverse on the same edges. Its finitely many earlier collisions miss the portal center by positive margins, so they miss the shrinking portal as well. In these neighborhoods the two terminal coordinates, measured from their centers, have absolute values a|s−s*| and b|s−s*|. Consequently both portals accept the open interval of width w/max(a,b) centered at s*. The corridor phase on it has span (ℓ+a+b)w/max(a,b), strictly greater than 2w under the displayed condition. Apply the [phase-matching proof](README.md). This threshold is independent of w. The hypothesis concerns exact alignment in the two limiting chambers. Two independently accessible centers need not supply the same slope; two fixed branches centered at distinct slopes eventually have disjoint windows.

## Turns and chains

The aperture derivative remains valid after turns *before* the door. For cov2929 the stored directions prove two particular pairs lit, but the geometry supplies no separating strip whose length can amplify the terminal phase. For a chain with a specified unfolded route through parallel door lines, write the admissible slope interval for door i as the interval centered at s_i that aims at its developed center, with half-width w_i/(2a_i). Here a_i is the developed longitudinal distance from the source. All these intervals must intersect, as must any further constraints excluding corner collisions. For two doors, disjointness already follows when |s₁−s₂| ≥ w₁/(2a₁)+w₂/(2a₂). This is an obstruction for those image choices. D58's full-channel argument can vary the image indices together. A single-interval estimate provides no guarantee that some choices overlap for a resonant chain. At a perpendicular door the coordinate in the same unfolded chart instead varies affinely in 1/s. A common positive interval and an endpoint match still have to be proved.
