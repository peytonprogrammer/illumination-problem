Advances: L1 — an area-uniform obstruction to the horizontal and vertical band-twist route. For every prime area p≥37 there is a normalized, strictly simple, eight-reflex BBBB room with giant monodromy and a nondirect same-parity pair whose ordered lift pairs are fixed pointwise by both band twists. The pair is lit by one floor reflection. No directly visible pair anywhere in the room can be transported to it by these generators, even if both endpoints may move.

### Fixed-point lemma

On the oriented room double Q, let A be generated by the horizontal and vertical multi-twists that fix band boundaries pointwise. A regular point on both a horizontal and a vertical band boundary is fixed by A, since it is fixed by each generator and its inverse. There is an intrinsic version: if its horizontal continuation meets a forbidden corner and its vertical continuation meets a forbidden corner, the point is on the critical graph in both directions. Boundary-pointwise multi-twists in the *maximal* horizontal and vertical cylinders fix it too. Indeed each point of an open horizontal saddle connection has a neighborhood on either side in a cylinder and is fixed by continuity from that cylinder's fixed boundary. The same argument applies vertically. It suffices for one continuation in each direction to reach a corner.

If all compatible lifts of a target have this property, only those very lifts can map to them. If all lifts of both marks have it, the A-orbit of each ordered lift pair is a singleton. Thus moving the source as well, composing arbitrarily many twists, or starting with a different direct chord cannot repair the transfer. This lemma concerns these two directions and the boundary-pointwise twists; other affine maps need not fix these points. ### An unbounded primitive family realizing the obstruction

For an integer t≥1 take the closed region above the floor y=0 with the following consecutive rectangular columns. At a change of height include the common vertical segment, as for the closed union of the rectangles.

| x interval | Height |
|---|---:|
| [0,1] | 2 |
| [1,2] | 4 |
| [2,4] | 3 |
| [4,6] | 1 |
| [6,8] | 4 |
| [8,10] | 3 |
| [10,11] | 2 |
| [11,12] | 4 |
| [12,12+t] | 1 |

Every column meets the next along a segment of positive length. The floor and stepped roof form a simple Jordan polygon with no pinches. Each of the eight changes of height contributes one reflex and one convex corner. Hence r=8, W=20. The area is 34+t. Unit horizontal and vertical wall lengths give normalization on both axes.

Take S=(2,2), T=(8,2). They are interior and even-even. Their horizontal chord leaves the room above the height-one neck. On the front copy, the leftward ray from S ends at the reflex (1,2), while the rightward ray from T ends at (10,2). The upward rays end at the reflexes (2,3) and (8,3), respectively. All four segments are interior until their final point. The rear copy gives the same physical segments. Thus both marks satisfy the intrinsic fixed-point lemma. Nevertheless the path S→(5,0)→T is legal. Its slopes are −2/3 and +2/3, and its heights at the two neck entrances are both 2/3, strictly between 0 and 1. It reflects at a smooth floor point. This verifies that failure of the transfer mechanism can occur for an explicitly lit, nondirect pair.

For any prime p≥37 set t=p−34. The connected pillowcase cover has transitive sheet monodromy G of prime degree p, hence is primitive. The reflex coordinates, in roof order, are

    (1,2), (2,3), (4,1), (6,1), (8,3), (10,2), (11,2), (12,1).

Exactly one, (10,2), is even-even. At the even-even base pole the local permutation has one 3-cycle, one for this reflex, and all other cycles have lengths one or two. Its square is a single 3-cycle fixing p−3 sheets. Jordan's theorem gives Aₚ≤G≤Sₚ. Furthermore the four interior grid points with x∈{2,3}, y∈{1,2} realize all four parities. Each pole permutation therefore has a transposition. The G-invariant equivalence generated by that transposition is nontrivial, hence universal by primitivity. All four canonical partitions are B. In particular no separating proper intermediate factor accounts for the fixed pair. **Where the route stops.** At unbounded prime areas the sheet group is giant while the specified lit pair has singleton affine orbits under the proposed twists. A direct-seed orbit theorem for these generators is therefore false even after replacing the unit neighbor by *any* directly visible compatible pair. The first outstanding lighting obligation for this route is a nondirect same-parity pair pinned on both critical graphs for which no independent seed is known. The example settles the orbit assertion; it supplies no dark room or area bound. No census, direction search, or symmetric area-22 check was run.
