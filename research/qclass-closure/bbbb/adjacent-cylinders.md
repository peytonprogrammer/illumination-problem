# Legal midpoints from the two rim trees

**Theorem.** Suppose the four-copy unfolding M has exactly two geometric cylinders in the primitive lattice direction v, and the half-turn τ exchanges them. Their images form a single open cylinder whose closure fills the room double Q=M/τ. Each of its two rims traces a finite metric tree. There is an interval test on the two trees which, when passed, proves both of the following:

1. Every pair of distinct real interior points in the room is lit, including pairs whose lifts all lie on the critical graph.
2. In a normalized room, every pair of distinct same-parity interior lattice points has a legal midpoint outside its source parity class.

There are at most 2r+2 unoriented tree edges in the entire test, independently of area. Constructing those trees from the square tiling still requires an area-dependent cylinder computation. The criterion holds on the named 10-wall room U2 in the saved direction (−1,1), and on every integral stretch specified below, at areas 46+8k. Its three reflex parities exclude the preceding [single-cylinder theorem on M](convex-cylinder.md). Separately, exact tests across adjacent cylinders bring the named-room off-class coverage from 724 to 747 of 747 pairs.

## The rim test

Use an integral, unimodular change of chart sending v to horizontal (reversing the signs of both coordinates if needed). Write C,H for circumference and height. Take the circle of abscissas modulo C on either rim. Split it at the actual physical convex and reflex lifts on Q. Each open edge occurrence (i,j) has a paired occurrence traversed in reverse: coordinate i+t on the first is the same Q point as m−t on the second, for 0<t<j−i. Develop j past C for a wrapping edge. Assign the following **open interval of differences** to this occurrence, modulo C:

    (i−m, i−m + 2(j−i)).

Take the union on each rim separately. The sufficient test is that **each union contain every nonzero circle difference**. Endpoints of intervals cannot simply be included: they represent a physical corner.

Here is why the model is valid. The open cylinder on Q is embedded; its core is a separating simple circle on the sphere Q. The two completed rims therefore belong to opposite components of its complement. On either side the finite critical graph is connected (the continuous image of that rim), and is a tree: the corresponding closed hemisphere retracts to the graph by collapsing the half-annulus radially. Equivalently, a cycle in one rim graph would separate an open face from the only cylinder. Each edge is traversed twice. On Q the two sides of such an edge must be identified by a half-turn because they belong to the same rim. The graph has valence one at a convex corner (angle π) and three at a reflex (angle 3π); ordinary points of angle 2π do not subdivide edges. A side with t reflexes has t+2 leaves and 2t+1 edges, giving 2r+2 across both sides.

Two ordinary points in Q with at least one in the open cylinder can be joined by a segment with open part inside, using the completed cylinder for any other rim endpoint. Points on opposite rims can also be joined. For distinct points on the same rim, choose their abscissas s,t. Follow a straight developed segment from s to some z on the opposite rim. At a regular point of the chosen edge it continues on the paired occurrence z'=i+m−z. Its velocity in cylinder coordinates reverses in both components (the coordinate transition is a half-turn). It next reaches the starting rim at t exactly when

    s−t = z−z' = 2z−i−m  (mod C).

Indeed the first horizontal travel is z−s up to full laps, and the second is its negative. Conversely the displayed congruence allows the two halves to use exactly these travels, so the join is straight in a local chart at the midpoint. There is no earlier corner: both open legs lie inside the cylinder. Taking z=i+u gives precisely the interval in the test. Distinct physical marks cannot have equal circle representatives. A successful Q path folds to a physical billiard path. This proves the real-pair conclusion. For a specified pair the test can use any choices of rim representatives; full coverage of each rim is only a convenient room-wide sufficient condition.

## Why the midpoint is off-class

The exchanged cylinders on M both have the same integral height H. Top of the first is glued along open edges to bottom of the second, and top of the second to bottom of the first. Indeed translations on M permit only top-to-bottom gluing, and self-gluing would identify the two distinct rim graphs on Q. Giving the two cylinders successive transverse coordinates [0,H] and [H,2H] thus defines a global transverse coordinate modulo 2H, including at vertices by continuity. Every absolute transverse period is a multiple of 2H. Normalization gives transverse periods generating 2ℤ: twice each wall vector is an absolute period by returning along the reverse half-turn image, and the horizontal and vertical wall gcds are separately one. Together with primitive v this gives 2 in the transverse period group. Hence H=1.

On Q the two rim levels have opposite transverse parities. Lattice points must be on the rims and marks of the same physical parity are on the same rim, since sheet reflections preserve coordinates modulo two. The path just constructed has its half-time point on the opposite rim. It is a lattice point: the unfolded end image agrees in parity with the start (all physical mirrors have integral coordinates), so the developed midpoint is integral, also after folding. Its transverse parity differs by one. The physical midpoint may be interior or on a smooth wall. Both count as regular under d82, including a specular reflection at the midpoint. Thus this test supplies exactly the kind of witness required by [d82](../../illumination-generalization-2026-11-06/fable/directions-2026-11-09/d82-review-necessity/review.md).

## U2 and its stretches

D69's U2 has a bottom bar [0,9]×[0,2], a left arm [0,2]×[2,9], a right arm [7,9]×[2,7], and a return [5,7]×[5,7]. The three reflex vertices (2,2),(7,2),(7,5) occupy three distinct parity classes. In its bottom bar insert 4k units of length at the vertical cross section x=7/2, translating everything to its right; k is any nonnegative integer. The maximal wall count remains 10. The area becomes 46+8k. Horizontal wall lengths include 2 and 9+4k, vertical lengths include 2 and 7, so normalization persists.

In direction (−1,1) the inserted bar has height 2. Crossing its extra horizontal length advances the unfolded vertical phase by ±4k, an integer number of full periods. A transverse section on either side therefore has exactly its original first-passage map including velocity signs. The section is inside a unit column, so old critical trajectories at this section have half-integer height and cannot graze a corner there. There are no new corners or trapped leaves in the insertion. Contracting each passage recovers the old two cylinders and the same contour pairings. Each traversal adds 4k longitudinal primitive units to that contour edge (the full transverse period returns the same vertical phase). For clarity, this also follows from lengths along the ray: each new traversal consumes the time of 4k primitive steps at |v_x|=1.

In the integral chart C=92+16k. On the first rim a leaf edge of length 15+4k gives all positive differences below 30+8k and their negatives (the two passes meet at the leaf). On the other rim a leaf edge has length 29+4k, giving differences up to 58+8k, again strictly past C/2. Both rims pass at every k. ## Independent application to the 23 outstanding saved pairs

The following arithmetic works in a general stored cylinder decomposition, without requiring a sphere with two rim trees. Choose a shared horizontal unit edge of a lower chart and an upper chart, allowing repeated charts. Let their circumferences be C,D; let the seam abscissas be e+u,f+u with 0≤u<1. Include u=0 only at a regular physical point. Let x,z be the endpoint abscissas, a,b their positive integer heights below and above the seam, h=a+b. An upward straight segment with longitudinal displacement d satisfies

    h u = a(z−f+D j) − b(e−x+C i),
    d   = e−x+z−f+C i+D j.

These follow by matching the two slopes; i,j are integral windings. Prescribe both winding parities to fix d modulo four (circumferences are even). Require (d,h) even and nonzero modulo four. The possible values of hu form one arithmetic progression with step gcd(2aD,2bC); intersect it with the edge window and solve the linear Diophantine equation. At fixed u the solutions give d=d₀+kL. In each open cylinder there are either no forbidden points or two convex fixed points at the middle height, half a circumference apart. At that height the crossing abscissa is affine in k, so the two forbidden points together exclude at most one residue class of k (possibly all or none). Integer determinants test the collision exactly. This uses the [convex-cylinder lemma](convex-cylinder.md).

Four consecutive k suffice for the two cylinders together. A modulus-one obstruction excludes all k. Two classes modulo two of opposite parity do likewise. Otherwise two residues cannot cover four consecutive integers: if one modulus is two it leaves two positions distance two apart, and if both moduli exceed two a class covering two of the four must cover the two ends, leaving consecutive middle positions. So any feasible placement at that u already has a four-trial witness.

Applied only to each pair's two previously saved D72 charts, this certifies 20 of the 23 outstanding pairs. The three residuals in DBw2 admit elementary paths: from (2,2) use displacement (4,14) to (6,2), from (12,2) use (−4,10) to (8,2), and from (8,2) use the direct displacement (4,2) to (12,4). For the first two, in chamber coordinates the mouth is three horizontal units away; the unfolded mouth heights are 25/2 and 19/2, folding to 5/2 in their respective chambers of heights 5 and 6. The remaining unit of travel in the width-two corridor ends at height 2. Their midpoints are (4,1) and (10,5), and the last midpoint is (10,3). All are off-class. The independent physical Fraction tracer checks the end and midpoint of every one of the 23 new certificates. Hence all 747 same-class pairs in the nine named BBBB rooms have verified off-class midpoints. [Adjacent-chart checker](adjacent-cylinders.py) · [Replay output](adjacent-cylinders.txt)

## Scope

The tree criterion is a sufficient theorem about arbitrary rooms with the specified decomposition. The U2 family proves it can settle a three-parity case at unbounded area. It does not show that a direction satisfying the criterion exists for every room with r≤8. The 747 checks concern the named rooms. D84 independently confirmed the closed-form U2 argument for every k, traced every same-parity pair at k=0,1,3, and certified off-class midpoints for all 747 saved BBBB pairs.
