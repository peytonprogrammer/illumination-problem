# The eight-sheet exception forces direct visibility

**Result (reviewed by d79).** Let S,T be distinct same-parity interior lattice points in a strictly simple orthogonal lattice room. If the eight unit cells incident to S or T form a single block of the room-double monodromy, the entire straight segment ST lies in the open room. The lemma applies to a block of any G-invariant partition. It requires no maximality, normalization, area bound or reflex bound. Maximality enters through the block system selected in the combined large-area argument. [Independent review](../../illumination-generalization-2026-11-06/fable/directions-2026-11-09/d79-review-eight-sheet/review.md)

In particular, combining this lemma with the [large-area valency dichotomy](three-sheet.md), in any normalized such room of area n ≥ 120 with at most eight reflex corners, every distinct same-parity interior lattice pair either sees each other directly or has marked valency d ≥ n−8. This settles the unbounded low-valency branch for these marks. The high-valency lighting implication is still open.

## 1. From sheets to actual cell neighbors

Use cell centers as coordinates. Each unit cell is one sheet of the cover of the pillowcase. For each axis and parity there is an involution on cells. For example V_i crosses the vertical edge whose x-coordinate has parity i if a neighbor exists there, and fixes the cell otherwise. Define H_j for horizontal edges. Write W for the group generated by these four permutations, and G for the monodromy on sheets of the oriented double. The four corner permutations, up to inverse, are H_j V_i for all i,j. They generate the subgroup of even words in the four edge involutions: products of two generators of the same axis can be written with two mixed products by inserting the square of an opposite-axis generator. Thus G is normal in W, of index at most two. Identifying the sheets incident to an interior lattice mark uses all four distinct cells around it, two sheets for each lift. [Cell involutions](../../illumination-generalization-2026-11-06/fable/directions-2026-11-09/d52-fold-enumeration/fold.py) · [Corner permutations and sheet labels](../../illumination-generalization-2026-11-06/fable/directions-2026-11-09/d4-monodromy-blocks/monodromy.py)

Let A be the eight-cell block in the hypothesis. The two four-cell sets are disjoint because distinct same-parity marks differ by at least two in some coordinate. V_i for i = S_x mod 2 also uses T_x mod 2. It interchanges the two cells on each side of the central vertical line in each star and preserves A as a set. The G-translates of A partition all cells by transitivity. Since V_i normalizes G and fixes A setwise, it permutes that partition. Together G and V_i generate W. Consequently **all four edge involutions act on the blocks**. This step uses the marked block; no mirror invariance of arbitrary intermediate covers is assumed.

## 2. What the other blocks look like in the plane

For positive half-integers u,v (integers plus one half) let F(u,v) consist of the four vectors (±u,±v), independently signed. Each block C has a representation

    C = (S + F(u_C,v_C)) ∪ (T + F(u_C,v_C))

as eight distinct cell centers. For A both offsets are 1/2. To propagate the representation, take an edge generator sending a represented block C to a *different* block D. No cell of C can be fixed, since C and D are disjoint. Thus all eight cells move across their actual edges, just as on the infinite square grid. On that grid a colored edge move commutes with both axis reflections through an integer mark and with translations by even coordinate vectors. Reflection preserves the parity of the crossed edge coordinate. Since T−S is even in both coordinates, the images of the two four-cell sets take the indicated form with the same new offsets; one offset changes by a unit up to sign, the other stays fixed. The new offsets are nonzero half-integers. The move is bijective, so all eight centers remain distinct. Reach every block along a spanning tree of the connected quotient graph, ignoring loops. Strict simplicity gives an edge-connected physical cell graph, hence a connected quotient graph. Corner contact alone would allow two 2×2 squares touching at only one vertex.

The offsets are intrinsic: the vertical span of C is |S_y−T_y|+2v_C and the horizontal span is |S_x−T_x|+2u_C. Thus the two four-cell sets in each block are distinguished by these formulas and their centers. Label their cells S or T accordingly. As a set of cells the room is the disjoint union of R_S and its translate R_S+(T−S), where R_S is the union of the S-labeled cells and is symmetric about both coordinate axes through S. For **any** horizontal physical move (crossing a vertical edge), the edge generator preserves the multiset of y-coordinates of a block, even if it sends the block to itself or fixes some cells. The source and destination blocks consequently have equal v. A vertical move likewise preserves u.

## 3. Connectivity forces a mirror line through both marks

There are cells of both labels and the room is edge-connected. Some adjacent cells therefore have opposite labels. If they are horizontally adjacent, their common y-coordinate satisfies

    S_y + ε v = T_y + η v

for signs ε,η and the same positive half-integer v by the preceding paragraph. The difference S_y−T_y is even; the only alternatives on the right are zero and ±2v, with 2v odd. Thus S_y=T_y. For vertically adjacent cells the identical argument gives S_x=T_x.

Suppose S_y=T_y. Reflection across that common horizontal line preserves every set S+F and T+F. It therefore preserves the entire room, with both marks fixed. In the other case use the common vertical line.

Strict simplicity also gives simple connectivity of the open room. For completeness, the intersection of an open simply connected reflection-invariant planar room with its mirror line contains the segment between any two of its points on the line. Otherwise let X be a missing point strictly between them. Take a path inside the room between the marks and fold it into the closed upper half-plane by reflection about the line. It remains inside the open room and avoids X. Follow it by the reverse of its mirror image. This loop winds once about X up to sign: the continuous polar angle about X on the upper half-plane changes from π to zero, and the return contributes the same change. A loop in a simply connected room cannot have nonzero winding about an omitted point. This proves direct visibility, including exclusion of an intermediate boundary touch.

## Consequence for the midpoint budget

Suppose a pair in the large-area range is dark. The eight-sheet alternative just proved visible is impossible, so d ≥ n−8. The earlier intersection formula is 2d=R+J with J≤2r≤16. Over a given base parity there are at most floor(n/2) regular two-cycles. Subtracting the maximum contributions of the other three parities shows that **each** parity has at least

    2n − 32 − 3 floor(n/2)

regular algebraic coincidences in the marked union. At n=120 this gives 28 candidates per class, and at n=121 it gives 30. These are algebraic candidates; the argument has not turned any one of them into a real midpoint. This separates the remaining unbounded obstruction from the finite exceptional degrees and from the different-parity and nonlattice endpoint problems. [Midpoint budget](midpoint-necessity.md)

All deductions here use the fixed room action and its planar embedding. No room, ray, direction, or reflection-word discovery run was used.
