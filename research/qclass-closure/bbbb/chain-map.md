# What the chain map gives after the fold count

**Fold-converse verdict.** The separated three-off-class fold costs at least nine reflexes. Step 1 is false in general by R34, and within BBBB its conclusion is impossible *before* testing darkness. Indeed, if a proper sheet partition Π gives a quotient with no regular point over x, both entries of every upstairs x-transposition belong to one Π-block. The finest invariant relation B_x merging those entries refines Π and cannot be universal. This argument needs only one corner-only quotient class. Thus proving such a fold from darkness inside BBBB is exactly as strong there as excluding darkness outright. [Corner count](fold-converse.md) · [Canonical-factor equivalence](canonical-transfer.md)

## Correct form of the chain map

On the four-copy translation unfolding, fix an oriented primitive integer vector w. The first lattice step in that direction is a partial bijection after cutting at all corner lifts, since reversing w gives the inverse on its image. On the half-translation double Q the globally defined object is the **unoriented** direction {w,−w}; an oriented germ, or a lift to the translation unfolding, is needed to say “downstream.” Two distinct regular same-parity lifts have a legal straight connection precisely when some primitive direction places them on the same uninterrupted leaf. Unfolding a connection gives an integral displacement and its primitive direction; conversely an interval on such a leaf gives the connection. One may use either lift of each physical mark. Closed regular leaves as well as corner-ended intervals occur. D25 bounded the number of interrupted chains but found no bound on the number of regular lattice cycles from the inlet count alone. ## An area-independent repair of the aggregate count

**Lemma.** For any fixed rational direction on Q, *all* regular points of Q can be partitioned into at most 2r+2 sets such that any two distinct points in the same set are joined by a legal straight segment. The connecting direction can differ from the partition direction. For lattice endpoints it therefore supplies a genuine primitive-direction chain, though not necessarily the chain used to build the partition.

In the chosen direction each convex corner of Q has one emanating ray and each reflex corner has three, since their cone angles are π and 3π. Following these rays on a square-tiled surface gives a finite graph of straight corner-to-corner segments: in a rational direction continuation between lattice germs is a finite partial permutation, and a ray issuing from a corner cannot enter a closed regular cycle by reversibility. There are exactly E=((r+4)+3r)/2=2r+2 saddle connections, counting a loop edge once. Their open intervals exclude their corner endpoints. The complementary periodic regions are open Euclidean cylinders: regular leaves there are closed, and the surface is oriented. This uses the room double itself, with both convex and reflex corners cut. [Periodic cylinder setting](../../illumination-generalization-2026-11-06/fable/directions-2026-11-09/d13-cylinder-cover/theory.md)

Match each cylinder to a different incident saddle connection. To see that this is possible, take any set of j cylinders and count edge-side occurrences on their boundary circles. Each cylinder has two boundary circles, each with at least one occurrence. Each saddle connection has exactly two sides on Q. Thus at least j different saddle connections are incident to the set, and the finite marriage theorem supplies the matching, even if both sides of an edge face the same cylinder.

Start with each of the E open saddle connections as a separate set and adjoin each open cylinder to its matched set. Two points in one open edge see each other along the edge. Two points in one cylinder see each other by a strip chord, using a horizontal arc if their heights agree. For a cylinder point and a point on its matched edge, choose the latter's representative on the completed rim and use a chord whose open part lies in the cylinder. None of these paths hits a corner. This proves the lemma, with no bound on the number of closed lattice leaves, no group assumption, and no search.

For q regular lifts in the source parity the lemma guarantees at least q²/E−q **ordered distinct lit pairs** by summing c_i(c_i−1) over the piece sizes c_i. This addresses D25's uncontrolled cycle term for illumination **across directions**; its fixed-direction chain count remains a different question. The gain at a prescribed pair can be stated exactly as a sufficient test. Form the graph on the q lifts consisting of all four source–target edges and their images under the pure affine group fixing the base parities. If this graph has chromatic number greater than E, every such partition has an edge inside one piece; an inverse affine map gives a legal segment on one of the four original lift pairs. Thus a physically dark pair forces the explicit bound χ≤2r+2≤18 on this marked orbital graph. It also forces every partition obtained above in every rational direction to be a proper coloring of that graph.

## The specified pair

The color bound does not force a collision: the four seed edges by themselves form a bipartite graph. Even a large orbit or a high edge count does not by itself force high chromatic number. In the primitive case at n≥49 it is the group on **sheets** that contains A_n, whereas the graph and its coloring use actual affine returns on **marked lifts**. In the imprimitive case BBBB also gives no such marked return. Counting directions with multiplicity or counting all pairs in the pieces therefore supplies no lower bound for this one graph meeting the interior of a piece. The physical direction of a strip chord is already legal; the missing implication is that some piece contains a source and target lift of the specified pair, possibly after an affine return, or that a different legal chain joins them. Neither branch of the general illumination theorem is closed.

## Following the direction question

Quantifying over **all** the partitions is already exact. Given a legal segment between distinct lattice lifts, take its own primitive direction. The segment lies on a regular closed leaf in an open cylinder or in the interior of one saddle connection, and hence both endpoints lie in one piece for *every* choice of the matching in that direction. The converse is the lemma above. This exactness alone restates reachability; restricting directions or forcing a quantitative incidence would require additional input.

There is a stronger limitation on the proposed angular-equidistribution argument. For a fixed pair of lifts, the primitive directions that light it are either empty or dense in the circle of unoriented directions, even on restricting to the primitive-vector parity of one witness. To see this, take a finite-index derivative group lifting to affine maps that fix every lattice mark and corner individually, and intersect with the principal level-two subgroup. The finite-index assertion follows from the finite set of fixed-degree pillowcase covers and the finite marking set. For each rational slope u different from a witness w, a positive power P of the integral parabolic about u belongs to this subgroup. Every P^j w still lights the *same* endpoint pair and has the same parity as w; its slope tends to u. Rational slopes are dense. This proves density in angle, not an area-uniform density estimate or existence of a first witness. [Finite-index and marked-direction argument](pole-winding.md)

So even an arbitrarily narrow angular sector detects every actually lit pair in principle. What is missing is **nonemptiness** of a witness orbit for the prescribed endpoints. An equidistribution theorem conditional on having that orbit cannot supply it. A genuinely stronger follow-on would give a positive lower bound on that pair's incidence from the room geometry *before* assuming any witness.

## Own-double parity: exactly what the star count excludes

Let R be the regular physical lattice sites (interior and smooth wall sites), and q_x their number of lifts on Q in parity x. D68 gives 2q_x=n−f_x−3z_x by counting cell corners, with f_x convex and z_x reflex sites. In BBBB every q_x is positive: q_x=0 would make B_x discrete. Thus the projection of R onto (Z/2)² is surjective at **every** area in the core. The R34 argument needs every parity other than that of the marks to lack a regular site. More generally, even a single missing parity is impossible in BBBB. Without assuming BBBB, if only one class has regular sites, summing over the other three gives 3n≤4r+4. [D68 star identity](../../illumination-generalization-2026-11-06/fable/directions-2026-11-09/d68-one-target/README.md)

**Stronger coset exclusion.** When n>2r+2, R is not contained in a translate of *any* proper additive subgroup of Z². The singular corner incidences over all cells total (r+4)+3r=4r+4. If every cell had at most two regular corners, there would be at least 2n singular incidences. Thus some cell contains three regular corners. Their differences generate Z², since any three vertices of a unit square form a unimodular triangle. More quantitatively, any fixed proper coset misses at least two corners per cell, hence at least 2n−4r−4 regular cell-corner incidences. Since a regular Q lift accounts for two incidences, at least n−2r−2 regular Q lifts have physical coordinates outside the coset. For r≤8 this exclusion holds throughout the open area range n≥21.

This kills a **global missing-residue** proof on Q, including confinement to an odd-index sublattice. It does not say the sites occur on a specified unfolded itinerary, on the particular marked chain, or at a common time for two trajectories. A congruence involving sheets or winding can still separate a pair.

## Following on: a simultaneous growth test on actual marked chains

Fix lifts s,t. Let F be a cylinder multitwist of Q in a rational direction, affine with one common derivative, with its cylinder boundaries fixed pointwise. Suppose s and t lie on regular open-cylinder leaves; they may be in different cylinders. Write C_s,C_t for the leaf circumferences and u,v for the respective periods under F. On each leaf F is a circle rotation. For a lattice billiard the periods are finite (or this can be taken as a hypothesis). Let g=gcd(u,v). Take any two arcs I,J on these leaves such that **every** point of I sees every point of J on Q. For example both arcs may lie in the same open cylinder of a *different* rational direction, where strip chords give the paths, or in one open convex chart.

There is an exact test for whether a common power of F places this particular pair in I×J. In Z/g form the two sets of residues
    
    A = {i mod g : F^i(s) is in I},       B = {j mod g : F^j(t) is in J}.

The test succeeds exactly when A intersects B. Indeed one common power exists for given i,j just when k≡i mod u and k≡j mod v are compatible; compatibility is equality modulo g. Apply F^(−k) to the resulting single legal chord to get a single legal trajectory from s to t. It develops in a primitive lattice direction, so this certifies a chain of the requested pair. No joining of shots or inference from the number of other lit pairs is involved.

**Growth consequence.** In any one residue modulo g the s-images form a uniformly spaced set of u/g points on their circle, and the t-images form v/g uniformly spaced points. Accordingly open arcs satisfying

    length(I) > C_s g/u,        length(J) > C_t g/v

give a witness, regardless of phase. More sharply, if just one arc beats its spacing, it meets every residue and the other need only contain *one* point of its full orbit. At equality open ends may miss. This is a sufficient geometric test, independent of B(r) and of any angular equidistribution hypothesis. For a hypothetical dark pair its residue sets must be disjoint for every such choice, in all four lift combinations.

The remaining obstruction is specific: the two twist orbits must encounter mutually visible windows in a *common residue*. Large u and v alone do not imply that: if u=v then each residue supplies just one position per endpoint. The star count gives regular sites but no such phase information, and alternating sheet monodromy does not prescribe joint affine returns. I have no positive lower bound for this overlap for all BBBB pairs. No room, ray, direction or reflection-word discovery search was run.
