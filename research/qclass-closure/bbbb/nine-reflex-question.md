# Where nine enters the 22-wall proof

**Decision.** Nine is forced by the staircase construction. We also have independent witnesses that each of those nine corners matters to the marked pair in the fixed room. Neither fact gives a corner count for a dark pair in a BBBB room. I would pause the sequence of sufficient lighting tests here.

**The construction.** The five-square staircase has three reflex corners. Each survives in all three reflected copies. The four seam endpoints smooth out pairs of convex corner incidences, so the finished room has nine reflexes and thirteen convex corners. The darkness proof uses the fact that an unfolded shot between the marked images passes an intermediate lattice point with an odd coordinate. Every such point in the staircase is a vertex. A smooth seam endpoint upstairs can give a reversal at a convex vertex downstairs, but retracing cannot get a ray from one marked image to the other. There is no step in that proof at which one picks a distinguished ninth corner. [Manuscript, parity and reversal lemmas](../../illumination-publication/repository/preprints/twenty-two-wall/manuscript.tex)

**The fixed-room necessity test.** Keep the walls in place. Allow a ray to pass straight through a specified reflex vertex if both adjacent open segments lie inside the room; continue to forbid convex vertices. The nine saved certificates each connect the same two marks and meet exactly one reflex, a different one in each certificate. Thus restoring the ordinary stop rule at any eight of the nine leaves a connecting route through the omitted one. All nine together stop every route by the darkness theorem. Eight certificates have their sole corner at the midpoint. In the ninth the legs to (0,−1) take one and three primitive steps. It is a concrete reason a midpoint-only account misses one of these witnesses. Physically erasing a corner to produce a different polygon also changes its reflecting walls; this test keeps those walls fixed. [Replayed certificates](corner-support.md)

**What survives beyond this room?** The reviewed fold count gives a nine-reflex lower bound for the separated-image, three-off-class fold mechanism. A BBBB room cannot have this quotient at any reflex count. The prime-degree 34-wall example also shows that darkness in general need not arise from a proper fold. A universal proof has to impose a cost on actual trajectories without first postulating the staircase quotient. The example cov4181 explains why simply budgeting three reflexes for each of three direction parities is unsafe: one of its classes has zero reflexes and its algebraic midpoint test still fails there. This is a failure of that test, not an example of a dark BBBB pair. [Fold hypotheses and counterexample](fold-converse.md) · [Parity accounting](corner-support.md)

**Question for a billiards geometer (unsent).** Let S,T be distinct interior lattice points of the same coordinate parity in a strictly simple orthogonal lattice polygon. Keep ordinary reflections on open edges and forbid every convex vertex. Choose at most eight reflex vertices as stops. At every other reflex vertex allow only straight continuation with both open legs inside the polygon. Is there a geometric reason some finite route from S to T must avoid all the chosen stops, or is there an example where eight stops suffice? Even for a polygon with many more than eight reflexes, an example would be informative: it would refute this route to the lower bound without settling the 20-wall conjecture.

This question is deliberately stronger than the lattice-pair goal. A positive answer handles the case r≤8 by choosing all reflexes. It does not require nine disjoint routes, three blockers per parity, or a fold. One can test the distinction already in rooms with more walls. Its answer for arbitrary marks of other classes is a separate question. The project has neither a universal proof nor a counterexample to this stronger statement. Ordinary finite-blocking results do not directly count these stops: the relaxed paths pass through cone points and obey a prescribed physical continuation there.
