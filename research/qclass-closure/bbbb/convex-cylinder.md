# A single cylinder on the unfolding can certify the whole room

**Theorem for review.** On the four-copy translation unfolding M of a strictly simple orthogonal lattice room, cut a periodic direction at leaves through reflex vertices only. Call the resulting maximal open annuli geometric cylinders. If M has just one such cylinder, every pair of distinct interior room points is lit, with no arithmetic restriction on the endpoints. More generally the same conclusion holds for distinct physical points having lifts anywhere in the closure of a single geometric cylinder preserved by the half-turn of M.

This is a condition on M itself. The earlier “one cylinder” on the room double DP can be a pair of exchanged cylinders on M. R22 has exactly such a pair in each of (4,7) and (7,4); neither passes the present test. The earlier open-source/closed-target lemma leaves open the case of two rim points. ## The boundary argument

Let τ reverse both sheet signs. It projects to the identity on the physical room, has derivative −I, and fixes precisely the lifts of polygon corners. A reflex corner is a singularity of M and lies outside the geometric cylinder. Convex corners are regular on M but remain forbidden billiard contacts.

If the cylinder is preserved by τ, use its metric completion (R/CZ) × [0,H]. Each point of a completed rim has a well-defined image on M, including where the rim folds onto a saddle-connection graph. In the interior and hence on the completion the involution is (x,y) ↦ (b−x,H−y). Consequently there are exactly two forbidden points inside the cylinder, at y=H/2 with abscissas differing by C/2. Conversely a geometric cylinder containing a convex lift is preserved, since its half-turn image is a cylinder in the same direction containing that lift. In a one-cylinder decomposition invariance is automatic and the completed cylinder maps onto M.

For two distinct physical interior points choose representatives in the completed cylinder. Each representative may be replaced by its τ image. Put both in the lower closed half.

* If at least one is strictly between the lower rim and the middle line, a segment between suitable developed representatives has its open part inside the annulus and below the middle line. The same holds with one on the rim and the other on the middle line. Such a segment is legal.
* If both lie on the middle line, τ there is reflection of the circle fixing the two convex lifts. Choose both representatives in the same open half-circle between the fixed points. The segment along that arc avoids both.
* If both lie on the lower rim, replace the second representative (t,0) by (b−t,H). Join (s,0) to (b−t+kC,H). The open segment stays inside. Its only possible forbidden contact is at half-time. Its midpoint abscissa is (s+b−t+kC)/2. This is fixed by τ exactly when s=t modulo C. That would identify the original two lower-rim representatives and give the same physical mark, contrary to hypothesis. **Every winding is legal in this case.**

These cases include boundary representatives without continuing a ray *along* a singular rim. Unfolding and folding the segments gives ordinary specular reflections in P. The proof uses no restriction on wall count or room size.

## A parity obstruction to the one-cylinder test

In a normalized lattice room with at least one reflex corner, a single geometric cylinder in a primitive lattice direction has **height exactly 2** in the integral chart sending that direction to horizontal. To see this, its bottom and top saddle connections are identified by translations on the critical graph. Reduction of the transverse coordinate modulo the cylinder height H is consequently a global circle coordinate: every absolute transverse period belongs to Hℤ. The absolute periods of M span 2ℤ². In particular, for each physical wall displacement e the wall and the reverse of its half-turn image form a closed path of period 2e, since its ends are fixed by τ. The horizontal and vertical wall-length gcds are separately one, and the map to the torus of periods 2ℤ² gives the reverse inclusion. The integral straightening matrix preserves this period lattice. Thus H divides 2. The cylinder has integral height, and its two convex fixed points at H/2 are integral relative to the rim, so H is even. This gives H=2 and C=2n by area.

It follows that **all physical reflex corners must lie on one affine parity line**, i.e., in at most two of the four classes. Explicitly det(v,z) modulo 2 is the same for every reflex corner z. This transverse parity is well defined on the physical room since changing a sheet sign preserves coordinates modulo 2. Every reflex lies on the critical graph, at rim height. Exactly two physical convex corners lie on the complementary parity line: all its lattice lifts have middle height 1, and only the two fixed points there project to physical corners. These are necessary shape tests. If reflexes occupy three parity classes, no direction can yield one geometric cylinder on M. R22 already fails this test, as does DB55 with its reflexes in all four classes. Conversely these parity data alone say nothing about the number of separate cylinders. For a normalized room that *does* have one cylinder, the opposite-rim proof therefore supplies an off-class midpoint for every same-parity pair in the two rim classes, without checking any winding numerically.

## Adding a rectangular bay without splitting the cylinder

Suppose a wall has a subinterval of width w, with endpoints strictly inside the wall. Attach an external rectangular bay of depth L on this mouth, keeping the polygon strictly simple. In the chosen nonaxial periodic direction put t=|tangential velocity / normal velocity|. Assume Lt is an integer multiple of w, and **every lift of each mouth endpoint is on the old critical graph** (the complement of the open geometric cylinders). Then a one-geometric-cylinder direction remains one after the attachment.

Indeed, replace the former wall reflection at any interior mouth point by the trip to the far wall and back. In the transverse unfolded coordinate the round trip adds 2Lt, a multiple of 2w. It returns at the same point with the original tangential velocity and reversed normal velocity, exactly the old signed reflection map. At the far wall the transverse shift Lt is a multiple of w. A corner hit there would require starting at a mouth endpoint. Conversely an interior trajectory in the bay has nonzero normal velocity and reaches the mouth in finite forward or backward time, except for the exceptional leaves through its corners.

For completeness, take a small interval transverse to the old open annulus that meets all its closed leaves once, or use successive local transverse charts of the annulus. Away from the two endpoint orbits the inserted detours vary continuously: their reflection word can change only at a bay corner, which maps back to a mouth endpoint. The regular leaves of the old annulus are therefore still one connected family of closed leaves, with the same transverse coordinate. This can also be seen locally at the old mirror by cutting its regular flow box and inserting the strip of round trips, whose exit map on signed coordinates is the original gluing. Every open family in the new surface meets old room interiors because the bay cannot trap a nonaxial leaf. Contracting its excursions gives old trajectories, and the finite old critical graph and the finitely many endpoint separatrices cannot contain an open family. Hence there is no second geometric cylinder. Old reflex corners are untouched. Mouth endpoints become reflexes; the two far corners are convex and belong to exceptional leaves. Disjoint attachments satisfying the same conditions can be made simultaneously by the identical argument.

## Application with twenty walls and no length threshold

D69's DBas is the union of [0,5]×[0,4], [5,9]×[1,2] and [9,13]×[−1,5]. In the archived direction (−4,1), M has a single cylinder of circumference 96 and height 2 in the integral chart straightening that direction. The area check is 96·2=4·48. Its only two interior convex lifts are at (11,1) and (59,1) in that chart. Thus **DBas lights every pair of distinct real interior points**, including pairs with both marks on the critical graph.

Attach one rectangular bay on 1≤x≤3 at the top of the left chamber (y=4), and another on the same x interval at its bottom (y=0). Write A,B for their positive integer depths. All lifts of (1,4),(3,4),(1,0),(3,0) are on the rims in the saved direction. The mouth width is 2 and t=4, so 4A and 4B are multiples of 2 at *every* permitted depth. The bays lie on opposite sides of the left chamber and touch the old room only at their mouths. In particular each bay adds two reflex corners, giving r=8 and exactly 20 maximal walls. Horizontal wall lengths include 1. Exposed vertical walls of the untouched right chamber include lengths 2 and 3. The room is normalized. Its area is 48+2(A+B)≥52, the left chamber represents all four interior parities, and both depths can vary independently without bound. At areas above 64 the existing proper-factor bound also puts it in the canonical BBBB core. At smaller areas the illumination proof is unchanged.

Every two distinct interior points of each room are lit, **including points in either bay**. This conclusion is stronger than transport of an old witness between two old marks. For A=2,B=3 the checker constructs the predicted normalized 58-cell room and finds exactly one cylinder, of circumference 116 and height 2. The uniform conclusion follows from the insertion proof, not from interpolation of control cases.

## The stronger off-class midpoint question

For lattice marks of one parity, a legal segment in an integral chart has even displacement (d,h). Its midpoint is outside the source parity exactly when (d,h) is nonzero modulo 4. In an invariant cylinder with endpoints on a common rim, the opposite-rim construction above yields displacement (d+kC,H). If H or C equals 2 modulo 4 it produces an off-class midpoint. In particular H=2 suffices for all such rim pairs in the DBas chart. The transverse measure of the old cylinder is unchanged by the bay insertion (the local return maps on transverse sections are unchanged), so this conclusion persists there. The all-real lighting theorem does not require an off-class midpoint and does not assert one for every middle-line pair.

There is also an exact test for segments with unequal heights anywhere in a cylinder. Index the two developed coordinates of the endpoints by (x,y),(X,Y), put h=Y−y and d=X−x+kC. For each convex point (a,b) strictly between the two heights, the segment hits it if and only if

    (x−a)h + (b−y)d = 0 modulo C|h|.

There are no interior forbidden points in an exchanged cylinder. In a preserved one the two convex points share their height and are C/2 apart. If that height is crossed at fraction α of the travel, the two collision tests combine into one linear congruence in k modulo the period of 2α in R/Z. Thus bad k are empty or one residue class (possibly all). For any prescribed parity of k, checking its first two representatives is complete: if both k and k+2 are bad, the bad period divides 2, so that parity is wholly bad. Windings 0,1,2,3 suffice for the unequal-height off-class test in that chart. Equal-height clean leaf segments can be checked directly. All congruences here refer to lattice marks in an integral straightened chart.

The one-cylinder theorem gives a sufficient condition for arbitrary physical endpoints. Its failure only leaves a room undecided. R22's two charts (4,7) and (7,4) each yield two geometric cylinders on M of size 30×1, containing no convex fixed point, consistent with its dark pair. The unresolved structural question is which rooms with at most eight reflex corners can evade this condition and the overlapping-cylinder tests in every direction. None of the present arguments forces a general room to have a one-cylinder direction. The d82 off-class reachability lemma also remains open.
