# A torsion-order bound from physical lattice specializations

**Result for review.** Conditional on the d89 dark-locus and uniform physical-denominator theorems, d115's physical real chart, and the projected torsion-section input of [the rank-two note](rank-two-periodic.md), the exact order of an endpoint measured from a labeled zero in the **canonical saturated abelian quotient** divides **4D(r)** on the persistent branch. D(r) is the d89 denominator for the original room's reflex count. Its value is unknown. The argument uses the full local dark locus around the arithmetic seed. A bounded physical denominator on one line of rooms, by itself, is insufficient. The bound gives no exclusion of non-Weierstrass torsion.

## Constant order and the choice of lattice

Work in characteristic zero on a connected smooth labeled branch. Filip's torsion conclusion gives an annihilator m for the section after the finite cover which names it. In a local integral homology frame the finite étale group A[m] is constant. The exact additive order is locally constant and hence constant on this branch, and is unchanged by monodromy permuting its sheets. This concerns smooth abelian fibers; specialization to a semiabelian boundary is unnecessary.

Use the original room's four-copy surface X. Let V be the real absolute period coefficient space of the full d89 locus, rational by d89, and take Λ=V∩H¹(X,ℤ), modulo torsion as usual. The abelian quotient of Jac(X) with this saturated cohomology lattice has Betti torus V*/Λ*, with Λ*=Hom(Λ,ℤ). Indeed restriction from integral absolute homology onto Λ* is surjective by primitivity. This fixes an integral model: an arbitrary isogenous presentation need not give the same numerical order. If b is a labeled reflex zero and x a labeled interior endpoint lift, write ℓ∈V* for a lift of the torsion character of the projected divisor x−b. The relative period w in one fixed path chart satisfies w(z)=ℓ(z), extending the real functional complex-linearly. To see the choice of lift, integrate the forms on the quotient along the projected relative path; in a labeled simply connected patch their Betti endpoint is the fixed torsion character modulo Λ*. Changing the path changes the lift by Λ*. Equivalently, the Abel–Jacobi logarithm of a flat torsion section is locally the same rational Betti vector modulo integral periods. In particular the order is the least N>0 with Nℓ(Λ)⊂ℤ.

A single square-tiled fiber only tests ℓ on the real and imaginary parts of its tautological form. For rank two there are four integral directions to test. A projection to an elliptic quotient can send a point of arbitrarily large order in its kernel to zero. The other directions must be tested before deducing an order bound from physical denominators.

## Probe all absolute periods with actual rooms

Take a smooth normalized physical seed of area n₀≥120 in the d89 construction; all four physical cross pairs are dark throughout its local real branch. Fix the reflected-copy coordinates so the basepoint is b and the chosen lift represents the physical mark in one copy. Use the lift ρ of the physical real structure for which ρ*ω=conjugate(ω). The marked real structure includes the prescribed permutation of mark labels. It preserves the unmarked rational absolute space and integral lattice, hence acts on Λ by an integral involution. By d89 and the local real-chart identification in d115, the projection from the marked tangent onto V⊗ℂ is an isomorphism onto the unmarked tangent and is equivariant for this real structure. Consequently every real fixed increment lifts uniquely inside the *physical dark branch* for sufficiently small parameter. In particular a real ρ-even increment varies only horizontal wall coordinates; an imaginary ρ-odd increment varies only vertical ones. [Physical real chart](accumulation.md) · [Uniform divisor and dependencies](dark-locus.md)

Fix u∈Λ and set v₊=u+ρ*u and v₋=u−ρ*u. They are integral, ρ-even and ρ-odd respectively. Test the two perturbations separately, using a large positive integer m:

    m z₀ + 2v₊,                 m z₀ + 2i v₋.

These are m-fold physical dilations of points arbitrarily close to the seed on the two real germs. All corners remain lattice points with b as origin. In detail, for a path γ between fixed lifts of physical corners, γ−Jγ is an absolute cycle, where J is the orientation involution. The unmarked tangent is J-odd, so its integral v on γ is read as one half of the integral evaluation on γ−Jγ. The factor 2 in each perturbation therefore gives integral changes in every relevant wall displacement. The orthogonal axis has unchanged displacements apart from the m-fold dilation. In a chosen physical copy w is the actual displacement of x from b up to fixed coordinate signs.

**Threshold after normalization.** For example the horizontal wall vector is m e+h with e the primitive seed vector and h an integral fixed increment. If h is proportional to e, separate axis normalization restores the original room shape and area n₀. Otherwise the horizontal gcd divides a fixed nonzero minor e_i h_j−e_j h_i (take the same minor with m e+h). The vertical gcd is m. The raw area is m²(n₀+O(1/m)), so the normalized area tends to infinity. The vertical test is identical. In both cases d89 applies to the physical marks for all sufficiently large m. They need not remain same-parity lattice marks on these auxiliary rooms: d89 bounds **all** dark interior marks. Undoing the two primitive normalizations multiplies coordinates by integers, so their denominators in the raw lattice also divide D(r).

The dilated seed displacement already has denominator dividing D(r). Subtract it from each tested displacement. Its horizontal or vertical change gives, respectively,

    2D(r)ℓ(v₊) ∈ ℤ,             2D(r)ℓ(v₋) ∈ ℤ.

Since v₊+v₋=2u, this proves 4D(r)ℓ(u)∈ℤ for every u∈Λ. This is a uniform annihilator; no sharpness claim is made. It does not follow by reading the seed's mark denominator, which may be one.

**Passing to the actual rank-two factor.** If a line's actual orbit closure is smaller than the d89 ambient locus, its absolute rational subsystem on the smooth generic branch is contained in V. Its integral lattice maps into Λ, so restriction of the character preserves the annihilator. For a downstairs minimal orientation cover, pull its integral absolute classes back to X; the resulting subsystem lies in the upstairs absolute tangent for the cover locus (finite Hurwitz incidence in the [minimal quotient reduction](minimal-quotient-rigidity.md)). Pairing the relative path with pullbacks gives the downstairs relative evaluation. Equivalently push forward its Abel–Jacobi class; no division by covering degree is needed in this direction. Take b over a downstairs zero. These passages prove divisibility, not equality of orders between the factors. The proof applies to marks inherited from the hypothetical d89 dark branch, not to arbitrary periodic points on every rank-two locus.

## What Mordell–Weil and Manin–Mumford actually give

| Source checked | Hypotheses, conclusion and use |
| --- | --- |
| [Möller, §§3 and 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0410262) | Theorem 3.1 gives a finite Mordell–Weil group for the specified trace-field abelian factor over a Teichmüller curve after a given finite unramified base change. Its proof uses the maximal-Higgs summand and its Galois conjugates. Theorem 3.3 sends periodic points, based at a zero, to torsion in this factor. Theorems 5.1–5.2 classify periodic points on non-torus-cover **genus-two Veech surfaces**, with additional geometric arguments in the two genus-two strata. Checked in the primary paper. Rank two here refers to the tangent of a higher-dimensional locus; already the smallest surviving normalization has genus four. |
| [Lang–Néron, statement and proof in Conrad, Theorem 2.1](https://virtualmath1.stanford.edu/~conrad/papers/Kktrace.pdf) | For a finitely generated regular extension K/k and an abelian variety A/K, A(K) modulo the image of the K/k-trace on k-points is finitely generated. In the application k=ℂ and K is the function field of a connected labeled algebraic base. Even with zero trace this gives finite torsion for that field, with no order or curve-incidence classification uniform in finite base changes. Used to delimit the proposed extension, not to prove the bound. |
| [Manin–Mumford, curve statement in Möller's proof of Theorem 3.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0410262) · [Bourbaki account of Raynaud's theorem](https://numdam.org/item/SB_1983-1984__26__213_0.pdf) | Over characteristic zero a nonelliptic irreducible curve in an abelian variety contains only finitely many torsion points. Checked the curve statement and Möller's application; no relative or effective version is assumed. Finiteness allows a specified torsion point on every fiber. The image curve here generates its abelian-surface factor. |
| [Filip, §5.1 and Theorem 5.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1311.2350) | Used as in the preceding note for the torsion input on the rational, zero-relative-kernel marked locus. The arithmetic probe above is a further deduction and needs independent review. |

**Where the extension breaks.** Even granting the proposed three-dimensional variation of the polarized abelian surface, for every m its primitive m-torsion points form a nonempty finite étale cover of the smooth base; on this cover there is a tautological section of exact order m. Variation is still three-dimensional. Thus dimension and Mordell–Weil finiteness cannot themselves distinguish the Weierstrass sections. This example concerns the ambient abelian scheme only. The missing restriction is that the section must lie on the *particular curve image*, with a non-Weierstrass preimage on its normalization. Even order two does not settle this if the curve image is singular at a two-torsion point. Nor does d105 exclude arbitrary covering constructions. [Scope of the classification test](rank-two-periodic.md)

The result replaces the union over all torsion orders by A[4D(r)] on the persistent rank-two branch, at most (4D(r))⁴ ambient points per smooth fiber. It gives no computable value for D(r) and does not identify which normalization branches persist. No non-Weierstrass exclusion or area bound follows.
