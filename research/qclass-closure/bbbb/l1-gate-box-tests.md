**R22.** The shortest vertical travel is 2. From S=(0,0) to T=(2,2) its only possible component sequence is the width-2 left lower slab followed by the width-4 upper slab. The gate is (0,1); the other opening is inaccessible from the lower component. Both travels are 1. The two generator columns are −2 and 4, so Λ=2ℤ. Reflect source and target images independently across side walls: the source images are 0 or −2 modulo 4, the target images ±2 modulo 8. The physical gate abscissa is their average. Each incident choice gives either 0+2ℤ or 1+2ℤ; both miss. The primitive relation is (1,−2) on moduli (1/4,1/8), dual gate coefficient 1/2 and width 1/2. This is a distinct-slab arithmetic relation; there is no repeated visit.

For one upper-slab roof–floor excursion the widths are (2,4,4,4), travels all 1, and the box is (0,1) × [(0,1) or (3,4)] × (1,3), choosing one open roof component. In each oriented branch the lattice has columns 2wⱼ(i/4−1[j<i]) for i=1,2,3 and j=0,…,3, up to coordinate signs. At travel 2 the developed crossing is the midpoint between even endpoint images, hence an integer; folding preserves integrality. The unit-width open roof component excludes it for every orientation. The dual coordinate vector e₂ has relation (2,4,−4,−4) and width 1. Repeated-slab difference relations exist, but this midpoint certificate uses all four visits. For the direct transverse image K=2, the first left roof and subsequent floor require 2K/H<1 and 3K/H>1, giving 4<H<6. Repetition permits only even H. The H=4 midpoint check covers additional transverse images at that fixed travel. Neither short check proves darkness for arbitrary H; the three-copy fold gives the global odd-coordinate vertex obstruction. [Fold proof](l1-periodic-obstruction.md)
