Advances: L1 — on a rational dark gap flat, obstruction for the open part of a fixed oriented itinerary is exactly a quotient-coset miss, with smooth boundary events tested on their own faces. A growing gap traversed once by a pair of compatible end legs must occur in a persistent relation with another growing gap. For a fixed itinerary its coefficients have a finite bound from the transverse data alone. This does not prove that every growing gap admits such an itinerary.

### Exact test at a generic point

Assume the dark flat supplied in the brief. Use the conical linear flat before box normalization, with rational transverse data y, and restrict to its positive marked chart. The accumulation input concerns primitive same-parity marked examples under its stated hypotheses. A conclusion about all dark families needs the corresponding input for those families. [Accumulation scope](accumulation.md)

Fix signed copies, horizontal progress/reversal events, and split the visits at refined x levels. There are m=g+1 visits. Write aⱼ=x_{γ(j)}, Pⱼ=2h′ⱼ, H=Σaⱼ, Yᵢ=Σ_{j<i}aⱼ. Use signed transverse event coordinates zᵢ, with endpoint values absorbed into constants so z₀=zₘ=0. For some fixed rational bⱼ the developed passage equations are

    aⱼ s = zⱼ₊₁ − zⱼ + bⱼ + Pⱼ kⱼ.

Here bⱼ includes the chosen end reflections and interval origins. The open box J uses the same signed coordinates. Assume the rectangular legs, true faces, and endpoint sectors of the gate-box model; treat an axial or subdivision-vertex passage separately. Elimination gives z=v+A k with vᵢ=YᵢΣb/H−Σ_{j<i}bⱼ and Aᵢⱼ=Pⱼ(Yᵢ/H−1[j<i]).

A real character ℓ of event space is integral on Aℤᵐ iff nⱼ=Pⱼ(Σ_{i>j}ℓᵢ−ΣᵢℓᵢYᵢ/H) are integers. The inverse formula is ℓᵢ=nᵢ₋₁/Pᵢ₋₁−nᵢ/Pᵢ with Σⱼ nⱼaⱼ/Pⱼ=0. At a point whose parameters in a rational basis of L_x are rationally independent this is exactly the pushed-forward condition D in the brief. Importantly,

    ℓ·v = −Σⱼ nⱼ bⱼ/Pⱼ.

Thus the persistent characters **and their affine levels** are fixed on the flat. The offset vector v itself can vary.

The closed-subgroup character theorem in Euclidean space gives the closure as the intersection of all congruences ℓ·z≡ℓ·v mod 1 (for the group itself use right side zero). Equivalently its connected direction is V=(span ℓ(D))⊥ and its image modulo V is a discrete lattice coset. Indeed A has full row rank, so the closure spans event space; a closed subgroup splits as a vector subspace plus a lattice on its orthogonal complement. The integer characters of that lattice recover exactly the stated congruences. Since J is open, meeting the closure is equivalent to meeting the actual coset. A generic miss therefore persists at every positive point of L_x, including rational specializations, where additional relations can only shrink the closure.

**D231 endpoint convention.** Use the actual partial travel from an interior-slab mark to its first level, or from the last level to the target. Its modulus is this travel divided by that slab's transverse period. It need not equal a later full-visit modulus. In refined gap coordinates, if a visit spans several subgaps, its travel is their sum and its coefficient contributes once to each in the push-forward. Reusing a physical slab alone does not give a universal difference relation.

For legal-event membership include an endpoint of Jᵢ when it is a smooth wall point continuing through the level and the incoming and outgoing sectors there give ordinary reflection. Keep forbidden corners excluded. The openness argument above applies to the relative interior of the original box. Merely closing an endpoint and testing the same closure can give a false hit: a dense group can approach a face without ever attaining its defining equality.

Here is the exact projection test for each allowed boundary face. Write its coordinate equalities as Rz=c. First impose RA k=c−Rv **over the integers**. If insoluble, discard that face. Given one solution k₀, the exact points on its affine span form z₀+Γ, where z₀=v+Ak₀ and Γ=A ker_ℤ(RA). Take the closure of Γ *in the face direction space* and quotient there by its connected part. The relative-open face is hit iff its projection meets the discrete quotient coset through z₀. This follows by density on relative-open sets and also covers a zero-dimensional face. Apply the sector check at each simultaneous event. The legal itinerary misses iff the open part and every admitted face miss. Persistence of the full-box interior congruences above should not be read as persistence of integer solvability of an added face equality at specializations. **Rank correction.** The real kernel of A is the line spanned by (aⱼ/Pⱼ). The integer winding group has rank m if that line has no nonzero integer point, and m−1 otherwise. Even on a flat of dimension ≥2, an itinerary using only one varying gap (or only proportional restrictions of gaps) has rational modulus ratios and rank m−1. The closure test needs no blanket rank-m assertion.

### Which growing gaps are tied?

Intersect L_x with the closed nonnegative orthant and normalize Σe=1. Because L_x contains a strictly positive point and has dimension ≥2, this section is a positive-dimensional compact rational polytope. An extreme point e has some zero coordinates (otherwise it is relatively interior). From any positive x₀, the ray x₀+t e stays positive in the same homogeneous order chart. A persistent relation has coefficient dγ=Σ_{γ(j)=γ}nⱼ/Pⱼ and satisfies both d·x₀=0 and d·e=0. Hence if eγ>0 and dγ≠0, a second growing gap must have a nonzero coefficient of the opposite sign. A relation between repeated visits can have d identically zero.

**Conditional tie lemma.** At a generic positive flat point suppose that a growing gap γ occurs just once, at visit M, on an oriented itinerary for which source and reverse target legs at the same slope regularly reach the two mouths of that visit. The transverse phases need not match. Their combined event vector x* lies in J and equals v+A k+τ A_M, with integer k and real τ: releasing just the middle winding joins the two developments. If all persistent relations had n_M=0, every character would annihilate A_M and x* would satisfy every affine congruence. Openness would give a legal chord, contradicting darkness. Consequently some n_M≠0, and dγ=n_M/P_M ties γ to another growing gap. This uses the open box itself, with no flatness estimate.

**Coefficient bound for the fixed itinerary.** The following finite recursion gives a bound independent of the longitudinal lengths and of the particular first-hit clearance. It is not needed for exactness. Start with the affine section E=ℝᵍ. At each node keep a flag of earlier independent persistent congruences imposed as equalities through x*, all annihilating A_M. The slice J∩E is relatively open and contains x*. Use any inscribed relative ball of radius r_E>0 (for instance half the relative inradius). Write F*=max_{1≤i≤g}Flt(i). The closed coset intersects E since x* differs from one of its points by τA_M. Its section group spans the direction of E: at each cut use a primitive character on the preceding quotient lattice. Its continuous part lies in every cut. Project onto the orthogonal complement of that continuous part inside E. The projected ball of radius r_E misses the projected lattice coset, otherwise J meets the closure. Dimension zero would already imply a hit. Flatness gives a primitive section character of norm ≤F*/(2r_E).

Lift this character to the original dual: on quotient lattices each primitive kernel is a direct summand, so restriction is surjective. Subtract earlier characters in reverse flag order to reduce each perpendicular component to at most half the corresponding earlier projected norm. Thus a valid full norm bound is

    B_E² = (F*/(2r_E))² + Σ_{earlier nodes U} (F*/(4r_U))².

The inverse potential formula and positive weighted mean zero give |nⱼ|≤Pⱼ√g B_E. If the lift detects A_M, stop. Otherwise cut through x* and continue. The dimension decreases; reaching the continuous part alone would contradict the miss. For a bound computable from the signed itinerary and rational y alone, take the maximum over the finite tree: enumerate all integer n within the displayed coefficient bounds, and all independent hyperplanes ℓ·z=−Σnⱼbⱼ/Pⱼ+k, k integral, meeting the open bounded slice. Discard empty slices. The hyperplane list and each relative inradius depend only on J,P,b and earlier cuts. Depth is at most g. This is a bound prescription, not a run. It improves on using the distance of x* to the walls because the ball need not be centered at x*: only the **next cut** has to go through x*. The bound still depends on visit count and y. ### Geometric scope and first remaining configurations

* **One separating rectangle, fixed ends.** If it is the only strip component in the growing gap, the marks are off the strip on opposite sides, both open doors can be sealed, and the end shapes and mark positions stay fixed, the existing opposite-end theorem lights the pair for every sufficiently large length. The finite-ratio refinement uses integral end templates up to common axis scalings. Even with other changing ends, the conditional tie applies at the generic point if the end legs avoid every other visit to γ. Fixed ends are unnecessary for that pointwise implication. [Long corridor](README.md) · [Ratio theorem](l1-two-gate-ratios.md)
* **Two tied gaps.** If only γ,η grow and the conditional tie applies, its pushed relation gives dγ eγ=−dη eη. This fixes their growth ratio for that itinerary; it does not show that this congruence misses the box or persists as a blocker on every alternative itinerary. No general unlocking ray follows.
* **Two strip components at the same gap.** A leg to one component may run through the other. Detecting a character at visit M then need not detect γ: for equal heights entries +1,−1 at the two visits cancel in dγ. This is a persistent *potential* relation, not a certificate that every itinerary is blocked. One needs control of the sum over all visits, or a route using that gap exactly once.
* **Mark on the strip boundary.** If the mark is on the open mouth, start the middle traversal there with zero end travel. For an opposite-end mark a finite regular backward leg supplies the open slope window, and sweeping the long middle gives a matching signed phase with fixed ends. Two marks on opposite open mouths are directly visible through the rectangle. A mark on a supporting line outside the actual mouth gains no such initial leg. Both marks on the same end can also defeat the separating-traversal hypothesis.

These are geometric residuals, not a classification theorem from r≤8. The first unproved implication is that a growing gap relevant to the marked pair must have nonzero **pushed** coefficient on some blocking itinerary. Even a tie for one gap does not exclude a positive-dimensional flat: its other independent parameter can change a different group of gaps. No relation locking every itinerary, general eight-reflex exclusion, or numerical N₀ has been established.
