# A constructive two-rectangle bound and the obstruction to chaining it

## Two rectangles

**Theorem.** Let two closed axis-aligned lattice rectangles share a positive-length side interval, with interiors on opposite sides. Take the interior of their union as the room, with no partition across the opening. Rotate coordinates so the common door is horizontal. Let D be the horizontal bounding-box span and H the vertical span. Any two distinct interior lattice marks admit a legal path with at most **6HD+5 wall reflections**, or **12HD+11 events** if passages through the original door are also counted. No parity condition is needed. This theorem concerns a full overlap: inserting a mirror along part of the overlap changes the problem.

The qualitative conclusion already follows from the project's eight-wall theorem: only the two ends of the seam can be reflex. Here is the quantitative construction. Slice the two rectangles *across* the horizontal door, using x as the direction of travel. Between the marks there can be up to three successive strips: a left wing occupied by just one rectangle, the entire overlap with height H, and a right wing occupied by just one rectangle. Travel monotonically in x. A crossing of the original horizontal door inside the overlap is always legal. The construction uses the folding charts of Theorems 2.1 and 5.1 of the eight-wall manuscript; the estimates below bound their slopes in integral units. [Manuscript, §§2 and 5](../../illumination-publication/repository/preprints/eight-wall/manuscript.tex)

If the marks see each other, use the chord. This includes two marks whose x coordinates are both in the closed overlap and both interior to the room, and the equal-abscissa case. If both outer wings traversed are occupied by the same rectangle, both wing marks are in that convex rectangle. Drop any zero-length end strip. At an interface, an interior endpoint lies strictly within the common physical cross-section. The remaining nondirect cases are:

* **One wing and the overlap.** Start in the taller overlap, reverse travel if necessary, and measure transverse distance from the outer wall common to both strips. Let the narrow height be B, let d,e be the distances from the respective marks to the interface, and let u,v be their transverse coordinates. Here d,e≥1; a mark exactly at the interface on the wing side gives a direct chord there, and a mark at the opposite edge of the overlap can serve as the wide-strip start. Pick an image z=±v+2mB with 0<|z+(e/d)u|≤B. Consecutive gaps in that image set are less than 2B, and at a tie with the specified center its nearer distinct neighbor is at distance at most B. Negate both developed coordinates if needed. The crossing coordinate is then |z+(e/d)u|/(1+e/d), strictly between 0 and B. The wide-side leg folds at most once at the common outer wall, and the narrow-side leg folds normally. The absolute transverse slope is bounded by (H+B)/d≤2H.
* **Opposite outer wings.** The middle strip has width w≥1 along x. Rotate by 180 degrees and reverse the endpoints if necessary so the entrance wing height B is at least the exit height E. In transverse coordinates the three ranges are [0,B], [0,H], [H−E,H] after a possible reflection. Let d,e≥1 be the mark-to-middle horizontal distances, and u the source height. Choose the smallest positive odd multiple K of H with K≥2Bw/d. Choose n nearest to (dK+w u)/(2Bw). Then n≥1 and θ=(2nB−u)/d satisfies |θ−K/w|≤B/d. For slopes in I=(θ−B/d,θ+B/d) the first seam is reached with signed coordinate z=u+d s−2nB in (−B,B). The developed middle exit u+(d+w)s−2nB covers (K−E,K+E) as s ranges over I: its center differs from K by at most Bw/d, and its radius is B+Bw/d. On the preimage of that exit window the developed terminal coordinate ranges over an open interval of length 2E(d+w+e)/(d+w)>2E. It therefore hits an image of the target in the correct top strip, spaced by 2E. Folding at the two seams gives the same local motion on both sides (respectively |z| and H−|z'|, where z' is displacement from K). A zero of z or z' is a smooth outer wall, while strict inequalities exclude the reflex ends. Since K<2Bw/d+2H, every selected slope satisfies |s|≤K/w+2B/d≤6H.

In at most three strips the total absolute unfolded transverse travel is at most 6HD. Within a strip of integral height at least one, successive wall contacts are separated by at least one in transverse travel. Counting interiors of the strips adds at most three to the travel bound; allowing one smooth wall contact at each of the two strip interfaces adds at most two more. Between successive reflections a straight leg crosses the original door at most once. A leg along the door can be shortened to the direct same-line case for such endpoints (the constructed nondirect paths have nonzero transverse slope). This proves both bounds. All indices above come from rounding or taking the first target image inside an interval; no search is involved.

**Localization in a larger room.** The construction never travels longitudinally beyond the two mark abscissas. Hence the same bound holds if the component traversed in that open x-slab has exactly the one-, two- or three-strip profile used above, with the stated solid lateral walls and no extra physical corner on a proposed contact. The rest of the room beyond the two end lines is unrestricted. This is one way to reuse the theorem along a chamber graph without treating an open side door as a mirror.

## A sufficient chain lemma that keeps a common slope

For a monotone chain use longitudinal travels d_i>0, cumulative A_i, transverse periods P_i twice the chamber widths, and open transverse overlap widths w_i. Fix the positive transverse sheet at each interface and allow signed slopes. At seam i the unwrapped crossing is S+A_i s minus the periods already subtracted; the physical positive-sheet window repeats every P_i. The endpoint condition for a fixed developed target image gives **one slope**. Only the intermediate door conditions are open inequalities.

One can propagate an entire open slope interval without assuming independent choices. At the first seam choose a translated open window, giving a slope interval of length w₁/A₁. If

    A_i w_(i−1)/A_(i−1) ≥ P_i + w_i       (2 ≤ i < number of chambers),
    A_last w_(last−1)/A_(last−1) > P_last,

then every terminal height in the last chamber is reachable. Indeed the earlier interval maps to an open interval of length A_i w_(i−1)/A_(i−1) before the next fold. Any interval of length at least P_i+w_i contains a whole translated *open* door window, including the endpoint-aligned equality case. Retain its preimage; its length is w_i/A_i, and all earlier seam indices and signs still work. At the terminal step an interval longer than P_last contains an actual target image. This proof assumes full rectangular runs between crossings and no unrecorded intermediate doors through which the reflected wall bounces could escape. In a horizontal band transit monotonically from its lower to upper seam only the solid lateral walls are used. This is a sufficient test in an explicit regime. It allows unrestricted chamber sizes but cannot be inferred just by counting unknown integer reflection indices. [General placement lattice and kernel-index test](chain-criteria.md)

## R22 and the failure already below 22

Use the manuscript coordinates S=(0,0), T=(2,2). An upward path monotone in physical y first travels in the run −1<x<1 and at y=1 must enter the run 0<x<4 through the open door **0<x<1**. Both ends are physical reflex corners. The crossing is halfway in vertical travel. Regardless of side-wall reflections, the developed endpoint abscissas are even, since both marks have even abscissa and reflections in integral vertical lines preserve that parity. The halfway abscissa is an integer and remains integral under folding. This excludes **all upward-monotone words**, with both transverse signs and arbitrarily many side-wall reflections. Separately either leg can reach the same interior door point: the two straight chords from S and T to (1/2,1) lie inside their respective runs and have different forward slopes. Compatible crossing position alone is insufficient. [Cell coordinates and all-words proof](../../illumination-publication/repository/preprints/twenty-two-wall/manuscript.tex)

This identifies the failing door for this monotone chain only. Turned paths change the halfway time. R22's all-words exclusion uses the staircase fold, dyadic parity and seam reversal, with the decisive physical stop depending on the itinerary. Treating an unused branch mouth as a reflecting wall while composing two-chamber paths can also create an illegal bounce. The two-chamber theorem is about isolated unions, not permission to seal the other doors of a component.

**The halfway-door failure is possible in W≤20 BBBB.** In named DB55, take S=(1,1), T=(9,1) and travel monotonically to the right. The first mouth at x=5 is halfway; its open height interval is (2,3). Both developed endpoint ordinates are odd under every reflection in integral horizontal walls. Their average and its folded height are integers. Thus every rightward-monotone shot misses that door or hits a lip, with any number of side-wall reflections. The room has W=12, is normalized BBBB, and the reviewed reversal path lights this pair. By dilation even the numerical hypothesis that all chamber heights are at least two cannot cure this failure in the unrestricted integral model; dilation leaves the normalized class. The original normalized example already precludes using this local failure to distinguish R22 by wall count. [DB55 geometry, reversal and review](reversal.md)

**Verdict.** The two-rectangle theorem holds with a constructive dimension bound. The chamber-chain induction holds under the stated interval inequalities. A universal chain theorem for W≤20 has not been proved: after the monotone word fails, one must select a legal turn or revisit that repairs all doors simultaneously. The local obstruction is present at W=12; no inference from it excludes the global R22 mechanism in BBBB. No discovery run or census was performed.

**D157 review.** The lattice-mark theorem and bounds were upheld. The 539 named pairs used at most 16 reflections in the reported construction checks, against bounds from 185 to 365. Localization requires the stated profile throughout the slab traversed. A separate pair of two-rectangle certificates at successive doors does not give a common slope.
