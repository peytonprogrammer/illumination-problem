# Effective descent with all ordinates fixed

**Theorem for review.** Fix an orthogonal wall-and-mark order cell and rational ordinates for all vertices and both distinct interior marks. There is a terminating algorithm deciding whether some choice of positive integral horizontal gaps gives a dark pair. One can require horizontal wall gcd one and equal horizontal mark parity. It works at arbitrary width, with no assumption on the number of reflex corners. Consequently, for any *supplied* integer h there is a finite decision procedure for the primitive same-parity lattice problem at height ≤h. The theorem gives neither a uniform h nor the answer to any unexecuted decision. It does not assume Filip, finite flatness, or rigidity of the actual dark locus.

## A neighborhood that survives inserting full vertical periods

Clear the fixed vertical denominators once. In each open slab between consecutive distinct vertical lines through walls or marks, the room consists of disjoint rectangles with physical top and bottom walls. Let K be twice the lcm of all their integral heights (in the cleared vertical coordinates). Their signed transverse phases all have K as a period. The marks lie on slab ends. Fix a lit rational witness x₀ in horizontal gap coordinates, and a legal path. Axis-parallel lighting implies a direct chord in one coordinate interval and persists in the entire order cell. Otherwise reflect the developed vertical coordinate convention if needed, and let s₀>0 be the absolute developed vertical displacement per horizontal unit. Its value is rational by unfolding. Make a finite record of the N full slab traversals to the target, with repeats counted, stopping at the target's vertical line on the designated visit. A reflection on a vertical slab end reverses horizontal sense. Horizontal reflections inside a slab need no entries. Put D₀ equal to the sum of the traversed widths. In global developed coordinates the original vertical displacement is s₀D₀.

There is a computable δ>0 such that any sufficiently large positive widths x' satisfying

    s₀(x'ᵢ−x₀ᵢ) = kᵢK + eᵢ,     kᵢ ≥ 0 integers,   |eᵢ| < δ

give a lit room, even if x' is very far from x₀. To see this, put D' equal to the sum of the actual widths over the same list of slab visits and prescribe the slope

    s' = (s₀D₀ + K Σ_visits kᵢ) / D'.

The displacement error on a prefix, after subtracting its prescribed whole periods, is the prefix sum of eᵢ plus (s'−s₀) times its actual horizontal travel. The total error at s₀ has magnitude <Nδ. Since the prefix travel is at most D', the prefix error at s' has magnitude <2Nδ. The terminal error is exactly zero. This relative bound is why the window does not deteriorate when some widths grow faster than others.

At a slab end the signed height lies on a doubled interval of circumference twice the active component height. On a legal crossing or vertical reflection the next component and the update in signed-height charts are locally fixed, with changes of origin or orientation only. At a simultaneous horizontal bounce on an artificial cut, the horizontal wall continues on both sides (otherwise there is a physical corner); the two one-sided states are one neighborhood in the doubled interval. Thus a sufficiently small open arc around each recorded state has the same transmission or reflection and continues isometrically in developed vertical coordinates. At the target use a regular endpoint chart. Choose 2Nδ smaller than every such arc radius and δ<K/4. These radii can be chosen rationally by inspecting the finite interval endpoints, including the one-sided charts at a continued horizontal wall. Induction over the finite record proves legality: an inserted full vertical period inside a slab has no intermediate vertical cuts and only smooth horizontal reflections. The prefix error is evaluated on the chosen developed chart; adding kᵢK at one traversal leaves its signed exit exactly unchanged before the residual error. At the last cut the vanishing error gives the target itself. This strengthens exact fixed-direction compression to an **open** set of transverse phases for illumination with a corrected direction. [Fixed-direction predecessor](thin-gap-periods.md)

**Named exact check.** In the d129 height-five dumbbell take a=10, U=20. The reviewed upward shot has three traversal widths (10,2,10), slope 1/11 and two open doors at 2<y<3. Set K=10, k=(2,5,1) and e=(1/1000,−1/2000,1/3000). The perturbed widths are (230011/1000,1103989/2000,360011/3000). The corrected slope is 98400/1082411. Folding successively in the two height-five chambers and the height-one connector gives the two door heights 15748422/5412055 and 15745364/5412055, both strictly between two and three; the final ordinate is exactly 2. This checks the prescribed unequal insertions and slope correction on a named route.

## Effective hyperplanes from one lit witness

Let A⊆ℝᵃ be a supplied rational linear space of horizontal gaps. Choose x₀ in its positive cell as above and let β=s₀x₀/K. Choose ε=min(1/8,δ/K) and an integer M with exp(−4Mε²)<(2M+1)^(−a). All quantities are computable for this witness.

If x∈A is positive and dark, there must exist an integer row n such that

    0 < ||n||∞ ≤ M,     n·x=0,     n does not vanish identically on A.

Indeed vary the scale of x by an arbitrary positive real λ, keeping all ordinates fixed. This preserves darkness for orthogonal walls. If there were no such n, average the product of cos^(2M)(π(λs₀xᵢ/K−βᵢ)) over λ. The constant Fourier coefficient of the unshifted product is at least (2M+1)^(−a), and every unshifted Fourier coefficient is nonnegative. A surviving character satisfies n·x=0 and has norm at most M. By hypothesis it annihilates A, hence n·β=0 and its shift has phase one. The mean is therefore at least the constant coefficient. Outside the open ε-box about β on the torus the product is at most exp(−4Mε²). There are arbitrarily large λ inside the box. Since each xᵢ>0, take λ large enough that all corresponding period indices are nonnegative. The preceding lemma lights λx. Scaling back lights x. This proves the row assertion, including when A already satisfies many short relations. [Fourier estimate](flat-resonance.md)

## Termination and scope

Start with A the full gap space. At a node construct one eligible positive integral witness x₀ by the one-axis test in primitive-dark-flats.md (for unrestricted gaps, ordinary rational feasibility and clearing denominators suffice). Discard an empty node. Test the witness by the finite labelled-origami orbit decision there, retaining a legal path if lit: record the generator word to the horizontal connection and pull the finite path back. Take all source/target lift choices and forbid convex as well as reflex corner lifts. Fixed rational ordinates were rescaled above, so this is an integral unfolded witness. A dark witness answers the existence question. For a lit witness with a nonaxial path, recurse on A∩ker n for the finite row list just proved, discarding rows vanishing on A. Each child has strictly smaller dimension. At rank one no proper child contains a positive point. Branching is finite and depth at most a, so the algorithm halts. The axial witness case terminates the node immediately. Positivity and mark-order ties are handled by the order cell and the original gap encoding. The optional parity/gcd promises are imposed when choosing witnesses, not during the real slope or scaling argument. At bounded integral height there are finitely many ordinate tuples after anchoring the bottom at zero and finitely many order types for fixed W. Apply the procedure to each admissible tuple. Nothing here estimates a practical running time; no decision tree or discovery search was run.

## Relation to rigidity and coefficient height

A rank defect among available route equations does not establish a deformation of a true dark locus. Even on an actual rational factor of dimension two a coordinate gap need not vary independently: in the positive gap plane (u,v,u+v) every coordinate vector is excluded. Extreme rays can lengthen several gaps together. This illustrates the geometric inference failure only. For a fixed opposite axis one can bypass it: the descent tests all coupled positive directions too. The resulting coefficient cap is recursive and effective from lit samples at that fixed ordinate tuple. If every eligible point is lit, the finite tree is a certificate; if a sampled point is dark, the existence algorithm stops there and asserts nothing about its factor dimension.

The unresolved uniform step is varying both normalized axes. The period K and the choice of the lit sample depend on the ordinate tuple. The argument would close the all-height problem given an effective height h meeting at least one counterexample whenever there is any. The reviewed accumulation theorem's existential fixed height for an unbounded family does not supply that input. Nor does this prove one-dimensional factors on every real dark flat. Both claims are stronger than the fixed-ordinate decision. The independent-axis equivalence used throughout this proof restricts it to orthogonal walls.
