Advances: L1 — the mod-4 test for a realized off-class midpoint does not produce a separating double cover. The first failure is within one state: darkness puts the marked lifts in different components, where their half-distance is undefined. It imposes no parity condition on cycles elsewhere. There is also a local obstruction to extending the half-distance rule for all direction classes to a topological cocycle.

### (a) The single-state parity

Fix the source pole a, a distinct pole x, and one primitive arc α from a to x. In the regular FC graph take the vertices over a. For each regular continuation a–x–a put an edge between its two a-incidences, identifying the two initial sheet choices belonging to the same regular a-point. Keep loops and parallel edges. Equivalently this is the graph on regular a-points with one edge per full two-traversal passage; a dead end with no passage contributes no constraint. Pull a vertex label back to either incident sheet if sheet notation is used.

On this graph the edge cochain h assigns 1 in F₂ to each edge. On a graph every such cochain is a cocycle. The additional issue is whether it is a **coboundary**, so its integral depends only on the endpoints. A path component always has this property. On a cycle of original FC length C, its period is C/2 modulo 2. Thus a vertex coloring c with h=δc exists exactly when every cycle in question has C divisible by four. Choose an origin in each component and alternate the labels; the choice of origin bit is independent in each component. For two lifts on the same component, c(t)−c(s)=D/2 mod 2. On different components no difference is intrinsic. A cycle of length 2 mod 4 obstructs the coloring but already supplies both midpoint residues for marks **on that cycle**. ### (b) Darkness does not impose compatibility

If two compatible marked lifts share any regular path or cycle component, the nonbacktracking subarc between them lights the pair. This includes distance 0 mod 4 between distinct lifts. Thus darkness is equivalent to separation of every compatible lift pair in **every geometric state and class**: a legal connection unfolds along a primitive direction, which the level-two shears reach from the base arc of its class. The composite-word calibration for the implementation is scoped separately. [Geometric proof and calibration](l1-large-monodromy.md) Failure only of an off-class midpoint is weaker: it allows disconnected lifts, or connected lifts at distance 0 mod 4 on a path or on a cycle of length divisible by four. Neither failure constrains a component disjoint from the pair. [FC, §§1–3](../../illumination-generalization-2026-11-06/fable/directions-2026-11-09/d61-fold-converse/README.md)

Its lowest open band is −15<y<−10 with horizontal section 5<x<10, since the original bottom row is the single square at (1,−3). On y=−12 the horizontal billiard in the double runs five units across the front and five back across the rear. **A local obstruction even to a nonexact global class.** Inside that same bottom square on copy 0 take A=(6,−14), B=(8,−14), C=(8,−12). The three sides AB, BC, CA each consist of two primitive traversals, so each is assigned 1 by its own state. The triangle bounds an ordinary disk containing no physical corner. Its boundary sum is 1 in F₂. A cocycle on the corner-complement of Q must evaluate to zero on that boundary. Consequently even the restrictions of the edge rules to these three *paths* cannot come from one class on that space, irrespective of component constants. Endpoints and midpoints here are regular sites; deleting all regular grid sites would also delete the proposed FC passages and change the extension problem. The same disk argument applies on any unbranched lifted disk carrying all three prescribed edges. In the reviewer’s copy-1 incidence convention neither diagonal passage from A reaches C; all three prescribed edges need only occur together on copy 0. The argument does not establish that such a disk occurs on the specified marked U.

The triangle uses three direction classes. A pure braid fixes the pole labels, so it cannot exchange those three classes. Within a fixed class one must instead compare two paths after transporting their sheet incidences under a change of arc. Conjugation can change which restricted 2-cycles meet and can cut or join the regular components. Even when both states admit colors, an identification requires compatible relative component constants. More precisely, after proposed vertex identifications, glue the two-step graphs. A shared vertex coloring exists iff every closed chain in the glued graph has even edge count, by summing the equations δc=1 around each chain and, conversely, integrating from a root. A chain may switch states and may avoid both marked lifts altogether. Darkness supplies no evenness test for it. This locates where a transported coloring could flip; it does not claim an actual within-class pure-braid flip in a normalized eight-reflex BBBB example. The FC criterion tests a path in one state only. [Block closure and state changes](l1-dark-implies-fold.md)

**If separation holds only for some classes.** For the fixed physical pair let P be the set of off-classes x for which a factor separates all compatible lift pairs in class x by the parity certificate. If no single factor handles all three classes, the room belongs to (iii), even when P is nonempty. A certified x can be discarded when seeking a witness. A coloring invariant for x alone does not even justify discarding x until it proves that every compatible lift pair fails in every state of x. Neither fact restricts the other classes. ### (c) What a genuine invariant would cover

Let B° be the pillowcase minus its four poles, Q° its preimage, and fix basepoints. Write G=π₁(B°) and H=π₁(Q°)⊂G. A nonzero character H→F₂ defines a connected double cover of Q° with subgroup ker χ **inside H**. An intermediate factor Q°→N°→B° requires a subgroup K **containing H**, H⊊K⊊G. Invariance of χ under available state changes does not reverse either inclusion. A character on U likewise constructs a cover above U. The completed room double is a sphere, so its own unpunctured first cohomology is zero. For a punctured sphere a character can instead yield a double cover branched at the punctures with odd peripheral value. Two such values already suffice topologically, with no requirement that either puncture be a reflex. None of this gives the downstream quotient zeros needed for r≥kz.

A different construction would be an equivariant two-color partition of the **generic sheets over B°**: each full monodromy permutation must preserve the partition, possibly exchanging its two classes. A nonconstant such coloring gives an intermediate quotient of outer degree two. One must still prove separation of all marked lift choices and the corner-only condition in that quotient. In fact an outer degree-two quotient has no local degree three over B and hence z=0. The nine-reflex count cannot follow from that quotient alone. Also, half-distance zero on a connected component would give *equal* colors for the endpoints, and disconnected components have free color offsets. Neither provides forced separation.
