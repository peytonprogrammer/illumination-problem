# Repair in a specified primitive class

**Verdict.** No general proof of arbitrary-arc deletion follows here. There is an exact criterion for producing the prescribed class by changing the windings of an existing shot while keeping its cylinder crossings fixed. The reviewed DB55 pair exhibits a sharp limitation: in horizontal cylinders, this operation cannot produce the horizontal class from *any* oblique seed in another class, although the legal horizontal-class shot (3,2) exists.

## A class-preserving question about the physical lift sets

Write R_c for the existence of a corner-continued connection on some arc of primitive parity c, and L_c for a legal connection on some arc of that same parity. The d148 conjecture is R_c ⇒ L_c for every c and every pair in the stated BBBB range. Always L_c ⇒ R_c. Thus three-class illumination is equivalent to the **conjunction** of all-class relaxed existence and arbitrary-arc deletion. D148 §4 lists even the weaker, some-class relaxed existence as open in general. In its four controls all 816 rows already have legal witnesses, so both hypotheses hold there. ## Windings with the crossing points fixed

Use the translation unfolding cut at physical corners. Choose an integral unimodular frame in which a primitive vector of the desired parity c is horizontal. Cut along all horizontal critical leaves through corner lifts; the open pieces are cylinders. Take a legal seed between the marked lifts of primitive parity other than c. Its developed displacement (u,h) has h≠0. Reverse the signs of both coordinates if needed so that h>0. In order along the path, let H_i>0 be its transverse travel on each cylinder leg and C_i its circumference, counting repeated visits separately. The endpoint lifts and the critical leaves have lattice transverse coordinates, so H_i,C_i are integers in this frame. A crossing of a critical leaf occurs at a regular point because the seed is legal.

Keep the same ordered cylinder legs, their boundary crossing points and endpoint lifts. Change the longitudinal-over-transverse slope by a rational number t. This gives a legal segment for exactly the values satisfying tH_i∈C_iℤ for every i, within this fixed data and with transverse orientation fixed. Indeed, on each cylinder leg the new chord has the same endpoints modulo circumference precisely under that condition. Its open interior is in the open strip. At a seam the same two germs glue at the same regular point and all legs have the same new slope. During a boundary-to-boundary leg the transverse coordinate is strictly monotone, so no unrecorded boundary contact can intervene.

These t form Pℤ with P the positive rational lcm of C_i/H_i (the positive generator of the intersection of their integer-multiple groups). The new displacements are (u+jPh,h). Here Ph is an integer since h=ΣH_i. Let b=v₂(h). The seed being in a class other than c gives v₂(u)≥b, allowing v₂(0)=∞. The new class is c precisely when v₂(u+jPh)<b. Hence **a shift into c exists if and only if every leg satisfies v₂(H_i)>v₂(C_i)**. Indeed v₂(P)=max_i(v₂(C_i)−v₂(H_i)); if it is negative, every odd j succeeds, and otherwise no j succeeds. The shear slope can be fractional. This is an iff for a fixed set of crossings on an existing legal seed, not a criterion that assumes a relaxed path can be used as a seed. ## A named obstruction quantified over seeds

In DB55 consider S=(2,2), T=(10,2) and use the physical horizontal axis as core. The strip 2<y<3 extends from x=0 to x=13. Its lifts are horizontal cylinders of transverse height one and circumference 26: a horizontal round trip reflects at both exterior vertical walls. Both boundary levels carry corner stops, whereas no leaf between them does. Neither marked point lies in the open strip. Every nonhorizontal legal connection must pass through its interior to travel between chambers. Any visit to one lifted strip is a full transverse leg of height one: neither endpoint is in its interior, and crossing a regular rim point leads out of that open cylinder in the cut decomposition. This includes visits adjacent to a horizontal corridor wall in the physical room, which unfolds to a rim crossing. Therefore at least one leg has v₂(H_i)=0 and v₂(C_i)=1, irrespective of the other legs. Exact winding with the same horizontal-cylinder crossings cannot repair the class of any legal seed in a different parity.

D148's legal (3,2) connection shows this obstruction concerns the method. It does not rule out shearing in a different integral frame with the same core parity, moving a seam crossing, or changing the route. It also shows why information about the presence of regular points somewhere in the fiber cannot alone validate this crossing-preserving construction. ## Letting the crossings move: an exact test for a prescribed route

There is also a finite arithmetic criterion *without* an initial legal seed. Prescribe a transversely increasing chain of cylinder legs, including a specific open seam interval between successive legs, and fix both endpoint lifts. Assume each transition interval avoids stops and is represented in both neighboring cylinder coordinates by intervals on which the gluing is a translation. Split a wrapping interval at its coordinate cut, using overlapping charts if the cut point itself is regular. Use integer longitudinal origins and lattice translations. The transverse travels H_i are now fixed by the rims and endpoint heights. Write g=gcd_i C_i, and define X to be the target coordinate minus the source coordinate minus the sum of the seam translation offsets (the offset is the outgoing chart coordinate minus the incoming chart coordinate). All these are developed integral coordinates.

For a legal route with slope s, summing the longitudinal displacements along the legs cancels every variable crossing coordinate and gives sh=X+Σ k_i C_i for integers k_i. Thus sh=X+mg. Conversely, for any such candidate slope there is just one forward propagation: in each cylinder add sH_i modulo C_i, require the result to belong to the prescribed open interval, apply the specified chart translation, and repeat. On the last leg require the exact target coordinate modulo its circumference. These tests are necessary and sufficient: success assembles a straight segment with regular joins, by the open-strip argument above. The gcd condition by itself is only necessary.

This yields a finite test including the desired primitive parity. Increasing s by P preserves all local coordinates, so the seam and terminal tests are periodic in m with period Ph/g (an integer). In the core class the extra condition is X+mg nonzero modulo 2^b, since the transverse displacement has valuation b. Hence one may take the joint period lcm(Ph/g, 2^b/gcd(g,2^b)). When b=0 no core-class chord exists on this chain. This test allows crossings to slide, including past a former corner contact into the prescribed open side. A relaxed path on the boundary of the allowed intervals does not imply that any residue hits their interiors. There is also no established bound on the necessary chains. No route or residue sweep was run.

## Consequence for total failure

If deletion holds for all classes, then a totally dark pair has no relaxed connection in any class, by contraposition. Getting a corner-only fiber would still require a further theorem: when every site has a regular point and all four canonical relations are universal, some *single* arc has a corner-continued connection between the specified endpoint sets. D68 Lemma B tests the contents of a fiber. It does not construct this arc; d145 counts the cost once a clean fiber exists. Moreover, proving illumination specifically in BBBB only implies that a dark pair lies outside BBBB. A nonuniversal canonical relation can also yield a proper intermediate factor. D64/d67 closes that branch for these lattice pairs, separately. Neither the arbitrary-arc deletion implication nor relaxed existence has been proved uniformly here. [Fiber and factor scope](exclusion-descent.md)

The next geometric question is whether some chain for a relaxed-connected class must have a residue in all of its open seam intervals with the required parity. Any such proof must also produce a legal seed or work directly from the relaxed connection; fixed-crossing winds on legal shots cannot start from total darkness.


## D150 replay and the equality case

I reran the four prescribed height-12 DB55 chain sweeps, 780 distinct residues each. Algebra and same-chain physical tracing agreed in all 3,120 cases. The seeds with longitudinal displacements 28, −32, 44, −52 respectively have 1, 6, 5, 5 passing residues and 0, 2, 4, 2 horizontal-class residues. In particular (−32,12) and (−42,12) use the same eight cylinder legs and the same open seam arcs. Sliding changes the class exactly as the reviewer reports. The original corner-continued horizontal reference has h=0, lies on a rim and has no such transverse chain; this replay starts from separately known *legal* seeds. [d150 sweep and full logs](../../illumination-generalization-2026-11-06/fable/directions-2026-11-09/d150-review-classrepair/review.md)

The core-class rigidity statement has a small but important qualification. If b=v₂(h)≤v₂(g), U modulo 2ᵇ is fixed: a noncore seed cannot become core. If b<v₂(g), the full primitive parity is fixed. At equality the two noncore parities can interchange because U modulo 2ᵇ⁺¹ may vary. For an actual DB55 example take the vertical cylinder across the left chamber and marks (1,1),(3,1). Its circumference is ten, the transverse travel is two, and the longitudinal displacements 0 and 10 both work: the classes are horizontal and diagonal in physical coordinates. This does not alter the reviewer's core-class rejection count of 83/119.
