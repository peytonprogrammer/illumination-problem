Advances: L1 — One lattice seed explicitly bounds a homogeneous equation of every dark axis factor on its transverse slice. This bounds the entire defining equation of a visible hypersurface. A product proper on both axes can escape both slices of the seed.

# Which flat equations a seed controls

The row-4 assembly and its use of maximal dark product flats are conditional on d132 and d134, as reviewed in d186. The following estimate itself needs only scaling along a dark ray, the d184 parallel-cut transfer, and a specified lit seed. Work in the full marked orthogonal order chart before imposing wall gcd one. Scaling one axis preserves darkness, although the scaled room may have a wall gcd greater than one.

**One bounded normal.** Fix integral transverse gaps y₀. Let b be the positive integral horizontal gap vector of a lit seed with nonaxial developed travels X,Y>0 and reduced slope p/q. Cut every horizontal-length gap the seed crosses at the d184 margin. Let k count distinct crossed gaps, M count crossings with multiplicity, and P be a positive common multiple of the crossed doubled strip heights. For k≥1 set

    ρ = 1/(32qMP),       K = 1 + ceil(8k / (π² ρ^(k+1))).

Let E be any rational linear subspace containing b. Suppose U⊆E is rational, meets the positive orthant, and every positive integral x in U gives a dark pair at y₀. Then there exists an integer h supported on the crossed gaps with

    ||h||∞ ≤ K,       h|U = 0,       h·b ≠ 0 (hence h|E ≠ 0).

For a real dark factor use the rational span of its integral points, provided it contains a positive integral point. For k=0 a positive integral ray eventually dominates b without changing the seed, so there is no such U. An axial seed persists under all parallel insertions by keeping its constant transverse coordinate and changing only travel, or translating the segment when the cuts are parallel to it.

**Proof.** Use the d184 cut placement giving retained gate margin 1/(2q) in vertical units. Put ε=1/(16qM) and choose a positive open interval J about s₀=Y/X with X|u−s₀|<1/(16q) and u>s₀/2. For a positive integral z∈U enlarge b to λz, taking integer λ sufficiently large. Write t=λz−b. A trial u∈J with every crossed phase tⱼu within ρP of Pℤ suffices. Indeed take Kⱼ the nearest multiple and correct the slope to s=(Y+ΣKⱼ)/(X+Σtⱼ), summing over crossings. The denominator times |s−u| is at most E₀=X|u−s₀|+Mε. At any retained abscissa the displacement is at most 2E₀: the correction acts over at most the full denominator, and the uncorrected prefix has error at most E₀. Thus 2E₀≤1/(4q), strictly inside the safe margin. For large λ the corrected slope is positive. Both endpoints and the prescribed cut sheets match. [Parallel insertion proof and gate margins](l1-gap-hyperplanes.md)

Average the d184 product of triangular bumps supported at distance ρ on the phase torus over J. Its constant coefficient is 1, all coefficients are nonnegative, and its Fourier tail beyond K has total absolute mass less than 1/4 by the bound 2k/(π²Kρ^(k+1)). If an integer character h annihilates z and b, then h·t=0 exactly. Its contribution is nonnegative. Every retained character with h·z≠0 contributes a quantity tending to zero: its phase frequency is (λh·z−h·b)/P. There are finitely many retained terms. Hence if all retained characters that annihilate z also annihilate b, the average is positive for large λ. This produces a legal shot, contradicting darkness at λz. A character nonzero at b must therefore annihilate z. Finally, positive rational points in U are dense in its positive part, and clearing denominators gives the same finite hyperplane cover of that part. Finitely many proper subspaces cannot cover an open subset of U. One h consequently annihilates all of U. This removes every affine offset in d184 and requires no estimate for the onset in λ.

**When this bounds all equations.** In relative codimension one the primitive equation on the ambient full gap space has coefficients ≤K (when E is proper, the bound is on an ambient representative of the relative equation). More generally, take E₀ to be the full gap space. After obtaining a normal, intersect E with its kernel. At each successive E strictly containing U one needs a lit seed on E at the *same* transverse y₀. If supplied, each new equation is independent of the preceding ones. After codim(U) steps the rows define U, with height bounded by the largest K of these seeds. The new seed cannot be replaced by the old one unless its gap vector lies in the new E. The direction and the constants can change. If U is maximal among rational dark subspaces on this slice, the finite-flat description ensures lit rational seeds in every strictly larger rational E meeting this positive part (otherwise E itself is dark); axis scaling clears the horizontal denominators. This gives existence, not a bound on the new seed data. For a maximal product U×V a generic probe in V avoids competing products with strictly larger horizontal factor: each such product meets V in a proper vertical subspace by maximality. A nongeneric slice can mask U. A generic *integral* probe requires the same avoidance in the rational part being tested.

**First case outside a single seed.** For a lit seed (b,y₀), a dark product U×V with b outside U and y₀ outside V need not appear on either parallel slice through the seed. Both normals can then be unknown even if each factor is just a hypersurface. For example, at seed ((1,1),(1,1)) the two full coordinate slices are disjoint from the positive product of lines x₂=m x₁ and y₂=m y₁ for every integer m>1. Its primitive equations have height m. This is a model of the information provided by slice exclusions, not a family of dark rooms. It shows why even exact information on the two slices, let alone their numerical seed constants, does not by itself bound the equations. Finite sets of transverse probes can likewise be missed by choosing a slope outside their finite list. Room dynamics could rule out these missed products.

For a *rational full-chart* dark hypersurface one axis factor is full, so the seed does see it by parallel insertion on the proper axis. For a general occupied crossing, the missing input is a bound on the height of transverse probes guaranteed to meet every feasible doubly proper product, or an estimate controlling both normals directly from a mixed transfer. A seed catalogue on each supplied slice does not furnish that input. Some needed slices can have minimum area above 20. No numerical cutoff or census request follows.
