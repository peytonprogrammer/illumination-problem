# Adjacent rectangular arms illuminate each other at every depth

**Theorem (local).** Put the common corner at the origin. The right arm has cross-section −H<y<0 and contains S=(X,−U), with X>0 and 0<U<H. The upper arm has cross-section −W<x<0 and contains T=(−V,Y), with Y>0 and 0<V<W. The central rectangle (−W,0) × (−H,0) is clear, with open doors to the arms along its right and top sides. Assume the arm interiors are clear to the marks and their parallel sides are solid smooth walls strictly between each mouth and the depth of its mark. There is a legal shot between S and T. It is direct or it reflects only on the parallel sides of **one** arm. The construction never reaches either end cap. Extra geometry beyond the prescribed pieces is irrelevant.

**Proof.** The direct line crosses x=0 at height (XY−UV)/(X+V). If XY<UV it crosses the right door strictly between −U and 0, then the top door strictly between −V and 0. This covers the direct case without a reflection.

For the remaining case, unfold only the right arm. Possible source images (X,−Z) have Z in the entire set {2jH±U : j integral}. The straight chord from that image to T crosses the open right door precisely when

    XY/V < Z < XY/V + H(1+X/V).

Indeed the crossing height is (XY−ZV)/(X+V), which must lie in (−H,0). Once through it, the chord automatically crosses the top door between −V and 0 and reaches T without another reflection. Folding the right leg fixes the mouth chart pointwise with unchanged velocity there. Contacts in the arm have 0<x<X and are therefore smooth. Both transverse signs of the physical launch are allowed.

Successive gaps between source images alternate between 2U and 2H−2U, both strictly less than 2H. If X≥V the displayed interval has length at least 2H, so it contains a source image strictly inside, including when X=V. If X<V and XY≥UV then Y>U. Reverse the endpoints and use the upper arm instead (interchange x and y and the two arms by an isometry). The corresponding interval has length W(1+Y/U)>2W, and its images have gaps strictly smaller than 2W. Reversibility supplies the desired forward path. This includes XY=UV, where the direct chord hits the shared corner. For a constructive image, take the smaller of the first terms of the two progressions strictly above the lower endpoint.

The multiplication of lengths enters through the sign of XY−UV. The other two alternatives are X≥V and Y≥U. Their union with XY<UV covers the full parameter domain because X<V and Y<U imply XY<UV. Thus eliminating the reflection count and allowing the choice of arm gives an identically true relation, even though individual image tests contain products. A single prescribed launch sign would have gap exactly 2H and would require extra care at equality. The earlier capped construction used two consecutive images to escape a cap corner; selecting an arm here eliminates the cap contact itself. [Earlier placement interval](cross-placement.md)

**Connection to the six-wall L.** In the parameters of [the one-reflection note](skeleton-decidability.md), divide at the reflex into the upper arm of width x+2, the right arm of height 2, and the clear central rectangle. The local variables are X=z, U=1, V=x, Y=y. In particular y≥1 for the stated lattice slice, so the upper-arm interval always has width at least twice its arm width. This explains how unrestricted side reflections supply all pairs beyond the curved boundary zy=3x. Explicitly, put A=x+2. Choose the least element Z of the two progressions 2jA±x strictly greater than yz. Their largest gap is max(2x,4)<2A, so

    yz < Z < yz + 2A ≤ yz + A(1+y).

In original coordinates join the upper-arm image (−Z,y) to (z,−1). At y=0 it crosses at abscissa (yz−Z)/(1+y), strictly inside (−A,0). Fold the portion above y=0 into the upper arm by its vertical sides. Below y=0 the segment runs inside the bottom rectangle to the target. This constructs a word for every input on the slice by two integer roundings and a minimum. For d160’s boundary control x=y=z=3 the formula gives Z=13. The physical path is (−3,3) → (0,9/4) → (−5,1) → (3,−1), crossing the mouth at (−1,0). Since zy=3x, d160 excludes zero or one reflection, so this two-reflection shot is shortest by reflection count. For arbitrary real placements the preceding reciprocal-arm argument covers y<1 as well (using the correct offsets of both marks).

## Whole-room consequence

Start with any rectangular hall. Independently on each side attach either nothing or one outward rectangular arm of arbitrary positive depth spanning that entire side. Take the interior of the resulting union. **Every pair of distinct real interior points is lit for all choices of dimensions.** Two points in the hall and a single arm, or in opposite arms and the hall, share a straight rectangular bar. A mark on an open mouth belongs to the hall closure; the chord to a point in any one arm lies in the hall plus that arm (interior except possibly at its interior endpoints on the seam). More explicitly a mouth point on a *different* side and the arm point lie in the rectangle obtained by extending the hall across the full arm mouth, with the chord immediately entering its interior. Two mouth points on the same side lie on the open physical seam. The hall corners are on the physical boundary. All remaining placements are in two adjacent open arms, to which the theorem applies after a rotation or reflection. The only resulting types are rectangles, L rooms, T rooms and plus signs (4, 6, 8 and 12 walls). No new BBBB room was established by the d161 checks. For a pair in a larger room, the central rectangle and both open doors must be clear, and the rectangular arm interiors and their smooth solid parallel sides must persist from each mouth up to its mark depth. A new opening in a required reflecting side invalidates that local use.

**Where this stops.** Every horizontal or vertical line meets the interior of a hall of this form in at most one interval (allowing an empty intersection). In R22 the line y=1/2 meets the column component containing x=0 and the separate interval 3<x<5, with a gap between them. Hence R22 fails the structural condition. Connected sections are only necessary; the S-tetromino and three-step staircase both pass that test and lack the required hall. More specifically, the axis-aligned bounding box of its marked pair is [0,2]², and (1/2,3/2) is interior to the room whereas (3/2,1/2) is outside. No local adjacent-arm chart satisfying the theorem can contain this pair in the indicated roles in any orientation, since the theorem would then light the reviewed dark pair. The disconnected slice proves whole-room failure without using darkness. [R22 room cells and proof](../../illumination-publication/repository/preprints/twenty-two-wall/manuscript.tex)

Failure is possible already in a normalized BBBB room with 12 walls. In DB55 the two 5×5 rooms join along a unit-height corridor at heights 2 to 3. At y=1 the horizontal section has two components. DB55 is lit and has connected vertical sections; its disconnected horizontal section excludes the hall geometry in either orientation. Thus the theorem supplies no general BBBB implication. A separated pair in that geometry can face two parallel mouths with a chamber between them; reversing endpoints no longer exchanges the two ratios from the proof. [DB55 geometry](doorway-word-bound.md)

**Named exact control.** For the odd cross with b=7, the marks (6,0) and (0,6) give X=Y=4, U=V=2, W=H=4 in coordinates based at (2,2). The right-arm interval is (8,20); select Z=10. The two reflection points in physical coordinates are (36/7,2) and (24/7,−2), and the two mouth crossings are (2,4/3) and (12/7,2). The shot reaches (0,6). Every larger cross containing the same portions of the arms admits this shot, and the theorem applies separately to all other pairs. No room, itinerary or direction search is used.
