# Periodic markings on the minimal rank-two sphere locus

**Verdict for review.** The published covering theorem does not classify the required one-point markings. There is a uniform algebraic test for them in rank two, starting at three simple zeros. It asks about torsion on a particular family of curves in abelian surfaces. I do not prove the absence of those torsion sections. In the physical degree-two branch, a dark pair would require a *nonreal* regular periodic image downstairs. Once such an image is specified, its upstairs physical mark is off the deck symmetry's fixed set. A periodic point is therefore insufficient to invoke the axis lemma.

## Setting and theorem audit

Use the hypotheses of the [minimal-quotient reduction](minimal-quotient-rigidity.md), currently under d119 review. Denote the minimal quadratic sphere by Y, its orientation cover by X, the covering involution by J, and the unmarked orbit closure of X by M. Here rank(M)=2, the field of definition is ℚ, and there is no relative tangent kernel. Y has z simple zeros and z+4 simple poles, with 3≤z≤8 in the residual case. The genus of X is z+1. All the indicated singularities lift to J-fixed Weierstrass points. Minimality rules out a further nontrivial **flat** translation quotient of X. A general holomorphic map of curves need not be such a quotient.

## A rank-two test on a family of curves

Pass to a smooth local branch and level/label covers as needed. Choose a labeled zero b of ω as origin. Write V=p(TM) for the rank-four rational absolute subsystem. With its integral lattice (up to isogeny) and its polarized weight-one Hodge structure, it gives an abelian-surface quotient A of Jac(X). Pullback from A contains ω. Define

    j_b : X → Jac(X) → A,     x ↦ [x−b] ↦ its projection.

**Criterion at the generic surface of M.** A periodic regular one-point marking gives a branch of j_b(x)∈A[N] dominating M for some fixed N. Conversely, a component of this torsion incidence dominating M with a generically regular marked point gives a periodic marking. The component and the common N concern the family. A torsion intersection at one special surface need not extend.

For the forward direction apply Filip's twisted cycle theorem to the periodic marked locus. Its dimension equals dim M and it still has zero relative kernel. In the notation of §5.1, W₀M=0, so its annihilator contains the relative divisor x−b (up to clearing the integral lattice index). The single embedding of ℚ removes the twist. Its Abel–Jacobi value in A is a torsion section. The connected section after local labeling has one bounded order, as in Filip's Remark 5.3. This proves the necessary incidence.

For the converse work where the incidence is a smooth finite multisection. Its fibers are finite because j_b is nonconstant and A[N] is finite. On a simply connected chart, N times the relative integral from b to x, evaluated on the holomorphic forms from A, is an absolute A-period. Lift the locally constant lattice element to rational absolute homology on X and clear the fixed denominator. In particular the relative period of ω is a fixed rational linear combination of its absolute periods. At a regular x this single *complex* equation uniquely continues x over a nearby base surface: the derivative in the point direction is ω(x)≠0. The incidence branch is that graph since it dominates the base. Linear changes of flat coordinates preserve the graph locally. On a generic orbit, invariance extends along the connected group: membership in the algebraic closure of the branch is a real-analytic condition on the group which holds near the identity. Thus the marked orbit closure lies in a variety of base dimension and projects densely to the base. This proves equality of dimensions, which is periodicity. All these statements are on the appropriate finite covers or their images. No uniform bound on N has been obtained.

**Why the test has the same geometry for every z.** The map j_b is birational onto its image B. Otherwise factoring through the normalization of B would pull back the invariant one-form on A to a holomorphic form on a lower-genus curve and then to ω on X. That is precisely a further flat translation cover (or a torus cover), impossible by minimality. Since b is Weierstrass, j_b(Jx)=−j_b(x). Hence B/±1 is a rational curve in the Kummer quotient A/±1. The sought mark is a point on the normalization of B that persists over a torsion section and is not J-fixed. It can even map to A[2] if two distinct normalization branches meet there; birationality does not rule this out.

There is a dimension check on how general A must be. Projectivized M has complex dimension three. At fixed polarized A and origin, the rational image curve in the Kummer quotient cannot move in a positive-dimensional family: its strict transforms would sweep the Kummer K3 resolution by rational curves. In characteristic zero this contradicts its nonzero holomorphic two-form (a generically finite ruling parametrization would pull that form back to zero). Consequently B is fixed on a connected fiber, being the full inverse image of that rational curve as B is symmetric. Its normalization recovers X. On fixed X the possible projective forms are finite: choose the designated z among the finitely many Weierstrass points as order-two zeros; that divisor determines the form up to scale. This gives generic fiber dimension zero for the abelian-factor map from projectivized M. Thus the factor varies in dimension three, the dimension of the moduli space of polarized abelian surfaces of the given type. This uses the variation for the factor with its polarization fixed in type; finite choices of level and labels do not affect dimension. [Rational curves and the Kummer construction](https://math.nyu.edu/~tschinke/princeton/papers/yuri/k3/k39.pdf)

For z=3 the required curve has normalization of genus four, ten Weierstrass points, and a pulled-back invariant differential vanishing twice at three of them. For any z, replace these counts by z+1, 2z+4 and z. Saying that a generic hyperelliptic curve in its **own Jacobian** has only Weierstrass torsion points would not resolve this incidence: the curve here is selected by the flat locus and maps to a two-dimensional quotient. The unresolved classification is whether any of these families admits the dominating torsion branch just described with the required physical marking. There is no assertion here that such a proper minimal locus exists for every z.

## Degree two: which points are fixed by the physical symmetry?

Let f:Q→Y be the minimal quadratic quotient of degree two, with deck δ and physical mirror ι on Q. Minimality makes the covering equivariant under conjugation: use the conjugate covering to transport the downstairs real structure. Equivalently δ commutes with ι by uniqueness of the degree-two minimal map and of its nonidentity deck transformation. Write τ for the resulting mirror on Y, so fι=τf. The topological double Q has two halves. A homeomorphism commuting with ι permutes their interiors and preserves their common seam. As in d99, δ is projectively a translation in quadratic charts; it induces a Euclidean sign symmetry of the physical room. This argument uses canonical minimality for equivariance at the irrational member, in place of a factor of a fixed pillowcase. [D99 physical axis argument](bbbb-split.md)

For a lift s of an interior mark, ιs≠s. A regular image u=f(s) is an ordinary unbranched fiber. Then

    τu=u  ⇔  f(s)=f(ιs)  ⇔  δs=ιs.

Thus a real regular image puts the mark on the axis of an exchanging reflection. A nonreal regular image excludes both that equality and a deck-fixed lift. It puts the mark off the fixed set of the induced symmetry. The distinction applies independently of periodicity.

- **δ exchanges halves.** Its fixed points lie on the seam; no interior lift can ramify, so both physical marks project to regular points. If both images were real, both marks would lie on the same axis and would see each other by the d99 lemma. A dark pair requires at least one nonreal regular image.
- **δ preserves halves.** The physical symmetry is a half-turn (a nonidentity translation cannot preserve a bounded room). No interior mark collapses with its mirror in a fiber. There are exactly two simple ramification points on Q, so at most one physical interior mark can contribute a ramified mirror pair. The other has a regular image, and necessarily a distinct conjugate image. Here the center is the only possible fixed interior physical position.

One can also place the downstairs singularities. Every downstairs zero has real preimages (all upstairs zeros are reflex vertices), hence is real. A nonreal pole can have no unramified preimage, since those are upstairs poles on the real seam. In degree two it consumes one ramification point. In the exchanging case both ramification points are on the seam, so **all poles are real**. In the preserving case the central half-turn has one fixed point in each half (equivalently an orientation-preserving order-two disk automorphism has one interior fixed point). Their two branch values are distinct, exchanged by τ, and are the **only nonreal pole pair**. These statements specify the physical real form to test at z=3 or 4. They do not place a generic periodic point on its real seam.

In either case, a surviving dark pair needs a nonreal regular periodic image u on Y and its distinct conjugate τu. On its orientation cover these lift to four distinct regular points exchanged in pairs by J. Finiteness of periodic markings allows such an orbit. D99 needs both physical marks on the same reflection axis; having a deck symmetry, or even one fixed mark, does not supply its hypothesis. The rank-three possibility in the z=4 degree-two branch is outside the rank-two abelian-surface criterion, but the real-placement argument applies there too.

**Status.** The torsion-incidence criterion and real-form restriction supply necessary tests across the requested range. No classification eliminating the required sections, universal interior-point exclusion, or 20-wall area bound follows. The first unresolved case is a dominant non-Weierstrass torsion branch in the genus-four family over polarized abelian surfaces; for the degree-two physical case it must also survive the conjugation test above.
