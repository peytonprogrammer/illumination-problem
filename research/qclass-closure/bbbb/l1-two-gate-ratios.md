Advances: L1 — for two fixed integral end templates, one specified lattice mark in each, and a single rectangular passage of variable length R, the pair is lit outside a finite set of positive rational R. The result survives common independent scaling of the two coordinate axes. For the six-reflex locked-gate example the remaining ratios have an explicit finite arithmetic test. General charts with independently varying ends remain open.

### The entrance window already exists

The earlier long-corridor theorem seals each end's door, chooses a common minimal direction, and follows each marked ray until its first regular hit on its own door. Both finite end itineraries persist on an open interval of slopes. This applies to arbitrary fixed strictly simple orthogonal end chambers, without a reflex budget. My preceding note left too much open: in this geometry the unresolved issue is quantitative control as the ends vary. The two-gate example showed why side reflections on the equal-travel legs alone cannot supply that control. [Long-corridor proof](README.md) · [Six-reflex example](l1-two-gate-boundary.md)

### Finite exceptional ratios for integral end templates

Use vertical travel as the longitudinal coordinate. Both doors open into the same rectangle of width w, horizontal coordinates measured from its left side. In template units all end vertices, marks and door endpoints are integral. Translating the upper end vertically with the middle length R does not change its local coordinates. Assume both marks are interior to their respective ends off the mouths. Set Q=2w. Use any bounded common open first-hit window I of positive slopes with width δ>0 and fixed initial sectors at both marks. Let E be the sum of the absolute vertical travels of the two end legs. It is constant on I, integral and positive: each regular horizontal turn and final door transmission is at a fixed integral height. Measure slope as the magnitude of horizontal travel per unit vertical travel.

Write M for a common multiple of twice every component width in the horizontal unit strips of the two ends in template units. At slope s+Mj, j≥0 integral, the two ends have the same signed first-door phases as at s∈I. In each unit of absolute vertical travel the extra transverse travel Mj folds to zero with the incident sign unchanged. Induction from the mark to the door preserves every sampled position, its incident sign, and every transmission or horizontal turn. Extra vertical-wall hits are smooth inside the open strip. This proof uses the full incident sheet and never identifies a target solely by its period coset. [Unit-strip folding argument](l1-periodic-corridor.md)

At s∈I the signed mismatch for one middle traversal is F_R(s)=(R+E)s+C modulo Q, with C constant on I and independent of R. This is the signed-phase identity in the long-corridor proof, including the reversal of the target leg. Increasing the slope to s+Mj preserves the two end phases and adds **RMj** to the mismatch. The translated interval need not have the original number of side reflections. The exact matching question for this union of windows is whether F_R(I) meets Qℤ−RMℤ₊. This applies the periodic-slope idea from the rectangular chain tests to both arbitrary integral end chambers. [Earlier chain criterion](chain-criteria.md)

* For R irrational the shifts are dense modulo Q. Some shifted window contains an exact match, since F_R(I) is open and nonempty.
* For R=p/q rational in lowest positive terms, the shifts modulo Q run through the grid of spacing g/q, where g=gcd(Mp,Qq). Every grid residue is attained with j≥0. Thus a match is guaranteed when δ(p+Eq)>g. Equality needs the actual open-interval test.
* Since gcd(p,q)=1, g divides MQ: it divides both MQp and MQq. Hence every failure satisfies **p+Eq ≤ MQ/δ**. This is a finite set of numerator–denominator pairs. Once I is supplied its size and the threshold are effective. Existence of I alone supplies no common numerical bound across different templates.

A match glues regular transverse door hits in position and velocity across the actual rectangle. Simultaneously multiplying all horizontal coordinates by t and all vertical ones by a preserves legal reflection itineraries (the diagonal map commutes with both axial reflections). Hence the theorem also applies with these two free scales and R equal to the physical middle length divided by a. A residual rational ratio can itself be scaled to arbitrarily large area.

### Explicit six-reflex test

In the preceding family's template units the middle width is 10, R=B/a, and the roof itinerary has E=8. On I=(3/7,4/7) the exact match for the original window is (R+8)s−13 ∈20ℤ. For these end legs the smaller shift **48** suffices: the lower leg travels one unit in width 8, the upper backwards leg travels three units to the roof and three back in width 9, then one unit in width 8. The additions 48 and 3·48 are divisible by 16 and 18 respectively. Roof endpoints and gate endpoints therefore remain avoided. [Tier geometry and roof itinerary](l1-two-gate-boundary.md)

All irrational R>0 pass. For R=p/q rational set g=gcd(48p,20q). The repeated roof windows light the pair exactly when

    ( 3(p+8q)/7 − 13q,  4(p+8q)/7 − 13q )  intersects  gℤ.

Here “exactly” refers to this family of windows only. It includes both the endpoint aim and the two strict gate clearances. Failures satisfy p+8q≤7g≤1680, since g divides 960 and in fact g≤240: gcd(48p,20q)=4 gcd(12p,5q) and the latter gcd divides 60. Equivalently, put H=48p/g and J=20q/g. These are coprime, R=5H/(12J), and failure requires 5H+96J≤1680, in particular J≤17. This identifies a finite residual without a room or direction search; the interval test can discard further ratios. For example at R=1, g=4 and the interval (−64/7,−55/7) contains −8. Thus R=1 passes, despite being far below the earlier sufficient threshold 132. At R=5/12 the interval is (−789/7,−688/7) with g=240, so this window family fails. Neither failure of the window nor its scaling proves darkness.

**First open case.** In this explicit family R=5/12 needs a different itinerary or entrance window. For L1 in general, a finite list per fixed pair of end templates does not control charts in which their normalized shapes or the marked positions themselves vary. The quantities needing control in this argument are the signed entrance-window width and the end phase period relative to the middle width. The theorem does not require, or establish, a ninth-reflex obstruction.
