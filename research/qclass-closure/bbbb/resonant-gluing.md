# First-hit gluing in rational chambers, and the reflex-corner limit

**Verdict.** For arbitrary orthogonal lattice chambers joined by a separating rectangular passage, two first-hit slope intervals glue by an exact gcd test. A common slope-translation period works simultaneously for every first-hit itinerary in the two chambers. The common period K depends on the mark abscissas as well as the chamber geometry; no common K for arbitrary real abscissas is asserted. The modulus also uses corridor length and width. For rectangular ends the first-hit argument below lights the whole real room at L>4d, uniformly in the aperture, where d is the larger chamber depth. Expanding stepped approaches admit a polynomial threshold for cross pairs. For unrestricted r≤8 chambers the shrinking-aperture case remains open.

## The induction interface

Seal the two ends of a straight corridor of width w and length L to form chambers A,B. The internal portal segments must separate the pieces, with no other opening along the corridor. In the usual attachment at the interior of a flat side of each chamber, two reflex corners are created at each mouth, so r=r_A+r_B+4. This formula must be checked anew if a mouth ends flush with another wall. The argument below needs no convexity of either sealed chamber. For induction on r, require the split actually to decrease r in both chambers. A theorem lighting every pair of interior marks in a sealed chamber neither prescribes a velocity at its mouth nor makes a path using that mouth as an intermediate mirror valid after opening it. Also the Q-class and proper-factor closures have class and endpoint hypotheses; they do not establish the induction assumption for every smaller real-mark chamber. [Q-class theorem](../twenty/README.md) · [Proper-factor scope](canonical-transfer.md)

Consider a source in A and a target in B. Trace a forward ray from the source and a **backward** ray from the target with the same positive slope magnitude s=|dy/dx|. Stop each at its first encounter with the open mouth; require no earlier corner contact or mouth endpoint. Record phase on the doubled interval of length 2w. At the A exit let φ_A be +y or −y, with y the height above the floor and sign the forward vertical velocity. At the B entry use φ_B with the **forward** sign, opposite the backward ray. Then the necessary and sufficient matching equation for these two legs and one full corridor traversal is

    F(s) = Ls + φ_A(s) − φ_B(s) ∈ 2wℤ.

Equality fixes both height and velocity. Earlier wall hits in the chambers are on surviving walls; all contacts inside the passage are smooth. Conversely cutting any nonaxial legal path crossing the passage once at its two mouths gives these first-hit legs. A horizontal witness may be checked directly. Paths returning through the corridor several times are outside this characterization.

## A common period for first-hit data in nonrectangular chambers

Assume the chamber coordinates and the marks' horizontal coordinates are rational in one common scale. Their heights may be real. Cut each chamber vertically at every vertex abscissa, both port and mark abscissas included. Every component of an open slab is a full rectangle. For each such rectangle record its width d and its doubled vertical height P. Choose K>0 so that Kd/P is integral for every rectangle in either chamber. For example take the least common positive rational multiple of all P/d. The cuts at the marks ensure that even the initial horizontal travel between cuts is a full slab width.

**Lemma.** For any regular first-hit ray at slope s>0, changing s to s+kK, k≥0 integral, preserves the sequence of vertical cut crossings and vertical wall contacts up to the first mouth, and preserves its physical exit height and velocity signs. The count of horizontal reflections can change.

Indeed, along one slab traversal the developed vertical travel increases by kKd, a multiple of the vertical period. Starting with the same height and signs gives the same signed exit state on that cut. Consequently the same next slab is entered, or the same vertical wall reflects the ray. A newly added horizontal-wall reflection inside the open slab is smooth. On the cuts, any forbidden corner or premature mouth hit would have occurred at the original exit state. An artificial cut through a smooth horizontal reflection uses the outgoing one-sided state. Induct over the finite traversal sequence. The same reasoning identifies the regular data for any two positive congruent slopes in either order. This period depends on the cut geometry and horizontal mark positions, not on which itinerary is used or how many slabs it visits.

On an open common first-hit chart J=(u,v) on which both original reflection words are fixed, let a,b be their total absolute horizontal travels. Signed unfolding gives φ_A'=a and φ_B'=−b; choose continuous real representatives there. Thus F is affine of slope L+a+b. On the translated chart J+kK the chamber phases agree modulo 2w at corresponding slopes; the full mismatch gains kLK. For rational L,w the subgroup generated by LK in ℝ/(2wℤ) is finite. Write

    g = gcd(LK,2w),     q = 2w/g,

where the rational gcd is the positive generator of the integer span. There are exactly q residue classes. Some nonnegative translate glues **if and only if**

    (F(u+), F(v−)) intersects gℤ.

Proof: for a hit F(s)=mg, Bézout solves F(s)+kLK≡0 modulo 2w; k may be taken in 0,…,q−1. Conversely every matched translate gives such a hit. In particular (L+a+b)(v−u)>g suffices. At equality endpoints matter. For a single parameter with verified first-hit legs one checks F(s)∈gℤ directly. For a specified finite collection of charts the condition is the disjunction of these tests. The phases before fixing s still vary continuously; it is the *translations at fixed s* that give finite cosets. Rational data alone do not supply a finite list of all first-hit charts. [Column compatibility](chain-criteria.md) · [Fixed-chamber phase formula](README.md)

**When positivity is known.** For fixed positive doors, almost every slope admits first hits from both marks by directional minimality on the two sealed rational-angle chambers. Taking one such slope gives a common open chart. Thus one can always choose paths avoiding earlier mouth contacts when their terminal data are free. The reviewed long-corridor proof gives a uniform tail over the marks, without requiring the period lemma. For changing doors the sharper strict inequality above is enough. But in exact resonance LK∈2wℤ one has g=2w; translating the slope brings no new signed phase. Shrinking width can shrink the chart at the same rate. Neither a count of reflex corners nor existence of sealed paths forces the image interval of some chart to contain the required residue. [Uniform positive-door case and shrinking-door limits](thick-thin.md) · [Exactly placed resonant examples](placement.md)

**Why a prescribed sealed leg cannot simply be truncated.** In the named DB55 left square take (1,1) moving right and up at slope 3/10. Its first hit of x=5 is at height 11/5 going up. In the sealed square the developed heights at subsequent visits to that wall increase by 3 per visit. Five visits later the developed height is 86/5, folding to 14/5 going down. None of the intervening vertical-wall hits is a corner: their developed heights are 11/5+3j/2, 0≤j≤10, none a multiple of five. A prescription using that later downward phase relies on reflection from the removed mirror at the very first visit. Truncating there changes both the prescribed position and the vertical sign. The first-hit criterion sidesteps that fault; it does not repair every specified sealed path. Unlike a formal convex-corner turnaround, an ordinary reflection at a mouth reverses only one velocity component and need not retrace the path to the mark.

The open induction step is quantitative and positional: at least one actual common first-hit chart (or a separately proved multiple-traversal construction) must place its mismatch over a matching residue in every resonant thin room in scope. The gcd characterizes the match exactly *after* those chamber data are known. Even universal cross-chamber coverage would leave same-chamber pairs whose sealed witnesses use the mouth, and pairs in the disappearing passage.

## Follow-on: the exact local normal form at one reflex corner

Choose a disk about O containing no other vertex or nonincident wall. Its intersection with the room is a sector of angle α=3π/2. Doubling gives the Euclidean cone of angle 2α=3π. Write two interior marks in polar coordinates (ρ,θ),(σ,ψ), with 0<θ,ψ<α. Their two angular representatives are ±θ and ±ψ modulo 3π. A corner-free path contained in this disk exists **if and only if**

    |θ−ψ| < π.

Indeed the least angular distance between any representatives is |θ−ψ|: both θ+ψ and 2α−θ−ψ are at least that large. A straight segment avoiding the apex changes its continuous developed argument by strictly less than π. Conversely for separation less than π the ordinary developed chord in the original sector stays inside it, and its radius never exceeds max(ρ,σ). At equality the candidate chord hits O. Thus a local path, whenever possible, already needs zero reflections. All other pairs must leave the disk; the cone alone cannot imply bounded global complexity.

**A sufficient global return for the entire corner neighborhood.** Orient axes so the missing quadrant is northeast. Suppose a nonaxial ray from O starts strictly southwest and has a finite itinerary ending at a convex corner V, with all intermediate reflections transverse and smooth and no other vertex hit. Then there is ε>0 such that **every** pair of distinct interior marks within ε of O is lit with a bounded number of reflections. If the outgoing itinerary has k smooth reflections, 2k+2 suffices.

To prove it, unfold the outgoing copies and let V* be the developed endpoint; its two coordinates relative to O are strictly negative. Continue through the convex corner in the unfolding and retrace the reference ray to O. The two perpendicular mirrors meeting at V give the half-turn about V*, so the final copy of a mark T has developed position 2V*−T (coordinates taken in the initial chart). For nearby marks S,T join S to 2V*−T. All intermediate smooth contacts persist with clearance outside fixed small disks about O and V. Near V the four reflected right quadrants tile a disk. The chord crosses from the original quadrant to the opposite one by two smooth reflections provided it misses V*. Near O the departure from S and the reversed departure from T both point strictly southwest. From any allowed nearby interior point this direction stays outside the missing northeast quadrant; both ends therefore clear O and its incident walls along the reference legs.

It remains to exclude an exact hit at V*. Pairs already covered by the direct local chord need no return. For the others one mark is strictly southeast and the other strictly northwest of O (an axis point in the room interior cannot make angular separation ≥π with an interior point except across antipodal axes, whose east and north rays here are walls). Thus their joining line has negative slope. Its southwest extension cannot pass through the fixed V*: for sufficiently small ε, V* is strictly to the left of and below **both** marks, impossible on a negative-slope line through them. A chord from S to 2V*−T through V* would require precisely such collinearity. This proves the uniform return claim, including the angular equality case. It is conditional on one convex-corner itinerary with the specified departure sector; its existence at every reflex corner of every r≤8 room is unproved. It treats two marks tending to the same reflex, not two different limiting corners.

## A linear threshold by following the first hit across itinerary changes

**Whole-room theorem.** Two bounded axis-aligned rectangular chambers of horizontal depths D₁,D₂ are joined along vertical doors by a horizontal rectangle of length L and common positive width w. The chambers may have different heights and door placements. Assume the ordinary separating layout with no other opening and a strictly simple boundary. Set d=max(D₁,D₂). If **L>4d**, every two distinct real interior points of the assembled room are lit. Neither rationality nor a lower bound on w is required. In particular exact phase resonance is allowed. For DBas, DB55 and DBw2, d=5, so L≥21 suffices at integer lengths. DBw2 already has a stronger L>10 result restricted to lattice marks. [Named dimensions and d110 replay of the former bounds](../../illumination-generalization-2026-11-06/fable/directions-2026-11-09/d110-review-length/review.md) · [DBw2 lattice result](chain-criteria.md)

**Two opposite chamber marks.** Measure physical height from the common door floor, so the open span is (0,w). Launch each ray horizontally toward its door side, treating the target ray backward. Use the same slope magnitude 0<s<w/(2d). For a mark below the opening aim upward; for one above aim downward. Write h>0 for its vertical distance to the nearer lip, D for its chamber depth and x∈(0,D) for its horizontal distance to the portal wall. With the wall sealed provisionally, successive visits have absolute horizontal travels

    A_j = x+2Dj,       j=0,1,2,….

The first index with sA_j>h gives an entry penetration p=sA_j−h. Except at lip hits s=h/A_j we have 0<p<w: if j≥1, then p<2Ds<w, and if j=0, p<sD<w/2. All earlier portal-side reflections are strictly outside the opening. Height moves monotonically from the interior mark to the first door, so there is no horizontal-wall or chamber-corner contact. For a mark already in [0,w], aim toward the more distant lip. The first visit at travel x remains strictly in the opening and encounters no corner.

Let f denote the signed first-hit phase **in the ray's own direction of tracing**, including for the backward ray. Below the opening f=p. Above it f=−w+p. For a start at height y in [0,w], f=εy+sx with chosen vertical sign ε. In all cases f(s)>f(0+), and the right limit at zero exists (p≤2Ds for outside starts). Away from lip hits the derivative is A_j or x. At a lip threshold, increasing s switches to the earlier visit: penetration drops from 2Ds to zero. Every genuine jump is therefore strictly downward. The exact lip slopes are forbidden and need not be assigned a value.

The full mismatch is F(s)=Ls+f₁(s)+f₂(s), with a **plus** for the backward ray because its required forward sign is reversed. Write B=F(0+) and let M be the smallest multiple of 2w strictly larger than B. Then M−B≤2w. Since Lw/(2d)>2w, there is a regular s₁<w/(2d) with F(s₁)>M. At sufficiently small regular s₀>0, F(s₀)<M. Only finitely many lip thresholds lie between s₀ and s₁. On each intervening open interval F is affine and strictly increasing; at each excluded threshold its right limit is strictly smaller than its left limit, including simultaneous switches. Hence some **open** interval contains a root F(s)=M: without such a root a downward jump could never take the function from below M to above it. This proves exact signed gluing without ever matching at a corner. The gain over bounding the width of a single chart is that all the jumps have the same sign.

**One mark in a chamber, the other in the corridor or an open mouth.** This case works at **every length**. Let the target be at depth z≥0 measured from the source chamber and height t∈(0,w). If the chamber mark is already in [0,w], the direct segment crosses inside the opening (at z=0 it ends there). If it starts h>0 below the floor, aim monotonically upward, reflecting only on vertical chamber walls. Choose the total travel A to the first doorway hit from the progression {x+2Dj : j=0,1,2,…}. To reach t without any strip reflection, take s=(h+t)/(A+z). The requirements for entry above the floor and for the preceding visit, if present, to be below it are exactly enforced by

    hz/t < A < hz/t + 2D(1+h/t).

This interval is longer than the progression spacing 2D and its lower endpoint is nonnegative. It therefore contains a permitted A. The doorway penetration is t−zs, strictly positive and at most t<w. All earlier visits occur below the doorway. A mark above the opening is covered by vertical reflection. Two marks in the same chamber or in the closed strip see one another directly whenever both are physical interior points. These cases exhaust the room, including both open mouths.

**Exact check at the new threshold.** In DBas at L=21, use S=(1,3), T=(27,3). Both start above the upper lip. The first qualifying portal travels at slope 1/13 are 14 and 17; F=−4+(21+14+17)/13=0. The d109 event tracer, given this velocity directly, reaches T through (5,25/13) and (26,22/13). The chamber-to-corridor formula from the same S to (6,3/2) gives velocity (10,−3); the same tracer crosses (5,9/5) and reaches the target. ## A polynomial bound for nonrectangular approaches

Here is a sufficient test when the walls between a mark and its doorway have steps. For each mark outside the door's height span, move monotonically in height toward its nearer lip. At each intervening critical level require the occupied horizontal run just below in the direction of travel to be contained in the next run. Thus the run can expand to either side, but it cannot bar progress in height. In the sealed chamber require a final constant run throughout the open door-height span, incident to the door wall, which contains the last approach run at the lip. For a mark already at door height require it to start in this final run (or its interior-side limit at a lip). These are conditions on the actual two approaches, allowing arbitrary geometry elsewhere. Assume integer wall coordinates in the physical lattice scale. The marks may be real.

Let d bound all run widths used on both sides. For each outside start list the positive vertical distances h_j to its intervening horizontal run changes and to the nearer lip. Combine the two lists, retaining multiplicities, and set

    N = Σ_j ceil(2d h_j / w).

Then **L ≥ 8d(N+1)** suffices for this cross-pair. If both approaches have at most m listed levels in total and their height ranges are at most H, this is at most 8d[1+m ceil(2dH/w)]. In lattice units w≥1, giving a polynomial in linear chamber sizes and the number of levels. This estimate concerns the specified nonrectangular approaches; the linear 4d result is sharper for rectangular ones.

**Proof.** Use t=1/s in the open interval (2d/w,4d/w). Before the lip the ray bounces only at vertical walls. At a run expansion it passes into the next band unless it hits an endpoint at that level. For fixed initial horizontal sign σ its unfolded abscissa at distance h_j in height is x_start+σh_j t. Reflections across integer vertical lines preserve integrality of abscissas. Remove every t for which this unfolded abscissa is integral at a listed level, including the lip. There are at most ceil(2d h_j/w) values at each positive distance. This removes every possible change in the sequence of vertical-wall contacts before that level, since a change requires a contact to move across a band endpoint level. Expansion ensures that all other rays reach the lip. From there the next portal-wall visit takes horizontal travel at most 2d, hence rises or falls by less than w and enters legally. The choice of whether an opposite-wall bounce precedes it is fixed on each remaining interval. Starts in the final run at door height use the first visit toward the door and the vertical sign with at least w/2 of headroom, as above.

In s the full interval has width w/(4d). Deleting at most N slopes leaves one open component of width at least w/[4d(N+1)]. Both first-hit words are fixed there. The mismatch has derivative L+a+b>L since both leg travels are positive. Its range thus has length strictly greater than 2w under the stated bound, giving an interior matching slope. This proof includes exact resonance. If an approach run contracts, some incoming rays can hit the obstructing horizontal wall and reverse before attaining the lip. This is the first step that fails for a general r≤8 chamber; a small reflex count alone does not imply the run-containment hypothesis.
