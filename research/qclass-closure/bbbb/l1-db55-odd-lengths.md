Advances: L1 — The DB55 two-box layout lights every real pair at every odd corridor length coprime to five, uniformly in the two integral box heights. At even normalized lengths the cylinder argument reduces any possible dark pair to a discrete grid.

# Changing the short corridor length

Use width-five boxes on [0,5] and [C−5,C], bottoms at zero, roofs at arbitrary integer heights H₁,H₂≥4. The corridor spans 2<y<3 and has positive integral length L=C−10. Separate horizontal normalization here requires gcd(5,L)=1. Assume that coprimality throughout. The proof includes unequal roofs because only their individual return periods matter.

**A two-cylinder direction for every normalized length.** Set horizontal travel per unit vertical travel t=5, with vertical speed of magnitude one. Use the upward section at y=2, including the outgoing reflection on the solid corridor floor. Unfold the horizontal sign into u=x or −x mod 2C. Section endpoints at physical vertices are deleted. In one traverse to y=3 the phase advances by t. A return from the upper bank above either box adds 2(Hᵢ−3)t horizontally, a multiple of the box period 10. The lower-bank excursion adds 4t, also a multiple of 10. Over the corridor a reflection at its ceiling or floor turns the vertical velocity immediately. Thus the upward return is u↦u+10 mod 2C. For noninteger u all abscissas at the seam, floor and roof tests remain noninteger. Every orbit away from the integral transverse levels meets this section.

Since gcd(10,2C)=2, the unit intervals with even and odd left endpoints form exactly two cycles. Suspending them yields clean cylinders, with even and odd section levels as opposite rims. At an interior physical mark the transverse invariant on its lifts is ±x±5y mod 2; the reference-level shift 2t is even. If some lift is off the rims, simultaneously reversing both signs gives a lift in the other cylinder. At an integral level a regular physical mark is in both closures by a small transverse displacement on either side. An open-cylinder lift therefore lights any second interior mark, and opposite-rim representatives also connect by an open cylinder chord. These are the same covering and chord arguments checked in [d183](../../illumination-generalization-2026-11-06/fable/directions-2026-11-09/d183-review-db55top/review.md).

A surviving mark must have x+5y and x−5y integral, hence x and 5y are either both integers or both strict half-integers. In the latter case flipping just the horizontal sign exchanges the parities of its two rim representatives, so this mark lights every mark too. Consequently any dark pair would have **x and 5y integral at both ends, with x−5y of the same parity at the two marks**. This reduction already holds at L=2. It uses t=5, which avoids the four-cycle obstruction encountered at t=10.

**Closure for odd L.** Now C is odd. The direction t=10 has the same two-cylinder property since gcd(20,2C)=2. On the residual grid 10y is even. Opposite x parities are therefore lit, leaving just equal x parities.

The same-box chord handles marks in one box.

**Bank-to-far-band lemma.** At every corridor length a mark in one box sees every interior mark in the other box with 2<y<3. Apply d112's chamber-to-strip construction. Its final leg has no strip reflection: it enters the open mouth and stays within the height span to the target. Extend the test strip horizontally into the target box to its abscissa; this last straight leg remains legal where the artificial strip walls disappear. A terminal height equal to 2 or 3 outside the physical corridor can instead be assigned to its adjacent bank in the construction below. Marks in the actual open mouth or corridor are covered directly by d112. Two marks strictly inside the middle band see each other. For opposite-box residual marks in the banks, let their abscissas be a and b=C−d, where 1≤a,d≤4 and a≡b mod 2. Use horizontal speed t=50k, k a positive integer. The bank legs from either mark to its seam preserve abscissa and horizontal sense: their vertical depths are multiples of 1/5. Treat a mark on the bank seam as having zero bank travel. From opposite banks, one middle traversal suffices since a+t≡b mod 2C is solvable: gcd(50,2C)=2. From the same bank choose the terminal sign ε and solve a+2t≡εb mod 2C. This too is solvable since gcd(100,2C)=2. The intermediate phase z=a+t must have folded abscissa different from 0,5,C−5,C. In doubled coordinates this is precisely

    a+εb ≠ 0, ±10   (mod C).

Indeed 2z≡a+εb mod C, and 2 is invertible there. For ε=+1 the tested residue is a−d. Since a+d is odd this difference is nonzero, of absolute value at most 3. It succeeds for C≥17. For C=11 a failure has |a−d|=1; then ε=−1 tests a+d, an odd integer between 3 and 7, safely away from 0,±10 mod 11. For C=13 failure requires |a−d|=3, so a+d=5 and ε=−1 succeeds. These exhaust the normalized odd spans below 17. At the intermediate seam the ray either turns on the solid corridor wall or visits a box cap and returns. The one-way cap travel is a multiple of 10 horizontally, hence its cap hit is regular. This completes every real placement.

**First case still open here.** At L=2, C=12 the first cylinder reduction still works. On its residual grid there is no longer an inverse of 2 modulo C. The two-traversal equation at t=50k can require divisibility by four even for equal x parities; the odd-span sign proof no longer applies. For example (2,1)–(8,1) survives the t=5 rim test and neither sign solves 100k≡±8−2 mod 24. This is a failure of the construction, not an example of darkness. D112 independently covers all lengths L>20. Among horizontally normalized lengths only the short even values 2,4,6,8,12,14,16,18 remain outside the combination of this theorem and d112 for the full height family.
