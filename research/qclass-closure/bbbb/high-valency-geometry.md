# Excluding irrational dark endpoints in the large-area range

**Theorem.** In a strictly simple normalized orthogonal lattice room with r ≤ 8 and area n ≥ 120, every pair of distinct interior points with at least one irrational coordinate is lit.

Wolecki’s finiteness theorem together with d16 would exclude irrational dark pairs at every area and reflex count in strictly simple orthogonal lattice rooms. Indeed, d16 shows that one such pair would give a continuum of physical dark pairs in the same room. However, d16 §6.4 identifies a gap in the proof of Wolecki’s Lemma 2.9 precisely for the family in which each endpoint has one rational and one irrational coordinate. The argument below is the project’s only proof that excludes this family without relying on that step. Its scope is n ≥ 120 and r ≤ 8 under the normalization above. [Wolecki](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.09358) · [d16](../../illumination-generalization-2026-11-06/fable/directions-2026-11-09/d16-real-to-rational/reduction.md) · [review and corrections](../../illumination-generalization-2026-11-06/fable/directions-2026-11-09/d16-review)

## Why bare distance is insufficient

The D69 dumbbell DB55 with S=(1,1), T=(9,1) is true BBBB and its marked valency is 52 at area 53. Every route to a regular coincidence on the ordered-pair surface has length at least |S−T|/2=4, since both physical projections have that length and end at the same point. Yet the corner (0,0) is only √2 from S. Thus the shortest-note clearance condition fails even on a known lit pair of maximal valency. The argument below instead uses the fact that darkness at an irrational mark would persist over a whole complex curve for each choice of lifts. That fact is unavailable for an individual rational mark.

## The moving family and its intersection bound

Write M for the four-copy unfolding, Q for its quotient by the half-turn τ (the room double), and E for the square torus with periods 2Z². The translation map π:M→E has degree n. Its quotient by sign on E is f:Q→B, the degree-n pillowcase map. The points of E[2] are the four lattice parities. Every physical vertex has one lift to M, fixed by τ. The local degree of π there is 1 for a convex corner and 3 for a reflex corner. The map M→Q has local degree 2 there. In particular Q is a Riemann sphere.

Assume a physical irrational dark pair exists. After exchanging axes, d16 Theorem 1 gives S=(x,y), T=(u,v), with x,u irrational and y,v rational. Fix lifts s,t in M. D16 Lemma F and Theorem 1 give nonzero coprime integers a,b such that the punctured component through (s,t) of

    a π(s') + b π(t') = c in E

is M-dark away from the diagonal. D16 uses periods Z². Passing to E just lifts its congruence to the specific constant at the marked pair: on the connected component the finite choice of lift is constant. Remove preimages of E[2] in either coordinate. Replacing t' by τt' identifies this family with the component for (s,τt) with coefficients (a,−b). Lemma F applies there as well because this lift pair too is dark and s is irrational. Consequently the two projections to Q form a **DP-dark pair at every generic point of the component**: a corner-avoiding path in Q would lift to one of the two excluded paths in M. This assertion concerns these two Q lifts; it does not assume physical darkness on an open two-dimensional set.

Parametrize the relation downstairs by ξ in E, with z=z₀+bξ and w=w₀−aξ. This parametrization has no kernel by coprimality and covers the relation (Bézout). Let F be the compact normalization of the chosen component, degree D over E in the ξ coordinate. The two projections to M have degrees b²D/n and a²D/n, by composition. The corresponding maps P₁,P₂:F→Q have twice those degrees. They differ at the initial pair. Since Q is a sphere their diagonal intersections have total multiplicity

    2D(a²+b²)/n.

This is the degree of the diagonal section, as in the [midpoint note](midpoint-necessity.md). At a regular point of Q, sufficiently close distinct points connect in a vertex-free coordinate disk. At a convex corner the local metric is a cone of angle π: choose developments of the close distinct points with angular separation at most π/2, so their chord avoids the cone point. Consequently every diagonal intersection of this dark component must be at a reflex corner.

For one such corner, both M endpoints must be its unique lift, and their E coordinates must equal its parity e. There is at most one parameter ξ for z=w=e. In local coordinates around that lift, π−e is a cube, so normalizing the two cube relations gives three branches in the full fiber product. Each branch is unramified over either M coordinate. On each branch the two maps to Q vanish to order two in a common local coordinate centered at the corner. More precisely take the cube-root charts of π−e, in which τ is negation and the Q chart is the square. The two cube-root coordinates differ by a constant factor because z−e and w−e are proportional. Writing the second cube-root coordinate as λ times the first, the difference of the Q coordinates is (1−λ²) times the square of the first coordinate. If λ²=1, this branch lies on the diagonal or the graph of τ in the M coordinates. It belongs to another component, since agreement of the Q maps on an open branch of F would force agreement everywhere on F, including at the distinct marked images. Each branch on F therefore contributes exactly two. This proves

    D(a²+b²)/n ≤ 3r.                                            (1)

There is at least one intersection. In particular c=(a+b)e for some e in E[2].

## Unequal absolute slopes are impossible

Use the sheet action on m blocks of size k as in [three-sheet.md](three-sheet.md), taking k=1, m=n in the primitive case. We have k≤8, m≥15, and the outer group contains the natural A_m. On the torus E the outer monodromy still contains A_m: removing the four branch values, passage from B to E restricts to a normal subgroup of index at most two. Its branch support on E is a nonempty set Z in E[2]. At e the monodromy is the square of the quotient's pillowcase branch permutation, so it is nontrivial exactly at parities carrying a quotient zero. A nontrivial proper quotient has a zero by corrected R1; for k=1 the room itself has a reflex corner (a normalized rectangle has area one).

Pulling the two systems back to the ξ torus gives branch supports of cardinalities b²|Z| and a²|Z|. For each system the outer group continues to contain A_m: multiplication on E gives an unramified abelian cover before deleting the finitely many exceptional values, so the restricted monodromy contains the commutator subgroup. Deleting further regular points does not change the image. If |a|≠|b|, a loop about a point of the symmetric difference acts nontrivially on one outer system and trivially on the other. The nontrivial permutation is a product of three-cycles. Its normal closure in the corresponding outer group contains A_m by simplicity. Thus the joint image on the two outer systems contains A_m on that side acting independently of the other side. To see independence on both sides, label this side 1. Write H₁,H₂ for the projections of the joint image G and Jᵢ for the elements of Hᵢ that occur with identity on the opposite side. Goursat’s lemma gives H₁/J₁ ≅ H₂/J₂. Since A_m ⊆ J₁ ⊆ H₁ ⊆ S_m, the common quotient has order at most two. The simple nonabelian A_m in H₂ maps trivially to it, so A_m ⊆ J₂ as well. Thus G contains A_m × A_m.

Each individual pullback on the full n sheets is connected. Here the normalization hypothesis is important. The absolute periods of M span the whole period lattice 2Z²: for a wall edge of displacement v on the positive room copy, follow the edge and then the reverse of its τ image. Its endpoints are fixed by τ and its period is 2v. Horizontal and vertical wall-length gcds are one. Loops may be perturbed off the finite exceptional set without changing periods. Surjectivity onto the period lattice gives connectivity after pulling back by any multiplication map, by the usual monodromy test for a pullback of an unramified torus cover.

In the joint group, the stabilizer of a first-coordinate block induces at least A_m on second-coordinate blocks by independence. Passing to the stabilizer of a particular sheet in that first block costs index at most k. Its image still contains A_m: otherwise the coset action of A_m of degree at most k would be faithful, impossible since m!/2 > k!. Hence each first sheet has partners in at least m second blocks in the chosen component. All n first sheets occur by connectivity, so D ≥ nm. Then (1) gives m(a²+b²) ≤ 3r. Unequal nonzero absolute integers give a²+b²≥5, whereas m≥15 and r≤8. This is impossible.

## Equal slopes and the real boundary of a block quotient

It follows for each choice of lifts that |a|=|b|=1. Now c=(a+b)e=0 in E, including for opposite signs. Thus for every choice of signs on the lift of T its E coordinate equals ± the E coordinate of the chosen lift of S. Compare (u,v) and (u,−v): because x is irrational their x-coordinates force the same overall sign, and hence 2v=0 modulo 2. Therefore v is integral, y is integral of the same parity, and u=±x modulo 2. Both marks lie in open horizontal unit grid edges mapping to the same non-pole point of B. The two Q lifts of each interior mark are distinct, and lifts of distinct physical marks are disjoint. The four points therefore label four distinct sheets of this regular fiber.

Use a nearby base face to label their sheets by the two cells abutting the horizontal edge, one for each lift, as in dictionary D0. For every one of the four choices of ordered Q lifts, the corresponding component of the normalized off-diagonal Q×_B Q is DP-dark at generic points. Indeed the equal-slope M family maps nonconstantly onto that component and is generically DP-dark by the first section. This projection is surjective on compact completions; deleting finitely many missing parameter points does not affect generic darkness. Lifts with the other first sheet follow also by reflecting both M coordinates in the horizontal axis of the unfolding. Add the transposed components, since lighting is symmetric. The resulting sheet relation is an undirected invariant graph containing K₂,₂ between the two pairs of marked sheets.

Let d be its valency. The diagonal budget 2d counts its coincidences on Q. Generic darkness excludes all regular coincidences, and there are at most two branches per physical reflex. Thus d≤r. In the primitive case the four sheets would meet the unique external orbit of valency n−1, impossible. In the imprimitive case the external orbit of the maximal block partition has valency n−k>r. The entire graph is therefore internal, and all four incident sheets belong to one block, so k≥4. Write φ:Q→N for that degree-k inner cover. The within-block components satisfy φ(P₁)=φ(P₂); distinct points on these components can coalesce only at ramification of φ. If r₀ counts reflex corners ramified for φ, the preceding budget sharpens to d≤r₀. At least k reflexes lie over a quotient zero, unramified for φ. Hence

    2 ≤ d ≤ r₀ ≤ r−k.

In particular k is 4, 5 or 6.

We need one more bound, involving the *physical* boundary. Here n ≥ 120 enters again. In this range the single external orbit makes the maximal proper block partition unique: any other proper invariant partition that identified an external pair would identify the entire external orbit and hence all sheets. Every proper partition refines this maximal one. Thus reflection of the double swapping its two room copies preserves the factor (it covers reflection of the base pillowcase and induces an automorphism of the monodromy block lattice by path transport). It descends to an antiholomorphic involution on the sphere N and φ is real for these involutions. Its real locus is a circle: it has a fixed point, the common image of the two conjugate Q lifts of S. The same fiber contains the conjugate pair of lifts of T. These four preimages are distinct and nonreal since the marks are interior. The value is regular since it lies away from the four base poles.

Below this range, cov3397, cov3608 and cov4474 each have two maximal partitions with blocks of size four. In those rooms uniqueness cannot justify descent of the mirror symmetry. [d80 saved-data checks](../../illumination-generalization-2026-11-06/fable/directions-2026-11-09/d80-review-irrational/review.md)

By k≥4 and r₀≥2 we have fewer than 2k reflexes available for unramified fibers over quotient zeros (r−r₀≤6<2k). Thus N has a unique zero. It is real, and all k preimages are real reflex corners at which φ is unramified. Near that zero a regular real fiber consists entirely of k real points, by local invertibility. Moving along either arc of the real circle from this all-real value to the marked value loses at least four real preimages. A simple real ramification changes their number by two; a real degree-three ramification changes it by zero; nonreal critical points cannot change the real count locally. These are all possible local degrees by Fact L. The two arcs have disjoint interiors and regular ends, so there are at least four distinct simple ramification points of φ. By Riemann–Hurwitz on the two spheres,

    2k−2 ≥ 4+2r₀,      hence d≤r₀≤k−3.

For k=4 this contradicts d≥2. For k=5 the last bound forces d=2; for k=6 the bound r−k forces d=2. A simple 2-regular graph containing K₂,₂ has that four-cycle as an entire connected component. The block stabilizer is transitive on the sheets of that block, so all its components have size four. Neither five nor six is divisible by four. This excludes the last cases and finishes the proof. The inequalities 4 ≤ k ≤ r−2 already exclude eight-cell blocks for these edge marks. The separate eight-sheet lemma concerns lattice marks.

## A stronger conclusion for individual lifts

The slope calculation also gives a statement about two distinct points on Q, even when other lifts of their physical room marks are lit. Suppose one Q point has irrational torus coordinates and that this particular Q pair is DP-dark. Both corresponding M lift pairs (s,t) and (s,τt) must be M-dark. By d16 Lemmas R and G, the second point is irrational and the first lifted pair satisfies a nontrivial rational relation with both coefficients nonzero: a generic orbit would be dense and meet an open set of lit pairs. Lemma F then gives exactly the component used in the intersection argument above. The unequal-slope proof and c=0 conclusion only require these two M lift pairs. Thus the two Q points have the same image in B. They are off the four base poles. The whole corresponding component of Q×_B Q is generically DP-dark. Its valency is at most r by the diagonal budget, hence it cannot be the external orbit. For primitive sheet monodromy no such distinct DP pair exists. For imprimitive sheet monodromy the two Q points have the same image in the maximal intermediate cover N, and the component valency is at most the number of reflex ramification points for Q→N. The four-lift and real-fiber arguments are what turn this geometric restriction on one lift pair into illumination of the physical room pair.

## Review targets and limits

Check the factor of two in (1), the passage from d16's two M-dark components to generic DP darkness, the independent alternating actions after pulling back by unequal multiplications, and the real-fiber count. The argument relies on d16 with its reviewed corrections, the dictionary, corrected R1, and the alternating quotient bounds in the [reviewed valency note](three-sheet.md). The local shortest-path clearance test plays no role in the proof. The room and orbit assumptions are for normalized lattice rooms; arbitrary real side lengths are outside the statement. Rational nonlattice marks of unbounded denominator remain possible under this argument. No new room, ray, direction or reflection-word search was run.
