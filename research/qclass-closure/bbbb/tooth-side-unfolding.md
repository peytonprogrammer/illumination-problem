# Where the one-floor family first fails, and what tooth reflections buy

## Sharp aspect-ratio transition

Use the U skeleton from §3 of the eight-wall manuscript. Two equal rectangular teeth of width u, with inner lips separated by gap g>0, open above a rectangular hall of height c. Put a mark on each tooth centerline at the same positive depth d above its mouth. The caps are strictly above the marks. Consider the original shot family: one floor bounce, no wall contact inside either tooth, any number of vertical side-wall bounces inside the hall, and no other vertical reversal. A shot in this family exists **if and only if gd<cu**.

Indeed, writing s for the absolute horizontal travel per unit vertical travel, each end leg shifts horizontally by sd. A straight end leg from the center can advance toward the gap by at most u/2. The two mouth positions must be separated by more than g if both are on the inner side; globally the separation is at least g, with equality only at the two forbidden inner lips. The middle leg has only 2cs horizontal travel, independent of any hall-side bounces. If gd≥cu, the bounds sd≤u/2 and 2cs>g contradict one another. Conversely aim at the other mark unfolded across the hall floor, with s=(u+g)/(2(c+d)). Then sd<u/2 precisely under the claimed inequality. Both mouths clear strictly, and the smooth floor midpoint lies under the gap. The direct same-height chord is blocked by the gap above the hall.

**Repair at every depth.** Write m for the midpoint of the gap and l for the left mouth center. Aim an unfolded left-mark image X at (m,0), with the floor at zero. The roof crossing is (cX+dm)/(c+d). Choose X from l+uℤ nearest to ((c+d)l−dm)/c. All these are actual center-mark images between the tooth side walls. The crossing differs from l by at most cu/(2(c+d))<u/2. All tooth bounces occur above the mouth and below the mark height. Below the mouth the segment joins two interior abscissas of the hall. Reflect the reversed first half across x=m to continue after the floor bounce; this changes precisely the vertical component of the incoming velocity. Thus at gd=cu the old family already fails, yet retaining one floor bounce and allowing tooth-side reflections lights the pair. For the single-bank U, full illumination for arbitrary real placements and dimensions is already known; the if-and-only-if here identifies a first failure of the particular stacked-hall construction. [U theorem, §3](../../illumination-publication/repository/preprints/eight-wall/manuscript.tex)

**Named replay.** In DB55 use the hall [0,13] × [2,3], with upper mouths (0,5) and (8,13), the same mouths on the lower bank, and the solid floor segment 5<x<8. For S=(5/2,19/4), T=(21/2,19/4), one has gd=21/4>5=cu. Select X=−5/2. The physical launch has direction (−36,−11), hits the left tooth side, exits at x=71/22, bounces at (13/2,2), and reaches T by symmetry. This construction also works with the lower teeth present because the chosen floor point is solid. It is distinct from the previously reviewed DB55 cap shot. No direction or word search is required.

## Two parallel mouths: use both sets of side walls

Here is an aspect-ratio-free result for marks that need not be mirror partners. Let two rectangular teeth of widths u,v open into a rectangular hall. They may be on opposite banks or on the same bank over a solid floor. Marks on their centerlines have arbitrary positive depths a,b. In the same-bank case use two distinct teeth and unfold at the opposite floor. In the opposite-bank case let C=c, and in the same-bank case let C=2c. There is a shot from one mark to the other with all side-wall reflections confined to the teeth. In the same-bank case it also reflects once on the floor. No length restriction or alignment of the mouths is required.

**Proof.** Reverse the pair if necessary so u≥v. Let D=a+C+b, λ=a/D, μ=b/D. For developed endpoint abscissas X,Z the mouth crossings are

    E₁=(1−λ)X+λZ,     E₂=μX+(1−μ)Z.

Let (X*,Z*) give the two mouth centers. This solution exists because the determinant is C/D>0. Round X* to a source image X, with error at most u/2. With that X fixed, choose the real Z₀ making E₂ exactly the second center; then round Z₀ to the nearest target image Z, with error at most v/2. Because the marks are centered, their image spacings are u,v. After the second rounding the errors at the mouths satisfy

    |ΔE₂| ≤ (1−μ)v/2 < v/2,
    |ΔE₁| ≤ [C/(a+C)]u/2 + [a/D]v/2 < u/2.

For the last strict inequality use v≤u and D>a+C. The coefficient of the first rounding error is det/(1−μ)=C/(a+C). Thus both mouth crossings are strictly inside. Between them the transverse abscissa interpolates between two interior hall abscissas, so no hall-side contact occurs. Fold each end leg into its own tooth, on the sheet that fixes its mouth. Positive depths and strict crossings exclude lip and cap hits. For same-bank teeth the floor contact is at (E₁+E₂)/2. The proof remains valid with other floor openings **if this abscissa is on solid smooth wall**. For opposite-bank teeth other openings on either bank do not matter.

For off-center marks replace u/2,v/2 in the rounding-error numerators by the covering radii of their image sets, respectively max(distance to either side of the tooth). Keep the strict doorway tolerances u/2,v/2 on the right. This gives a checkable sufficient condition. The radii can approach the full widths. The centerline result therefore does not prove the arbitrary-placement statement.

**An opposite-bank cap can replace a missing floor.** For the symmetric pair in the first section, suppose the midpoint m now lies at the center of a rectangular lower tooth symmetric about x=m. Apply the opposite-bank theorem from the left source to the **center of the distal lower cap**. The proof allows a terminal mark on that cap: side-wall contacts on the approach are strictly before the terminal height, and the center is smooth. Reflect the reversed path across x=m to continue there. The change of velocity at the join is exactly specular reflection at the cap; the path returns through the lower tooth, hall and right tooth. This holds at all three depths and widths (the two upper teeth are mirror partners), even if other teeth interrupt the former floor. A sloping incident leg is allowed. For this repair symmetry is needed on the traversed pieces only.

## A concrete obstruction with both banks open

The old two-travel proof chose inward launch signs and changed the winding about the hall by an integer k. For same-height upper marks the fraction from the source to the floor is exactly 1/2. For fixed signs **all** candidate floor contacts therefore lie in two folded positions: increasing the endpoint image by 2kA changes the floor abscissa by kA. Extra hall windings with those signs cannot escape if both positions are openings.

In DB55 take S=(4,301/100), T=(11,301/100). Both are in the upper teeth. The farther-side choices are left at S and left backward at T. With positive developed horizontal velocity the source image is −4 and the target image is 11+26k. The two folded floor abscissas are 7/2 and 19/2. Both are open mouths into the lower teeth. At k=0 and 1 the end-leg displacements are respectively 15/202 and 41/202, so both teeth themselves clear. Every further winding with these signs still lacks a reflecting floor at the required point. Change just the source sign: the images 4 and 11 now give displacement 7/202 in each end leg and floor contact x=15/2 on solid wall. This exhibits a genuine failure of the prescribed signs, a successful repair and no dark pair.

**Scope.** Arbitrary lengths are handled for all centered opposite-bank pairs in single-level teeth, for centered same-bank pairs over solid floor, and for the stated missing-floor cap repair. The eight-wall U theorem handles all real pairs in its own skeleton. For general nested stacks or arbitrary off-center marks in rooms with 14–20 walls there is no all-length conclusion here. The next missing lemma is whether one can choose both sets of endpoint images so that both mouths clear for arbitrary offsets; for same-bank pairs over a perforated floor the midpoint of those two crossings must also be usable, or the path must continue into a lower tooth.
