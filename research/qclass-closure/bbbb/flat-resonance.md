# A routing constraint on the two factors of a candidate dark flat

**Result for review.** A hypothetical dark product flat must satisfy a rational identity coupling horizontal band widths on its horizontal factor to vertical travel distances on its vertical factor. The identity comes from a route on the actual rectangle tree, with an excursion allowed when two doors are on the same side. On any one regular placement the coefficients in this identity have a computable bound in terms of physical clearance. They do not yet bound the height of the flat itself.

## A route with independently adjustable displacements

Take the maximal horizontal rectangles of a strictly simple room, with their door tree. Let the marks be distinct and interior to the physical room. For now allow an *independent* transverse displacement per visitable rectangle, measured per full traversal of the prescribed vertical depth. Fold modulo twice the rectangle width. This is an auxiliary placement; a physical shot will need one common slope.

Follow the unique tree path from source to target. For a mark on an open seam start the path at the corresponding point on the dual edge (equivalently subdivide that edge in the graph); its first physical band is the one on the way to the other mark. End similarly. Two points on one open door see each other. Prescribe a freely chosen abscissa inside every intermediate open door. At every designated event use the increasing horizontal sheet. In each path rectangle proceed as follows.

- Between opposite horizontal sides, use as displacement the exit abscissa minus the entry abscissa. For an interior endpoint use a partial traversal directly toward the next event. A chord inside a common rectangle settles the pair if its open segment is physically interior.
- If the two required events are on the same side, traverse the full band twice with the same displacement, half the difference of their abscissas. On the opposite side the intermediate abscissa is their average. When this point is on solid wall, reflect there. When it is on another door, take a vertical excursion there, reflect at the first physical wall, and retrace to that door. Assign zero transverse displacement to each rectangle of the excursion. This returns with the same horizontal sheet and reversed vertical sense. In this auxiliary calculation, zero is a **phase**; its physical realization may include many transverse reflections.
- Choose all free seam abscissas so every such average misses the finite set of vertical wall coordinates. Each required exclusion is a proper affine hyperplane as long as one abscissa in the average is free. The vertical excursion consequently avoids all corners and has a regular turnaround. It stays off the tree path: its door is on the other side of the rectangle, and deleting the rectangle disconnects that door's component from both required neighbors. Excursions from different path rectangles use disjoint off-path components.
- There is one fixed-average case to address. If the endpoints are on two different open doors on the same side of one rectangle, begin in the neighbor behind the source door. Traverse that neighbor and return to a *free nearby position on the source door*, by the same average construction, before crossing the original rectangle. Now both averages include the new free position. The tree separates the added excursion from the remainder. For endpoints on the same open door use the direct chord.

Thus there is a placement with at most two traversals per visited rectangle. Each used rectangle i has exactly one required increment βᵢ modulo one (normalized transverse displacement) and one positive vertical travel dᵢ per traversal, possibly partial at an endpoint. For the doubled traversal the two depths are equal. The direct common-rectangle cases need no construction. The selection of auxiliary positions can, for instance, use the first rational point in an open box outside the finitely many forbidden hyperplanes when the data are rational. No physical direction is tested.

## Replacing all the displacements by one slope

Write uᵢ for the band widths, ν for the number of traversals, U=max uᵢ, and D for the total vertical travel, counting multiplicity. The actual frequency for rectangle i is αᵢ=dᵢ/(2uᵢ). Let η>0 bound from below the transverse clearance at each prescribed boundary event from the ends of its chosen open door or smooth reflecting interval, and from the ends of the applicable run or runs. Include clearance from the vertical sides at each endpoint and at the terminal landing, shrinking η as necessary. In the direct cases dispense with the test. Put

    ε = min(1/8, η/(8νU)).

If the circle distance from sαᵢ to βᵢ is less than ε for every i at some arbitrarily large positive s, the pair is lit. Indeed choose representatives so each signed increment differs by <2Uε in physical units. While the prescribed sheets are maintained, these errors add, including over repeated visits; their total is <2νUε. This lies inside each event's open window, proving the required itinerary by induction. Extra transverse reflections between events occur on physical walls in the open height range of a rectangle. At the target level the actual abscissa on the chosen sheet has derivative D with respect to s: every leg contributes its positive travel and the signed exit and entry sheets agree across successive events. Correct the final error by a slope change of magnitude <2νUε/D. The additional error at every earlier event is <2νUε, so all the windows stay legal throughout the correction. This also handles a target on an open cut. The construction gives an exact endpoint, rather than just a sequence approaching it.

**Effective resonance test.** Let k be the number of used rectangles and choose any positive integer M with exp(−4Mε²)<(2M+1)^(−k). Unless there is a nonzero integer vector n with max|nᵢ|≤M and n·α=0, arbitrarily large such s exist. To see this, average the trigonometric polynomial ∏ᵢ cos^(2M)(π(sαᵢ−βᵢ)) over a long time interval. Its Fourier support has indices |nᵢ|≤M. Under the stated absence of a relation, only the constant coefficient survives the time average; it is [binom(2M,M)/4^M]^k≥(2M+1)^(−k). Outside the open ε-box the product is at most exp(−4Mε²), by sin(π ε)≥2ε and (1−z)^M≤exp(−Mz). This contradicts eventual avoidance of the box. All estimates are independent of the initial wait, and give the stated necessary resonance for darkness. Full rational independence is sufficient but stronger than necessary.

## Consequence on a product flat

Use any relatively open positive part of a product of linear spaces Vx×Vy in one order cell. This applies to the rational parts of the candidate dark flats in [the primitive-flat theorem](primitive-dark-flats.md), if they meet the positive cell. Choose one regular auxiliary placement as above and retain its combinatorics in a small relative neighborhood. Door positions can be chosen continuously there (for instance by their initial fractional positions in the doors); positive clearances persist and U stays bounded. A single positive ε and a single M work throughout a sufficiently small neighborhood. Here uᵢ is a linear form in the horizontal variables and dᵢ a linear form in the vertical variables; the excursion has only full band travels.

If that entire neighborhood is dark, it is covered by the finitely many zero sets

    Σᵢ nᵢ dᵢ(y) ∏_{j≠i} uⱼ(x) = 0,        0 < max|nᵢ| ≤ M.

A nonzero polynomial on a real vector space cannot vanish on an open subset. Taking the product of the finite list therefore shows that at least one of these relations holds **identically on the product space**. This implication can also be used to reject a supplied rational candidate by exact linear algebra and polynomial identities; no slope-orbit enumeration is needed.

Group the restricted width forms by proportionality on Vx, writing uᵢ=cᵢℓ_g within each class g. The reciprocals of distinct nonproportional linear forms on Vx are linearly independent over ℝ: multiply by their product and restrict to a generic point of the vanishing hyperplane of one form. If there is a single class the conclusion is immediate. Consequently each class in the identity separately satisfies Σ_{i in g}(nᵢ/cᵢ)dᵢ=0 on Vy. In particular **some two or more used rectangles must have proportional width forms and a linear dependence among the depth forms within that class**. On a rational product space the cᵢ can be taken rational. The transposed statement is equally necessary. The result is for a selected route, not a claim that quantifying over additional routes is finite. It extends the earlier nonproportional-width monotone-chain test to a tree with same-side turns and to resonance of the actual ratios dᵢ/uᵢ.

## What this says about effectivity

The test does not give the unconditional coefficient bound H. Its M uses the clearance of a placement on the candidate space. More fundamentally even small n bounds a **relation between the two factors**, not the coefficients of each factor. Algebraically, u₂/u₁=d₂/d₁=(N+1)/N makes the two frequencies identical for n=(1,−1), while the reduced rational ratio has arbitrarily large height and stays between one and two. This is an example about the inference from the identity, not a claim that such a room is dark. To turn these conditions into H requires either independent identities pinning down the proportionality constants or a geometric treatment of the surviving width classes. The separate primitive-gcd problem is handled once those factors are bounded.
