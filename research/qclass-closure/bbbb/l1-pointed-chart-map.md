# The pointed chart map

**Statement.** Work first over a full real order cell, before imposing integrality, mark parity or wall gcds. Label the corners and the two physical marks. For a fixed turn type with r reflexes, the pullback of total billiard darkness to prevertex and mark arguments is semialgebraic over real parameters on the physical Schwarz–Christoffel domain, in the sense of restriction of a semialgebraic test. The associated dark set in wall-and-mark coordinates is definable in the o-minimal structure used by d132. This applies to cells where some mark and wall coordinates coincide. It requires distinct *points* in the interior. For r=0 the room is a rectangle and the chord suffices. Assume r≥1 below.

## 1. A family over an explicit affine base

Put g=r+1, let I₊ index the r reflex vertices and I₋ the r+4 convex vertices. There are 2g+2 labelled prevertices. Start over ℂ with affine coordinates a₁,…,a₂g₊₂, the scale c, and four arguments ξ₁,…,ξ₄. Invert c and the discriminant of the combined list of a's and ξ's. Its real physical specialization will have arguments s, s̄, t, t̄. Write F(z)=∏(z−aᵢ), B(z)=∏ᵢ∈I₋(z−aᵢ). Adjoin four variables ηⱼ with ηⱼ²=F(ξⱼ). The resulting affine scheme S is finite étale and surjective over the chosen base: each equation is monic and 2ηⱼ is a unit. No global analytic choice of four square roots is required.

Over S glue v²=F(z) to V²=∏(1−aᵢu) by u=1/z and V=u^(g+1)v. The maps to the two standard affine charts of the relative projective line are finite locally free of rank two and glue. Their composite with the projective line projection is proper, flat and finitely presented. The relative hypersurface Jacobian criterion gives smoothness: either 2v is a unit or the root of F is simple. At infinity V=±1. Each geometric fiber is connected because F has simple roots and hence is not a square. The double cover branched simply at 2g+2 points has genus g. The sections (aᵢ,0) and (ξⱼ,±ηⱼ) are disjoint by construction. This proof works over the entire distinct-argument open base, with no compactness restriction on the parameters.

Define the relative differential ω=cv dz/B(z). Away from the branch sections and infinity it is a unit times dz. At a branch point, v is a relative parameter, and dz=2v dv/F′(z) locally. If the point is reflex, B is a unit and ω has order two. If it is convex, v²/B=F/B is a unit there, so ω is regular and nonzero. At infinity substitution gives −cV du/∏ᵢ∈I₋(1−aᵢu), a unit times du. Thus the relative zero divisor is exactly twice the sum of the reflex sections, including multiplicities in families.

## 2. Sixteen algebraic tests with the right labels

For each choice of one of the four source sections and one of the four target sections keep exactly those two sections and every corner section. Forget the six unused endpoint lifts. The resulting ordered family has r sections of order two and r+6 distinct regular sections. Its target is the **nonprojectivized** stratum H(2^r,0^(r+6)) with these labels; scaling c has not been quotiented out.

To specify the morphism, on the stack of smooth genus-g curves with these labelled sections take the Hodge bundle whose fiber is H⁰(C,Ω¹). Smooth proper base change for the Hodge bundle gives the pullback π_*Ω¹ on this family. A section of its total space is precisely a relative form on the family. Restriction of that form to the Cartier subscheme twice each reflex section is algebraic. Set all these restrictions to zero and remove the zero section. Fiber degree 2g−2=2r accounts for the whole divisor, so the incidence conditions here give the stated stratum on the distinct-section locus. The pointed family defines the stack map by the moduli functor and the displayed ω supplies its lift to the stratum. Compose with the morphism to coarse moduli. This is the downstairs stratum in d134: its objects and their isomorphisms are exactly smooth labelled curves carrying the nonzero relative form with the prescribed divisor. No universal family on the coarse space or lift of a coarse moduli point back to a family is needed. Let E denote the relatively closed algebraic locus supplied by d134 for a pair with no straight connection avoiding all forbidden corners. Its inverse image under each coarse map is closed relative to S. Intersect all sixteen inverse images to obtain Z. In particular Z is algebraic on the affine S, even if no equations for E have been computed. Taking complex coefficients to real and imaginary parts gives a semialgebraic test on the realification of S, with the inequalities defining its principal open retained. Changes of the η signs only permute the sixteen tests. One may therefore existentially eliminate the η's by real semialgebraic projection. Impose real ordered prevertices, upper half-plane source and target arguments, and conjugacy of the two lower arguments, all by real polynomial equalities and inequalities. This constructs the promised prevertex test.

## 3. Agreement with physical darkness

Choose the preimage of infinity at a smooth point of an edge by a real Möbius reparametrization of the half-plane. This allows every prevertex to be finite. On the upper half-plane the Schwarz–Christoffel derivative is c∏(z−aᵢ)^(εᵢ/2), where ε is +1 at a reflex and −1 at a convex corner. On a chosen sheet this is exactly ω/dz. Across an open edge, physical reflection changes the local Euclidean coordinate by a translation composed with conjugation or negative conjugation. For axial edges its squared differential therefore gives the doubled quadratic differential c²F(z)dz²/B(z)²: on real prevertex intervals the coefficient is real when the physical edges are horizontal or vertical. The sign ambiguity of its square root gives precisely the v cover above. Equivalently the two half-plane copies form the mirror double, and choosing a sign over each gives the four physical copies. Continuation is simply branched at every corner, as the local orders above verify. The source and target are the four sections over each conjugate pair of arguments. Having the same x-coordinate or y-coordinate in the room does not identify two arguments of a conformal bijection unless both coordinates coincide.

A legal shot unfolds to a straight segment between one of these sixteen pairs, and projection of a straight connection avoiding all corner sections yields a legal shot. A segment from an interior start cannot first meet a smooth seam tangentially: it would agree locally with that seam, and backward continuation would stay on the seam to a forbidden endpoint or the start. Artificial grid lines impose no stops. Forgetting unused endpoint lifts in each map avoids imposing artificial obstacles. Consequently the algebraic test on any physical parameter tuple has exactly the billiard meaning asserted.

Finally the wall and mark coordinates are obtained by integrating the upper-half-plane derivative along the segments from i to the prevertices and the two upper arguments, then subtracting the value at the anchor vertex. Choose upper-half-plane square roots factor by factor. Real and imaginary integrands, including the segment velocity, are jointly semialgebraic; use zero on a constant segment. Their only possible endpoint blowup has order −1/2. Extending by zero outside the parameter interval gives integrable fibers. Cluckers–Miller's stability under integration gives definability in ℝ_an,exp. Restrict the resulting boundary to the prescribed simple polygon with the labelled cyclic order and orientation. The primitive maps the boundary intervals monotonically onto the stated edges, including the smooth point at infinity; the argument principle on the disk gives degree one inside that Jordan curve and zero outside. Thus no inadmissible multiple cover contributes to the projection. Conversely Schwarz–Christoffel uniformization supplies such a tuple for each physical room and both interior marks. Projection of this definable graph intersected with Z gives exactly the dark set in the physical cell. The semialgebraicity claim was only for the prevertex test.
