### (b) An exact count in one state

There are 4n unit squares and 4n lower-left corner **incidences**, but only 4n−2r distinct lattice sites on X. Each reflex lift accounts for three incidences of the same site. Removing F leaves M=4n−4r−4 regular sites, including smooth wall lifts. Do not subtract the endpoint labels.

Write wᵢ,hᵢ for the positive integral circumferences and heights of the cylinders cut at F and L=Σwᵢ. The area identity is Σwᵢhᵢ=4n. The regular circles carry Σwᵢ(hᵢ−1)=4n−L lattice sites. There are E=3r+(r+4)=4r+4 oriented-right saddle connections: each outgoing right separatrix from F runs to the next forbidden point in this rational direction. Their lengths ℓⱼ are positive integers, and Σℓⱼ=L by counting the lower boundaries of the cylinders with multiplicity (each oriented saddle connection has one incident side above). Thus their open intervals carry L−E distinct regular sites. Including F itself gives L−2r sites on critical levels. The bounds E≤L≤4n follow. A corner count controls E; it does not determine L.

At n=22,r=8 there are 88 incidences, 72 distinct sites, 20 forbidden lifts and **52 regular sites**. Exactly 88−L are on regular circles and L−36 are inside critical intervals, with 36≤L≤88. These estimates alone give no positive lower bound on 88−L. Even positive circle occupancy need not put the two marked lifts on the same circle.

One can count *every* unordered distinct regular-site pair joined horizontally in that state, allowing either sign. The exact number is

    Σᵢ (hᵢ−1) binom(wᵢ,2) + Σⱼ binom(ℓⱼ−1,2).

This follows by partitioning the regular sites into disjoint circles and open intervals. The sum includes pairs of different lifts of the same physical point. Summing over states counts incidences for all pairs with multiplicity. To deduce lighting of a specified physical pair one needs a positive sum for its compatible lift pairs. The unmarked totals give no such lower bound. A horizontal shear (x,y)↦(x+ky,y) sends each horizontal leaf to a horizontal leaf and preserves the incidence verdict for transported marks. A returning power gives cylinder twists but never transfers a mark to a different level. Repeating shears within one horizontal direction therefore cannot supply the missing pair incidence. No inequality proving L1 at 22 follows from these counts.
