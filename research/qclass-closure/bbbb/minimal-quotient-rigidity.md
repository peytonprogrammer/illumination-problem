**Verdict for review.** A mark moving affinely on a nonradial d89 line passes the local period-rigidity test automatically. Incorporating d89/d115/d116 into the earlier d20 and d19–23 classification does narrow a hypothetical whole component: the map from the quadratic room double to its minimal quadratic quotient has degree one or two. ## The hypotheses at an irrational parameter

Assume the d89 counterexample reduction with its d80 inputs. Write X for the four-copy translation unfolding, Q=X/J for the quadratic sphere double of the physical room, and M for the conical GL⁺ orbit closure of X with optional regular labels forgotten. Use a smooth local branch. D116 shows that all irrational parameters of this marked line have the same marked closure, and hence the same unmarked closure. The ratio of two nonproportional rational affine horizontal wall lengths is irrational at any irrational parameter: a rational value would solve a nontrivial rational linear equation for the parameter. Twice the lengths give parallel absolute periods, so X is not a torus cover. The surface may still belong to an orbit closure defined over ℚ. ## Direct test of the proposed rigidity

On a smooth periodic-marking branch over M, the forgetful map between tangent spaces is an isomorphism. In homogeneous period coordinates, each extra relative period is therefore a fixed real-linear-coefficient, complex-linear functional of base period coordinates on this branch. Relative path choices and the functional can change by monodromy. On d89's rational branch the functionals may be taken rational on its projected tangent space. Periodicity means there is no independent motion of the point *at a fixed surface*.

There is an elementary interpolation test that makes the distinction decisive. Use two signed doubled wall periods 2(e₁+t u₁), 2(e₂+t u₂) with e₁u₂−e₂u₁≠0 and a nonzero vertical period 2ib. All coefficients are rational. For **any** proposed mark displacement a+vt+ic with a,v,c rational, solve

    2(e₁ x+e₂ y)=a,     2(u₁ x+u₂ y)=v,     2b z=c.

The solution is rational. Thus the proposed displacement is x times the first period plus y times the second plus z times the third, identically in t. This works for any number of marks and their signed copies. Merely fitting a fixed rational functional to the line cannot reject even one such motion. Existence of the actual marking on the whole invariant locus, including its monodromy, is stronger; on the hypothetical d89 branch it is already part of the hypothesis. EFW finiteness bounds the number of periodic markings of a fixed locus. It permits a finite collection of moving local sections. For a geometric example, the half-turn of the named four-reflex cross picks out its center, whose horizontal displacement from the left end is 7+t. This is one periodic interior point, without a second dark point. [Cross and proof of periodicity](generic-line.md)

## Use the full minimal quotient instead

Here “minimal” concerns translation/half-translation coverings, independently of the action of the sheet permutations of a lattice room. By AW Lemma 3.3 the composite X→Y factors through Q. Write f:Q→Y, k=deg f. The quotient Y is a sphere by Riemann–Hurwitz. For a point of local degree e over order a the order upstairs is e(a+2)−2, necessarily −1,0 or 1 in Q. It follows that:

- Every zero downstairs is simple and has k distinct unramified zero preimages. Above a regular point there is no branching.
- Above a pole, e=1,2,3 gives respectively a pole, regular point, or zero upstairs. Writing z for the number of zeros in Y and c_i for the number of points with local degree i≥2, we get r=kz+c₃ and c₂+2c₃=2k−2.
- z≥1: the orientation cover of a four-pole sphere is a torus, and X would cover it if z=0.

Use the *downstairs* unmarked orbit closure in Q(1^z,−1^(z+4)). Its rank is h. Indeed all branch values are already marked singularities, so for a fixed labeled topological covering there are finitely many choices over a downstairs surface. Local deformations pull back periods, and pullback is injective on absolute cohomology by transfer. Equivalently use the corresponding finite Hurwitz incidence and the orientation covers; forgetting or adjoining the cover data adds no tangent directions. Both quadratic sphere loci have zero relative tangent kernel. In the same incidence an upstairs periodic marking over a regular downstairs point gives a periodic marking downstairs: the covering and the marked preimage are finite choices for the given downstairs data and dimensions agree. In degree two the downstairs tangent is the inverse image of the rational upstairs tangent under the injective pullback on periods. Pullback acts on integral cycles, so these equations downstairs are defined over ℚ. This is the descent in d20 §5, applied on the dense orbit and its smooth covering branch. [D20, local types and periodic descent](../../illumination-generalization-2026-11-06/fable/directions-2026-11-09/d20-periodic/periodic.md)

If z≤2, Apisa's classification and h≥2 force the downstairs locus to be a full cover locus over a genus-zero stratum. Minimality of Y rules out a further cover of degree greater than one, so the downstairs stratum is full. For its orientation-double locus AW Theorem 1.4 applies: z≥1 avoids its three exceptions, and the sphere with its generic configuration of 2z+4≥6 singularities admits no nonidentity Möbius symmetry. Regular M-periodic endpoints in Q must therefore map to **poles of Y**, with e=2. A regular image of Y would lift to a nonfixed periodic point of its full orientation-double locus, and an image at a zero would require an upstairs zero. The marked pair now consists of two of the discrete ramification labels over one or two poles. Over the full base the connected marked covering branch is the full family of that topological type: there are only finitely many covering and label choices, so the closure has full base dimension, locally open on the branch and closed in the stratum. This is precisely the P1/P2 case of the d19–23 full-family exclusion for physical dark pairs. For k≤2 the four distinct regular lifts of the marks would all have to ramify over poles, whereas c₂≤2k−2≤2. For k≥3 the irreducible orbit closure fills one connected Hurwitz component, the braid orbit used by d22. The d22 census gives a lit member in every other family (d121 checked the family grouping, including family_check_small). Across all 300 members of the three remaining A₄, A₅ and S₅ families, c₂=2; four distinct periodic lifts require c₂≥4. Thus the count suffices. D121 confirmed enumeration completeness and replayed 20 saved slices. Independent verification of the individual LIT witnesses is the outstanding project check H1. This excludes an entirely dark family; special members require their own argument. Consequently z≥3. Since r≤8, k≥3 is impossible. The surviving possibilities are:

| k | Minimal quadratic sphere | Necessary periodic marking and rank |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Q itself, z=r≥3 | Both distinct interior marks give regular non-J-fixed periodic lifts. The primitive downstairs locus is proper, defined over ℚ, with 2≤h<(r+3)/2. |
| 2 | z=3, r=6 | A proper primitive downstairs locus of rank 2 with a regular periodic lift away from its orientation involution's fixed set. |
| 2 | z=4, r=8 | The same requirement, of rank 2 or 3. |

For k=2 there is no e=3, hence r=2z, and exactly two points ramify. The two distinct interior physical marks give four distinct regular points on Q, of which at most two can lie over poles. At least one regular downstairs point must therefore have a periodic lift not fixed by its orientation involution. The downstairs upper rank bound follows by applying the unmarked high-rank theorem to its orientation cover of genus z+1. At high rank the stratum-component possibility fails by dimension (2z+2<3z+1). In a full holonomy-double locus its negating involution must be the given one on the minimal translation surface: the proof of Apisa–Wright Lemma 3.3 gives uniqueness of the involution negating the form on the minimal translation surface. [Apisa–Wright, proof of Lemma 3.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1708.03411) Thus the downstairs quadratic stratum would be full and its regular nonfixed periodic marking impossible by AW 1.4. The same argument gives the first row's properness. This table sharpens the target for a *wholly dark d89 line*. The degree split for general higher-rank loci already appears in d20; d89/d116 supply the higher-rank periodic markings needed to apply it here.
