Advances: L1 — A rectangular pocket gives an explicit return map. Its use for illumination depends on matching the incoming and outgoing corridor legs.

Use coordinates (0,w) × (0,h) in the pocket, with the mouth on y=0, a solid roof, and solid sides. Start at a regular mouth point a, directed upward and right, with c=|dx/dy|>0. Unfold the side walls. At the roof the horizontal coordinate is a+hc; at the return it is a+2hc. On the phase circle of length 2w, the latter coordinate specifies both the folded position and the horizontal sign. Thus the pocket translates the oriented phase by 2hc modulo 2w and reverses the vertical velocity. Exclude a+hc in wℤ (roof corner), a+2hc in wℤ (mouth endpoint), and any return outside the actual open gate. For a leftward start use incoming phase −a instead. The proof is straight travel in the reflected strip; intermediate side hits away from endpoints are regular.

### Matching a source and target

Suppose the pocket mouth is (0,w) above a clear horizontal band of height g. Put the source and target on the lower edge of that band at local abscissas U and V, aiming right on approach and departure. The band edge need only contain the marks as interior points of the room; the open approach and departure legs must lie in the room. Coordinates may also be images obtained by unfolding a known smooth outer wall. Let M=U+V. For entry a and return b the two straight band legs impose c=(a−U)/g and b=V−gc=M−a. The full-mouth criterion for this itinerary is therefore

    max(0,M−w,U) < a < min(w,M),
    (2+2h/g)a ≡ M+(2h/g)U   (mod 2w).

Indeed these conditions put entry and return strictly inside the aperture, fix positive speed ratio c, and give a+2hc ≡ b with rightward exit. Also 2(a+hc) ≡ a+b=M modulo 2w. Since 0<M<2w, roof clearance is automatic. Conversely a legal path with these legs and this single roof excursion satisfies both conditions. For a partial mouth additionally require a and M−a to lie in the actual opening. For a full mouth, solutions to the congruence are spaced by wg/(g+h); a strict interval longer than this spacing guarantees a solution. An explicit choice is the first progression value strictly above the lower endpoint, obtained by one floor operation. This is a condition on pocket dimensions, corridor distances **and mark offsets**. A nonzero shift alone does not guarantee matching. This itinerary can reverse horizontal velocity inside the pocket, so a midpoint obstruction derived for a monotone transit does not apply.

### Unequal corridors: all interior lattice pairs

Take three 3×3 chambers starting at 0,D₁,D₁+D₂ with unit-height corridors at 1<y<2. Here Dᵢ are integral start-to-start distances, so the actual corridor lengths are Dᵢ−3. The proof covers all Dᵢ≥4 independently, including equality, without any congruence assumption. Each mark has horizontal offset 1 or 2 and height 1 or 2. Same-chamber pairs connect directly. Different-row pairs connect by an open segment in the common band. Reflect vertically to put the remaining pairs at height 1.

**Outer chambers.** Put P=D₁+D₂, Δ=D₂−D₁; let u,v be the two offsets. First try a bounce at height 2 with a smooth outer vertical reflection. Reflect the source to −u across x=0. The bounce midpoint relative to the middle chamber's left face is (Δ+v−u)/2. If negative, the bounce lies in the first corridor. Alternatively reflect the target across x=P+3; the midpoint relative to the middle chamber is 3+(Δ+u−v)/2. If this exceeds 3, the bounce lies in the second corridor. In the first case the absolute midpoint (P+v−u)/2 exceeds 3; in the second the midpoint (P+6+u−v)/2 is less than P, since P≥8. Thus neither hits a corridor endpoint. All open legs stay strictly in the band except for the smooth bounce at its top, and the reflection at the outer vertical wall is regular.

If neither strict test holds, then u−v ≤ Δ ≤ v−u. Equal offsets give Δ=0. Otherwise (u,v)=(1,2) and Δ is −1, 0 or 1. Use the pocket of the middle chamber with w=3 and h=g=1, U=u−D₁ and V=D₂+v. In every residual case M=Δ+u+v is 2, 3 or 4. Since U<0, the entry interval has length min(M,6−M)≥2, greater than the progression spacing 3/2. The lemma gives a regular rightward return and the second band leg reaches the target. Both legs stay strictly in the open unit band between their endpoints. This handles the mod-3 obstruction even when the two distances differ.

**Adjacent chambers.** Write D for their spacing. The ordinary opposite-edge bounce is at local abscissa (D+u+v)/2. It lies strictly in the corridor precisely when D>max(6−u−v,u+v). For D≥4 the only exceptions are D=4 and u=v. Reverse the whole room and the path if necessary so that the pair occupies the first two chambers. Unfold the source at the left outer wall and use the upper pocket of the first chamber, with U=−u, V=4+u. Then M=4 and the entry interval is (1,3), again longer than 3/2. The incoming leg reflects regularly at x=0 while still in the band; the outgoing leg reaches the second chamber. This completes the unequal-length theorem, including corridors of length one.

### What the corner budget guarantees

A local pocket and a pocket usable by specified marks are different assertions. Every strictly simple orthogonal room has a local rectangular return region: take a topmost horizontal wall and a strip directly below it, stopping strictly before the next distinct wall height. Its two side walls and roof are solid and its base is interior apart from its endpoints. This guarantees a local return map at any wall count, but supplies no through corridor or accessible band legs.

A sufficient geometric class for the full-mouth test consists of rooms with a rectangular protrusion above a through band, a solid roof and two solid flanks, an unobstructed base, and source and target legs in that band satisfying the criterion. In the aligned three-chamber layout each strictly inset corridor contributes four distinct reflex shoulders (two at each end). Two such corridors exhaust the eight reflex corners allowed at W≤20; a third disjoint corridor of this type is excluded. This is an upper bound on how many such necks can occur. It does not force two necks, alignment, or phase matching. Even a rectangle has no two reflex shoulders flanking a pocket above a through band. The usable-pocket conclusion is thus established for the stated geometric class, with no general guarantee for every W≤20 room or every mark pair.

**R22 control.** D154 reports that every physical odd–odd lattice point of the closed dark base room is a corner. This excludes an integral, full-mouth pocket of width at least 3 and height at least 1 of the kind just used. Its horizontal span would contain an odd integer strictly between its ends, and its closed vertical span an odd integer. Their intersection would be an interior point, an interior mouth point, or a smooth point of the solid roof. Each contradicts that clean parity fiber. This argument does not exclude narrower or partial pockets. At arm length two the reviewed controls lose that fiber and are lit; the base-room exclusion cannot be transferred to them by wall count. **First open case for general L1.** No unequal-length aligned 3×3 instance remains. A next geometric case is a pair across two necks with vertically displaced apertures, lacking the common unit band used for both external legs. The eight-reflex budget permits moving an aperture along a taller rectangular chamber wall while keeping both its shoulders. It does not supply a single speed and two clear legs for the specified marks. The pocket formula itself remains valid there; applicability to all pairs is open.
