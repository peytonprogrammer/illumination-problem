# M3: remove one orientation of dents with at most three portals

The d163 ledger lists 3,544 OPEN turn patterns with dents in both orientations, including 2,672 at W=20. The following geometric reduction works for every length assignment, even when nonadjacent walls are collinear. It reduces the number of necessary interfaces; it gives no COVERED designation. [Ledger](../../illumination-generalization-2026-11-06/fable/directions-2026-11-09/d163-pattern-ledger/ledger.md)

**Cut lemma.** A dent is a maximal wall whose two endpoints are reflex. If there are h horizontal and v vertical dents, then at most min(h,v) parallel interior cuts split the room into pieces each having connected sections parallel to the cuts. The cuts can be made along lattice lines in lattice rooms. A cut is an artificial transparent portal, not a new physical mirror.

Choose the cheaper orientation, say horizontal. At one endpoint P of a horizontal dent extend its supporting line away from the wall into the room until the first boundary point Q. The open segment is interior: locally at P an extension away from the incident horizontal edge points strictly inside the 270-degree sector. Boundedness gives Q. This crosscut of the simple polygonal disk gives two simple polygonal disks. At P it splits a reflex angle into a right angle and a straight angle. At Q on a smooth wall it creates two convex angles. If Q is a vertex, an axial arrival through the interior can meet only a reflex vertex (at a convex corner an axial approach would already run along a wall). At such a Q the angles are again 90 and 180 degrees.

Here is why the horizontal dent count drops, including a same-height tie. No unchanged horizontal edge becomes a dent. Count the seams on the two new boundaries after suppressing straight angles. For a smooth Q, each new seam side has a convex end at Q and cannot make a new dent. For a reflex Q, if the two straight continuations occur on opposite copies of the seam, each combined edge still has a convex end. If they occur on the same copy, the seam joins the old horizontal edges incident at P and Q. Their concatenation can be a dent only if **both** old edges were dents; two old dents then become at most one. In all cases the selected dent disappears and the total decreases by at least one. No vertical edges are concatenated, since every new straight continuation is horizontal. Repeat on the pieces. In applying the construction iteratively an old portal may be subdivided at an endpoint of a later cut; the number of distinct cutting operations still bounds the number of pieces by one plus that number.

**Why the slices become connected.** Sweep a horizontal line through a simple piece with no horizontal dent. Use open bands between consecutive distinct vertex levels. Within each band every component is one interval. At any intervening level, if an interval immediately above overlaps two intervals immediately below in open passages, a gap between two consecutive such lower intervals closes there. Its finite horizontal base at that level has reflex ends: immediately above the gap is room, and immediately below its two lips are room. That base is a horizontal dent. The converse sweep gives the same contradiction for a split. One can use overlapping open abscissa intervals here because vertical sides are fixed throughout each adjacent open band. Distinct incident horizontal edges at one height have disjoint relative interiors. A vertex-only connection cannot connect the interior of the simple room. Consequently in the band-component adjacency graph each interval has at most one neighbor above and one below. The graph is connected by interior path connectedness, and therefore has at most one interval per height band. At an event height the interior section is the intersection of the two neighboring open runs: an open neighborhood must extend both above and below, and an abscissa interior to both has such a neighborhood. This section too is an interval or empty.

**Wall budget.** With r reflex vertices, the total number of adjacent RR pairs in the cyclic boundary is r minus the number of reflex runs for r>0, hence h+v≤r−1. At r≤8 the cheaper orientation costs at most three cuts, giving at most four pieces. At W=20 the ledger's reported profiles give sharper bounds: 1,932 of the 2,672 OPEN two-direction patterns require at most one cut by this rule, 721 more at most two, and the remaining 19 at most three. These are sums of existing profile counts, not a new enumeration. They measure geometric decomposition only.

**What must transfer.** Even the one-cut case needs more than full illumination of the two sealed pieces. At a fixed slope magnitude in a nonaxial direction, a portal state consists of the physical crossing position and the two velocity signs. In either piece propagate to the first of a target hit, a forbidden corner, or a portal encounter; reflect only on original smooth walls. At a portal use the other incident piece and continue with the same physical velocity. Include a branch that never produces another event. An unfolded shot is exactly a finite legal concatenation of these first-event legs ending at the target. Tied cut endpoints are treated by their physical germs, as in d159/d160; a run along an artificial seam ignores the seam until departure. An all-length portal theorem would have to guarantee such a finite concatenation for some *one* velocity slope. Using a different slope on each piece or replacing the portal by a mirror loses this equivalence. There is no first-shot or slope-selection proof here, including for the one-cut group.

**Named control.** In the d163 room LLLLRLLLLRRR below, the horizontal dent (1,4)–(3,4) and vertical dent (3,4)–(3,2) share an endpoint. The cut (1,4)–(0,4) removes a unit-square ear below the cut and leaves a horizontally section-connected main piece. In the sealed main piece the path (1/4,17/4) → (1/2,4) → (3/4,17/4) is a legal mirror shot. In the original the middle point is interior and the same launch enters the ear. This is an exact failure to transport a witness, not a dark pair (the displayed endpoints see one another directly). The smallest representative already exhibits the remaining interface issue.

**Decision.** The 3,544-pattern group has been reduced to illumination with at most three transparent cuts between one-direction-convex pieces, not to finitely many named metric rooms. Lengths and portal states still vary. The largest concrete next subproblem at 20 walls is the single-cut case. Neither these counts nor a lit ledger representative warrants marking a pattern COVERED.

## What is the smallest W ≤ 20 room this result doesn't cover?

Across the full ledger, a smallest OPEN example is W=10, pattern LLLRLLLRLR: (2,0), (4,0), (4,1), (3,1), (3,4), (0,4), (0,3), (1,3), (1,2), (2,2). Within the chosen two-direction OPEN group the smallest wall count is 12. D163's LLLLRLLLLRRR has vertices (2,2), (2,0), (5,0), (5,1), (4,1), (4,5), (0,5), (0,3), (1,3), (1,4), (3,4), (3,2). The missing conclusion concerns all lengths of each turn word; these coordinates identify the ledger representatives.
