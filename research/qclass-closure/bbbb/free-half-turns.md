# Free half-turns on a mixed room unfolding

**Theorem for review.** Let P be a bounded strictly simple polygon with genuine turns, using both axial and diagonal walls at multiples of π/4. Let X be its eight-copy translation unfolding. Its fixed-point-free affine involutions of derivative −I correspond exactly to reflections preserving P whose linear parts are signed permutation matrices and whose axes meet the boundary only at vertices. For given distinct physical interior points S,T, at most one such involution sends any lift of S to any lift of T. This concerns the full physical unfolding; arbitrary intermediate covers of mixed unfoldings are a separate question.

### Descent to the polygon

Let R be the copy rotation of derivative +π/2 and τ=R². The quotient Z=X/⟨R⟩ is the conformal sphere double of P; its common boundary is a circle in a Schwarz–Christoffel half-plane parameter. The intermediate curve Y=X/⟨τ⟩ double covers Z with deck involution r induced by R. All branch values of X→Z are the distinct corner prevertices and conversely: at angle aπ/4 the cyclic ramification index is 4/gcd(4,a)>1. Every true zero has even order and τ fixes each corner lift. [Reviewed covering description](parametric-orbits.md)

Suppose η is free with derivative −I. If X is a torus such a map z↦b−z has fixed points, so assume genus at least two. Pick a true zero. Both τ and ητη⁻¹ fix it, because η permutes the zeros and τ fixes every zero. Two −I involutions fixing the same point agree. Indeed in a coordinate u with centered flat primitive u^(k+1), each sends u to λu with λ^(k+1)=−1 and λ²=1, hence λ=−1. Equality of germs implies equality on X. Thus η commutes with τ. The nonidentity translation δ=τη is an involution and is also free: at a fixed point the corresponding λ would satisfy λ²=λ^(k+1)=1 with k even, hence λ=1.

The common map b induced by η and δ on Y is a **free involution**. Over a fixed singleton fiber of X→Y, η would have a fixed point. On a two-point fiber a fixed y would require both η and δ to swap its points, although their product τ also swaps them. This is impossible. The identity on Y is excluded by the same argument.

Consequently Y is not a sphere. If its genus is at least two, r is its unique hyperelliptic involution (its quotient is Z); hence b commutes with r. If its genus is one, a free holomorphic involution is translation by a nonzero two-torsion point, since an elliptic automorphism with nonidentity linear part has a fixed point. Such a translation commutes with r(z)=c−z. In either case ηRη⁻¹ equals R or τR upstairs since both candidates lift r through X→Y. Their derivatives differ; conjugation by −I leaves DR unchanged. Thus η commutes with R. [Hyperelliptic uniqueness, §2](https://msp.org/obs/2013/1-1/obs-v1-n1-p23-s.pdf)

The descended map on Z is holomorphic, hence Möbius. It permutes the branch values of X→Z, at least three distinct points on the boundary circle. Three determine the circle, so it preserves the circle and either preserves or swaps the two open halves. Mapping the image half back to P by the physical copy projection gives a Euclidean isometry σ of P: on a connected open half its derivative in physical coordinates is the same orthogonal matrix, obtained from −I by the two fixed sheet orientations. The induced physical map is the same on both halves, since a Möbius map preserving a circle commutes with reflection in that circle. In particular σ²=1 and its linear part L lies in D₄. This descent uses the marked corner geometry of an actual room. A general surface with an affine half-turn need not have it.

### Which room isometries give a free lift?

In physical copy coordinates (p,g), g∈D₄, the only possible lift of σ with derivative −I is

    (p,g) ↦ (σp, −g L⁻¹).

Indeed an interior target sheet g' must satisfy g'L=−g. Conversely the formula respects every wall gluing since σ conjugates incident wall reflections to the corresponding target reflections. For an involutive σ it squares to the identity. At a fixed physical point p a sheet is fixed precisely if −L⁻¹ belongs to the subgroup generated by the linear reflections in the walls incident to p. That subgroup is trivial at interior points.

If σ is the identity this gives τ, which fixes corners. If σ is a nonidentity orientation-preserving involution it is central symmetry. Its center is in the interior: the closed Jordan polygon is a disk so the symmetry has a fixed point by Brouwer, necessarily its center; a boundary point cannot have its single local interior sector preserved by rotation by π. Here L=−I and every lift of the center is fixed.

It remains to take σ a reflection. Its restriction to the Jordan boundary reverses orientation and fixes exactly two boundary points. For instance the local interior side of the boundary orientation is reversed by a planar reflection, and a circle involution reversing orientation has exactly two fixed points. If one fixed point lies in an open edge, its wall is perpendicular to the axis (a boundary edge on the axis would exchange inside and outside). The wall reflection there has linear part −L, giving fixed lifts. If both are vertices, the incident wall rays at each fixed vertex are exchanged. The axis is a D₄ mirror and the two distinct incident wall lines are perpendicular: the remaining allowed directions off the mirror and its normal form the pair at ±π/4 to it. Their reflections generate a four-element subgroup containing neither the axis reflection nor the perpendicular-axis reflection −L. There are no fixed lifts there or over interior axis points. Thus the lift is free, proving both directions of the classification. As a separate necessary check, a free involution on Y requires odd genus(Y) by Riemann–Hurwitz.

### Effect on the complete-orbit conjecture

Any reflection sending S to T for distinct points is reflection in their perpendicular bisector. The formula above leaves exactly one possible lift of derivative −I for that reflection. Meanwhile any −I involution with a nonempty fixed set confined to physical corner lifts must agree with τ by the common-fixed-point argument, and τ never changes the physical point. Hence for a *fixed source lift*, at most one of eight target lifts can be accounted for by **any** −I involution all of whose fixed points are forbidden, even if different involutions are allowed for different pairs. This rules out that direct midpoint mechanism on the mixed unfolding at every wall count. It does not prove the seven other connections exist. The theorem for orthogonal translation quotients in [darkness-symmetry.md](darkness-symmetry.md) has different hypotheses; the present argument does not assert descent of τ through arbitrary mixed quotients.

**Named six-wall check.** In d138's convex hexagon with vertices (0,0),(2,0),(3,1),(2,2),(0,2),(−1,1), the reflection across y=1 fixes the two tips (−1,1),(3,1). Both are vertices with perpendicular diagonal walls. The criterion gives a free −I lift. Take S=(1,1/2), T=(1,3/2). Their physical vertical chord lies entirely in the interior. Its endpoints on the same sheet are not the matched partners of the free involution (which changes the sheet). The pair is rational; doubling the whole marked configuration makes it same-parity integral without retaining scalar primitivity. The example demonstrates existence of the symmetry and physical lighting simultaneously; it is not a no-common-cylinder example. No search or ray enumeration was run.
