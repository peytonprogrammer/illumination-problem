Advances: L1 — short relations do not propagate to every visit of a blocked itinerary. A six-reflex gate box can have an arbitrarily long middle visit absent from every flatness-short relation. Repeated excursions retain the same distinct-end obstruction. Global total resonance for a genuinely dark pair remains unproved.

**Quantitative consequence of flatness.** Write cⱼ=nⱼ/(2wⱼ), dᵢ=|Jᵢ|, F=Flt(g). The relation is Σhⱼcⱼ=0. A short vector has Σdᵢ|cᵢ−cᵢ₋₁|≤F. Since its weighted mean is zero, its range contains 0 and |nⱼ|≤2wⱼ F/minᵢ dᵢ. More precisely the difference between any two c's is at most F divided by the narrowest intervening gate. For a two-term relation this yields μⱼ/μₖ=|nₖ/nⱼ|≤2wₖ F/min dᵢ, with the reverse bound on exchanging indices. For general support one has only μⱼ≤Σₖ≠ⱼ(2wₖ F/min dᵢ)μₖ when nⱼ≠0. Even bounded coefficients in μ₁+μ₂=μ₃ give no bound on μ₂/μ₁. No bound follows for an omitted visit.

**Failure of the proposed decoupling step.** Use the monotone six-reflex itinerary in the tests, in units t=a=1. Take any integer B>40Flt(2). The moduli are (1/16,B/20,1/16). The unit-width gates imply |n₀|,|n₂|≤16F. The relation reads 5(n₀+n₂)+4Bn₁=0. Consequently n₁=0 for every short relation. Nevertheless the box misses for every B, by the distinct-end congruence in the tests. The middle is already a free rectangle. Independent entrance windows at the two ends do not supply a common slope **for this itinerary**. The long-corridor theorem varies the end itineraries, precisely what the inference about every visit to this itinerary cannot do. This refutes the itinerary-level implication, not the conditional statement with actual pair darkness as antecedent. This family is lit for large B. **Persistence under excursions.** Insert m full roof–floor excursions in that middle slab while retaining the first and last unreflected axial end legs. The two end travels are still equal. Pad (1,0,−1) by zeros at every middle visit. It is dual for all m, with nonzero gate coefficients only at the first and last transmissions. Their total projected width is 1/8, and their coset misses the box by the very same residue computation. This persists even if one grants every intermediate turn test. The thin direction comes from two *different* end rectangles of equal width and travel with displaced mouths. More generally, for equal end widths w and travels, endpoint residues ±S,±T in appropriately translated coordinates must match the signed sum of the gate abscissas modulo 2w. Narrow openings and displaced residues can miss regardless of the intervening travel. The repeated-slab relations do not explain this projection: their end entries vanish, whereas this blocker's end entries are nonzero. Excursion count changes the ambient box dimension, so a covolume decay for a fixed-dimensional projection would in any event give no lower bound on clearance in this surviving direction.

Only one exposed upper floor is needed to reject the backwards approach for every m. Its endpoints are one reflex and one convex corner; the complete room has six reflexes. Charging a different reflex for each excursion, or assuming only same-slab repeat relations can persist, fails here. No exhaustive list of universal wall configurations could restore that implication while ignoring these distinct-end locks.

**First open implication.** To obtain total resonance for dark pairs one must use the failure of *other* end itineraries to constrain the omitted visit on the original itinerary, or derive a different bounded set of charts sufficient for L1. Flatness supplies one relation per empty oriented box and gives neither implication. R22 passes the short-itinerary checks. No 8-versus-9 count, global refutation by a dark pair, or census authorization follows.
