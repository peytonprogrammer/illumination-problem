Advances: L1 — an area induction for insertion descent. The geometric cutoff is explicit. The uniform choice of a band that works at its **actual** height remains open.

## A long band and a legal smaller parent

Let R be a strictly simple orthogonal lattice room with r≤8 reflex vertices; merge collinear edges. This argument applies to orthogonal rooms only. Rooms with 45-degree walls require a separate reduction. There are r+4 convex vertices, hence W=2r+4 walls and r+2≤10 walls in each orientation. Sort the distinct y-coordinates of the horizontal walls, including the extrema. The open slabs between consecutive levels are the horizontal bands. There are at most r+1≤9. At any height in a given band the section is the same disjoint union of open intervals with integral endpoints: vertical boundary edges can neither start nor stop there. The same statements hold after swapping x and y.

For **any integer h₀≥2**, if every band on both axes has thickness at most h₀−1 then both box spans are at most (r+1)(h₀−1). Thus

    area > A₀ = 81(h₀−1)²  ⇒  some band has thickness h ≥ h₀.

In particular take **h₀=2, A₀=81**. More quantitatively the largest thickness is at least ceil(√area/(r+1)). This is an area bound from box containment, with no assertion of sharpness. A section with k intervals uses 2k different vertical edges, so 1≤k≤⌊(r+2)/2⌋≤5. A single interval can lead to a terminal cap or a bridge between irregular pieces. Multiple intervals are separate corridors at that level; their connections outside the band are unrestricted.

For a horizontal band of height h≥2 and widths w₁,…,wₖ, compress its entire height to one, translating the upper piece down by E=h−1. The piecewise-linear increasing change of the y-coordinate is a homeomorphism of the plane. The parent is strictly simple, has the same turns, keeps integral vertices, and loses exactly E Σwᵢ units of area. This gives the strictly decreasing induction parameter, independently of which band is chosen. Work in all lattice rooms, without imposing a new coprimality normalization on the parent.

Under reinsertion the retained marks are the marks on or below the bottom interface and on or above the translated top interface. Positivity of all band heights preserves their incidence. There are exactly E Σ(wᵢ−1) other marks, on the E intermediate integer levels. Pairs of them in one component have a direct segment. Two new marks in **different** components need their own proof, as do a retained and a new mark. A rectangle spanning the gap between components need not be in the room.

## Follow-on proof: simultaneous parallel insertion for retained pairs

This is the retained-pair part of the multiple-interval lemma, the first extension needed beyond d210's single-interval statement. It specializes the common-period idea in d184. The proof below covers a path that visits several components repeatedly, with no hypothesis on their ordering. It supplies no certificate for a mark created by the insertion. [d184 review](../../illumination-generalization-2026-11-06/fable/directions-2026-11-09/d184-review-gaphyper/review.md) · [d210 review](../../illumination-generalization-2026-11-06/fable/directions-2026-11-09/d210-review-reaim/review.md)

Take a parent seed between retained marks. Axis-parallel seeds carry over directly, as do seeds missing the band. Otherwise write s₀=u/v>0 for its reduced transverse-to-axial speed ratio; use signs from the seed. Let D be total axial travel, m>0 its number of full unit-band traversals, b(t) the number completed at unfolded integer level t, and

    Q = lcm{wᵢ : the seed traverses component i},
    M = max over integer 0≤t≤D of |t − Db(t)/m|.

The endpoints are outside the open compressed band and a traversal has no axial reversal in its interior. Its transverse transfer is translation on a circle of length 2wᵢ, in signed coordinates relative to that interval's own side. On **each** traversal subtract 2QN, with the same integer N. This is a whole number of periods on every visited circle, even when their widths or offsets differ. Set

    s=(Ds₀+2QmN)/(D+mE),       δ=s−s₀.
    e(t)=[t−Db(t)/m]δ.

By [d210, Claim 2](../../illumination-generalization-2026-11-06/fable/directions-2026-11-09/d210-review-reaim/review.md), an ordinary unit step advances on the same doubled interval in the parent and child. The transverse error increment there is δ. On a selected step it is (1+E)s−s₀−2QN=(1−D/m)δ. At t=0 the error vanishes. If the base chart agrees through t−1, this increment puts the next developed coordinate at X₀(t)+e(t). Provided it stays between the nearest strictly lower and strictly higher integers about X₀(t), the next gate and component persist in the unfolded chart. At an integral regular base point both sides are in the same regular local chart: strict simplicity rules out a diagonal pinch. Within the step the only available reflections are on that component's straight sides. This proves the assertion inductively, so the period correction for the next visit is indeed valid there. At D the error vanishes.

In particular, rounding Es₀/(2Q) to the nearest integer gives a positive s and |δ|≤Q/(E+D/m). Thus **every integer E>QvM−D/m** works. At smaller E one can use the signed gate inequalities just given for any N with s>0. Call a seed passing these inequalities a passing seed; include the direct carry-over cases. This is a sufficient arithmetic test on a parent path. With transverse box span B, Q≤B⁵, since at most five widths occur. With a first-hit seed, D≤(4A−4r−4)v for A=area(parent): if I and B are its interior and boundary lattice-point counts, the regular-state count is 4I+2(B−W)=4A−4r−4 by Pick’s theorem. The common-period factor is therefore polynomial in the box, but the proof still needs a seed and sufficient clearance at the proposed E.

## Exactly what would finish the induction

The following are **sufficient conditional implications** with numerical census limits. The selection statements quantify over parent seeds and check explicit inequalities. Existence of a path in the larger room does not by itself establish those inequalities.

**Room-level version: parallel retained transfer + band selection + collars + census through A₁=81 imply L1.**

1. **Parallel retained transfer.** Reviewed in d212, including alternating components; d210 covers k=1. The lcm permits one correction on every component. [d212 review](../../illumination-generalization-2026-11-06/fable/directions-2026-11-09/d212-review-descent-skeleton/review.md)
2. **Band selection at actual thickness.** For every room of area >81 whose nontrivial band-compressed parents are fully lit, some band of thickness ≥2 has a passing parent seed for *each* of its retained pairs at E=h−1. The same band must work for the entire room in this version. Open. The threshold inequality or the sharper signed gate test can be used.
3. **Collars for that band.** For a band supplied by item 2, prove lighting of every mixed pair and every new/new pair across components, at that very E. It suffices to prove this for all bands satisfying item 2. Same-component new/new is proved by convexity. For k=1, d209 reviews flat terminal caps under an entry and length hypothesis; d210 reviews the two named tails. D211 reviewed the arbitrary-foot vertical-entry and rectangular-shoulder extensions as correct with gaps. The vertical-entry seed denominator is at most 2Bᵧ−1. These results require the stated access and length conditions. [d211 review](../../illumination-generalization-2026-11-06/fable/directions-2026-11-09/d211-review-general-collar/review.md) General short bridges, partial entrances and parallel-component collars remain open. None of the tail statements covers all single-interval bands unconditionally. [Wide insertion](l1-wide-insertion.md) · [Re-aiming](l1-reaim-threshold.md) · [General collars](l1-general-collar.md)
4. **Base census.** Assume full lattice illumination for all rooms through area 81. At the least dark area above 81 all the parents in item 2 are smaller and fully lit. Items 1–3 then light every pair, a contradiction. The reviewed contiguous census reaches 21; 22–81 are outside that result. This is a proof dependency, not a request to run those areas.

**Marked version: parallel retained transfer + marked band selection + census through A₁=121 imply L1, without a collar lemma.** For a proposed dark pair insert both marks' coordinates into both lists of wall levels, discarding duplicates. Each axis now has at most r+3≤11 gaps. If area >121(h₀−1)² some refined gap has thickness ≥h₀. Its section is constant and neither mark lies strictly inside the band. Compression to one preserves both interior lattice marks, even if they lie on opposite interfaces: those interfaces remain one unit apart. The same area drop and transfer proof apply, including when a new cut level passes through no wall vertex. The **marked selection lemma** needed at h₀=2 is that, when all smaller parents are lit, at least one such refined band admits one passing seed for this pair at E=h−1. Under that hypothesis a least-area dark pair above 121 is impossible. This version changes the quantifier from one band for all pairs to one band for the specified pair. The distinct price is the larger sufficient base range, not a new illumination assumption about strip interiors.

**Why a polynomial denominator alone does not supply selection.** A bound v≤F(box of parent) makes the conservative threshold at most Q(4·area(parent)−4r−4)F². The band pigeonhole bounds h from below in terms of the *original* box. It gives no inequality comparing h−1 with that parent threshold. For instance, take the nine-row staircase of widths 9,8,…,1, left aligned, and scale both axes by t. It has r=8, area 45t², and every wall gap on either axis equals t. Compressing any one band leaves area at least 36t². Even setting F=1 in the coarse upper estimate does not certify E=t−1 by this comparison for large t. This shows a failure of the estimate, not a dark pair or a failure of the signed gate test. At t=1 the area is already 45 and neither axis admits any nontrivial band compression. Thus a descent restricted to this move cannot use the area-21 census as its only base evidence. A larger census or a separate theorem for such base rooms is essential.

## A reduction for comparable gaps that grow in fixed proportions

There is another valid induction move when several large gaps defeat the one-band estimate. Work with the **marked** chart, so equality and ordering of all wall and mark coordinates are fixed. Write its positive gap vectors as gₓ and gᵧ. If the entries on either axis have common divisor >1, translate that axis to its minimum and then divide by that divisor. This produces a strictly smaller lattice room with the two interior marks still distinct. Any parent seed lifts exactly: independent positive scalings of the axes commute with reflections in the corresponding axis-parallel walls and preserve straight segments and corner misses. One may therefore restrict a minimal dark marked room to gcd one on **each** axis. Scaling wall coordinates alone would not justify this conclusion for arbitrary marks.

A version also covers coprime gaps close to integer templates. Fix a smaller lattice room and a legal seed for a specified marked pair, with marked gaps (u,v). There is an effectively calculable ε>0 such that any realization of the **same marked chart** with

    max(‖gₓ/λₓ − u‖∞, ‖gᵧ/λᵧ − v‖∞) < ε   for some λₓ,λᵧ>0

has a legal path for that pair. To prove this, first remove the two scale factors by the exact reflection argument. Unfold the finite ordered list of wall hits of the template seed and join its source to its final target image in the perturbed copies. Each intended hit is transverse and in the relative interior of its edge. Its intersection parameter and position therefore vary continuously; strict order and positive distance from the two endpoints persist. In each copy the closed leg misses every boundary edge other than its designated entrance and exit edges by positive distance. The same is true under sufficiently small perturbation. Transversality and the interior starting point then carry the path through the copies in order. The direct segment case uses its positive clearance from the boundary. For an effective radius, enumerate dyadic radii about the given template and verify these strict conditions on the coordinate box by rational interval bounds: reflected endpoints are affine in the levels, transverse line intersections are rational functions with nonzero denominator at the template, and the distance from a leg to a nonincident edge has a positive rational lower bound obtainable from squared segment distances. The interval bounds converge to the template values as the radius decreases: each fixed rational expression has nonzero denominator at the template. For segment clearance use subdivision or persist a strictly separating line for the two disjoint closed segments. There are finitely many strict checks, so convergence gives termination. Take the radius smaller also than the least template gap.

In particular, bounded errors cₓ,cᵧ in gₓ=tₓu+cₓ, gᵧ=tᵧv+cᵧ are allowed whenever ‖cᵢ‖∞<εtᵢ on both axes. The normalized parent need not be obtained by dividing by an integer, and these varying lattice gaps can be coprime. The template may supply its seed from the census or from strong induction if its area is smaller. This is a simultaneous change of coordinates; it gives an alternative to proving that a single band passes. A sufficient **weaker marked selection lemma** above area 121 is that each marked room either passes one refined band test or lies in a certified neighborhood of a smaller lit marked template as just described.

The open quantifier is coverage of all marked gap ratios. For any fixed finite collection of templates the radii above are computable, but neither coverage by their neighborhoods nor a bound on the areas of additional templates follows from openness. A dense set of template ratios alone does not give coverage by unspecified radii. Coprime comparable gaps outside the certified neighborhoods, as well as ratios tending toward a degenerate chart, still require a selection argument.

**First open step in this skeleton.** After the parallel retained transfer, prove marked band selection, or prove room-level selection together with the outstanding collar classes. The multiscale case to resolve is a room with several comparable growing gaps: after any one is compressed, the others still enter the seed and clearance bounds. No unconditional numerical dark-witness bound follows from the current results.
