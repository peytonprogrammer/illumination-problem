# What survives every route change?

**Result.** For a fixed lattice room and a specified separating horizontal door there is an exact, finite obstruction on the physical door: in each of finitely many primitive directions the forward and backward sets of signed crossing residues are disjoint. The two sets must include *later* visits as well as first visits. The bound obtained below depends on area. Local thickness does not bound it. A simpler obstruction based only on the fraction of the journey elapsed at the door can disappear under a cap excursion even when no legal placement has yet been produced.

## Three tools, with their hypotheses

1. **Absorption.** On two already legal outer legs fix the abscissa and sheet at each crossing or horizontal reflection. A run of width uᵢ traversed through vertical distance dᵢ preserves its signed end state under a slope change Δ exactly when dᵢΔ ∈ 2uᵢℤ. The common shift period is P=lcmᵢ(2uᵢ/dᵢ). In a remaining band of width u and travel d the signed target phase minus the entry phase minus ds is F. Matching these placements is equivalent to F∈gcd(dP,2u)ℤ, by Bézout. [Derivation and sheet conventions](band-absorption.md)
2. **Variable first entry.** In a rectangular end chamber of height H with a door on its ceiling and a solid floor, let t₀ be the vertical travel to the first ceiling visit in the chosen launch sense. The successive ceiling times are tⱼ=t₀+2Hj. In unfolded transverse coordinates the positions are X+tⱼs folded with period 2u, where u is the width. Entry on visit j is legal exactly when that visit is in the open door, every earlier ceiling visit is on a solid smooth part, and all intervening floor contacts avoid corners. Other openings, if present, must be excluded at each event. This follows by folding the straight line until its first exit. For a virtual target B another d units beyond entry one may prescribe s=(B−X)/(tⱼ+d) and check these conditions. Changing j generally **moves** the earlier contacts. Once a legal j and s are fixed, shifts from (1) preserve the signed contacts. The distinction matters when another chamber requires the eventual exit velocity.
3. **Route change.** Let a crossing occur after vertical travel A out of D, 0<A<D, and write A/D=m/k in lowest terms. If all vertical mirrors are on x∈gℤ, the developed endpoints in any fixed sheets belong to x₀+2gℤ and x₁+2gℤ, where x₀=±X and x₁=±Y. The crossing must lie in the developed coset ((k−m)x₀+mx₁)/k+(2g/k)ℤ. This is exact for independently translated endpoint images by gcd(m,k)=1, and necessary for actual images. Reflected door images must be included. A route is excluded if every endpoint-sheet choice misses the door. Recompute m/k on changing visits. Even a common physical door need not have a common time fraction.

For example, if the pre-door travel increases by jC, C>0 an integer, and the remaining integral travel b stays fixed, the reduced denominator of (A+jC)/(A+jC+b) is at least (A+jC+b)/b. Consequently the relaxed crossing mesh goes to zero, uniformly in the endpoint sheets. Every fixed open door eventually contains a member of each such relaxed coset. This disproves an inference of an all-route missing residue from the fixed fraction alone. It does not guarantee any of the proposed intervening reflections.

## Exact all-route obstruction on one physical door

Let S,T be interior lattice points in different components after cutting the room at the door, away from the cut. For a horizontal cut whose endpoint is smooth on a vertical wall, include that endpoint as a possible simultaneous wall bounce and cut passage. An equivalent convention for such endpoints is to use the local reflected chart and identify incoming/outgoing germs by the smooth reflection. Corner endpoints remain forbidden. For a primitive developed direction w=(a,b) with b≠0, launch from all lifts of S on the four-copy translation surface. Trace until the first physical corner or until a regular lattice point with the same developed direction repeats on that surface. Record every visit to the cut, with its physical position and velocity germ. From all lifts of T trace in −w and record the *forward* germ for joining. Velocities are expressed in physical coordinates, so opposite sign copies are handled automatically. One can equivalently retain the copy and developed direction on both sides before projecting. Positions on the horizontal cut are of the form j/|b|: integral reflections preserve integrality of unfolded wall levels. Retain the actual integer j (or its residue in a modulus larger than the entire scaled door span after shifting the origin). Do not reduce away the position or sheet. A horizontal path cannot join opposite components without changing height across this cut.

**For w, a match is necessary and sufficient for a legal physical path in one of the chosen lifts/directions.** A connecting shot crosses the separator, so its restrictions supply matching states. Conversely two recorded legs with the same physical forward germ join by uniqueness of continuation at a regular point, including the reflected chart at a smooth wall. A repeated lattice state closes a periodic trajectory; one complete period includes all its cut visits. Regular lattice lifts are points over the torus origin. There are at most d=4 area(P) such points, so d+1 primitive steps suffice per launch, stopping earlier at a forbidden corner. The steps can contain several door visits. No assumption about the number of band reversals enters.

There is also a finite *direction* reduction for the yes/no outcome. Encode the d-square translation surface as two permutations of d squares giving its torus cover away from the origin. Color its origin fiber by membership in forbidden corners, source lifts, target lifts or the remaining points. These sets are disjoint. There are at most I=(d!)²4^d isomorphism classes of such decorated covers of this degree: a labelled permutation pair determines the punctured cover and its completion, and the fiber has at most d points. SL₂(ℤ) acts by changing the torus marking, transporting the colors. Its stabilizer lifts to affine maps preserving the three distinguished sets. Hence this subgroup has index at most I. This elementary count supplies a loose bound for the finite-index step in [chain-map.md](chain-map.md).

Take representatives of the cosets H A in H\SL₂(ℤ), using right multiplication by the usual elementary generators (quarter-turn, unit shear and their inverses). The finite connected coset graph has representatives of word length at most I−1. Every primitive vector is A(1,0) for some A∈SL₂(ℤ). Thus it suffices to test the first columns of the representatives. Indeed an element of H transfers a legal lifted connection to a legal lifted connection with the same physical endpoint labels, and preserves forbidden corners. It need not preserve the door: the transferred connection crosses that door because its endpoints remain separated. This is a reduction of **existence**, not an identification of two crossing sets in different directions. The maximum absolute coordinate of a representative direction is at most 2^I by multiplication of the generators.

Thus darkness is equivalent to disjoint forward/backward signed crossing sets in every representative direction. This is an exact finite arithmetic obstruction for the specified room. Different directions may fail for different residues or sheets. The argument does not imply a single coset missed by every route, and the finite reduction was not executed. It applies just as well beyond eight reflexes, so an additional geometric bound or forced intersection is essential. For a pair in different interiors of tree pieces any separating tree edge suffices, with axes exchanged as necessary. Marks on cuts require selecting a separator disjoint from them or using the uninterrupted marked-leaf test directly.

## Thin and fat consequences

For the resonant cov279 control the one-cap route from heights 1 to 5 has A/D=3/6. At 3|q every vertical wall lies on 3ℤ. With both abscissas divisible by three and the same parity, tool (3) puts the midpoint in 3ℤ on every sheet, outside the open door. The putative later entries have A=3+4j, b=3. Their arithmetic mesh is at most 18/(6+4j), below the door width three for j≥1. Hence the original missing residue cannot account for all these routes. Earlier ceiling hits and simultaneous upper placement remain uncontrolled. This is a corollary about the obstruction, not a lighting verdict for the family.

A bound τ≤τ₀ and at most nine rectangles controls the sizes of junction regions but leaves d and the cut-direction moduli unbounded. I cannot force an intersection of the signed crossing sets with those hypotheses. In the fat regime a contained τ-square gives a piece with both sides ≥τ/(r+1), as previously proved. It still does not bound the access cost: in the named symmetric st279 end stretches the two end squares have side m, joined through two width-three horizontal runs with a unit overlap to each end. If one uses an end square as the large piece, the opposite attachment contains another m-square. The all-start travel in the x direction to a fixed vertical door line in such a chamber, if using the rotated access construction, or in the y direction to its actual horizontal door, is at least of order m by the farthest-start distance. In particular its actual normal travel bound cannot feed a depth test quadratic in that bound inside the first m-square. This concerns the uniform all-start depth argument; the presence of the other large square does not itself imply darkness or rule out a different pair-specific path.
