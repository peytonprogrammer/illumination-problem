# Effective rational candidates in each lattice Ferrers room

D169 confirms the reduction to opposite terminal cells. The following elementary consequence of the terminal-strip theorem leaves an explicitly finite four-coordinate candidate set in each fixed integral Ferrers room, even for arbitrary real marks. It does not bound the room lengths or prove the set empty.

Use the widths and heights of ferrers-terminal-strip.md, set the left and bottom walls at zero, and write S=(x,y) for the upper mark and T=(z,t) for the bottom-right mark. The opposite terminal cells have 0<x<w, 0<t<h. Put Q=floor(W/w) and choose the least integer middle slope period P. Form the finite set F of t in (0,h) for which **both** P(h−t)/(2W) and P(h+t)/(2W) modulo one are rational of reduced denominator at most Q. In practice it suffices to generate t from the first fraction by taking the finitely many residues j/q with q≤Q and the integer translates within the indicated interval, then test the second. Apply the same definition in the transposed room to get a finite rational set E in (0,w). Darkness implies t∈F and x∈E by the reviewed rotation test. If a set is empty there is full illumination.

Here is a bound on the two remaining coordinates. Let C be an integer common multiple of twice every horizontal run width. For fixed t∈F let K=den(t) in lowest terms. On the four-copy unfolding, use the affine horizontal shear of derivative [[1,CK],[0,1]] that fixes the integer horizontal levels pointwise. On each unit-height horizontal strip, in developed coordinates (ξ,η) with local height in [0,1], the formula is (ξ+CKη,η). Each strip is periodic with circumference twice its run width: a ray crosses the run and reflects back on the other horizontal-velocity sheet. The shifts agree on both rims, including artificial subdivisions; their derivatives in translation charts agree. Thus the map is invertible, fixes all forbidden corner lifts, and carries legal segments to legal segments. Integer origins and the two vertical signs give t or −t modulo one for lifts of the target. Every power fixes its **entire** lift set because Kt is integral.

If the physical pair is dark, every source lift is hidden from every target lift. Apply powers to just one source lift while the target set is fixed. Each resulting lift is still hidden from the whole set. If its projection were an interior point lit from T, unfolding that physical path and applying a physical sheet symmetry would give a legal segment to the selected lift from some lift of T. Thus each projected interior point is dark from T. Its height is still y, so its abscissa must belong to E. A projected point on a side wall can add only the two abscissas 0,w. It cannot coincide with T at the other height.

In the horizontal cylinder the unfolded orbit of that lift is rotation by ±CKy modulo 2w (integer horizontal levels contribute full circumferences). At most two coordinates on this circle project to one physical abscissa. Consequently its order is at most 2(|E|+2); this includes a marked point on an integer level, where the twist is the identity and y is integral. In particular CKy/(2w) modulo one must be rational of denominator at most 2(|E|+2). This gives an explicit finite list for y in its bounded terminal interval. Transpose and reverse the endpoints: with a vertical common multiple C′, the reduced denominator of C′den(x)z/(2h) modulo one is at most 2(|F|+2). This gives the fourth finite list. The argument uses no ineffective periodic-point count and does not assume that the shear preserves every physical four-lift orbit.

On the named grid (0,10,11,13,100), P=286, W=100, h=Q=10. Listing the fractions r with reduced denominator at most 10 and 0<r<143/10, then retaining those with den(143/5−r)≤10, gives |F|=|E|=142 by symmetry; the orbit order bound there is 288. In particular the example pair (5,55)–(55,5) *is lit*: its direct rotation is 143/20, whose denominator 20 exceeds Q. This checks endpoint-specific clearance even though the room-wide inequalities fail.

For each candidate one still has to supply a path or an exclusion. The sets just bounded grow with the room, and at integral marks the chosen shears fix everything. Thus this proves neither full illumination of the room with symmetric grid (0,10,11,13,100) nor a universal reduction to finitely many rooms.
