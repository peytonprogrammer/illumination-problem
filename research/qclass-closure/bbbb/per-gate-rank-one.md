# Unequal gate windows and strict rank-one failure

**Verdict for review.** Full gate widths give a two-variable convex feasibility problem. At most four gate constraints witness failure within a fixed chart and endpoint-lift choice. On a ten-wall lattice staircase, the natural chart on each axis fails strictly at rank one for the same two real marks, for every endpoint-cap choice, even with its full unequal windows. R22's equality in the reviewed vertical chart therefore cannot be inferred merely from failure of the tests. The all-itinerary problem remains open.

## Exact per-gate test

In the setup of d173, let e₀,e₁ denote developed transverse errors at the two endpoints, and let tⱼ be the fraction of the full developed travel to gate j. If its signed window relative to the base beam is (lⱼ,rⱼ), impose

    lⱼ < (1−tⱼ)e₀+tⱼe₁ < rⱼ.

Intersect these strips to obtain K. Include each required solid-cap window, in a chart and winding progression preserving its window, or independently verify the cap. Let Θ be the endpoint mismatch in physical transverse units, D=diag(2w,2V), and α the rotation of d173. Then placement is equivalent to K meeting Θ+D(ℤ²+ℤα); the sign of the cyclic generator does not affect this set. This follows from affine interpolation in the development, exactly as in d173. For open strips one can take the closure of the group because K is open. The statement is per signed chart and lift; smooth endpoints requiring different charts are separate.

At rank one with generator m of the relation group, placement is equivalent to the interval m·D⁻¹K meeting m·D⁻¹Θ+ℤ. Thus failure includes an interval lying strictly between consecutive admissible values as well as tangency. At rank two (including lattice marks with rational P) the closure is a full lattice in physical coordinates. More generally the closed phase set is affinely equivalent to ℝ², ℝ×ℤ or ℤ² according to relation rank 0, 1 or 2. Mixed-integer Helly gives respectively 3, 4, 4 strips as upper bounds on the size of an infeasibility witness. These are subsets of gates for the same chart; different charts can have different witnesses. [Averkov and Weismantel, Transversal numbers over subsets of linear spaces](https://arxiv.org/abs/1002.0948)

## Quantitative clearance where a beam exists

For integral developed gates (Lⱼ,Rⱼ), integer longitudinal coordinates sⱼ relative to the first gate, put gⱼ=Rⱼ−Lⱼ, G=max gⱼ and H=max(1,max |sⱼ|). If a line strictly traverses these gates, there is one with clearance at gate j at least

    δⱼ = gⱼ/(6HG).

Maximize ρ with Lⱼ+ρgⱼ ≤ x+sⱼv ≤ Rⱼ−ρgⱼ. Bound x,v by large integers containing a strictly feasible solution and impose ρ≥0. The optimum is positive and attained at a vertex. An independent triple of active rows has integer determinant of absolute value at most 6HG, by determinant expansion in the x,v,ρ columns. Its positive rational ρ has numerator at least one before reduction. Thus the bound holds simultaneously. The inequalities in the placement test remain strict. This is room- and chart-dependent; it supplies neither beam existence nor a bound depending on transverse widths and reflex count alone. For the shared-wall staircase below the exact signed radii are simply the three successive narrow widths.

## Ten walls, two axes, full windows, strict gaps

Use the d174 family (2,3,5,90m;40n−5,1,1,3) with 5 dividing m and 3 dividing n. In bottom-left coordinates take S=(1,30+η), T=(75+η,2), η=√2/10. Both lie in the opposite open terminal cells. In each orientation take the shared-side chart at zero and all integer shifts of slope restoring every complete middle-band period. Allow *any* affine correction inside the three full signed windows, ignoring cap guards to strengthen the exclusion. At the first and last gates the corrected line takes values E,F with |E|≤w and |F|≤v. If their longitudinal separation is d+e, every possible corrected terminal abscissa Z obeys

    |Z| ≤ v+B(w+v)/(d+e).

This is extrapolation, Z=F+B(F−E)/(d+e). It ignores the middle gate entirely. Compare this envelope with the necessary terminal lift distance to PBℤ+2Wℤ.

| Sweep | w,v,d+e | B direct / cap | Envelope direct / cap | Lower bounds on required distances |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Original | 2,5,2 | 1 / 5 | 8.5 / 22.5 | 15−η / 75−η |
| Transposed | 3,5,3 | 1 / 3 | 23/3 / 13 | 10−η / 30−η |

Indeed the first period is 30 and 2W=180m, so the progressions generate 30ℤ and 150ℤ. After transposition the period is 20 and 2W'=80n, giving 20ℤ and 60ℤ. The terminal abscissas are 75+η and 30+η. Changing either lift sign leaves the distance unchanged. Initial cap reversal has no effect on this necessary projection. On each axis the source depth is irrational in both cap choices while the terminal depth is rational. Thus the relation group genuinely has rank one, generated by a pure terminal character. Every tabulated miss is strict, even for closed gates; the failure is already witnessed by two gates. This excludes all corrections in this winding chart, including sloping reference lines staying in the same developed middle-gate copies. Changing relative middle windings beyond the common period, turning back through a gate, or using a different development is outside the conclusion.

D173's R22 equality concerns its specified zero-middle-band chart, where P is free. The review does not assert equality in all charts. For a lattice pair with middle bands and rational restoring period, the exact test instead has rank two. A wall budget obtained by counting rank-one equalities would therefore need new theorems both about chart selection and about the lattice case.

## What is the smallest W ≤ 20 room this result doesn't cover?

No word moves. Ferrers LLLLLRLRLR remains PARTIAL. A smallest OPEN word is LLLRLLLRLR (W=10), realized by (2,0),(4,0),(4,1),(3,1),(3,4),(0,4),(0,3),(1,3),(1,2),(2,2). The unresolved step is to rule out simultaneous misses across genuinely different relative middle windings or return itineraries, rather than across just the four endpoint choices within one chart per axis.
