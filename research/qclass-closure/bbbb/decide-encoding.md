Advances: L1 replacement — per-pattern decidability for all integral lengths and all interior lattice marks.

**Assessment.** No uniform decision procedure for the whole word follows from the available results. The finite-cover bound gives one useful logical fact: a dark instance is decidable once the lengths and marks are fixed. Thus a failure of the all-length statement is recursively enumerable. Termination on a fully lit word is the missing direction.

**(a) One-parameter rays.** If lengths *and marks* are affine in t and the itinerary has fixed length, unfolding gives univariate polynomial gate tests. Their signs are decidable on integer t by real-root isolation, even when they are quadratic. This does not cover arbitrary marks. With a fixed finite direction menu and a constant reflection cap, arbitrary marks pose no difficulty: each unfolded leg has displacement equal to a real travel time times a fixed vector, so the finite disjunction uses only mixed integer/real linear arithmetic.

The finite direction menu already fails on one affine ray of the one-neck word LLLLRRLLLLRR. Take chambers [−2t,0] × [−1,2] and [1,3] × [−1,2], joined at heights (0,1) across 0<x<1. Put S=(−t,0), T=(2,1), t≥1. The direct chord crosses both open mouths. For any proposed height cap H, take t divisible by 1,…,H. Before first entering the neck, any nonvertical ray hits x=0 after horizontal travel an odd multiple of t, including after end-wall reflections. For reduced slope p/q with q≤H its unfolded height there is integral. Folding at integral horizontal walls keeps it integral, so it misses the open unit mouth. Vertical rays never reach it. This excludes every constant denominator bound, even with unlimited reflections. A t-dependent bound leaves a growing menu. A ray decision would also need a uniform way to cover independent length tuples.

**(b) Quantifier order.** The intended order is “for every room and pair there exists a witness.” Choosing a slope before the instance requires one slope to work for all instances, already excluded above. Negation preserves witness dependence. Quantifiers over itineraries do not eliminate the gate determinant: at zero reflections the previous note's affine family has the exact threshold u>m(m+1). A different encoding would have to eliminate that distinction after taking the union over *all* legal paths. Nondefinability of the zero-reflection test does not settle definability of this union.

**(c) Other theories.** Real closed fields decide fixed finite itinerary tests over real data. Scaling transfers rational counterexamples to lattice counterexamples when the whole word is free, but a nonempty real semialgebraic failure set need not contain a rational point. One needs rational realizability for these particular failure sets and a finite description covering every itinerary. **Hilbert’s tenth barrier:** adding an unrestricted integer predicate to the ordered real field interprets integer polynomial equations, so its general existential decision problem is undecidable. No reduction of Hilbert’s tenth problem to illumination is known from these arguments.

**Surviving conditional route and first test.** Use the finite modular-cover orbit to decide each fixed instance, and prove an effective bound B(w) such that if any marked instance is dark, one is dark at area ≤B(w). This would decide the word because only finitely many integral rooms of bounded area occur up to translation. The current orbit bound depends on the input area and controls paths *within* one cover. The first open test is a computable area bound for the least dark instance, if one exists, of the one-neck word with both apertures, chamber dimensions and marks free. No inter-area reduction or such bound is established.
