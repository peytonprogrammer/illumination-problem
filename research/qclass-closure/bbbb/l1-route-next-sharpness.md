Advances: L1 — The shear-orbit proposal has no identified mechanism separating eight reflex corners from nine.

**Where the budget enters.** For a simple orthogonal room with r reflex corners, the turning-angle identity gives r+4 convex corners. In each orbit member, partition the blocker count B by the physical corner responsible for each occurrence. This gives r reflex contributions and r+4 convex contributions, all counted with multiplicity. That partition is the only step of the proposed comparison where r currently enters. Passing from r=8 to r=9 adds one term of each type to the count of terms. It gives no comparison of their sizes, and the two rooms need not share an orbit or candidate arcs.

**The missing estimate.** Write m(a) for the number of intervening corner occurrences on candidate arc a. Across the whole orbit the margin is exactly

    Σ C − Σ B = number of arcs with m(a)=0 − Σ over blocked arcs of (m(a)−1).

Thus strict positivity requires enough clear arcs to outweigh *all repeated blocker charges*. Shear invariance permits reindexing the sums, but supplies no estimate making that balance positive through eight reflex corners. At nine, darkness forces the clear-arc count to vanish, hence the margin is nonpositive, including when there are no candidates. This gives the necessary sign for a dark example; it does not identify a ninth-corner event that changes the sign. D152 concerns changing an arm length and gives no estimate for these incidences.

**Viability verdict.** I cannot locate a sharp eight-versus-nine mechanism. The first open step for this route is a geometric constraint on the ordering or multiplicity of corner hits that forces a positive margin at r≤8 and can fail at r=9. Even a successful ten-wall test (r=3) would leave that step open. The L1 area bound remains unchanged.
