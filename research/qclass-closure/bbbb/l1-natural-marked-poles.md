# Which poles matter for a marked pair?

Let n be room area and G contain Aₙ in its natural action, n≥21. Fix distinct same-parity interior lattice marks. On the pillowcase take the connected cover Y of unordered pairs of distinct sheets (connected by two-transitivity). This is an auxiliary surface, not an intermediate room or the physical subset-action branch excluded by d176. Start at the images in Y of the compatible ordered marking germs of the two endpoints. Restrict to straight segments whose open interiors avoid physical corners in **both** projections, lifting to the ordered cover to evaluate that condition.

A pole on Y is a fixed two-element set of the site's local permutation. Exactly two possibilities exist: the permutation interchanges its two letters, or fixes each independently. In the first case the two ordered endpoints meet at a regular point of the room double. Locally the ordered-to-unordered map branches by order two: reversing the incoming line at its endpoint interchanges the legs (in simultaneous physical charts the two coordinates are u and −u). Thus a clean segment from a marked start to this pole joins S to T by following one projected leg forward and the other backward. This is the regular-midpoint argument in the quotient by coordinate exchange. In the second case the ordered limits are two distinct convex corners, so the same conclusion does not follow.

Conversely, a legal billiard path between the marks lifts to the room double. Run inwards from its two ends at equal speed. The two base developments agree on the pillowcase by the same-parity midpoint argument. At their regular midpoint the two ordered coordinates coincide on the branch exchanged by coordinate swap. Passing to Y therefore gives a clean straight segment from a compatible marked start to a swap pole. An earlier regular lattice crossing is allowed. Together these implications give an equivalence for distinct same-parity interior lattice marks only. The numerical sufficient test below reformulates the existence obligation and gives no area improvement. If the four site types have fₓ fixed letters, qₓ transpositions and zₓ three-cycles, the two counts over x are exactly qₓ and binom(fₓ,2). Hence Y has 2n−2−2r swap poles by Riemann–Hurwitz on the original room double (sum(qₓ+2zₓ)=2n−2), while it has at most binom(r+4,2)≤66 other poles. For n≥43 and r≤8 swap poles are already a strict majority (at least 68). In particular, reachability of more than Σ binom(fₓ,2) distinct poles from the compatible starts, by clean open segments, suffices for illumination. This is an endpoint-specific criterion; a pole count on its own supplies no clean segment. For d146's DB55 data (n=53, r=4, fₓ=2 at all four sites), the counts are 96 and 4. The number 96 agrees with the archived ordered regular-coincidence count. No new room or trajectory was tested.

**Why the majority does not prove L1.** Even a segment avoiding all singularities in its open interior on Y can fail the two-projection condition. A pair consisting of a fixed letter and a letter on a transposition has local orbit of length two on Y. The corresponding point is metrically regular there, but one leg projects to a convex corner. There are exactly Σ fₓqₓ such ordinary forbidden points. For the unbounded DB55 prime-degree portraits this is 4(n−5). Thus simply treating Y as a surface with at most 66 obstacles would give a false count. The bounded quantity counts false *terminal poles* only. The first open case is to force a clean segment from the specified starts to one swap pole in a primitive natural BBBB room. No explicit area bound has followed.
