# What can be charged to a blocked direction class?

**Verdict.** There is a sharp overlap inequality for class costs measured in physical reflex stops. Its inputs are minimum hitting numbers for actual trajectories. R22 attains nine with no sharing. Orbit exclusion in cov4181 costs at most one such stop for its populated failed class. No uniform positive cost for that mechanism follows from its orbit verdict. The twelve mixed 24-wall controls admit a different, directly checkable budget of 16 + 17 − 9 = 24 incident walls. Their accounting does not transfer to R22.

## What is being counted

Fix an orthogonal room, two distinct same-parity interior lattice marks of parity a, and a blocked primitive class with first-step site x≠a. There are two exhaustive geometric cases: x has no regular lattice site, or it has regular sites and no single cut leaf in the class contains a lift of each mark. The second is a verdict, with possibly several causes. D142 excludes common-quotient, full-fixed-fiber midpoint witnesses for a *whole pair* in the stated wall range. It does not exclude a midpoint witness for one class or one relative lift. D86 bounds the number of hidden points in a different space and does not supply a wall charge. An empty class has a geometric cost even before specifying a path. Write n for the number of unit cells, fₓ for convex corners and rₓ for reflex corners of parity x. Counting the x corner of every cell gives n=fₓ+3rₓ for an empty class. Thus it occupies at least ⌈n/3⌉ distinct boundary vertices of that color. Distinct empty colors use disjoint vertices. In contrast, a populated-class orbit exclusion counts no obligatory corner without proving that a candidate route actually reaches it. ## A sharp overlap bound for three reflex-stop costs

Use the relaxed trajectories of the [nine-stop proof](corner-support.md): straight continuation through a reflex is allowed when both legs are inside, and convex corners remain forbidden. Let tₓ be the minimum number of reflexes hitting all these routes of class x, zero if the family is empty. Consider a pair blocked in all three classes so these numbers are finite. Let T be the joint minimum and let A be the number of physical source-parity reflexes. If t₁≥t₂≥t₃ are the sorted costs, then

    T ≥ max(t₁, t₁+t₂−A, t₁+t₂+t₃−2A).
    W ≥ 4 + 2 max(t₁, t₁+t₂−A, t₁+t₂+t₃−2A).

Indeed, lattice hits along a developed primitive line alternate between colors a and x. In any joint hitting set with u source-color reflexes and sₓ reflexes of color x, tₓ≤u+sₓ. Sum this over the largest k costs, for k=1,2,3. Their sum is at most T+(k−1)u, and u≤A. The wall inequality follows from r≥T and W=2r+4.

This is the best bound from the four numbers alone **as colored set incidence**. Take A available shared labels in a fixed order. For class i prescribe one singleton-supported route at each of the first min(A,tᵢ) labels, and tᵢ−min(A,tᵢ) more with private singleton supports. The union has exactly the displayed size (sort the three tᵢ and distinguish how many exceed A). This sharpness statement does not assert realization of arbitrary incidences in a polygon. The [shared-reflex control](shared-reflex.md) does realize the crucial local feature: three prescribed routes in different primitive classes, for the same pair, have the same sole reflex stop in a normalized BBBB room. Its pair is lit by another trajectory. Any stronger theorem needs global restrictions on *which complete route families* can share stops.

The full set-dependent identity in corner-support.md can improve the inequality when one knows which source reflexes the three class optima actually require. Neither result permits charging corners simply for the existence of an excluded orbit.

## Named controls

- **R22.** For each off-source class there are three distinct singleton witnesses from the archived nine routes. Every route in a class meets a corresponding reflex, and there are only three such corners and zero source-parity reflexes. Thus (t₁,t₂,t₃;A)=(3,3,3;0) exactly, giving W≥22 with equality. Charging just the boundary walls incident to these indispensable corners would only give 16 distinct walls. The remaining six are accounted for by the orthogonal turning identity.
- **cov4181.** The empty site class (1,0) has seven convex and seven reflex corners, contributing 7+3·7=28 cell incidences. The populated failed class (0,0) has ten regular sites and **zero reflexes**. Its relaxed nonaxial routes could only pass through the single source-color reflex (6,3). Hence its hitting number is 0 or 1; the class exclusion does not decide whether such a relaxed route exists. The horizontal class lights the pair. This accounts for both failures at W=20 without attributing three reflexes to each. [Saved all-direction verdict](dyadic-classes.md)
- **Parity-W24-0 through -11, including Castro at -5.** Directly checking each archived polygon gives 7 even-even, 8 odd-odd and 9 mixed-parity corners. Every off-source lattice site in the closed polygon is a vertex. Reflections in the canonical diagonal walls may swap coordinate parities, so the two mixed parities must be grouped; odd-odd is preserved. This is the clean-parity obstruction of d11. The 8 odd-odd corners touch 16 walls; the 9 mixed corners touch 17 walls; exactly 9 walls join the two groups. Their union contains all 24 walls in **every** control. This is a count of actual incident walls, not a proof that every dark room must spend that many. Castro is one of the twelve. [d11 named rooms and clean-parity certificate](../../illumination-generalization-2026-11-06/fable/directions-2026-11-09/d11-census-45/README.md) · [Exact geometry checker for these controls](class-wall-budget.py)

For disjoint sets of boundary vertices the wall overlap just used is exact in general: a wall counted by both groups must have one endpoint in each, and conversely. For three disjoint groups no wall can be counted three times. In the orthogonal case the two off-source colors a+(1,0) and a+(0,1) can never share a wall in this sense because they differ in both coordinates. These facts control *incident edges*. They do not constrain how many directions a single source-color corner can obstruct.

**What remains.** To derive W≥22 by the stop budget one needs enough trajectories that stay indispensable after shared source-color reflexes are accounted for. The counts alone suffice if one class needs nine stops, two need at least 9+A in sum, or all three need at least 9+2A. Those conditions are sufficient, not established consequences of darkness. In BBBB all three classes have regular sites, so none of the empty-color charges applies. The concrete missing input is a lower bound on *private* stops after the source-color stops have been used. Current orbit exclusions do not furnish one. No room or trajectory discovery search was run.
