Advances: L1 — propagation of lattice illumination along a wide horizontal cut. Old-pair replay is d110. A return from a flat terminal wall covers the new marks in two nonrectangular families, including the collar next to the old chamber.

## Signed replay and the missing hypothesis

Cut at y = c = j + 1/2 through the single interval (a,a+w), translate the upper piece by the integer E ≥ 0, and insert the strip between the copies. Coordinates a and w are integral. Offsets of either adjoining chamber are unrestricted. For a crossing of a seed with primitive signed direction (p,q), q ≠ 0, unfold the transverse interval to the circle of circumference 2w. Its coordinate determines both the folded x relative to a and the sign of the horizontal velocity. With the orientation chosen to increase with transverse travel, the added phase is E|p|/|q|. The **least full-state replay period** is

    T = 2w|q| / gcd(|p|, 2w|q|).

This gives T = 1 for a vertical seed. A horizontal lattice seed never meets this half-integral cut. At a crossing coincident with a side reflection, use the state by continuation through the smooth vertical wall. For E divisible by T, each inserted traverse has the original outgoing state, including the axial velocity. Its side reflections are all regular. Reattach the next original leg on its translated side and repeat in chronological order, including downward returns. The translations preserve every corner miss. A seed missing the cut needs no divisibility. This proves simultaneous old-pair replay for the least common multiple P of the chosen periods. [d110 review](../../illumination-generalization-2026-11-06/fable/directions-2026-11-09/d110-review-length/review.md)

There are exactly E(w−1) new marks, at x = a+1,…,a+w−1 and y = j+1,…,j+E. Two new marks see each other by a segment inside the inserted rectangle. A new mark and a retained mark outside the strip are the additional pair type. Full lighting at the base length supplies no record with that new endpoint. Knowing the phase after a *whole* traverse does not specify a hit at an intermediate depth.

**General reduction.** If the room is fully lit at E₀ and the new-to-retained pairs are covered at each E₀+kP, replay proves full lighting at each such length. D110 discharges this additional hypothesis for a base corridor already long enough to put the cut between the two end collars: each inserted mark is then at sufficient depth from either end for the one-leg phase sweep. In a homogeneous passage containing both collars one may choose the cut between them, since the lengthened room is the same. A short arbitrary base with two irregular ends still needs a collar argument. This is the first unproved implication in the unqualified reduction in the brief. ## A terminal rectangle covers the new marks

Suppose the component below a horizontal entrance line is exactly a rectangle [a,a+w] × [0,H₀], with a flat reflecting floor. Insert in its interior, so its new height is H = H₀+E. For the all-pair conclusion assume that every strictly upper lattice mark has a finite, corner-free first entry transverse to the **open** entrance. This is a geometric hypothesis even when H₀ = 1 and there is no lower lattice mark to use as a target. An interior mark on the open mouth itself sees the cap by a segment.

A regular vertical entry also suffices. Unfold its finite prefix up to the mouth. Starting at an interior point, its vertical ray hits only horizontal mirrors, each in its relative interior and transversely. The mouth crossing is likewise in its relative interior. The finite unfolded segment has positive clearance from all endpoints of these crossed edges and from the other boundary edges of its itinerary away from these crossings. A sufficiently small nonzero rational tilt preserves the ordered crossings and enters that same open mouth. Use the tilted entry's reduced ratio s₀ = u/v > 0, its vertical travel D > 0, and its initial signs for the window below. A vertical ray ending at a mouth corner does not meet this hypothesis. With an integral entrance line and lattice start, D is integral. [d209 review](../../illumination-generalization-2026-11-06/fable/directions-2026-11-09/d209-review-wide-insertion/review.md)

The first entry persists on the open slope interval |s−s₀| < 1/(vD), with s > 0. Here are the details that permit an explicit interval. Unfold the original entrance itinerary. At every potential change through a corner or entrance endpoint, its image has integer coordinates, and its axial distance from the starting image is an integer t between 1 and D. The critical ratio i/t differs from u/v by at least 1/(vD) if distinct. At s₀ the finite path is regular and crosses the open entrance, hence its itinerary persists locally, even if it crosses regular grid vertices. Following the same itinerary through either half of the interval cannot meet a first boundary event there. Horizontal or vertical tangency is excluded by positive finite slope. The far endpoint remains at axial distance D in this unfolding. Since uD ≥ 1 the whole open interval has positive slope.

Once in the cap, continue down to the floor and back up to height y. Total axial travel is L = D+H+y. The developed transverse endpoint is affine in s with derivative ±L. On the fixed entry itinerary, the cap target's transverse images contain a coset of 2w. At the center of the cap the two sign choices combine into a coset of w. Thus a slope exists if

    2L/(vD) > 2w,     or  2L/(vD) > w for a center target.

There is one further legality check: the floor hit must miss both bottom corners. For a general target the phase sweep alone does not ensure this. In the two families below only the center column occurs, and the following two-image argument removes this exception at every width.

**Removing the floor corners at every width.** In the fixed entrance chart the developed x-coordinates of floor corners form one coset of wℤ. Write F = D+H for axial travel to the floor. Two consecutive target images in the same transverse coset, spaced by 2w, yield floor coordinates differing by 2wF/L. Since 0 < y < H ≤ F and L=F+y, that difference is strictly between w and 2w. It cannot join two floor corners. For a center target the consecutive images are spaced by w and the corresponding difference lies strictly between w/2 and w. Consequently **an open image interval longer than 4w suffices for every new target, or longer than 2w for a center target**. It contains two consecutive images, at least one with a regular floor hit. The resulting seed has no further entrance visit. The slope is rational for lattice endpoints. A pair within the cap has a direct segment. This proves a collar bound for a terminal rectangle of any fixed width and an arbitrary upper chamber whenever the stated first-entry itineraries are available. One can enlarge the base replay period to clear the explicit threshold simultaneously for all upper marks.

## Ranks 11 and 18

Use the interior cut y = 3/2. In both base rooms H₀ = 2. In rank 11 the cap is [0,2] and its entrance opens on (0,1); in rank 18 the cap is [1,3] and its entrance opens on (2,3). The sole mark below the entrance is respectively (1,1) or (2,1). All other marks are strictly above the entrance. Rank 11 has row masks 3,3,25,63,33,60,12; rank 18 has 6,6,28,14,3,63,56, bottom first. For every positive multiple E of the displayed P, the image interval exceeds four: L = D+E+2+y with y ≥ 1, so it suffices that E+3 > (2v−1)D. For rank 18 the table gives at most 360 on the right. For rank 11 use the actual first-entry D at (4,3): the ray rises from y=3 to y=4 and descends to y=2, giving D=3. At (3,6) the ray descends to y=2 with D=4. The right sides are 21 and 36. Both ranks therefore meet the strict inequality already at k=1. The archived base settles k=0; replay settles retained pairs; the direct segment and floor-return argument settle every new pair. **The entire rank-11 room is lattice-lit at E=80k and the entire rank-18 room at E=1,440k for every k≥0.** These are sufficient periods for the progression, with no least-period claim for lighting.

**What “compatible” means here.** The new marks have legal seeds at every length in the very same progression. The slope can depend on depth and length; an individual new seed need not have replay period dividing P. For example, in rank 11 at E=80, take the translated (3,6) and target (1,2). Here D=4, H=82, L=88, F=86. Launch from (3,86) with velocity (−422, −88) for unit time. Its slope magnitude 211/44 lies within 1/20 of 24/5. The transverse displacement is even, the target is on the center column, and 44 ∤ 422, so the floor is regular. This seed's full-state replay period is 176, which does not divide 80. Whether some other seed always has period dividing the base P is a stronger question left open. The existence proof above does not require it.

**An open case for the general lemma.** Even a single stepped floor or L-shaped terminal foot leaves open how to guarantee a regular return to a new collar mark: the two-image calculation uses one horizontal floor spanning the cap. A further open configuration is the following. A short width-two bridge between two nonrectangular ends, with a retained chamber mark and the new center-column mark in its adjacent collar. The opposite end now opens into a chamber at the location where the proof used a flat floor; its return itinerary and signed phase require a separate argument. The two terminal-cap progressions settle this collar for their geometry only.
