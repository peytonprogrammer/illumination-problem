Advances: L1 — all-length illumination or a least-dark-area bound for the one-neck word LLLLRRLLLLRR.

**Verdict.** PARTIAL. A common integer carrier gives a uniform sufficient condition for a pair below the neck at both ends. The horizontal strip test and this carrier both miss an explicit parameter region. The full group-ring test still makes sense there, but I have no uniform positivity proof or area descent. This is the first failed implication, under the requested stop.

**Geometry and common-slope construction.** Put the neck at [0,L] × [0,g]. Let u,v be the chamber widths and take two marks at distances d,e from their respective mouths, at heights −a,−b with a,b≥0. Assume d,e are strictly between zero and the respective chamber width. The individual chamber floors and roofs may be anywhere strictly beyond the neck edges and containing their marks in the interior. Same-rectangle pairs see each other by convexity, and a segment whose open part stays in the common open band is legal. A general cover would also have to include marks in the neck and at regular mouth points when g>1.

Launch from both marks toward the neck and upward, reversing the target leg when joining. Use horizontal travel per unit rise c=n+η>0, with n a nonnegative multiple of 2 lcm(u,v). At height zero the horizontal advances an and bn fold away with direction preserved. The remaining distances to the mouths are D₁=d−aη and D₂=e−bη, provided 0<D₁<u and 0<D₂<v. Up to this point all side hits are on solid chamber walls below the neck. If 0<Dᵢ<gc, both rays cross regular mouths at heights Dᵢ/c, directed into the neck and upward. The solid chamber walls, the endpoint checks and the clearance of the band legs justify this folding even for different chamber floor depths.

The neck unfolds to a strip of period 2g. The two prefixes join at the required opposite vertical signs exactly when L/c=2jg−(D₁+D₂)/c for an integer j≥1. Thus use

    η = (L+d+e−2jgn)/(a+b+2jg).

Together with the strict inequalities above this proves a legal connection, with every intermediate neck reflection regular. In particular if L+d+e is divisible by 4g lcm(u,v) and (L+d+e)/2>max(d,e), take j=1 and n=(L+d+e)/(2g). This gives a uniform family of lit pairs with independent chamber depths. The divisibility is sufficient only. Reflecting vertically gives the upper-side version.

**First region without a uniform witness from these calculations.** Take u=6, v=12, L=6 and g=d=e=1. Give both chambers the vertical interval [−h,h+1], and take integral depths a≥1, b>23(a+1), with H=2h+1>11(b+1)+23(a+1). The two marks are one unit from their mouths and lie at different depths below the neck. These inequalities define an unbounded region. The horizontal span 24 shares a factor with both widths, so the earlier coprime-span argument does not apply.

* No horizontally monotone path crosses both mouths, even allowing a preliminary reflection at either solid outer end in the forward or reversed prefix. More precisely the horizontal travels to the first mouth in these four strip lists are A in {1,11} from S and B in {1,23} from T. Unfolded images of the unit mouth in either chamber are exactly (0,1)+kH because the aperture is centered. For a common absolute vertical speed r per unit horizontal travel we therefore need Ar congruent to ±(a+θ) and Br congruent to ±(b+φ) modulo H for some 0<θ,φ<1. Elimination requires B(±(a+θ))−A(±(b+φ)) to be a multiple of H. Its absolute value is strictly positive by b>23(a+1), and strictly less than H by the height hypothesis. This contradiction permits all horizontal-wall bounces in both chambers and any number of neck-wall bounces. It proves zero for all four of these strip tests.
* The displayed carrier fails for every n,j. For n≥24, η is negative with absolute value (2jn−8)/(a+b+2j). The target-side gate needs b|η|<11. But b(48j−8)≥11(a+b+2j) for j≥1, b>a and b≥2. For n=0 the mouth heights would be 1/c−a and 1/c−b. Their difference exceeds one.

The missing implication is that another itinerary has positive group-ring coefficient for every such h,a,b. Additional hits on the inner shoulder and outer wall before entering can alter A or B beyond the four tested values. An opposite-chamber pocket excursion or another choice of entrance sheet can change the carrier congruence as well. Neither calculation proves simultaneous failure of those tests, or darkness of any pair. The whole word remains open and there is no explicit least-dark-area bound here.

**Dependence on the word.** Convex visibility, reflection in a strip and the period 2g are local and apply in other words. The construction specifically uses two rectangular ends with solid side walls below the same band and a single intervening neck: two regular mouth crossings at matching phases finish the path. Extra mouths could interrupt either chamber prefix. The disjoint-interval obstruction further uses equal transverse chamber intervals, centered unit mouths and equal distances of the marks from them. It is an obstruction to the specified itinerary inside this word.
