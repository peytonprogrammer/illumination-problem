# Several isolated scars in one limiting chamber

This records the endpoint result reached before the switch to the whole-component question. Take a fixed square-tiled chamber, fixed distinct rational interior marks with a legal connection, and a bound k. A blocking set here consists of regular physical points distinct from the marks that collectively meet every legal connection.

**Finite obstruction.** There are only finitely many inclusion-minimal blocking sets of cardinality at most k, and every point in them is rational. In fact a finite family of witness paths tests all placements of at most k point obstacles.

Start with an empty partial set A. At each node A consists of rational points. If A blocks already, stop that branch. Otherwise take a legal witness avoiding A. After clearing denominators, the affine stabilizer of the unfolding fixing all lifts of A, the endpoints, and all physical corners has finite-index derivative in the integer modular group. The images of the witness give legal shots avoiding A in arbitrarily many distinct absolute slopes. Select k−|A|+1. Every extension of A by at most k−|A| points meeting all those shots must hit at least two with one new point. The shots have finitely many straight pieces. Distinct absolute slopes make each pair of pieces nonparallel and their intersections finite. All are rational by unfolding between rational endpoints. Branch on each intersection other than the marks or points already in A. At depth k a single further avoiding witness rules out that branch. The tree is finite. Any placement meeting all the tree's witness shots contains a true blocker at a terminal node. Conversely every true blocker hits them all. This is an existence proof; no paths were searched for. For a fixed rational lit pair this handles coincident point scars and multiple scars together. If the scars move affinely while the chamber and pair remain fixed, and the constant scars do not already block, only finitely many parameter values can block: one of the finitely many minimal sets must be covered, and each moving scar takes any prescribed position at most once. If several short slits shrink to a point set which does not block, a witness avoiding that set has positive clearance and persists under the usual surviving-edge hypotheses. A slit of positive limiting length and scars approaching the marks need separate treatment.

**A hidden convex lip really occurs.** Cut the named R34 room at x=4 and take its left eight cells. Their rows from bottom to top are (2,3), (1,4), (0,3), (1,2), each one unit high at ordinates −2 through 2. Its only interior lattice site is the original S=(2,0), and every boundary edge has unit length. The convex lip p=(4,0) is hidden from S: an unfolded connecting segment has nonzero even-even displacement, hence an intermediate lattice point of different parity, necessarily a forbidden vertex. Attach the named adjacent strip [4,7]×[−1,0] after translating it up by 1−t, 0<t≤1. The two-lip room has r=8 and tends to a point pinch at p. With T=(6,2/3−t), the direct chord works for 2/3<t≤1. At t=2/q for odd multiples q of three sufficiently large, vertical dilation by q gives primitive same-parity lattice marks and area 11q, hence BBBB by d69. This example disproves automatic convex-lip access within the reflex budget; it does not exhibit darkness in the open room. For these specific marks there is a shot at every positive t. Launch right and down with slope magnitude s=(t+2/3)/X, where X is total horizontal travel. For 0<t≤1/3 choose X=4+6j strictly between 2+4/(3t) and 4+8/(3t). This interval has length greater than 6 for t<1/3, and X=10 works at equality. Then t/4<s<t/2. Entry at x=4 is strictly in the mouth, and the bottom reflection occurs after horizontal travel t/s between 2 and 4. At travel 8 the first return to x=4, if needed, is above the mouth; subsequent returns climb. Folding the strip-end reflections takes X to x=6 at the prescribed ordinate, before reaching the top. For t>1/3 take X=6. Entry is again strictly in the mouth, the bottom bounce occurs before the right wall (2<t/s<5), and the ray ends before a return to the mouth. This illustrates why lack of a limiting access path alone cannot force darkness nearby.
