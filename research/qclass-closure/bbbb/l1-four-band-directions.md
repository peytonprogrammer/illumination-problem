# Unequal depths on the room double

Use developed coordinates parallel and transverse to a periodic direction, scaled to integral lattice units. Two clean open cylinders meet along a regular edge of length l. Orient its coordinate u identically on the two developed rims, with origins o₁ and o₂. Choose lifts at abscissas x₁,x₂ and positive transverse depths h₁,h₂ on opposite sides, with circumferences C₁,C₂. Include other occurrences of the edge and other lift choices as separate tests.

A chord crossing just this edge exists exactly when some 0<u<l satisfies

    (h₁+h₂)u ∈ h₂(x₁−o₁)+h₁(x₂−o₂) + gℤ,
    g = gcd(h₂ C₁, h₁ C₂).

Indeed its two parallel travels are o₁+u−x₁+iC₁ and x₂−o₂−u+jC₂. Dividing by their respective depths must give the same slope. Cross multiplication and Bézout give the displayed condition, and the two open legs stay in the clean cylinder interiors. This also works from an opposite rim with a legal inward germ. Smooth endpoints of artificial cuts can be tested separately. At equal depth one recovers the earlier rim formula. This is the room-double version of the reviewed [band-width test](twist-overlap.md).

For **every available chart** a miss therefore requires (h₁+h₂)l ≤ g and the indicated residue to miss the open interval. A direction with no adjacent incident cylinders imposes no inequality at all. The latter possibility can occur simultaneously in all four directions.

## A four-direction miss at unbounded prime area

Use the [stretched DB55 boxes](stretch-stability.md) of horizontal depth 5, with common top line H=15 and the usual unit mouth at heights (2,3). Leave the corridor length L variable as in the [corridor theorem](resonant-gluing.md). Take any prime p≥173, L=p−150. In local coordinates from the lower left corner of each box put both marks at (2,12). Their global abscissas differ by 5+L, an even number. Both marks are interior. The two boxes and their corridor have area p, four reflexes and twelve walls. The horizontal wall gcd is gcd(5,L)=1 and the vertical gcd divides gcd(15,2)=1. The prime-degree action is transitive and primitive. Each parity has an interior lattice point in the left box, hence a transposition at that site. Its canonical relation is non-discrete and therefore universal. In fact the four mouth reflexes occupy different parities, so the square of a site generator is a single 3-cycle. The same Jordan argument as in the [reviewed natural-action note](l1-natural-four-sites.md) applies.

Horizontally each mark belongs to its own upper band above y=3. Vertically each belongs to its own box band. In each orientation the physical projections of the two band closures are disjoint.

For either diagonal, seal each box temporarily. Unfolding its vertical sides gives mouth-wall images at X=5+10j in left local coordinates and X=10j in right local coordinates. A ray through (2,12) of slope ±1 has developed height 12±(X−2) there. Modulo 10 these heights are 5 or 9 on the left and 0 or 4 on the right. In the height-15 box the **closed** mouth has unfolded heights 30k±[2,3], whose residues modulo 10 lie in [2,3] or [7,8]. The sets are separated by at least one unit. Both horizontal launch senses and both diagonal slopes are included.

Consequently all sufficiently close parallel leaves stay inside their sealed box for all time. This includes neighbors on both sides if the marked leaf meets a corner. Every clean cylinder incident to the mark thus projects into the closed box: in the interior of a periodic cylinder the cyclic wall-crossing itinerary is locally constant, since a change requires a corner contact. Equivalently a transverse cylinder of leaves exiting the box must cross the open mouth on each interior leaf, including leaves arbitrarily near the mark if incident. Its limiting leaves would have to approach the closed mouth, contrary to the uniform residue separation. Here the box-exit itinerary can also be read directly on the sealed-box transverse coordinate: its endpoints are corner leaves, cut in the clean decomposition. The closed boxes are disjoint because L>0. Thus no same-cylinder or adjacent-cylinder chart, including a regular rim endpoint, can join the two marks in either diagonal decomposition.

Every room in this family is fully lit by the reviewed rectangular-chamber theorem: L>4·5. At p=173 the two marks are (2,12) and (30,12), and L=23. All four failures occur before a circumference gcd is tested. This is a symbolic specialization of the named stretch, with no discovery search.

**First open L1 case.** A natural-action BBBB room above area 20 with a same-parity pair outside the four single-edge charts still needs a different lighting mechanism or an area bound for actual darkness. The example settles the proposed implication even on that branch. The first structural feature left out by these charts is a passage through an intervening band. D112 handles that feature for rectangular end chambers. General end chambers remain open.
