Advances: L1 — the area-22 imprimitive branch reduces to a degree-11 sphere with four zeros and two distinguished branch poles. In a BBBB room, separation of the physical marks’ images in this quotient never supplies the separated-fold certificate. The marked external orbital has exactly 24 regular midpoint candidates. Which lift a straight quotient arc reaches remains decisive.

### (a) The quotient and its four fibers

Let f:Q→N be the quotient by the eleven blocks of two, and h:N→B the degree-11 map. N is a sphere by Riemann–Hurwitz applied to f from a sphere. The connected double cover f has a deck involution δ and exactly two distinct simple branch values b₁,b₂. Branching over an h-regular lattice site (local degree 2) or a zero (degree 3) would give local degree 4 or 6 upstairs. Branching away from the pillowcase poles is also impossible. Thus b₁,b₂ are poles of N, each with one smooth preimage. In the fold count c₂=2 and c₃=0, so **8=2z and z=4**. N has eight poles and twelve regular sites over the four pillowcase poles: q=2·11−2−2·4=12. The two branch poles can lie over the same base class. [d208, §4](../../illumination-generalization-2026-11-06/fable/directions-2026-11-09/d208-review-condition-i-low/review.md) · [Fold count](fold-converse.md)

The pole-by-pole description requires parameters; the dimensions alone do not specify the four branch permutations. For each base pole x write the cycle type of hₓ as 1^(pₓ) 2^(qₓ) 3^(zₓ), and let bₓ count the distinguished poles above x. Then

    pₓ + 2qₓ + 3zₓ = 11,    Σ(pₓ,qₓ,zₓ,bₓ) = (8,12,4,2),    0 ≤ bₓ ≤ pₓ.
    type of gₓ upstairs = 1^[2(pₓ−bₓ)] 2^[2qₓ+bₓ] 3^[2zₓ].

An unbranched point has two copies with the same local degree. At a distinguished pole two degree-one copies are replaced by one degree-two site. These formulas specify ramification, subject to the product-one, transitivity and room hypotheses; they are not a uniqueness claim for N.

**BBBB in this branch is exactly qₓ≥1 at every x.** If qₓ=0, every upstairs transposition there stays inside one two-sheet block, so its monodromy closure Bₓ refines the pair partition. If qₓ>0, a transposition over a regular N-site joins two different blocks. By d208 the external ordered pairs form one orbital, so the invariant equivalence generated by that transposition contains every external pair and is universal. In particular 1≤qₓ≤5 in the core. At most five of the twelve regular N-sites fit over the source class a. At least seven are off-class, so all three off-classes cannot be corner-only in N **even without BBBB**. This excludes the (2,11) row from case (ii) by its hypotheses. A different proper factor cannot rescue that row here: the only possible block size is two, and the stabilizer suborbits have sizes 1 (self), 1 (partner), 20 (external), so a sheet has a unique invariant partner. ### (b) What it means for the marks to coincide in N

Let I(S) be the set of f-images of the front and rear lifts of the physical interior mark S. The reviewed kernel lemma says δ commutes with the physical mirror and descends to a sign symmetry σ of the room (axial reflection or half-turn). Indeed δ preserves the corner circle on the double and descends to an isometry with diagonal sign derivative. For distinct physical marks,

    I(S) ∩ I(T) ≠ ∅   iff   T=σS,   in which case I(S)=I(T).

A common image of distinct lifts forces the two lifts to be deck partners; conversely applying δ to both lifts of S gives both lifts of σS. The fact that a *sheet* has a partner says nothing about an independently prescribed T. If T=σS≠S, the marks are off the fixed locus; each image set has two distinct regular points. Ramification at an interior lift would fix that mark, and collapse of its mirror lifts would also fix the physical mark. If both distinct marks instead have collapsed mirror images, the symmetry is axial and the marks lie on its common interior axis segment, so they see each other directly. [Degree-two symmetry and axis lemma](bbbb-split.md)

This gives a numerical distinction between the two mark cases. The marked union always contains the external orbital of valency 20. Its regular coincidences are precisely the two lifts of each of the twelve regular sites of N, hence **24 in total and 24−2qₐ≥14 off-class**. Indeed the transpositions there exchange sheets of different blocks, whereas the two smoothed branch poles give internal transpositions. The valency-one partner orbital is also marked exactly when T=σS: a sheet in the incident cell star of S must have its partner in the star of T. Its cell is then incident to both σS and T. These have the same parity because f is over B, and two vertices of a single unit cell of the same parity coincide. The converse follows by applying δ to the marked star. Thus d=20 for disjoint image sets, and d=21 for partners, with a separate degree-one component adding precisely the two smoothed branch sites. Neither count realizes a path. ### Exact surviving path condition on the 11-sheet quotient

Fix the source class a, one off-class x and one geometric state. Form the graph of the primitive arc a→x on N. Keep regular vertices and the distinguished poles b₁,b₂ if incident; delete other poles and all zeros. These are exactly the vertices with a regular preimage in Q. Retain edges between kept vertices. Each component K is a path, a cycle or an isolated vertex. For any chosen lifts s of S and t of T write u=f(s), v=f(t). The following test applies also to **distinct** u,v.

* If u,v lie in different components, this state fails for these lifts.
* If their common component meets a distinguished pole, its full preimage in the regular graph is connected. Each chain away from that pole lifts twice and the two copies meet at its single smooth preimage. A regular continuation through it switches sheets and projects as a reversal. Thus all choices over u,v connect.
* A path component missing both branch poles lifts to two disjoint copies. The lift from s of its unique subarc u→v selects one endpoint over v; the state passes precisely when that endpoint is t.
* A cycle missing the branch poles lifts to one cycle if it separates b₁ from b₂ on the sphere, and to two cycles otherwise. In the first case either target sheet is reached; in the second, transport along the cycle selects just one. This is because the character of the punctured double cover is odd around either branch value and even around both. Parallel edges forming a two-edge cycle obey the same rule.

For the proof, the inverse graph away from the two distinguished poles is an ordinary two-sheet cover. At a distinguished pole the two copies of its incident edge are attached at the regular degree-two vertex upstairs. This describes the entire inverse graph; each connected component upstairs has degree at most two, so a nonbacktracking subarc joins any two distinct vertices in it. Isolated vertices provide no connection between distinct lifts. [FC and degree-two specialization](../../illumination-generalization-2026-11-06/fable/directions-2026-11-09/d61-fold-converse/README.md)

In particular, for a deck-partner lift pair over **one regular u**, a two-traversal witness exists in this state exactly if u has an adjacent distinguished x-pole, or belongs to a two-edge cycle with odd transport. In the path case going out to a distinguished end and back costs twice its distance from u. In the cycle case a swap first returns over u after a full odd-monodromy circuit. A deck partner alone guarantees neither adjacency nor the short circuit. A physical pair of partners can also use the other target lift, whose image differs from u. For disjoint image sets, two legal arcs between the same regular u,v with opposite transports suffice, but one arc with the correct transport is enough. A target over a branch pole has no sign choice once its quotient component is reached.

**First open configuration.** In a positive normalized area-22, eight-reflex BBBB room of this branch, take a nondirect same-parity pair. In the disjoint-image case its marked degree is exactly 20, every class of N contains a regular site, and at least 14 marked external candidates are off-class. What remains possible by this argument is that for every state, class and lift choice the quotient endpoints are disconnected, or their common component splits and sends s to the other lift over v. For partners, the equal-image choices require a component meeting a branch pole or a cycle separating the two poles, and the cross-image choices still have the transport test above. These conditions specify the residual exactly by the reviewed FC equivalence. I have no proof excluding it in either case and no dark example. The double-cover graph test applies at any area with an inner degree-two factor; a general lighting theorem would additionally have to force a passing state.
