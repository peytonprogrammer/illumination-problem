Advances: L1 — commuting cycle translations determine the developed endpoint, but can lose both the sheet and the forbidden-corner label there. This already happens in an area-seven pinned room, the smallest possible area for two distinct interior pins. Its branched tier graph and its actual illumination can both be determined by hand, for every positive stretching of its four support levels on each axis.

### Which quotient preserves straight paths?

Fix a source copy and orient the developing coordinates. For two based cycles returning to the same signed cell, their derivatives are the identity. Consequently AB and BA have equal translation v_A+v_B. This statement holds for combinatorial walks before straightness is tested. It does not say that both words are cutting sequences, that an edge of the developed picture has the same legal continuation on every overlapping copy, or that all periods of the recurrent component lie in the subgroup generated by these two. For a specified initial sheet and a direction, continuation is deterministic until a forbidden point. The sequence of crossings of a rectangular grid downstairs can be a mechanical word; which of those crossings are walls, transmissions or fatal hits still depends on the transported sheet. [Template development](l1-witness-selection.md)

For the full lattice room there is a natural construction with this distinction built in. On the connected four-copy orientation surface X use the group P of **all** absolute translation periods. Integration from a base point modulo P defines a map to the period torus when P has rank two: two choices of integration path differ by a closed period. Two independent cycle vectors give a subgroup Λ of P. Integration modulo Λ is well-defined on X only if P is contained in Λ as well. Passing to an additional cover can make the integration well-defined, but does not remove the need to identify which lifts represent the endpoints. Even modulo P the map is generally many-to-one. In particular the sector at a reflex lift has total angle 6π, or three turns about its torus image. Slits can encode this if their banks carry the sheet gluing.

**Exact test for a proposed period displacement.** Project the segment to the period torus, start on the specified source sheet, and continue it through the actual edge gluings. It must avoid forbidden lifts along its interior and terminate at a compatible lift of T. Over a projected branch or marked value, use the actual incident germ: a legal regular lift can project to the same value as a forbidden lift. This test is necessary and sufficient by folding and unfolding. Equivalently, on the full marked origami a rational-direction witness lies on a common regular cylinder level or between the same consecutive forbidden points of a critical leaf. On a piece with cuts, one must also enforce its exit or gluing conditions. The d221 orbit is finite per integral realization; its proof uses the permutations of 4n squares at area n. It gives no uniform chart bound on that orbit or a positive incidence count for the two specified marks. ### Smallest pinned example and a wrong-sheet displacement

From the square [0,3]² remove the southwest and northeast corner unit cells (take the interior of the union of the seven remaining closed cells). The reflexes are (1,1) and (2,2). The marks S=(1,2) and T=(2,1) lie on two wall supports each, strictly inside the room. Their direct chord traverses the central cell. Every interior lattice mark requires all four incident unit cells. For two distinct marks these neighborhoods have union of size at least seven unless the marks are axis neighbors. In the latter case equality at area six forces the entire room to be their 3-by-2 rectangular union, which has no interior pins. This proves area minimality among orthogonal lattice rooms. Reflex minimality also holds: a rectangle has no pin and a one-reflex room has at most one internal wall level on either axis, hence at most one possible pinned position.

Every witness here is nonaxial since both coordinates change. In this tier graph all signed cell states belong to one recurrent component. Indeed one can follow a row to its boundary and back to the same cell with the horizontal sign reversed, preserving the vertical sign. The column move reverses the vertical sign. Therefore the directed graph can follow any cell adjacency and select the needed signs at its destination. This is a statement about graph connectivity; each proposed straight path still has to pass the gate tests. At the central cell with signs (+,−), pure row and pure column cycles are both simple graph cycles, of developed displacements (6,0) and (0,−6). The room thus meets the literal requirement that its witness live in a two-cycle component. The direct witness itself completes neither cycle. Requiring every witness to *use* both cycles is a stronger, unresolved request for which this example supplies no minimality claim.

The center cycles generate 6ℤ². A short row and a short column each have width or height two, giving periods (4,0) and (0,4) up to sign. To compute absolute periods one may base these loops at the central cell by transporting to them and back; the connecting paths cancel in the integral. Conversely every closed development returning to its initial signed copy has both translation coordinates even, by reflection in integral walls. Thus P=2ℤ². In particular even the proposed quotient by the **two central cycles** fails the path-independence test. The correct period torus has area 4 and X has area 28, so the torus projection has degree seven (away from branching it preserves area locally).

There is a direct hand check on this smaller torus. The displacement (1,−1) from S lifts to T through the central cell. The displacement (5,−1), in the same initial (+,−) sector, also projects from S to T modulo 2ℤ² and its open segment contains no lattice point, so it misses every projected corner in its interior. Starting on the ordinary physical copy it instead hits x=3 at (3,8/5), reflects in that wall and ends at **(0,1)**, a forbidden convex corner. The terminal torus value is the same as that of the regular target. Discarding all endpoints over that value would discard the legal chord as well. The error is detectable only by retaining the terminal sheet or an equivalent incidence label. The two directions here test the full period proposal; they need not belong to one prescribed pair of central-cycle counts.

**Large gaps in the example.** Replace 0,1,2,3 independently on each axis by any four strictly ordered integral levels. The two opposite corners of the central open rectangle corresponding to S and T remain interior marks of the room. The open diagonal remains inside that rectangle, so this pair is lit for all positive gaps, including simultaneous growth without ratio restrictions. This local proof does not need a torus-count estimate.

**First open L1 case.** In a branched recurrent component with a nondirect pinned pair and growing gaps, one must find a period displacement whose continued segment reaches the specified target sheet before a forbidden contact. This example rules out dropping that condition even when the displacement is primitive and the projected segment has no interior corner value. It leaves open both a uniform sheet-sensitive existence theorem and the stronger example where every witness must traverse two distinct return cycles. The general large-gap conclusion does not follow from commutativity.
