# Regular fibers and the first shot

## Orthogonal completeness

Use the standard pillowcase projection Q→B for a strictly simple orthogonal lattice room. A factor here has outer degree greater than one and includes Q itself. The degree-one base must be omitted: its four distinguished sites are poles. The following test applies to each nontrivial sheet cover, independently of the marks.

Fix a site x. Form a graph on the sheets by taking the two letters of every regular transposition of g_x as an edge and adding every monodromy translate of each edge. Its components give exactly B_x. For any invariant partition, a transposition crossing two blocks induces a regular two-cycle on blocks. Conversely a two-cycle of blocks must lift entirely to two-cycles upstairs, since local degrees upstairs are 1, 2 or 3. Thus having a regular point over x on **every** factor is equivalent to this graph being connected. If disconnected, its own component partition gives the offending quotient (possibly Q). This is precisely the reviewed BBBB hypothesis at x. In particular no stronger pointwise incidence information arrives by imposing it on all four graphs again. [Canonical factor proof](canonical-transfer.md) · [d94 review](../../illumination-generalization-2026-11-06/fable/directions-2026-11-09/d94-review-transfer/review.md)

**Attempt to construct the cylinder.** In a marking adapted to one primitive arc from a to x, draw just the two matchings of regular swaps of A and X, omitting fixed letters and three-cycles. An alternating cycle gives a closed leaf avoiding the marked corner stops: the repeated physical sequence returns after finitely many steps and, if needed, doubling resolves the tangent sign on the translation unfolding. A thin parallel neighborhood of the closed regular leaf is a cylinder. If the source and target incident sets meet that cycle, its core gives the requested path. A common open chain also supplies a legal segment between the marks, even though its extension ends at a stop. This distinction matters if cylinder existence is used as a necessary step. For same-site endpoints the reviewed test for either kind of connection uses powers of A³X³ with the lift labels in that same marking. [Arc dictionary and algorithm](cyclic-separation.md)

The connected graph above permits a *different* monodromy conjugator for every traversed edge. In the cylinder construction one marking determines both matchings, both endpoint labels, and every step. A graph path with independently translated edges does not construct such a marking. Even proving the associated group acts transitively on pairs of sheets would leave the word-to-single-arc implication to prove.

**A quantitative check using the full hypothesis.** The normalized d129 dumbbell with a=10 lcm(1,…,b), room area 20a+7 and four reflexes has every graph above connected by the normalized large-area criterion. Nevertheless from its marked S every nonvertical primitive direction with horizontal component of absolute value at most b, regardless of the vertical component, terminates at the same lower doorway lip before reaching T. Vertical rays stay in the first chamber. Increasing b defeats any preselected finite list of rational cylinders even with the every-factor condition. The legal first shot has direction (a+1,1) and one reflection. This excludes a direction menu fixed before the geometry is known. BBBB membership here uses the cited classification and was not independently recomputed. This example allows a geometry-dependent construction and is no counterexample to completeness. [Reviewed dumbbell argument](thin-gap-periods.md) · [d129 review](../../illumination-generalization-2026-11-06/fable/directions-2026-11-09/d129-review-gapper/review.md)

I have no proof of the completeness implication. The missing geometric assertion is simultaneous incidence of the two marked sets in one legal component for one arc. The Q-class and proper-factor closures, together with such an assertion for every interior lattice pair in BBBB, would cover the orthogonal interior lattice problem. The same-site calculation above handles the same-parity branch only. An interior-lattice conclusion alone does not discharge arbitrary real marks.

## Mixed walls: count the degree of a clean fiber before counting gaps

Let P have integral vertices and genuinely mixed axial and diagonal walls. Use physical lattice units. Let X be its eight-copy unfolding, T any translation torus quotient with derivative normalized to the identity, V the area of a fundamental parallelogram of T, and J a nonempty set of distinct torus sites whose entire preimages on X are forbidden physical corner lifts. A corner lift may be metrically regular. Write D_x for the sum of the local degrees of the corner lifts over x. For x in J this is the full degree 8 area(P)/V. Across all sites the total corner degree is 4(W−2): eight copies supply total angle 8π(W−2), and local degree is cone angle divided by 2π. Therefore

    area(P) ≤ V (W−2)/(2 |J|).

This is d145's clean-site count converted to physical area. It remains valid for a coarser period torus. Forbidden images alone do not suffice: every preimage of the site must be forbidden. [Degree accounting](clean-site-cost.md)

**From area to an axis bound.** If the horizontal span is an integer L, every strictly intermediate integer abscissa has positive vertical-section length, since the interior is connected and projects onto the open span. Its intervals have integral ends (all walls have slope 0, ∞ or ±1). For the trapezoidal integration formula use the one-sided section lengths, since vertical walls may cause jumps. At each strictly intermediate column both one-sided lengths are at least one: some interior point has an open disk there, and limiting endpoints on each side are integral. Section length is affine between successive integer columns. Summing those trapezoid areas gives area(P) ≥ L−1. The vertical span obeys the same bound. Hence every axis gap satisfies

    G ≤ floor(1 + V (W−2)/(2 |J|)).

This proof allows a diagonal wall to cut right across the slab. It needs no constant cross-section.

**Removing scale.** If the gcd of all anchored vertex entries is one, the corner-relative lattice Λ_rel has covolume at most two by the D₄ sign changes, coordinate swap and Bézout. The copy half-turn fixes every physical corner lift, so any relative path followed by the reverse of its half-turn image gives twice its period as an absolute period. Thus 2Λ_rel is contained in Λ_abs. The period lattice of any T contains Λ_abs, giving V≤8. Consequently area(P)≤4(W−2)/|J| and G≤1+floor(4(W−2)/|J|). At W=20 one clean site gives area≤72 and axis span≤73. Scale matters: dividing out the vertex content can take integral marked endpoints to rational coordinates. [Relative-lattice proof](rigidity-area.md) · [Half-turn and absolute periods](diagonal-periods.md)

**V-notch check.** Named B has vertices (0,0),(4,0),(4,4),(2,2),(0,4), area 12 and W=5. Its torus quotient has degree 3, so V=8·12/3=32 in those coordinates. The four clean sites give the area bound 32·3/8=12, with equality. Its scalar content is two; after dividing coordinates by two one gets area 3, V=8 and the same equality. The large period cell accounts for the four clean sites in this example. [V-notch quotient check](../../illumination-generalization-2026-11-06/fable/directions-2026-11-09/d143-review-perclass/review.md)

**Extension to a higher-genus intermediate translation quotient.** Suppose X→Y→T has inner degree k, genus(Y)≥2 and outer degree m. Here permit a weaker cleanliness requirement on Y: every point over J is the image of at least one physical corner lift, even if other preimages upstairs are legal. For each such Y-point choose one corner preimage. Its composite local degree is at least the local degree of the Y-point over T. Distinct Y-points use distinct lifts, hence m|J|≤4(W−2). Riemann–Hurwitz gives k≤(g(X)−1)/(g(Y)−1). In mixed rooms there is at least one lift per physical corner, so 2g(X)−2≤4(W−2)−W=3W−8. Thus in scalar-primitive units

    area(P) = kmV/8 ≤ 4(W−2) floor((3W−8)/(2(g(Y)−1))) / |J|.

For W≤20 this gives at most 1872/|J| even in genus two. This extension is a necessary size bound for that explicitly marked translation factor. It proves neither that trajectories downstairs lift legally nor that a dark pair has such a factor. It does not extend to arbitrary half-translation quotients without an orienting-cover and marking check. A torus with marked *images* only also cannot use this higher-genus argument, because its Euler characteristic gives no bound on k.

**Verdict.** The mixed-wall follow-on admits an effective clean-fiber area and gap bound in the correct period units. Orthogonal completeness still requires converting independent monodromy translates into one physically straight itinerary; the every-factor condition was already present in that problem. No discovery search or census was run.


## D155 normalization and scope

Here |J| counts distinct points of T, even when several belong to one symmetry orbit. In scalar-content-one coordinates V=8 can occur only for the checkerboard corner-relative lattice. After anchoring a vertex, the inverse similarity (x,y)↦((x+y)/2,(y−x)/2) then gives integral vertices and half the area. Marks outside the checkerboard lattice become half-integral. In units with corner-relative lattice ℤ² one has V≤4. Thus the clean-site bounds at W=20 are area≤36/|J| and axis span≤1+floor(36/|J|); the higher-genus upper bound becomes 936/|J|. Span refers to the axes of the chosen representative.

The V-notch attains the degree bound in every tested torus quotient with a clean point. The reviewed diagonal strip of span L and area L−1/2 places the general span inequality within 1/2 of sharp. D11 first sees a clean room at W=24 within its checked range N≤32. For W=20 the conditional bound in d11's canonical tile units can still require N≤144. A dark pair without a clean site has no size bound from this argument.
