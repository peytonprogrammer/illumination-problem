# Two winding parities in a degree-two room quotient

Let f:Q→N be a degree-two factor of the room double, δ its deck involution, F₁,F₂ its two fixed points and b₁,b₂ their images. The F's are regular on Q; the b's are poles on N. Choose source and target lifts s,t of interior lattice marks. First assume u=f(s), v=f(t) are **distinct and regular**. We give a wall-coordinate sufficient condition, its exact exceptional placements within one cylinder, and a finite characterization of global exceptions for a fixed lattice room. The fixed-room characterization has no area-independent complexity bound.

## A wall band giving both parities

Take an open horizontal band in the room whose chosen row component is the same interval (L,R) at every height in (d,e), with smooth vertical walls at L and R throughout. Rotate the description for a vertical band. Its double is an open flat cylinder D of circumference C=2(R−L). Suppose both marks lie in this component and the physical symmetry defining δ preserves the band. Two cases suffice to read the test off the walls.

* For reflection about the vertical line through the midpoint of (L,R), the lift exchanges room halves. Every pair with distinct regular quotient images has both winding parities in f(D).
* For a physical half-turn about ((L+R)/2,(d+e)/2), the lift preserves halves. Both parities occur in f(D) **exactly when the marks are not both at height (d+e)/2**. If they are both on that line, they already see each other horizontally in the physical room. The missing winding parity inside the band still matters when testing transport between specified lifts.

Here and below 'both parities' means that the relative loop of two legal quotient segments represents the nonzero class modulo two in the sphere punctured at b₁,b₂. This definition works for intersecting or winding segments as well as for an embedded bigon. It has the following direct check: from the fixed s the two lifts end at t and δt. Each yields a legal physical trajectory to the physical mark of its own endpoint; the segment ending at t lights the requested pair. We require the upstairs segments to avoid both F's so their projections are legal downstairs. Other legal upstairs paths may go through an F. ## Cylinder proof and exact local obstruction

More generally let D be any embedded open Euclidean cylinder on Q, free of room corners, invariant under δ and containing s,t. Since δ is a nonidentity orientation-preserving isometry with derivative ±I, in cylinder coordinates (X mod C,Y) centered at the middle height its restriction has one of two forms. For +I it is translation by C/2. For −I choose the X origin so it is (X,Y)↦(−X,−Y). This follows by lifting to the strip: boundary preservation precludes vertical translation in the first case and puts the reversal axis at the middle height in the second. Identity on the cylinder would imply identity globally. In wall-band coordinates the sign follows by using increasing horizontal abscissa on the front copy and decreasing abscissa on the back.

In the translation case D contains no F. Strip chords from s to representatives of t and δt stay inside and project legally. At equal heights use horizontal arcs.

In the reversal case both fixed points lie on the middle circle at X=0,C/2. For an intended endpoint z∈{t,δt} strictly on the other side of the middle circle from s, the winding-j chord crosses Y=0 at abscissa

    X_s + λ(X_z−X_s) + jλC  mod C,       λ=|Y_s|/(|Y_s|+|Y_z|).

For all these crossings to hit the forbidden two-point set requires λ=1/2: otherwise the subgroup generated by λ modulo one has more than two elements. At λ=1/2 all windings fail exactly when X_s+X_z=0 modulo C. Together with Y_z=−Y_s this says z=δs, contrary to u≠v. A chord on one side avoids both fixed points automatically. When just one endpoint is on the middle circle the only contact with that circle is the regular endpoint. Thus both lifts are reachable whenever at least one mark is off the middle circle.

When both are on the middle circle, every straight segment contained in the cylinder between them is horizontal (develop in the strip). Removing 0 and C/2 leaves two intervals, and δ interchanges them. Exactly one of t,δt lies in the interval containing s. This proves failure of the two-parity test *within f(D)*, since f⁻¹(f(D))=D. It says nothing against a second segment leaving f(D). This argument gives both endpoint sheets where [convex-cylinder.md](convex-cylinder.md) used an invariant cylinder on a different surface to obtain a single physical shot.

**Two-direction corollary.** If a half-turn room has horizontal and vertical invariant wall bands about its center and both physical marks lie in their overlap, distinct marks cannot both lie on both middle lines. Thus both parities are available for every choice of lifts with distinct regular quotient images. No aspect-ratio or area condition is needed.

## Which placements fail in the whole room?

For same-parity lattice endpoints there is also an exact fixed-room answer, which distinguishes a local middle-line obstruction from global failure. It can be specified before choosing the pair. It is a finite description by incidences on straight leaves, not a uniform list of coordinate types.

Mark all corner points and F₁,F₂ as forbidden on Q. Fix the subgroup of the affine group preserving these labels and fixing pointwise the finite set of regular lattice lifts over the four base poles. Use a finite-index subgroup of PSL(2,Z) realized by its derivatives, in lattice coordinates. To justify finite index, the square-tiled pillowcase cover is specified up to isomorphism by four permutations of finite degree. Base integer linear maps permute the finite set of such coverings (allowing the base poles to permute), and those preserving its isomorphism class lift affinely. These lifts permute the finite lattice and corner sets over the poles; fixing those sets pointwise costs only finite index. The F's lie in that set, since f branches only over poles of N and the entire tower is over the original pillowcase. The resulting group has finitely many orbits of primitive unoriented integer directions, since PSL(2,Z) is transitive on them. No commutation with δ is required for this test: both candidate endpoint sheets are fixed individually.

For one representative of each direction, cut its straight leaves at all the forbidden points. Record which marked regular lattice lifts occur on each common uninterrupted leaf (open interval or circle). Only leaves through a marked point need recording. Each such trace terminates at a cut or repeats: projecting a rational direction to the pillowcase gives a finite itinerary between base poles, and the continuation uses finitely many labeled lattice germs upstairs. Circle components have a positive-length return to the starting mark. For each eligible s let V(s) be the union of the other marks sharing a component with s in at least one representative; include s itself only if there is a circle return.

Then for every regular v with distinct u,v as above the exact obstruction is

    v ∈ f(E(s)),    where E(s) is the set of eligible marks missing from V(s).

Here eligible marks are the regular lattice lifts on Q outside the cuts. Equivalently, both parities occur exactly for those v whose **entire two-point fiber** is in V(s). Indeed each legal quotient path lifts to a straight Q segment avoiding all cuts. Its development between same-parity lattice points has a nonzero integral displacement up to sign, hence rational direction. A label-fixing affine return carries it to a representative direction with both endpoints unchanged. Conversely each recorded common leaf gives a legal segment and its endpoint identifies its transport. The two transports differ precisely when the endpoints upstairs differ. This also works for regular u=v if one asks for *positive-length* quotient loops: use the stated circle-return convention for the even transport. A branch endpoint has only one preimage and the two-sheet test does not apply. To fail this certificate for a physical pair every choice of source and target lifts with regular images must fail it (or there must be no applicable choice). Failure still permits physical illumination by one transport or by a segment through an upstairs F.

This finite description is related to the existing finite pure-braid/state dictionary. It handles all directions in principle and requires no chosen length cutoff; no cusp list was computed here. An area-independent geometric characterization of the global exceptional placements remains open. For a fixed regular source, their number among distinct regular projected targets is bounded non-effectively in terms of r: upstairs E(s) away from the source is contained in the ordinary shadow with the two additional F's forbidden. The [fixed-stratum shadow argument](shadow-budget.md) applies on the orientation double with their four order-zero lifts additionally marked. Projection to N cannot increase that count. This finiteness gives no effective classification across rooms.

**Primitive branch.** The two-sheet certificate uses a nontrivial homomorphism to C₂ on punctured N and two different lifts of v. A primitive room with n≥73 has no such proper intermediate factor. Changing a chord winding in a cylinder constructs a second trajectory, not an affine automorphism fixing the source. Thus this argument supplies no marked affine return in the primitive case.

**Next step.** In the half-turn branch, focus on marks outside a common invariant band. A concrete extension is to vary winding in a cylinder containing the source and attach the target across one regular boundary interval. The question is whether that interval admits legal continuations to both t and δt, possibly through different intervals. The single-band proof identifies the quantity to control: the attainable crossing positions modulo the circumference. Degree-two BBBB and primitive BBBB both remain open.
