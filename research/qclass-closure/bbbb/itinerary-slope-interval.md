# What an itinerary move must return

A useful move takes a source and a proposed route to an **open door** and returns an actual open slope interval or a pair of conflicting bounds. Hitting an exact fixed target is a separate equation. There is generally no open interval of slopes hitting that point with a fixed developed word.

## Exact construction for a specified move

Fix the sign of longitudinal travel and the initial transverse sign, and unfold the proposed visited rectangles, one copy per visit. Use coordinates with source (0,0), increasing longitudinal coordinate X and ray Y=sX. Traverse a transverse wall by reflecting the next copy in its developed wall line. At a door attach the neighboring copy without reflection. At a longitudinal turn reflect the next copy as well, so X still increases along the developed ray. Split a side at true corners and changes between open doors and solid wall. Keep the entry and exit **subsegments** for every visit, including each return to a partially open side. Coordinates below refer to developed copies.

For a nonaxial ray and a nondegenerate chart, clip every prescribed gate to X>0. A visible open segment with endpoints (a,b),(c,d) at positive abscissas contributes the open interval between b/a and d/c. At X=0 use its one-sided slope limit; omit empty or radial windows. Require the ray to point inward at scheduled entries and outward at scheduled exits in each unfolded rectangle. Include the initial sign restriction. These orientation checks depend only on the quadrant. The same rule covers horizontal sides, vertical sides and the proposed turn. For an axial ray test the chord directly. Events exactly at artificial cuts through a smooth physical point can use a separate limiting chart or omit the artificial cut. An actual stopping corner never serves as an open gate.

Take the maximum lower bound l and minimum upper bound u. **If l<u, the move returns (l,u).** Every slope in it realizes the prescribed prefix. In a convex rectangle a nontangent oriented line that hits the specified entry and exit in their relative interiors meets them in that order and cannot first exit through some other side. Induction through the copies proves the assertion and enforces the solid-wall guards. Conversely such a prefix must satisfy each interval. If l≥u, gates attaining those bounds (possibly the sign restriction) certify failure of that chart. This construction needs a combinatorial word with specified unfolded copies, but no previously successful slope. Changing an itinerary preserves the prefix bounds and requires recomputing the suffix bounds in its new copies. Nothing guarantees positive width after that recomputation.

**Width and terminal placement.** For an integral source and lattice room, end the prefix on a transverse door at integer developed distance M≥1. Clip the copies to 0≤X≤M before forming bounds. Finite endpoints of the resulting slope intervals are ratios of integers with positive denominator ≤M: clipping axis-aligned segments at 0 or M preserves integrality. Hence a nonempty bounded chart has width ≥1/M² by the integer determinant of its two distinct endpoints. Their mediant supplies an interior rational slope of denominator ≤2M. A one-sided unbounded interval offers arbitrary finite width. This is a bound in terms of actual accumulated travel, which may grow with revisits. If the last door enters a full rectangular cap chamber of transverse height H, the d157 terminal-cap lemma now gives a sufficient condition **B>4HM²**, where B is the longitudinal travel including the cap turn and return to target depth. A constructed interval gives the sharper test B(u−l)>4H or, when narrower, the exact indexed test. This corollary still needs some legal prefix to that particular door. ## Exact interval through the R22 edit

There is also a convenient way to certify a joint **physical wall-word** interval from one saved path without enumerating directions. Up to integer longitudinal travel M, a physical corner collision can occur only at a slope b/a with integers 1≤a≤M, since all unfolded corners are lattice points. A horizontal-wall contact moving across the terminal section can change the wall word only at a slope of that same form. Vertical contacts on the terminal section are handled by their one-sided convention. Between consecutive such slopes the ordered physical reflection word is constant whenever realized: inside the fixed travel slab a nontangent ray can change its next wall only at an endpoint or at the terminal section. There are finitely many grid crossings for slopes in a bounded interval. At a central rational slope with no corner hit and an interior terminal point, openness joins the two neighboring intervals. Extra artificial door endpoints on the central ray may require different subcharts on its two sides.

For R22-L1-005 the saved start S=(0,0), developed travel M=18 and slope −2/3 terminate at T=(2,2) with final physical velocity (18,12). The adjacent fractions of denominator at most 18 are −11/16 and −11/17: the determinants with −2/3 have magnitude one, and the respective denominator sums are 19 and 20. Thus the whole open interval

    −11/16 < s < −11/17,     width = 11/272,

is a certified physical reflection chart in the edit. This is a guaranteed interval, not a maximality claim. Its transverse arrival at physical x=2 is y=2−18(s+2/3), ranging from 2−6/17 to 2+3/8. The exact target equation selects s=−2/3, on both sides of which the physical shot stays legal. A chamber cut through (1,−2) can change its crossing order there, since that point is now interior. This is why the chart claims a common physical reflection word, not a single list of artificial grid-edge crossings. In original R22 the central slope really hits the old reflex (1,−2); the same open physical chart containing that slope is impossible. The first-shot existence in the edit came from its archived witness, not from the denominator bound. [Edit and visit necessity](cap-insertion-law.md)

For a smooth cap with an actual specified upstream door word, the direct R22 top-arm example needs no saved seed. Its transverse gates at longitudinal distances 1,2,3,4 impose respectively 0<s<1, 1<2s<3, 1<3s<3 and 1<4s<3 for the branch with no side reflections. Clipping returns **(1/2,3/4)**. The return target requires 4s=2, at the excluded lower endpoint. In DB55 the saved turned word instead gives **−37/8+(−5/56,3/56)**; the exact target slope is strictly inside despite B·width=16/7. These are concrete, opposite outcomes of the same interval-and-endpoint test. [D157 review](../../illumination-generalization-2026-11-06/fable/directions-2026-11-09/d157-review-doorway/review.md)

**Remaining obligation.** The two-rectangle theorem constructs its own window for the full slab profile it covers. For an arbitrary BBBB chain, the calculation here constructs or rejects a window for each specified move. It gives no theorem selecting a move whose interval is nonempty and whose developed target lands inside. Thus the general itinerary-change theorem and W≤20 conclusion remain open.
