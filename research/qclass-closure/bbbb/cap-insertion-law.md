# What a cap insertion changes

**Verdict.** Inserting a reflection reflects the developed suffix and changes the slope needed to hit its endpoint. It does not independently reverse the signs of downstream door constraints with the earlier crossing positions held fixed. R22-L1-005 is a normalized strictly simple 20-wall BBBB room that retains the R22 obstruction for **every** modified itinerary avoiding a specified pair of bottom edges. It has a legal shot through the changed region. Thus even an unlimited number of cap insertions confined away from that region cannot repair this pair.

## Exact system and insertion rule

For a fully specified word, develop each chamber copy into the plane starting with the source copy fixed. The unknown trajectory is the straight segment from S to the developed target T'. For each prescribed crossing require intersection with the relative interior of the developed window, in the prescribed order. For a bounce require a solid smooth side, with the two neighboring copies related by reflection there. Require every open leg to stay in its chamber without hitting unused boundaries. These tests characterize legality, including corners; longitudinal words with unspecified lateral reflections represent a union of such systems. Signs record the orientation of the *same* segment after folding. They are not independent Boolean variables.

Algebraically, if P is the prefix isometry, E the suffix isometry and R reflection in the proposed wall line in local coordinates, then the new target is PRE(T)=(PRP⁻¹)PE(T). The same conjugated reflection acts on the developed suffix windows. It leaves the prefix *geometry* fixed. Inserting this letter gives a physical itinerary only after checking the new wall guard and continuity through the proposed copies. For a terminal turn in the longitudinal x direction, take the source as x=0, old target abscissa D and cap abscissa C>D. The new target abscissa is D'=2C−D. Its transverse image Y is unchanged. An earlier door at distance A now sees heights

    y_old = S_y + A(Y−S_y)/D,       y_new = S_y + A(Y−S_y)/(2C−D).

For A>0 and Y≠S_y these differ. If instead one fixes the old slope, earlier crossings persist but hitting the new developed target needs a fresh equality. The longitudinal velocity changes sign at the physical cap, while transverse velocity does not. The DB55 right turn works by changing the total travel from 8 to 16 and hence retiming the first doorway from one half to one quarter. [Saved construction](terminal-cap-placement.md)

**Exact meaning of a pinned terminal cap on a specified incoming chart.** Use A,L,t,H,β,u,I from the terminal-cap lemma, B=A+2L−t, C=A+L. For each σ=±1 the admissible integers k form the consecutive interval defined by (σu+2Hk−β)/B∈I. The prescribed completion (arrival after the cap) fails on this chart exactly when for each sign the interval is empty, or it consists of one integer k and

    β + C(σu+2Hk−β)/B ∈ Hℤ.

This assumes a full cap with stopping corners at both ends and no further windows before the target. The reason a longer interval cannot fail is that adjacent indices move the cap height by 2HC/B strictly between H and 2H. A union of prefix charts admits no such completion iff each chart fails. An earlier visit to the target is already a legal shot and need not complete the prescribed word. For a partial cap, replace the corner test by membership in the actual open solid wall window; whole intervals can then be forbidden. The criterion depends on source, target, incoming chart and geometry, not just cap corner parity. ## A 20-wall BBBB control with the same resistant cap routes

Use R22 and the named d8 edit R22-L1-005, which adds the one cell [0,1]×[−3,−2]. Both keep S=(0,0), T=(2,2), the top opening 1<x<3 at y=2, and the far cap at y=3. The cap ends (1,3),(3,3) are odd–odd convex corners in both rooms. From S, launch with horizontal travel s per unit upward vertical travel, 1/2<s<3/4. At y=1 the abscissa s is strictly inside (0,1); at y=2 the abscissa 2s is strictly inside (1,3). At the far cap 3s is strictly inside (1,3), and on return the abscissa is 4s∈(2,3) at the mouth. There are no side bounces. Thus a whole open beam reflects off the cap smoothly and comes back through the door in *both* rooms. At s=2/3 it hits the cap at (2,3) and returns at (8/3,2). A prescribed insertion can fail at the target even though physically executing the turn is easy. D157 proves that, allowing arbitrary lateral bounces, no upward launch with just this vertical reversal reaches T on the return: the outward passage at y=2 would have to hit T already.

**Necessary visit for all successful words.** Define Γ to be the union of the two closed edges of the added cell that touch the old room:

    Γ = ([0,1] × {−2}) ∪ ({1} × [−3,−2]).

Every legal S→T shot in the edit meets Γ. Until its first meeting, a path cannot enter the added cell, and the old and new geometries agree at every contact it makes. A path avoiding Γ would therefore be legal in dark R22. This argument applies to any number and placement of insertions, revisits, lateral windings or directions whose resulting path avoids Γ. In particular **all upper-room repairs fail**. More precisely, an edited shot must enter the open added cell or visit the old reflex (1,−2). At an interior point of either removed edge a transverse crossing enters or leaves that cell. The other two old endpoints (0,−2),(1,−3) are now smooth wall points; a nonaxial shot contacting either has an adjacent open leg in the new cell. An axis-parallel shot cannot join S and T in an orthogonal room. The third endpoint (1,−2) is now interior and allows corner continuation. This is a necessary alternative, not a sufficient entrance test.

Exact checks on the named cell sets give canonical degrees (1,5,15,5) in R22 and **(1,1,1,1)** in R22-L1-005. The edit has 16 cells, W=20 and edge gcd one on each axis. The saved physical displacement (18,−12) first reaches (1,−2), then arrives legally at T in the edit; in R22 it stops at (1,−2). Thus the obstruction for paths avoiding Γ is genuine even with all four canonical relations universal. On R22 itself total darkness excludes every S→T itinerary; neither here nor in d154 is there a classification of totally dark BBBB rooms at W≤20. ## Consequence for the proposed chain theorem

D157 reviewed the two-rectangle bound and the conditional terminal-cap lemma as sound with gaps. The flip implication needed to compose them fails. A local cap can share its entire upper geometry with a dark room and admit smooth bounces even in a lit BBBB room. D145's complete-fiber count is global; it supplies no path from the source to the portion of the geometry which distinguishes the rooms. The geometric question now exposed is **reachability through a specified opening or newly regular site with a continuation to the target**. A one-sided accessibility result at a cap cannot establish it. The example gives a 20-wall BBBB room with a region resistant to *every move staying there*, not a 20-wall room resistant to every move everywhere. All-itinerary resistance would itself be a dark pair, which is still open in this domain. No discovery search was run.
