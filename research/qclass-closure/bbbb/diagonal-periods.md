# Mixed diagonal parities and the absolute torus

**Verdict for review.** Every integral room using the four allowed directions has a canonical45 representative under similarity. For a scalar-primitive room with both parities of diagonal intercept, the least possible canonical area is twice its given area. In that parity class the index between absolute and corner-relative periods is exactly two, and all integral endpoint lifts of a same-parity pair lie over one point of the absolute torus. These results specify the census gap and the degree target. They give no upper bound on degrees of genuinely dark rooms.

## Similarity and census units

Apply F(x,y)=(x−y,x+y)=(X,Y). Old axial walls x=a and y=b go to X+Y=2a and Y−X=2b. Old diagonal walls become integral axial walls. Every new diagonal thus has even intercept. This is a rotation and uniform enlargement by √2, so it preserves corner hits, legal billiard segments, darkness, wall count and strict simplicity. Area doubles. If old lattice marks have the same checkerboard parity x+y mod 2 (in particular if they have the same coordinate parities), both images are A points or both B points. Opposite checkerboard parities give an A–B pair outside d11's same-class check.

If old diagonal intercepts have the same parity, a single integral translation makes them all even. With both parities present, translation and lattice isometry preserve their difference of parity. Anchor a vertex, and assume the gcd of all anchored vertex entries is one. The vertices cannot then all have x+y even: different intercept parities give vertices in both checkerboard colors. For a similarity mapping these wall directions to the allowed directions, its linear part, up to a signed coordinate permutation, is t Id or tF (reflection included), t>0. In the first case integrality of the image vertices requires t integral by scalar primitivity. Odd t preserves the parity obstruction, so canonicalization this way requires t≥2. In the second case the gcd of the entries of all transformed anchored vertices is one: it divides two by adding and subtracting the image coordinates, and an odd entry occurs. Hence t is integral here as well, and t=1 works. Anchoring removes all translation freedom except an integral shift of the anchored image. This proves minimality in area, including similarities not initially assumed integral.

For the supplied M, twice the shoelace area is 27, so the least canonical representative has area 27 and **54 half-tiles**. The old diagonal offsets 3 and 6 certify the obstruction. M itself is convex, so all its interior pairs see each other directly. More generally the existing d11 A/B cutoff N≤32 covers all such same-checkerboard pairs via F when the original area is ≤8. If one independently proved a physical-area bound of 20, then representation of all such pairs by this route would need canonical A/B inputs up to N=80, restricted to images of the original rooms. No census was run. [D11 tiling and pair convention](../../illumination-generalization-2026-11-06/fable/directions-2026-11-09/d11-census-45/README.md)

## Absolute period lattice from the actual mirrors

Choose an integral origin O at a vertex where an axial wall meets a diagonal one. In coordinates relative to O their linear reflections generate D₄. Let G be generated by affine reflections in all wall lines. The absolute period lattice of the eight-copy unfolding equals the translation subgroup of G. For one inclusion develop a generic based loop across walls, avoiding all corner lifts; it ends in the starting copy and its holonomy is the translation of the word. Conversely realize any reflection word with linear part I by a continuous path through the indicated open sides in successive labelled copies. Each copy is connected, so the interior arcs exist; close at the same interior basepoint in the original copy. Straightness of this topological path is unnecessary for computing a period. Since the two walls through O already generate linear D₄, the translation subgroup is the additive span of the D₄-orbits of the translation terms of the remaining reflections: multiply each affine reflection by its linear part to obtain its pure translation, then collect terms in any word.

The translation terms of x=a and y=b are (2a,0) and (0,2b). Those of x+y=k and x−y=k are respectively (k,k) and (k,−k). Every generator therefore has even coordinate sum. In the two-parity case some k is odd even after anchoring. The corner-relative lattice is ℤ²: by scalar primitivity and D₄ symmetry it contains 2ℤ², and some anchored corner difference has coordinates of opposite parity, whose D₄ orbit spans both coordinates mod 2. The half-turn lemma gives 2ℤ²⊆Λ_abs. The odd diagonal translation term adds (1,1) mod 2. We conclude

    Λ_abs = {(a,b)∈ℤ² : a+b even},   Λ_rel=ℤ²,   I=2,
    d_abs=4 area(P),                   d_rel=8 area(P).

In particular M has d_abs=54 and d_rel=108. Its canonical similarity has exactly the same two degrees. Every developed endpoint lift has position kS+v relative to O with k∈D₄ and v∈Λ_abs. Modulo Λ_abs this is just S: D₄ preserves coordinate sum mod 2. Similarly for T. Same-checkerboard marks thus put all sixteen lifts over one of two torus sites; both sites are fixed by the quarter turn. All forbidden corner lifts project into those same two sites, and both sites occur among the corners. This statement is about corner images; a regular corner lift need not be a branch point.

Consequently an absolute-degree proof in the mixed-intercept class must show that darkness is impossible for d_abs>80 to obtain the physical-area-20 bound. It must handle source and target in the **same torus fiber**, so any test requiring their projected sites to differ cannot prove it. Conversely a coordinate calculation settles the canonical, scalar-primitive c=1 branch: after anchoring all diagonal offsets are even, hence Λ_abs⊆2ℤ², and the half-turn gives equality. There d_abs=2 area(P), so its target is 40. The distinction is exact. No degree bound for darkness follows from the two-site support, as the number of regular sheets over a site has not been bounded.

**Directional consequence for cylinder avoidance.** Use (1,1),(1,−1) as the absolute lattice basis. The two corner sites differ by (1/2,1/2) in these coordinates. A primitive closed torus leaf of direction (p,q) based at the marked site encounters the other corner site exactly when p and q are both odd: the only nonzero point of order two on this circle is its half-period. Thus two of the three primitive congruence classes mod 2 avoid the other site's entire corner fiber. In either such direction, break the lifted leaves at the forbidden sites over the marked torus site. If a physical corner there has angle aπ/4, its gcd(4,a) lifts contribute exactly a outgoing longitudinal germs altogether (each has cone angle 2π a/gcd(4,a)). Hence there are exactly the sum of these a values over the visited site many directed corner-to-corner chains starting there, with regular cycles possible in addition. This counts outgoing stopped chains, not hidden points. If a source and a target lift occur on one open chain or cycle they are joined by its subsegment. The remaining degree question is whether these direction classes can keep all eight source lifts separated from all eight target lifts as the number of regular sheets increases. A bound on the number of chains alone does not force a meeting of the two specified sets.
