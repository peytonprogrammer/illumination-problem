# What a direction class remembers

**Verdict.** Classwise separation gives a finite invariant on a *marked direction and a lift*. Descending that invariant to a fold of the room is a stronger assertion. The implication already fails for an individual class in a named 20-wall room. The simultaneous implication for a physically dark pair remains open. For the existing Par, Cut and Rig definitions, the proposed follow-on is already Theorem F in the orthogonal rational-interior setting.

## A finite invariant and its exact descent test

Take the half-translation double Q of an orthogonal lattice room over the unit pillowcase. Fix a parity site a, let V be the regular points above it, and take all lifts of the two distinct same-class interior lattice marks. Fix a nonzero primitive-vector parity c. For an unoriented primitive direction v of parity c, partition V into sets on the same straight leaf after cutting at every physical corner. Call the equivalence relation E_v. A regular leaf has unique continuation in both senses, so membership of two distinct lifts in one part is exactly visibility along that leaf. In particular, a regular lattice point in the interior of a saddle connection belongs to a part. Testing cylinder interiors alone loses such segments. The saved cov446 control has a visible horizontal chord despite failing the open-cylinder test in every direction ([d72](../../illumination-generalization-2026-11-06/fable/directions-2026-11-09/d72-cylinder-test/README.md)).

There are finitely many relations E_v, even without a Veech-group computation, since V is finite. A constructive indexing is by cusps of the finite-index subgroup of Γ(2) that lifts and fixes V pointwise and preserves the forbidden corner set. Indeed the covering of the punctured pillowcase has finite degree and hence finitely many possible labeled monodromy tuples. Stabilize its isomorphism class, lift the maps, then take the kernel of the finite action upstairs on the marked fibers and project back to Γ(2). The projected subgroup still has finite index. Its affine returns identify the cut leaves and fix their labels. Representatives of its cusp orbits therefore suffice. Write E₁,…,Eₖ for the resulting relations in class c.

* For a fixed lift pair, class darkness means it is absent from the **union** of the Eᵢ. For physical darkness in this class the condition applies to every source–target lift choice.
* A label on V alone that is constant on every legal class-c segment can separate a given lift pair **if and only if** the pair is absent from the **equivalence relation generated by the union**. Proof: every such label is constant on a chain of segments even when the chain changes direction. Conversely, the connected-component label of the union graph has the required property. A single label separates the two entire endpoint sets precisely if no component meets both.
* A label allowed to depend on the cusp always exists for a class-dark pair: take the Eᵢ-component at cusp i. This is an exact finite record of visibility for that room. Its construction supplies neither a map on sheets nor a bound uniform in area. Even a label independent of the cusp would still need compatibility with monodromy to define a quotient cover. This test concerns endpoint labels constant along segments. It does not classify invariants on developed itineraries that retain extra state.

Thus taking a join of partitions introduces broken trajectories. For example the partitions 12|3 and 1|23 both separate 1 from 3, but their join does not. This illustrates the logical issue only. A counterexample to the actual *class-local fold inference* is available without assuming that this abstract example is realized.

## Named obstruction to a class-local proof

In cov4181 take S=(2,3), T=(4,3), and a=(0,1). D66 gives a complete pure-braid orbit verdict at target d for all four lift pairs, no recognized certificate there, and direct visibility in class b through (3,3). The room has 20 walls and has a Q-type class as well as B-type classes. It is a control for a proof on all rooms, not a BBBB counterexample. [d66, named pair](../../illumination-generalization-2026-11-06/fable/directions-2026-11-09/d66-pair-level/README.md)

Here is the direction-to-arc implication needed to interpret that negative verdict. Starting above a, the projection of a primitive step v is an embedded pillowcase arc from a to x=a+v mod 2. In the plane two internal parameters 0<u,t<1 project to the same point only if (t−u)v or (t+u)v is in 2ℤ². Primitivity gives t=u in the first case and excludes the second. The next step traces the arc backwards in the pillowcase, so a legal trajectory between a-lifts traces a walk along this single arc with regular intermediate vertices upstairs. The arc is in the pure mapping-class orbit of the reference straight arc with those endpoints: complete the two primitive vectors to oriented integral bases with matching reductions modulo 2 (add the first vector to the second column if needed). The change of basis belongs to Γ(2), acting about a. Thus a geometric witness in the primitive first-step class gives a witness in the d61 arc graph tested by d66. This implication uses the first step, irrespective of where the midpoint of the entire segment falls. [d61, arc model](../../illumination-generalization-2026-11-06/fable/directions-2026-11-09/d61-fold-converse/README.md)

It follows that all directions with first step to d fail in this example, although the defined class certificate fails there too. A completeness proof for physically dark pairs must use a hypothesis absent here, such as darkness in the other two classes or the BBBB condition. This example alone cannot decide either restricted implication.

## Why the orbit-closure argument stops

Apisa–Wright Theorem 3.6 assumes a translation surface outside the torus-cover case. For finitely blocked marks its conclusion allows two periodic points or zeros as an alternative to identification on the minimal half-translation quotient. Even on its stated domain the theorem does not unconditionally give a fold. The authors explicitly discuss finite blocking on torus covers. [Apisa–Wright, Theorem 3.6 and §4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1708.03411)

On a square-tiled surface with rational marks, rescale the grid to include the marks and branch values among its vertices. Finite-index returns preserve this finite marked grid fiber, and a further finite-index subgroup fixes the marks. Passing to Γ(2) retains a rank-one closed arithmetic orbit with the marks. Hence periodicity is automatic here, including for visible pairs. Algebraicity of the marked orbit closure supplies no additional deformation that changes the relative lifts. D93's staircase block calculation verifies the fold for R22. It does not construct blocks from separation in another room.

## The follow-on and the remaining theorem

Theorem F already allows different Par, Cut and Rig certificates in the different classes and excludes a fully certified pair of rational interior points in a strictly simple orthogonal room with at most 20 walls. Its proof combines structural reductions and finite computations. Its write-up identifies the review status of the constituent steps. Neither mixed 45° rooms nor a newly defined itinerary invariant falls under that statement. R22 supplies a positive control at 22 walls. [Theorem F, §3.3](../../illumination-generalization-2026-11-06/fable/directions-2026-11-09/d70-writeup/RESULTS.md)

**Next theorem.** In a BBBB orthogonal room with at most eight reflexes, prove that for each same-class interior lattice pair the union of the single-direction leaf relations over the three parities meets the product of the two physical lift sets. For the defined certificates, d66 shows that this simultaneous assertion in the open core is equivalent to the lighting goal there. The join test above explains why a proof by classwise connectivity cannot establish it. No lower bound or new finite census follows from the reduction.
