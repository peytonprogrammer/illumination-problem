# Portal degree at a reflex graze, and reflection across a cap

**Decision.** The local cone does not give a cancellation lemma for a fixed pair of marks. At the saved 43/33 graze, other launches recover the missing target phase as a *limit* after round trips. Their multiplicities differ across the graze, and every launch at the exact slope hits a corner before the target. A finite-family crossing formula has an explicit jump term; there is no general length threshold from discarding it. Reflection across a cap does give a positive theorem for arbitrary fixed junction geometry.

## What a degree calculation must include

On each regular one-traversal branch the lifted mismatch F has positive derivative L+a+b. More generally cut a path with 2j+1 corridor traversals at its **last** forward exit from the left chamber. Follow the source through j full round trips first; follow the target backward only until its first mouth. The derivative of their final mismatch on a fixed itinerary is (2j+1)L plus the sum of all absolute horizontal chamber travels. This follows by unfolding, since signed vertical displacement grows by horizontal travel times the change in slope, and the vertical sign at the last door is retained. This representation counts each candidate with a specified traversal number once per launch choice.

For finitely many piecewise affine branches on an interval, at a test residue θ away from endpoint and jump-limit values, put H(u)=floor((u−θ)/(2w)). For each regular increasing piece (a,b), the number of hits is H(F(b−))−H(F(a+)). Adding the pieces of a branch gives its endpoint change **minus** the sum of H(F(s+))−H(F(s−)) at the excluded grazes. Sum over the launches. One can instead work with open-interval counts at a limiting equality; illegal endpoints must be excluded. This is the degree identity before any cancellation hypothesis. Summing over unbounded j requires a convergence or normalization argument; simply adding the positive derivatives normally gives an infinite sum even in a periodic cylinder.

At a reflex corner the room double has cone angle 3π. The two ways of missing the apex leave in directions separated by 2π around the cone (angles +π and −π relative to the incoming radius). They are **distinct** outgoing germs. The cone pairs incoming and outgoing sectors if the entire transverse section is varied. It gives no second ray from the marked source realizing the other incoming sector at the same slope, and no identity of its later portal phases. Reversing a ray generally puts its origin at the door, not at the prescribed source. A degree for a complete transverse interval therefore cannot simply be evaluated on four marked launches.

## Exact control over every traversal number at 43/33

Use the marked room, launch conventions and width w=1 of [general-gluing.md](general-gluing.md). Label launches by horizontal and vertical signs in the order ++,+−,−+,−−. Use units of 1/33 for signed phase modulo 66. The first forward corridor contributes 172 units. All four target backward first-hit phases are regular at the central slope and are respectively −1,−25,5,−23. For the previously prescribed target launch −−, the required source exit phase is therefore 49 modulo 66.

| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| ++ | 37 | 37 | 5 / 11 |
| +− | 47 | 53 | 11 / 17 |
| −+ | 57 | 57 | 17 / 11 |

For the first traversal alone, the jump 47→53 skips 49. **None of the other three launches compensates** at that target phase. Allowing repeated crossings changes the answer. Below the graze, launch −+ reaches 49 after **five** round trips. Above it, launch ++ reaches 49 after **seven**, and −+ after **three**. These are the first occurrences for those launches. Each repeats with the cycle length in the table. Thus the limiting residue is represented on both sides. Over 935=5·11·17 consecutive round-trip counts per launch, its multiplicity is **55 below and 170 above**. Even assigning a fixed sign ±1 to each launch cannot equate the single contribution of magnitude 55 with two contributions of magnitude 85. This conclusion is for the specified backward target launch and fixed launch weights.

Over *all* four backward target launches, limiting exact-match counts in that block happen to agree, **450 on each side**. The full boundary-phase distributions still differ: at mismatch residue 4/33 there are 450 below and 335 above. Agreement at zero alone does not identify the two boundary chains or create a legal match at a puncture. The direct central-slope traces from ++,+−,−+,−− stop respectively at (9,2),(3,4),(1,0),(1,4) before reaching T. Thus none of the limiting matches listed above is a legal central-slope shot. This comparison does not exclude a differently weighted, nonlocal degree theorem or nearby witnesses with traversal counts tending to infinity.

**Verification of the one-sided limits.** At central slope 43/33 start a parallel ray at (x,y+sign(dy)·δ), with δ=±1/132 on the two sides. In a development, every physical corner and mouth endpoint has integer coordinates. At a potential contact on a vertical integer line the central developed ordinate is a multiple of 1/33. The offset has magnitude less than 1/33, so no nonzero corner clearance changes sign. A central equality resolves on the indicated side. For each *fixed finite* itinerary this is the same resolution as a one-sided slope perturbation: in the developed initial chart its vertical displacement at positive horizontal travel t has sign sign(dy)·sign(Δs) and magnitude t|Δs|. This argument does not exchange the infinite-time and small-angle limits. At fixed offset use the exact polygon tracer to go from portal to portal until the identical signed exit state repeats. Project back to the central phase by rounding in 1/33 units. This proves the periodic list of germs for arbitrary finite j. ## A capped arm really can be doubled

Let a rectangle of length ℓ and width w attach to a junction J along one full mouth and terminate at a flat cap with two convex corners. Glue two copies of the assembled room along the cap by reflection. The doubled arm is a 2ℓ separating passage between J and its mirror. If the reflected planar drawings overlap elsewhere, keep the copies abstractly distinct; the local chamber and corridor arguments only use the prescribed attachments. Do not apply a theorem requiring a simple planar embedding or a reflex-count hypothesis without verifying those extra conditions on the double.

**Projection lemma.** A legal one-traversal connection between opposite junction copies projects to a legal path between the original physical marks, provided those marks are distinct. The only possible new forbidden contact is an old cap endpoint. An interior cap crossing folds to an ordinary cap reflection. At an endpoint, the crossing instead coincides with a smooth lateral-wall reflection in the double. Folding reverses both velocity components at that instant. The outgoing part then retraces the incoming part until the nearer of the two marks is encountered. Remove the intervening excursion to the corner; the remaining subsegment, reversed if needed, connects the marks without touching it. The same deletion applies to a corridor target placed in the far half, since there is only one crossing of the old cap in these constructions.

**Whole-room tail.** For any fixed strictly simple bounded orthogonal junction with a fixed positive-width mouth, all distinct interior pairs in the capped assembly are lit for sufficiently large ℓ. For two junction marks put them in opposite copies and apply the uniform [cross-chamber theorem](README.md) at length 2ℓ, then project. For a junction mark and a strip mark at depth z, place the strip target at depth 2ℓ−z≥ℓ in the double. The uniform one-door first-hit bound applies, then the projection lemma. Both strip marks see each other directly. Mouth marks can be treated as strip marks (two on a mouth see each other), including z=0 in the doubled construction by using the opposite mouth as the terminal portal. More explicitly, the one-door matching ray terminates at the selected open mouth point before any chamber wall collision. If δ is a common lower bound for admissible closed slope widths for the **two-junction** construction, and δ₁ for **one-junction** access, a sufficient threshold is ℓ≥max(w/δ,2w/δ₁). The bounds can be taken uniform on compact nondegenerate junction-and-open-mouth families by joint first-hit stability. For a rectangular junction the earlier chamber-to-strip proof actually works at every length, and every same-junction pair sees directly. [Rectangular proof](resonant-gluing.md)

**Limit of the structural conclusion.** This handles a long arm attached to a *fixed* hub, and permits any uniform cross-passage estimate to be used on the corresponding double if its hypotheses hold. In the odd crosses, removing one arm leaves three others growing with it. The fixed-hub bound gives no uniform conclusion on that sequence; its lighting has a separate [capped-arm placement proof](cross-placement.md). Also a global scaled shape may degenerate by a tiny notch in a macroscopic wall. For example a square [0,m]² with an open rectangular indentation of width one and depth one from an interior portion of its top side has r=2, integer wall gcd one on each axis and area m²−1. For m large it is BBBB by the d69 area criterion. Center the notch for odd m. In normalized coordinates its two gap ratios tend to zero. The indented side removes material; it creates neither a genuine two-mouth corridor nor a protruding arm whose proper full mouth has two reflex lips and whose two long sides run to a cap. The two reflex vertices are at the notch bottom; the rectangle proceeding from them toward the top is exterior, and below them its putative lateral sides are interior. This disproves the proposed compact remainder with the gluing definitions even after adding genuine long arms. It is a symbolic geometric obstruction, not a dark pair. Collapsing indentations would need a separate lifting or induction theorem.
