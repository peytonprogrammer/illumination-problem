# Comparing neighborhoods in a spare block

**Result, reviewed by d78.** For a normalized strictly simple orthogonal lattice room with r ≤ 8, suppose a maximal proper block system has blocks of size three. If the area n exceeds 36, every pair of distinct same-parity interior lattice marks has marked valency d ≥ n−3. More generally, for n ≥ 120 the only possible low-valency configuration (3d < 2n) has blocks of size eight, with the eight sheets incident to the two marks all in a single block. In this configuration r = 8 and all eight reflex vertices project to one zero of the intermediate cover, hence have the same coordinate parity. This is a restriction on marked monodromy; illumination of the remaining configuration is open.

## A permutation lemma

Let a transitive group G preserve m blocks of k sheets, and let the induced group contain the natural alternating group A_m. Assume m−2 ≥ 5 and (m−2)!/2 > (k!)². Then either all ordered sheet pairs in distinct blocks form one orbit, or there is a G-invariant partition into q classes, 2 ≤ q ≤ k, each meeting every block.

**Stabilizers.** Fix one or two specified blocks individually. Their setwise stabilizer induces a group containing the alternating group on the other blocks (lift even permutations fixing the specified block indices). The subgroup fixing all sheets of the specified blocks pointwise is normal there, of index at most (k!)². Its image on the other blocks is normal in the induced group and has at most that index. The alternating group there is simple and has order greater than this index, so it is contained in the image. In particular the pointwise stabilizer is transitive on the remaining blocks.

**Construction.** Suppose O is a nonempty proper orbit of external ordered pairs. For a sheet s outside block D, write N_D(s) for its neighbors in D under O, with s the first coordinate. For two sheets s,t declare them equivalent if N_D(s)=N_D(t) for a spare block D containing neither. Choosing a different spare block gives the same answer: the pointwise stabilizer of the blocks containing s,t carries the first spare block to the second while fixing s,t. To prove transitivity of the relation, compare three sheets in one common spare block. This gives an equivariant equivalence relation, even for directed O.

Every equivalence class meets every block. Given s and a desired block C different from its own, choose a spare D. The pointwise stabilizer of D can send the block of s to C. The image t of s then has *exactly* the same neighbors in D because D is fixed pointwise. Thus s and t are equivalent, and the number q of classes is at most k. If q were one, all sheets outside D would have the same neighborhood in D. That neighborhood is nonempty by transitivity on blocks, and invariant under the setwise stabilizer of D, which is transitive on the sheets in D by transitivity of G. The neighborhood would be all of D, for every D, contrary to O being proper. Thus q ≥ 2.

## Application to room doubles

Use [d10, Fact L and Proposition R1](../../illumination-generalization-2026-11-06/fable/directions-2026-11-09/d10-mechanisms/theory.md). Any proper intermediate cover of the base pillowcase is genus zero and has a zero; otherwise it is an intermediate pillowcase, excluded by normalization. Every zero of the quotient has as many distinct reflex preimages as the degree of the inner cover, because its local degree three leaves no further ramification upstairs. Consequently every nontrivial invariant partition with at least two parts and fewer parts than sheets has part size at most r. In the lemma's second alternative every part meets m blocks, so for m > r that alternative is impossible. **All external ordered pairs therefore form one orbit**, of valency k(m−1)=n−k.

For size three, the quotient on m blocks is primitive by maximality and has at most floor(8/3)=2 zeros. Squaring a branch permutation at a zero-bearing pole produces z three-cycles, 1 ≤ z ≤ 2. [D14 Theorem G(a)](../../illumination-generalization-2026-11-06/fable/directions-2026-11-09/d14-lemma-h/proof.md) implies A_m or m ≤ max(6z,z²) ≤ 12. At n>36 we have m≥13, so the order inequality and m>r hold. Each interior mark has four distinct incident sheets, too many for one size-three block. The marked union meets the external orbit, yielding d≥n−3.

For the uniform statement choose any maximal proper block system if the sheet action is imprimitive; its block size satisfies 2 ≤ k ≤ 8 by the same reflex-preimage argument. For k=2 the primitive quotient bound is m≤24 unless it contains A_m, for k=3 it is m≤12, and for k≥4 it is at most 12 because floor(8/k)≤2. At n≥120 we have m≥60 for k=2 and m≥15 for every k, excluding all those exceptions. The order inequality holds even at k=8 and m=15 since 13!/2 > (8!)². Thus all external pairs form a single orbit. If the original action was primitive, d14 already gives A_n at n>64 and d=n−1.

The two distinct interior marks of the same parity have disjoint incident sheet sets of size four: their four local two-cycles in the source branch permutation are distinct. Unless all eight sheets are in one block, their product contains an external ordered pair, and d≥n−k≥n−8>2n/3. If they *are* all in one block, k=8, and their marked orbits remain internal, giving d≤7. The quotient has at least one zero, and each zero uses eight reflex preimages; hence r=8, exactly one quotient zero, and all reflex vertices lie over its base pole. This proves the stated dichotomy without assuming that low valency implies darkness.

For n ≥ 120 this leaves one configuration. A [subsequent proof of direct visibility](eight-sheet.md) treats it and was independently reviewed by d79. Finite exceptional areas and the high-valency geometric step remain open. The 747-pair audit in the [previous note](low-valency.md) contains no low examples and is not used in this proof. No room, ray, direction or reflection-word search was run.
