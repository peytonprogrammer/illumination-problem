# Lighting across a long corridor

**Result.** Fix two bounded strictly simple orthogonal end chambers, one interior mark in each, and a doorway of the same width w in each chamber. Join the doors by an empty horizontal rectangle of width w and length ℓ, with physical walls along its two long sides. It must be possible to seal each door to recover its chamber, and we assume strict simplicity of the joined room at each length ℓ under consideration. Keep the chambers, marks relative to them, and door placements fixed. **For every sufficiently large real ℓ, a corner-avoiding billiard path joins the marks.** It crosses the connecting corridor once. One threshold works simultaneously for all pairs with one interior mark in each fixed chamber. No common global shear or wall-count hypothesis is needed.

For the named DB55 family this yields an explicit bound. With the two original 5-by-5 chambers and centered unit doors, **every distinct interior lattice pair is lit for every integer corridor length ℓ ≥ 450**. Independently, the previously saved paths prove illumination of every same-class interior lattice pair for every ℓ = 3 + 6k, k ≥ 0. The general BBBB problem remains open.

## Proof of the long-corridor statement

Seal each portal temporarily with a mirror. Choose a common positive slope magnitude s for the two sealed rooms such that directional flow on both unfoldings is minimal and both chosen rays avoid polygon vertices and door endpoints. This is possible since only countably many slopes are excluded. Directions from a fixed mark through a developed vertex or door endpoint form a countable set. We can start from the left mark and trace backward from the right mark with either initial choice of signs. In a minimal direction the forward ray crosses the interior of the portal in finite time. Indeed, an open set immediately upstream of a smaller open portal segment flows across it; density reaches that set. Stop at the first portal hit. Each finite leg persists with the same reflection sequence and the same first door hit on an open slope interval. Intersect the two intervals and take a closed subinterval [s₀,s₁] of positive length. For rectangular chambers, density here also follows from irrational rotation of the transverse coordinate at successive visits to a vertical side. For general chambers the proof of Theorem 1.8 establishes density for every infinite forward trajectory in the chosen directions. See [Masur and Tabachnikov, proof of Theorem 1.8](https://math.uchicago.edu/~masur/handbook1.pdf).

Measure doorway heights y₁(s), y₂(s) in (0,w) from the bottom. Let ε₁ be the sign of vertical velocity leaving the first chamber, and ε₂ its required sign entering the second chamber, opposite the sign on the leg traced backward. The unfolded transverse coordinate of a unit-speed-in-x corridor traversal advances by ℓs. In that coordinate, phase +y means physical height y with positive vertical velocity, and −y means height y with negative vertical velocity. The position **and** direction match precisely when

    F(s) = ℓs + ε₁ y₁(s) − ε₂ y₂(s) ∈ 2w ℤ.

Let a and b be the total absolute horizontal travel on the two chamber legs. They are constant on the interval since reflections at vertical walls occur at fixed x coordinates and the itinerary stays fixed. Unfolding each leg gives (ε₁y₁)' = a and (ε₂y₂)' = −b. Thus F has slope ℓ+a+b. If

    (ℓ+a+b)(s₁−s₀) ≥ 2w,

its closed interval of values contains a multiple of 2w. The corresponding physical trajectory has legal chamber legs and passes strictly inside both doors. Every intermediate corridor reflection is on a smooth wall. This proves the statement for all lengths satisfying the displayed bound. The slopes used in the matching step may be rational even though a generic slope was used to obtain the initial open interval.

**Uniformity over the marks.** Partition each closed chamber into finitely many closed axis-aligned rectangles with disjoint interiors. Work on one compact product of a left rectangle and a right rectangle. At each pair of points in that product, including points on the boundary, choose initial signs pointing strictly into the selected rectangles at any active edges. At a polygon corner this uses the inward ray as a limiting start. Choose the common slope outside the countable exceptional set so that neither ray hits a vertex or door endpoint at positive time. Even a ray starting at a corner is dense after a short initial segment, by minimality applied at a regular point on the ray. Both rays reach their portals at positive time. The reflection sequences and the identity of the first door hit are jointly locally constant in both starting points and the slope. Indeed, after the initial inward segment each reference collision is transverse and strictly inside its edge. Its time and position vary continuously with the start and slope until an edge endpoint or grazing collision intervenes. Induction through the finitely many collisions gives a product of a relative neighborhood of the pair and a closed slope interval of positive length with the same two itineraries. For the initial segment this holds because the velocities point inward at active rectangle edges; after that segment both trajectories have positive clearance from every corner they pass. For a start on the sealed portal, launch inward and use its first positive-time return. Nearby interior starts follow the same leg.

Take a finite subcover of this compact product, and repeat for the finitely many rectangle pairs. The minimum δ of the retained slope widths is positive. For each actual pair use a neighborhood containing it. Then ℓ ≥ 2w/δ suffices for **all** such pairs at once. Boundary starts serve only to obtain a cover; the conclusion concerns interior marks. This compactness step fixes both chamber geometries and their positive-width doors. It allows an unbounded number of corridor reflections through the integer in the phase equation and gives no finite set of full billiard itineraries across lengths.

This also gives a one-door version for a blind strip. Trace the stabilized first leg from the source and use a fixed target at transverse height t, depth h inside the strip. Replace F by hs + ε₁y₁(s) − t. The criterion becomes (h+a)(s₁−s₀) ≥ 2w; the path stops at the target before the cap. This gives a sufficient condition even when the D58 image rotation Lh/(wD) is an integer (exact resonance for the direct family; replace h by the reflected depth for the cap family).

## Named checks

**DB55 and Lemma U.** The room has a four-by-four set of interior lattice marks in each chamber. The corridor and its mouths have no interior lattice marks since the portal width is one and its ends have integer heights. Marks in the same chamber see each other directly. For the 256 cross-chamber pairs, the following one interval works for all of them, independently of their parities.

Start from each mark toward its portal wall and upward, using the leg from the right mark backward. Write d ∈ {1,2,3,4} for distance to the portal wall, y ∈ {1,2,3,4} for height. At slope 1/10, the unfolded heights at successive visits to that wall are y + d/10 + j. The two copies of the centered door occupy (2,3) and (7,8) modulo 10. Among any five consecutive visits one hits a door. The horizontal travel to the first hit is at most 4+4·10 = 44. At every vertical side hit before and including it the unfolded height stays at distance at least 1/10 from every integer, since the horizontal travel is d+5i. Perturbing the slope by at most 1/450 changes any of these heights by at most 44/450 < 1/10. The door itinerary persists, including avoidance of corners. The common closed slope interval has length 1/225. With w=1, ℓ≥450 suffices in the formula above. Thus all 496 unordered lattice pairs in each such room are lit.

There is also an exact check reaching back to the original short room. For its 64 cross-chamber same-class pairs, replay the previously saved *lit directions* in D72. Inserting length Δ reproduces the exit height and velocity on each crossing of a unit corridor provided Δ|q/p| is an even integer for path direction (p,q). This applies independently to each crossing, going either way. Twenty saved directions have insertion period 3 and 44 have period 6. Taking Δ=6k preserves the parity classes and all the paths. The 48 other same-class pairs lie in single chambers. The [check](check.py) reads only existing witnesses and verifies these counts. This argument makes no claim of exhaustive coverage of the shorter lengths in other congruence classes.

Lemma U bounds the number of reflections from below when one endpoint lies deep inside a narrowing strip. A lattice point cannot be in the interior of a unit-width lattice corridor, so that hypothesis does not describe DB55's cross-chamber lattice pairs. For arbitrary real marks it can apply. The phase integer in the long-corridor proof can grow with length, permitting arbitrarily many reflections. The theorem assumes fixed w and does not give a uniform threshold when w shrinks and the accessible slope interval changes with w.

**cov2929.** The two turning pairs are (3,1) to (5,3) and (3,3) to (5,5). D72 already gives common open cylinders in directions (4,3) and (−4,3), respectively. Its saved direct lighting lines have directions (−2,3) and (2,3). These verify that both turns can be accommodated in a single global unfolding. There is no long straight bridge hypothesis for those two contacts in the actual staircase. The argument above does not replace the saved witnesses. This room has a Q class and is a stress test for turning, outside the true BBBB class.

**D58 double resonance.** In D58's constructed examples the two image-rotation numbers are 2^(k−2) and 3·2^(k−2). Each phase shift is zero modulo the transverse period. The H phases 1.90 and 1.75 lie in [1.1125,1.95], and the cap phases 1.70 and 1.25 in [1.2375,1.95]. Repeating those images leaves both failed windows unchanged. Earlier cap retracing addresses a corner on the cap after a valid door crossing. These four phases already fail at the window. The one-door criterion above allows other sealed-chamber itineraries, and applies at resonance when its slope interval is wide enough. D58 shrinks the aperture and sets D=16h, ℓ_cap=2h. We have no lower bound on the interval width in those changing chambers sufficient to imply the criterion. The ten saved follow-up instances were already lit by D58; no general double-resonance conclusion follows.

## Where the Q shear stops

1. On a unit-height unfolded band of physical width u, a horizontal shear of coefficient L fixes both rims pointwise when 2u divides L. A fixed room admits the common multiple over all bands. In DB55 with corridor length ℓ the long row has physical width 10+ℓ, and other rows have width 5. The standard horizontal construction needs lcm(10,2(10+ℓ)); the vertical one needs only 10. A large horizontal period alone therefore cannot explain the failure. More intrinsically, at **lattice endpoints** every power of either integer-row-period shear fixes every lift, since it fixes each unit-band rim pointwise. The displacement A used in the Q proof is zero at the very marks in the open lattice problem. For any seed direction, a common-cylinder witness after pulling the direction back by such a shear exists exactly when the seed already has it, by affine equivariance and the fixed lifts. The lattice-path census supplied this case in Q.
2. Using a different shear coefficient on adjacent bands can fix their shared boundary, but a transverse straight line pulls back with horizontal velocity vₓ+Lᵢvᵧ on side i. Two different coefficients produce a kink at an internal seam. A common coefficient on only the bands visited by a *known* path is enough if the local charts glue there, the full segment stays within them, and corners remain excluded. Choosing endpoint bands alone supplies none of those path conditions. Rational reference heights can make the parameter A nonzero, but the successful transformation remains a power of the same integral shear fixing lattice marks.
3. For arbitrary real endpoints, Q first bounded every remaining pair inside specified tile products, then verified actual cylinder intersections or reflected paths throughout finitely many boxes. BBBB gives neither these enclosures nor the intersections. Even if L is bounded in a favorable axis, the phase bound contains L times the box radius, and a bounded phase interval alone certifies no path. D72 found common cylinders for 747/747 same-class pairs in nine true BBBB rooms. It also found exact failures of that stronger criterion in lit rooms with proper factors. Having a regular point of each parity excludes Q; identifying that with every Nₓ=B requires first removing the proper-factor branch.

**First missing step for a general proof.** Even for rooms with a separating bridge, the door argument needs a common slope interval large enough relative to w/(ℓ+a+b), or a different phase-matching argument. Generic access and compactness give a positive slope width for fixed chambers and doors, with no estimate in terms of the reflex count alone. Rooms without a separating straight strip need a different way to match a turning path. The corridor theorem removes a particular unbounded-length regime; arbitrary BBBB room shapes and varying apertures remain open.
