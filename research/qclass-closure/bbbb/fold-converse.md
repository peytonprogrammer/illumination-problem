# Corner count first, then the fold converse

**Verdict.** The R22 parity-fold mechanism requires at least nine upstairs reflex corners for a dark same-class interior pair, but the universal proper-fold converse is false: the strictly simple 34-wall room is already dark by parity on its own double of prime degree 19.

## Step 2. Which parity class, and what does it cost?

In R22 the two marked staircase images are **smooth wall points** (0,0) and (2,−2). They are the only regular physical lattice positions on the staircase. The **other three** parity classes contain only vertices. The unfolded obstruction uses an intermediate point with at least one odd coordinate. Thus requiring the *endpoint class* to consist only of vertices would exclude the model example itself. D93 identifies its unique proper system as five blocks of three sheets. [Manuscript, parity lemma](../../illumination-publication/repository/preprints/twenty-two-wall/manuscript.tex) · [d93 block computation](../../illumination-generalization-2026-11-06/fable/directions-2026-11-09/d93-review-blocks/review.md)

Take the corrected R22 hypothesis: Q→N→B is a factor of the lattice pillowcase map, of inner degree k≥2 and outer degree m>1. All four endpoint lifts have regular images over the marked parity a, and the two image sets {f(s), f(ιs)} and {f(t), f(ιt)} are disjoint. All three off-classes are corner-only in N. Write z for the number of zeros of N. Local degrees over B are 1, 2 or 3, and N is a sphere since Q is a sphere. Its z zeros have angle 3π and its z+4 poles have angle π. If q counts regular points over the four base poles, Riemann–Hurwitz gives q+2z=2m−2. All q belong to a, where each consumes two sheets. Hence

    2 ≤ q = 2m−2−2z ≤ m/2,       z+2 ≤ m ≤ 4(z+1)/3.

Each quotient zero has k distinct unramified reflex preimages, since an additional local degree there would exceed three over B. More exactly, if c_j counts local degree-j points of Q→N over quotient poles for j=2,3, then r=kz+c₃ and c₂+2c₃=2k−2. The c₂ points account for quotient poles which become smooth upstairs, including the R22 seam reversals. Such smoothing cannot remove any of the kz forced reflexes.

If r≤8, the entire numerical residue is as follows. In every row q=2.

| z | m | possible k | upstairs area km |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 4 | 2, 3, 4 | 8, 12, 16 |
| 3 | 5 | 2 | 10 |
| 4 | 6 | 2 | 12 |

For a physical polygon base as in R22 there is a direct elimination. A four-cell room with two reflexes is the T or skew tetromino up to lattice symmetry (the straight and square tetrominoes have none and the L has one). The T's two smooth lattice sites are consecutive along its length-three wall. In the skew tetromino take the two rows [0,2] and [1,3]; its smooth sites are (1,0) and (2,2). In both rooms the sites have different parities. Neither can supply the z=2 row. In the remaining rows k=2. Two distinct *physical* regular base positions with only two regular lifts in total must both be smooth wall positions. Therefore both mirror lifts of each upstairs interior mark collapse to one quotient point. The same deck involution then exchanges halves at both marks; its physical action is an axial reflection fixing both. Strict simplicity makes their common axial segment interior, so the marks are directly visible. [Degree-two axis lemma, reviewed in d99](bbbb-split.md)

Even for an abstract intermediate sphere without an embedded polygon base, the table bounds the upstairs area by 16. D9 exhausts strictly simple rooms through area 20 and excludes all dark same-class interior pairs with r≤8 there. Thus no seam arrangement in these numerical rows supports the claimed darkness. The bound is sharp: R22 realizes k=3, m=5, z=3 and r=9. [D9 census](../../illumination-generalization-2026-11-06/fable/directions-2026-11-09/d9-census-w22/README.md)

### Census-free elimination for separated fibers, including abstract bases

Assume the images of the two Q lifts of S are disjoint from the images of the two lifts of T and all those images are regular, as they are in R22. This is the endpoint separation needed for the R22-type parity certificate. We can then eliminate the abstract rows above without d9.

First replace the proposed partition by the finest sheet-monodromy-invariant equivalence that merges every upstairs transposition at all three off-classes. It is the join of their three canonical equivalences, hence is invariant under the four physical edge involutions by R3.3. It refines the proposed partition, since every specified merge already occurs there. Its quotient still has no regular off-class point, and the marked images remain regular and separated: their local degrees over the pillowcase must still be two by multiplicativity through the original regular images. The outer degree is at least the original m. [R3.3 as used in d64](../../illumination-generalization-2026-11-06/fable/directions-2026-11-09/d64-finite-case/README.md)

If the refined partition is discrete, all three off-classes are corner-only upstairs. Each cardinal neighbor of S and of T has at least two incident cells from the corresponding interior star, hence must be reflex. If two neighbors coincide, the marks are two horizontal or vertical units apart and directly visible. Otherwise these are eight distinct reflexes. At the east neighbor of S the third cell, beyond the two cells of the S-star, gives one of the two eastern diagonal neighbors of S at least two incident cells. That neighbor too must be reflex, and its parity differs from those of all eight axial neighbors. This gives the ninth. [D14, Lemma R6](../../illumination-generalization-2026-11-06/fable/directions-2026-11-09/d14-lemma-h/proof.md)

If the refined partition is proper and r≤8, the same table applies to its degrees. In every row there are just two regular quotient points. Separation puts the mirror lifts of each mark together over one of them. For inner degree two the same-axis argument above therefore gives direct visibility, without needing N to be a polygon double.

The only remaining outer degree is four. Label the four quotient blocks. In each parity class the quotient corner permutation is a product of one horizontal and one vertical edge involution. At a its type is (2,2); at the three other parities the types are (3,1), (3,1), and identity, because there are exactly two zeros. All four corner permutations are even, so all four edge involutions have the same sign (all four horizontal–vertical combinations occur). If even, they lie in the Klein four group, which cannot produce a 3-cycle. Thus they are odd involutions of four letters, hence transpositions. At a name the vertical-edge swap V=(12) and horizontal-edge swap H=(34); disjointness follows from their product type.

Consider the mark above the regular a-cycle (12). Its four actual incident cells all have labels in {1,2}. Crossing the central horizontal edge leaves the label fixed by H, so the two cells on the eastern side have the same label and the two on the western side have the other label, since crossing the central vertical edge swaps them by V. Suppose the x-adjacent parity has a 3-cycle. Its edge generators are H=(34) and the opposite-parity vertical-edge generator. To produce a 3-cycle their transpositions share one letter, so precisely one of 1,2 lies in that cycle. On the side of the mark carrying that label, the two equal-labeled cells share the neighboring vertex of this adjacent parity. This is impossible: the corner cycle there upstairs must map onto the 3-cycle, and has length at most three. It must therefore contain each of the three quotient labels exactly once. The two cells are in the same actual corner cycle because they meet along an edge ending at that vertex (equivalently the upstairs point above a quotient zero is a reflex with three incident cells). Thus this interior mark forces the x-adjacent parity to have identity monodromy. Exchanging the axes and using the other mark over (34) forces the y-adjacent parity to have identity monodromy too. Only one off-class has identity. This contradiction excludes the final row independently of seam enumeration.

**Weaker, one-class reading.** If N has merely *some* corner-only parity x, the three-off-class count does not apply. Nevertheless, in the normalized setting the canonical B_x refines the given proper partition: every upstairs two-cycle at x lies inside a block. If there are none, x is corner-only upstairs and d102 excludes darkness at r≤8; otherwise N_x is proper and d64 excludes darkness for the requested upstairs pairs. D102 concerns interior marks in the physical room; applying its conclusion directly to the staircase's boundary marks would be invalid. One corner-only class alone does not give the staircase proof in every direction. ## Step 1. Named counterexample to necessity of a proper fold

For R34-base the 19 saved cells have 19 convex and 15 reflex vertices, no pinch, two interior points (2,0),(6,0), and just two smooth boundary lattice points (4,0),(8,−2). These four regular physical sites are all even-even. This is a direct star count on the named room only. For any proposed shot between its marks the nonzero unfolded displacement is even in both coordinates. Its first primitive lattice step lies strictly before the target, and has at least one odd coordinate relative to the even-even start. Folding back preserves both parities. This gives a corner on the proposed shot, proving darkness with no search or seam assumption.

But deg(Q→B)=19 is prime. A factor with k≥2 and m≥2 would give 19=km; the degree-one outer pillowcase identifies both same-parity marks at the same pole and has no distinct regular marked images. D93 independently found no nontrivial sheet partition. Thus the collective parity obstruction can live on Q itself (inner degree one). Proper-fold necessity fails even after correcting the parity wording. R34 already belongs to the corner-only branch; it supplies no BBBB counterexample below 22 walls. **Fold line closed for BBBB at every reflex count.** If a quotient has even one corner-only class x, its partition must coarsen B_x. In BBBB this is already the universal relation, so the outer degree is one. The corner count applies outside the core; inside the core the proposed proper fold is impossible for lit and hypothetical dark pairs alike.
