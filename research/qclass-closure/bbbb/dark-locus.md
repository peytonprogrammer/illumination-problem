# Unbounded dark rooms would force a proper periodic marking

**Theorem, reviewed in d89.** The conclusions below are conditional on d80 and its inputs, in particular its exclusion of irrational dark interior marks at n≥120. [Independent review](../../illumination-generalization-2026-11-06/fable/directions-2026-11-09/d89-review-dark-locus/review.md)

Suppose there are strictly simple normalized orthogonal lattice rooms with at most eight reflex corners, containing a dark pair of distinct interior points, of unbounded area. Some subsequence with fixed reflex count r lies in a single marked affine invariant locus with all the following properties:

1. Every source lift is dark to every target lift on the room double. All four labels on the double are retained.
2. Forgetting the four labels has zero-dimensional fibers locally on the smooth branch. On the generic underlying surface each of the four labels is a periodic marking (adjoining it does not increase orbit-closure dimension).
3. The unmarked locus, viewed on the orientation cover of genus g=r+1, is defined over the rationals and has rank k satisfying 2 ≤ k < g/2+1. Its generic surface is not a torus cover. In particular only ranks 2 through 5 can occur at r=8.
4. There is also a uniform denominator consequence: for 1≤r≤8 there exists a finite integer D(r) such that every coordinate of every such dark interior pair in normalized rooms of area at least 120 has denominator dividing D(r), in the physical lattice coordinates. This gives no numerical D(r). It concerns arbitrary interior marks; the denominator assertion alone gives no information about the already integral marks in the midpoint problem.

Parts 2 and 3 sharpen the proposed sequence-to-locus alternative. A family with a freely moving pair over a fixed underlying surface cannot supply the sequence, even if its two points move together. No realization of an off-class midpoint or explicit area bound follows.

## Carrying the mirror and all the endpoints

Work on the quadratic sphere Q of the doubled room, in the stratum with r simple zeros and r+4 simple poles. Label its corners separately. The two points in each physical interior fiber are conjugate under the room mirror. Label them s+,s− and t+,t−. Impose darkness on Q for all four cross pairs, avoiding all the singularities. The other endpoint labels are ordinary points which can be traversed. This closed condition is invariant under GL⁺: a legal segment persists under small perturbations and affine changes preserve it. On physical doubles this is equivalent to physical darkness, by unfolding and folding. All singularities have odd order, so each has exactly one lift on the orientation double. The translation cover carries eight regular endpoint labels if we label both preimages; this is only a finite change of marking.

We use the marked-stratum form of the closed-invariant-set theorem, by taking the orientation double and retaining the finite involution/label data. Equivalently the orienting-double locus is a closed invariant locus in a marked Abelian stratum; the deck involution negates the one-form and acts by the prescribed label permutation. This persists inside the smooth stratum (or work with the local charts of the finite cover specifying the involution). Apply the area-one theorem and restore scaling. The eight upstairs labels may be interchanged within fibers when passing between charts; every condition on Q below is independent of these choices. [EMM, Theorem 2.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1305.3015) · [Apisa–Wright, §1, quadratic strata and markings](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1708.03411) · [Marked-stratum conventions](https://msp.org/gt/2020/24-1/gt-v24-n1-p05-s.pdf)

After discarding n<120, d80 ensures that all physical marks of every example are rational. For a fixed r choose an irreducible conical affine invariant locus N of **least dimension** in the four-pair dark set containing physical examples with unbounded n. This exists by the finite-union theorem. The rectangle case r=0 has no dark interior pairs by convexity. In using closed invariant sets here and below, take closures inside the stratum, where the marked points are distinct. [D80 theorem](high-valency-geometry.md)

Let σ be complex conjugation of the quadratic surface followed by exchanging the two s labels and the two t labels. Every physical tuple is fixed by σ. This map normalizes the GL⁺ action. Thus N ∩ σN is closed invariant and contains the same physical examples. If σN differed from N the intersection would be a finite union of strictly smaller loci (equivalently use irreducibility and Filip's algebraicity), contrary to minimality. Likewise the singular/self-intersection locus of N can be discarded: it is invariant, closed and lower-dimensional. We can therefore choose a physical example with n≥120 at a smooth σ-stable branch. This last step matters: a conjugation exchanging two distinct local branches would not supply a real tangent in either branch.

Forget the four labels, keeping the labeled corner differential fixed. On a smooth period chart the forgetful map is linear projection. Its fiber tangent inside N is a complex vector space K of locally constant dimension. If K is nonzero, σ acts antiholomorphically on the smooth fiber germ at our fixed tuple. Its fixed germ has real dimension dim_C K. For example, choose linearizing coordinates by averaging a holomorphic chart with its conjugate; the fixed tangent is a real form. Thus it contains a real arc of distinct marked tuples on the **same surface Q**.

On that fixed Q the antiholomorphic identification must be the physical mirror: two such identifications differ by a holomorphic automorphism of the sphere fixing all corner labels, hence fixing at least three distinct points and equal to the identity. Consequently every tuple on the fixed arc is two genuine mirror pairs. Near the original tuple the labels remain distinct and away from the seam, so they project to distinct interior physical points. The map from these tuples to ordered physical pairs is locally injective after fixing the two sides. All these pairs are dark by the four cross conditions. D80 allows only rational physical coordinates in this same normalized room, a countable set. This contradiction proves K=0. It uses neither a uniform bound for ordinary shadows nor any transfer of midpoint candidates.

The projected orbit closure M has the same dimension as N: on a dense orbit the projection has orbit closure the closure of its image, and the algebraic forgetful image has dimension equal to the local projection rank. Each individual endpoint marking also has this dimension by sandwiching its projection between N and M. This is periodicity relative to M. Finite orientation and corner labeling does not change dimensions. In particular any generic explanation via a moving common quotient fiber is excluded; periodic endpoints can still share a quotient image.

## Rank and the primitive degree

In the orientation-cover chart the kernel from relative to absolute periods for the **unmarked quadratic sphere stratum** is zero. Indeed relative cohomology adds reduced functions on the inverse images of the singularities; all of them are individually fixed by the deck involution, and the quadratic tangent uses its minus eigenspace. Forgetting optional regular labels on the Abelian cover has the same property here for marked lifts of poles, since those are also fixed. Thus dim_C M=2k, where k is rank. The same absolute projection is injective on the tangent of N by the mirror argument.

N has rational period equations. One way to see this without assuming that an arbitrary analytic plane through a rational point is rational: take the closure of the union of GL⁺ orbits of the selected rational physical examples in N. Minimality and the finite-union theorem force that closure to be N. Matrices in GL⁺(2,Q) are dense and send rational complex periods to rational complex periods. Consequently rational points are dense in any smooth period patch (integral changes of marking preserve rationality). A homogeneous linear plane with dense rational points is rational. This also gives rationality of the projected plane. Finite orientation label choices can be made on the local charts. [Wright, independent field-of-definition description](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1210.4806)

If k=1, M has complex dimension two with no relative deformation. Locally this is precisely a GL⁺ orbit, since varying a real two-by-two matrix gives four independent real parameters on two independent absolute periods. Connectedness then gives one orbit. On a torus cover the primitive torus degree is area divided by the covolume of the full absolute period lattice; the ratio is invariant under GL⁺. For our normalized rooms these numbers are 4n and 4, respectively. The orbit cannot contain unbounded n. This proves k≥2. More generally, on a smooth chart of rank at least two the torus covers lie in a countable union of proper linear subspaces: any discrete rank-two absolute period group imposes rational expressions for every absolute period in two chosen periods, giving a subspace of complex absolute dimension at most two. This proves the generic non-torus assertion. [Wall-gcd period proof](convex-cylinder.md)

For the upper bound forget all marks on the Abelian orientation cover. Its underlying curve is hyperelliptic with its canonical involution J, since its quotient is the sphere Q. If k≥g/2+1, the high-rank classification gives either a full Abelian component or a full holonomy-double locus. For r≥2 the first case is impossible by dimension: the room-double locus has complex dimension 2r+2, strictly less than the Abelian component dimension 3r+1. At r=1 the component is H(2), whose regular periodic points are Weierstrass points, fixed by J. In the second case let I be the holonomy involution. It equals J: otherwise IJ is a nonidentity translation automorphism of finite order (g≥2), and quotienting by its group is a nontrivial translation covering of strictly smaller genus, excluded in high rank by Apisa–Wright Lemma 2.1. Thus the quadratic base is the sphere Q. Its generic configuration of 2r+4≥6 singularities has no nonidentity Möbius symmetry: a permutation of the points would have to preserve all cross ratios, which a generic configuration does not. A hyperelliptic quadratic component in the sense of Apisa–Wright would carry a nonidentity holomorphic involution preserving the quadratic differential on each generic base surface, hence preserving its unordered singular set. The absence of such a Möbius map excludes that hypothesis. For r≥1 the orders (1^r,−1^(r+4)) also avoid all three exceptional strata in their Theorem 1.4. Its periodic points upstairs are therefore fixed by J, by the periodic-point theorem. In both cases the endpoint lifts of a regular point of Q are exchanged by J and cannot be periodic. This contradicts the preceding section. [High Rank Invariant Subvarieties, Theorem 1.1, Lemma 2.1 and proof of Theorem 2.8](https://public.websites.umich.edu/~alexmw/highrank.pdf) · [Marked Points on Translation Surfaces, Theorem 1.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1708.03411)

## Uniform denominators for rational marks

Argue by contradiction with fixed r: if no common denominator exists, choose physical examples at n≥120 with reduced coordinate denominators tending to infinity. Repeat the least-dimension argument with “unbounded denominator” throughout in place of “unbounded n”, using the original physical lattice scale to select examples before taking GL⁺ saturation. The σ argument and d80 give the same zero fiber tangent. The density argument again gives a rational homogeneous marked period plane N. No rank assumption is needed here.

In one integral period chart on its orientation cover, restriction of the absolute-coordinate projection to this rational plane is injective. Linear algebra over Q consequently expresses **each relative period** as a rational linear combination of absolute periods on N. Take a common denominator D for a finite integral set of relative generators. Throughout the connected immersed branch,

    D · Per(relative homology) ⊆ Per(absolute homology).

The regular locus of N is connected: a dense connected GL⁺ orbit lies in the invariant regular locus and meets each of its open components. Thus the relations continue analytically along the smooth parameter manifold after parallel-transporting the integral homology bases. Integral basis changes and permutations of lifts preserve the inclusion and D. Equivalently take the universal cover of the parameter manifold and continue the original homogeneous equations there. The selected examples off the smaller singular locus still have unbounded denominators. At each, absolute periods lie in 2Z². The path from a corner to a marked point on one physical copy has period equal, up to sheet signs, to its physical displacement. The corner has integer coordinates. Each reduced coordinate denominator therefore divides D, a contradiction. This rules out unbounded denominators; taking the least common multiple of the finitely many possible denominator values gives the stated D(r). Neither N nor its period equations have been enumerated, so no computable cutoff is claimed here.

## Remaining obstruction

An unbounded sequence now requires a **proper**, higher-rank, rational locus with zero relative dimension, four distinct periodic regular marks on its quadratic sphere, and physical conjugate pairs at the square-tiled specializations. The upper bound rules out classified high rank, leaving k=2,3,4,5 when r=8. Apisa's classification of higher-rank genus-zero loci in strata with at most two zeros cannot simply be applied to eight simple zeros. Periodicity does not itself imply darkness. To exclude the remaining possibility one needs a restriction on periodic markings of these proper loci or a physical lighting argument for their real specializations. Even exclusion of all such loci would by itself bound the areas non-effectively; exceptional small rooms and an explicit census cutoff would still need work.
