# One dent in each orientation

The 944 OPEN words in the d163 list split into 410 in which the dents share a tip and 534 in which their endpoints are disjoint. This is a tally of the saved records: select OPEN and dent_profile [1,1], then test the cyclic turn word for RRR. The RRR test is exact because both dents are incident to its middle vertex, and a third dent is excluded. U2 is one additional PARTIAL word in this profile. [Ledger and realizations](../../illumination-generalization-2026-11-06/fable/directions-2026-11-09/d163-pattern-ledger/ledger.md)

**Shared-tip theorem.** Each of the 410 words has, at every strictly simple realization, a single axial cut partitioning it into two orthoconvex rooms. Either axis works. Making both cuts gives three orthoconvex rooms with at most r−1 reflex vertices in total. At W≤20 at least one of the three has at most two reflex vertices and therefore satisfies the reviewed all-real illumination theorem when sealed.

Let P be the shared reflex. Extend the horizontal incident dent through P, away from its wall, up to first boundary contact. Its open extension lies in the room. By the dent-cut lemma a horizontal cut of this kind removes the sole horizontal dent even in the presence of coincident wall heights. At P the new angles are 90 and 180 degrees. The vertical dent loses its reflex end there; no vertical edges merge, since the sole possible straight continuation at a cut endpoint runs horizontally. Both pieces have neither kind of dent, hence are orthoconvex. Interchanging axes proves the other choice. [Dent-cut lemma](dent-cut-transfer.md)

For the simultaneous cuts the second extension is in the piece with straight angle at P, which it divides into two right angles. At its far end it cuts a smooth wall into two right angles or a reflex angle into a straight and a right angle. A merger with the second seam can create no dent because that seam has a convex end at P on both copies. The two open cuts are disjoint. The exact remaining reflex total is r−1 minus the number of far endpoints that were originally reflex. This proves the stated bound by averaging. The subroom theorem at r≤2 concerns a physically reflecting boundary; a passage through a cut in the original room must continue undeflected.

**Disjoint-tip case.** Extending the horizontal dent at one endpoint removes it. It creates no perpendicular dent. The sole original vertical dent can survive in at most one piece. Consequently one piece is orthoconvex and the other is convex in at least one direction. If the vertical dent survives, a cut there leaves at most three orthoconvex pieces in total. This is a reduction of the interfaces for all 534 words; it carries no assertion that illumination composes over those interfaces.

**Exact controls.** In the smallest ledger fork below, P=(3,4) and the far endpoints are (4,4) and (3,5). The three pieces, listed as (unit cells, reflex vertices), are (1,0), (4,1), (7,2). All three sealed pieces are lit for arbitrary interior marks by d15. In R22, P=(1,1), the cuts terminate at (1,2) and (0,1), and the pieces are (1,0), (7,3), (7,3). These counts were obtained by removing just the cell adjacencies across the two stated cuts and tracing the boundary of each connected component. They sum to the respective room areas 12 and 15. R22 has a dark pair despite this three-piece structure. In particular, even the presence of a rectangular piece at the shared tip cannot by itself guarantee transfer.

The missing step is to connect a prescribed pair across a transparent cut at a common crossing position and incident velocity, allowing repeated returns. A closed sealed-piece illumination theorem asserts neither of these conditions. Neither branch currently earns a COVERED entry.
