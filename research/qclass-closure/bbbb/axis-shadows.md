# Axis marks and an effective shadow bound on an affine orbit

**Result for review.** For a mirror-invariant finite orbit where the full affine group is transitive on ordered pairs in different derivative-kernel blocks, bounded mirror orbit has a geometric explanation: on that orbit an interior mark off the horizontal and vertical symmetry axes has mirror suborbit size exactly N−k. Here N is the orbit size and k≤4 is the block size. Moreover, if N>(4r+4)k, every pair of lifts in different blocks is lit. The common shadow from a mark and its mirror **inside this orbit** is empty off the axes, and has at most k points on the axes. These statements use no ineffective B(r). A single visible pair in different blocks suffices in place of the size inequality.

The hypothesis is on affine permutations of point lifts. High valency for the pillowcase sheet action does not imply it. D91's saved cross computation satisfies it on specific orbits. The general axis classification and an r-only bound on the whole common shadow remain open.

## Proof

Let G be the orientation-preserving corner-preserving affine group of the double Q. Its normal kernel K of projective derivative is the group of physical coordinate-sign symmetries lifted with positive orientation; |K|≤4. Its orbits partition each finite G-orbit Ω into equal-sized blocks. Write N=|Ω| and k=|K s|. Suppose N>k and G_s is transitive on Ω outside K s. Equivalently, there is one G-orbit of ordered pairs of points of Ω in different K-blocks. To check this it is enough to exhibit one suborbit of size N−k known to lie outside K s. [Kernel proof](stabilizer-orbits.md)

Assume ιΩ=Ω, with ι the physical mirror. If S is interior, then ιs∈K s exactly when a physical horizontal or vertical symmetry axis passes through S: the element must exchange halves while fixing the physical mark. Thus on an axis e(S)=|G_s · ιs|≤k−1, and off the axes e(S)=N−k. This conclusion is independent of illumination or the numerical size of any shadow.

To light the large suborbit, triangulate the simple physical polygon with its 2r+4 corners as vertices, and use the 2r+2 triangles on each copy in Q. Assign each regular point to any closed triangle containing it. Two distinct regular points assigned to one triangle are joined in Q by their chord. A nondegenerate triangle in a polygon triangulation has no other polygon vertex in the interior of any of its edges, so the chord has no forbidden interior contact even if both points are on a shared edge. This is a Q statement: a segment along the seam is a regular geodesic there. When subsequently transported to paths between physical interior endpoints, such a path cannot glide along a physical wall: following a straight wall at a regular point forces the line to continue along it to a vertex or an endpoint. Geodesics between interior endpoints therefore fold legally.

If N>(4r+4)k, at least one triangle contains k+1 assigned points of Ω and hence two in different blocks. Transport the resulting visible pair by G. Every ordered different-block pair in Ω is lit. Thus the Q shadow of s restricted to Ω is contained in K s (with the source itself excluded by convention). Intersect the two restrictions at s and ιs: distinct blocks are disjoint. For smaller N the trivial bound N≤(4r+4)k applies, giving the unconditional-in-size bound 16r+16 **within one orbit satisfying these hypotheses**. If an explicit visible different-block chord is already known, use it directly and drop the N threshold.

There is a corresponding physical conclusion for axis sources. If a target lift in Ω lies outside K s, the preceding argument lights it. If it lies in K s, its physical point is a coordinate-sign image of S and lies on the same physical symmetry axis as S (the physical symmetries commute). The interior intersection with that axis is an interval, so distinct physical marks there see one another directly. Thus under the visible-pair condition an axial S illuminates every distinct interior physical point represented in Ω. This does not assume every point of the room has a lift in Ω.

## The d91 test distinguishes the two orbit questions

The archived full-group logs list unique orbits of size 40 at b=7 and 56 at b=9, containing the lifts of S=(6,0). The quarter-turn of the cross has an orientation-preserving affine lift to Q sending the physical S to T=(0,6); the kernel symmetry fixing the physical T exchanges its lifts. Hence both lifts of T belong to the same orbit as s. Each K-block has size four: no nonidentity kernel element fixes a lift of this noncentral interior point. D91 gives suborbit sizes 36 and 52, exactly the respective complements of the source block. The logs likewise give the unique size-48 orbit at b=7 for S=(5,1) and suborbit size 44 over T=(1,5). Physical transposition of the two coordinates has a positive-orientation lift switching halves. Both target lifts are in the size-48 orbit since the mirror normalizes G and the size-48 orbit is unique. T lies outside the sign orbit of S. The size-48 orbit is mirror-invariant and k=4. It follows **without any new group enumeration** that e(5,1)=44 at b=7. In contrast, e(6,0)≤3 even when the orbit of a lift of (0,6) under G_s has size 52. The single-mark ratio between the periods of (6,0) under F and ιFι cannot grow beyond this bound. The two-mark ratio at S and T is a different test; d91 establishes neither a single-element explanation nor an asymptotic formula for the cross-mark suborbit.

The saved orbit maps also give visible witnesses if desired: on the horizontal axis (6,0) and (4,0) lie in the same size-40 (respectively 56) orbit in different blocks; in the height-one row at b=7 the same is true of (5,1) and (3,1) in the size-48 orbit. One can use front lifts for these chords: in each case the orbit is unique of its size, hence mirror-invariant, and the logged orbit map records that size at the two endpoints. Thus the effective different-block lighting conclusion holds on these named orbits, even below the sufficient triangulation threshold. This check uses direct chords and recorded group degrees alone.

## Off-axis exceptions and what effectivity still requires

There is a genuine geometric exception beyond physical reflection axes. Suppose the room has a physical half-turn and no horizontal or vertical symmetry axis, with its rotation center S interior. K then has order two and its nonidentity element is central in G. Its fixed set on Q is exactly {s,ιs}. Fixing s fixes the other point, so **e(S)=1**, whenever S is a lattice point as required for the lattice question. No lower bound growing with area can exclude such a center by local orbit size. Its orbit of size at most two is consistent with the classification above. This symmetry alone gives no proof that every other interior point sees the center. Direct visibility does certify all targets when the center lies in the polygon's visibility kernel.

For an interior mark invariant under mirror at the level of its finite absolute orbit, e(S)=1 has a further exact group-theoretic description. If h s=ιs, then e(S)=1 iff h normalizes G_s: the stabilizer inclusion has equal finite indices. More generally e(S)=[G_s:G_s∩ιG_sι]. Thus a bounded off-axis mark in a large mirror-invariant orbit forces a **small additional suborbit outside the kernel block**; in particular the action cannot be transitive outside that block. If Ω and ιΩ are distinct, the same index is a bounded-degree G-invariant correspondence between two different orbits and the single-orbit proof does not apply. No complete geometric classification of these correspondences is known here.

Finally, the common shadow has an exact interpretation. For a distinct physical interior target T, its two Q lifts are hidden from **both** s and ιs if and only if T is physically hidden from S; if any of the four paths exists it and its mirror reach both target lifts from the two-source set. A smooth-wall target has just one lift. Hence on any chosen set of distinct physical targets the shared Q count is twice the number of physically dark interior targets plus the number of physically dark smooth-wall targets. Counting common shadows rather than ordinary shadows does not remove the physical darkness problem. In the same-parity lattice sample one always has the area-dependent bound floor(n/2) from the pillowcase local-degree count. A bound independent of area for the *entire* shared set still needs control of different absolute orbits and of actions with more than one outside-block suborbit. No room, ray, word, or direction search was run.
