# What a darkness certificate has to remember about a corridor

**Verdict.** There is a locality theorem for a certificate verified against an explicit set of permitted door transitions. Finiteness of a proof, or a list of corners in its final contradiction, does not establish this hypothesis. There is also an area bound for clean-fiber certificates: at most 64 physical cells in a normalized orthogonal room with 1≤r≤8. This bound is already implicit in d63/d68 and covers the entire room. None of these results bounds the support of a hypothetical BBBB darkness mechanism.

## Define support by what a verifier may change

Fix the room convention, marked endpoints, and the classes and lift choices claimed to be excluded. For total darkness the certificate must cover every admissible launch. Its **physical support** is the retained cells, incidences, stops, and marked lifts on which its soundness proof depends. Separately record its **interface assumptions**: the allowed relation between entry and exit position and signed velocity at every unretained passage. Treat distances between retained pieces, a global covering map, and “all preimages are forbidden” as hypotheses to verify. They cannot silently be treated as data supplied by a few marked cells. For a separating corridor, compare the retained pieces up to independent translations, identifying each door in its local coordinates.

**Locality lemma.** Suppose the certificate proves exclusion even when one may use *any* transition in its recorded relation at each visit to an omitted corridor. If after shortening, the retained pieces and marks match and every actual transition belongs to that relation, the certificate still excludes the pair. Indeed split a purported new shot at all door crossings; its retained segments obey the old local rules and all intervening transitions are permitted. This gives precisely a walk forbidden by the verifier. Apply this to every launch/lift covered. The doors are open; corner hits remain stops. For a parity-specific assertion require the identifications to preserve the stated parity labels. A mark in the deleted material is outside this lemma.

In particular, a certificate checked with completely free exit phase at that corridor is length-independent there. For a certificate that requires the *old* phase, the exact signed phase update through width w and length L at slope s is addition of Ls modulo 2w. Deleting two units preserves this update iff 2s belongs to 2wℤ; it does not preserve it on an open interval of slopes. No lookup in a central corridor cell is necessary to use this global datum. The earlier three-chamber example demonstrates the distinction on line visibility. It is not a counterexample involving an actual dark pair. [Exact period](length-periodicity.md) · [Named shortening control](parity-shortening.md)

**Limit on genuinely length-blind support.** Suppose the two marks lie strictly inside opposite sealed chambers, joined by an empty full-mouth corridor satisfying d124. A verifier which retains those chambers but reads neither the corridor length nor a length-dependent restriction on the two doors cannot certify that pair dark. Replace the corridor by an arbitrarily long one of the same width and length parity. Both chamber checks and the length-blind interface proof would persist, whereas d124 supplies a legal cross-pair shot at all sufficiently large lengths. This uses no known example of a dark cross pair. It limits the *form of proof* that could certify one. It does not apply to a one-way verifier which explicitly records an upper bound on admissible lengths, or to marks on the same side. [Reviewed cross-pair theorem](effective-chamber.md)

## A bounded physical support where the count actually applies

Use the standard pillowcase B, the half-translation double Q of a normalized strictly simple orthogonal lattice room, and n physical cells. Suppose Q itself is clean over some site x (no regular point there). Then n=f_x+3r_x≤4r+4. This is an equality of the *entire* fiber with multiplicities, not a way of sampling a few corners to certify that all other sheets are absent.

More generally suppose there is a proper factor Q→N→B with inner degree k and outer degree m>1. Write z for the number of simple zeros downstairs on N; N is a sphere and has z+4 simple poles. Normalization rules out the intermediate pillowcase-isogeny case, so z≥1. Each zero consumes k distinct reflexes upstairs, hence kz≤r. Two consequences follow.

* **Bounded outer degree suffices without any cleanliness.** If even one required factor has m≤M(W), then the *whole room* has n=km≤rM(W). This is an upper-bound hypothesis. D146 excludes m=2,3,4 in BBBB, a lower bound. The normalized odd crosses have proper factors with m=n/4 growing without bound despite r=4; a bound on *factors needed by dark pairs* would need darkness as input.
* **A clean factor bounds its own size.** If N is clean at one site, m=f'_x+3z_x≤(z+4)+3z=4z+4. Thus n≤4kz+4k≤8r. For 1≤r≤8 both the direct and proper clean-fiber cases have at most 64 cells. One can retain the entire cell complex: for example its cells, distinct unit edges and lattice vertices together number at most 9n before marking the endpoints. The factor labels and per-class exclusion argument still need verification. The area bound alone does not establish darkness. [d63 bound](../../illumination-generalization-2026-11-06/fable/directions-2026-11-09/d63-realization/README.md) · [Factor/clean-fiber equivalence](canonical-transfer.md) · [d146](exclusion-descent.md)

In BBBB all four canonical relations are universal. A clean fiber on a positive intermediate factor of outer degree >1 would force its partition to absorb all regular swaps at that site, contradicting universality. A degree-one base by itself gives no endpoint separation. Thus clean-factor completeness for a totally dark BBBB pair would exclude such a pair **immediately**, before applying a length bound or choosing a corridor. A finite per-room orbit table is a different type of certificate; its sheet labels and size can depend on n.

**Controls.** R22 uses a degree-three fold to a five-square staircase and correct continuation or reversal at four smooth seam endpoints. Verifying the fold obliges one to check the room pieces and how they glue. Cov4181 excludes only a specified class of a physically lit pair by a factor with four blocks of seven. Its exclusion uses where the marked lifts land and the branch data, including a clean site *different* from the site traversed by the failed arc. It supplies no total-darkness certificate. More generally preserving an exact factor of outer degree m under a two-column deletion through a corridor of width w requires m to divide 2w, because the new degree n−2w must still be divisible by m. This necessary condition is independent of whether any named corner was removed. [R22 fold](tokarsky-converse.md) · [cov4181 factor](exclusion-descent.md)

## Completeness and choice of corridor

The unproved hypothesis is **every totally dark pair in the claimed domain has a certificate of a specified known type**. One must name the type. For a direct or proper clean-site mechanism the bounded-area result above applies; the existing Q-class/d64 closures already handle those branches for the relevant orthogonal lattice marks. For Par/Cut/Rig, Theorem F already gives its own certificate-side exclusion for rational interior pairs and W≤20, with the review qualifications recorded in the write-up. D146 does not classify orbit exclusions on primitive or larger imprimitive BBBB rooms. These are not independent reasons to expect completeness. [Closure scope](../README.md) · [Theorem F, §3.3](../../illumination-generalization-2026-11-06/fable/directions-2026-11-09/d70-writeup/RESULTS.md)

Even a bound on *how many* cells a verifier retains does not imply a whole untouched corridor: one retained cell can meet each of the few corridors. If a straight corridor has L interior unit columns and at most f protected column indices, then L≥2f+2 guarantees two consecutive unprotected columns (otherwise the f+1 runs of unprotected columns each have length at most one). This gives a possible deletion site, not permission to change the phase relation across that site. Nor does it handle a large chamber or indentation with no separating corridor. Under the clean-factor hypothesis there is already an area cutoff; under a general bounded-cell hypothesis these interface and geometric assumptions are additional requirements. There is no unconditional f(W) for darkness certificates established here.

## Route assessment

| Route | What it would need to finish | Judgment |
|---|---|---|
| First-shot existence | Some legal straight leaf through compatible endpoint lifts in one class, with a proof uniform over BBBB. | The only one of the three that can cover an intrinsically sheet-dependent obstruction without classifying it first. A W-only direction-height bound is already false. No near-term proof is apparent. |
| Uniform corridor tail | Control independent of other chambers, including rooms without a useful separating neck. | Lowest priority. For real marks on a dilation-closed domain, a uniform specified-corridor tail implies invariance down to length one. Two comparable necks already defeat the size-based induction. |
| Certificate descent | Completeness for a concrete obstruction type, plus either an area bound or a verified interface that survives shortening. | The shortest **conditional** finish is via bounded factor degree. For clean fibers, descent adds no leverage beyond d63/d64. For broader obstructions the missing support bound is substantive. |

**Single most consequential lemma.** Every totally dark same-parity interior lattice pair in a normalized orthogonal W≤20 room has a physical corner-only site or a proper standard-pillowcase factor clean over at least one site. A proof closes that branch with existing results; a counterexample is itself a dark BBBB room. In light of the existing closures this is equivalent on the residual branch to the core conjecture, not a separate simplification. I would pursue first-shot existence over further support bookkeeping unless a new structural reason for this completeness implication emerges. Different-parity, nonlattice and mixed-wall targets require their own scope checks.
