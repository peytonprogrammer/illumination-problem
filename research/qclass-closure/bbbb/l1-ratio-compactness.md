Advances: L1 — chart-wise compactness with explicit face hypotheses. The marked offset staircase is lit for every pair of nonnegative real gains, including all relative growth rates. The general interior and face certificates remain open.

## What a finite subcover would prove

Fix a strictly simple orthogonal marked order type, including coordinate equalities, cyclic wall incidences and the two distinct interior marks. If there are k and l refined gaps, translate each minimum to zero and normalize the two spans independently to one. These operations preserve billiard paths because diagonal scalings commute with axis reflections. The parameter space is the product X of the two open unit-sum simplices of positive gap coordinates (dimensions k−1 and l−1). Its compactification K uses the two closed simplices with their ordinary product Euclidean topology. A zero coordinate retains the label of the missing band. One need not identify two boundary data just because their collapsed polygons look alike.

Precisely, suppose each z in K has a relatively open neighborhood V in K and a proof of illumination for **every** realization in V∩X. Then finitely many such neighborhoods cover K, and their certificates prove the chart. For the lattice-only conclusion it suffices to certify all rational ratio vectors in V∩X, which have integral representatives after separate axis scalings. A certificate may choose a different itinerary at different realizations. A finite subcover of geometric constructions need not be a finite list of wall itineraries.

At a face this hypothesis quantifies over all nearby positive thicknesses at once, with no restriction on their relative rates. Merely proving illumination of a collapsed room does not do this. A full cover leaves zero lattice exceptions. An uncovered face neighborhood can contain infinitely many distinct rational ratios of unbounded primitive height; finiteness of its lattice residual needs a separate theorem. Neither compactness nor existence of a subcover supplies effective radii or an algorithm to find the certificates.

## Interior: the fixed-room quantifier

For a fixed rational-angle polygon, Lelièvre–Monteil–Weiss give finitely many dark targets for each source. Wolecki gives finitely many dark pairs in the entire polygon. Orthogonal rooms with real side lengths satisfy the angle hypothesis. These are **fiberwise** conclusions: the room is held fixed. [Lelièvre–Monteil–Weiss, Theorem 2 for surfaces and Corollary 3 for polygons](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1407.2975) · [Wolecki](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.09358)

A legal finite itinerary between interior marks gives an open neighborhood by unfolding, strict transversality at wall hits, and clearance from corners. A direct chord uses clearance alone. Thus the dark locus is relatively closed in X. In a fixed room, any positive-dimensional family of allowed mark positions has at most finitely many exceptional pairs. This proves generic lighting there but does not certify an exceptional position by approximation: the clearances can tend to zero. In particular a chart can pin both marks to intersections of supporting lines. Its mark fiber can then be a singleton, entirely compatible with Wolecki's finite exceptional set. Even a closed graph over a continuum of room shapes has singleton fibers. Wall-rank membership alone cannot remove that obstruction; the known 22-wall dark example already has interior marks with a marked order type. It is outside the eight-reflex budget. The first interior case I cannot exclude in scope is an exceptional *specified* pair in one positive, nondegenerate chart, including two pinned marks. Neither paper gives an eight-reflex exclusion. ## Uniform face neighborhoods by codimension

A sufficient certificate at a codimension-c point consists of a developed chord with stable marks and regular reflections in surviving pieces, together with the following data at every collapsed seam it encounters. Stable marks lie outside every collapsing layer. Keep the itinerary fixed while allowing the developed direction to vary with the endpoints and mirrors. Require a transverse crossing in the common open overlap through **all** layers to be restored there. In a neighborhood of the crossing require a small rectangular passage through this stack, with positive lateral clearance. Keep the chord away from intersections of perpendicular collapsed seams, collapsed marks, and vanishing mirrors. These are sufficient hypotheses on the labeled pieces; a boundary point need not itself be a strictly simple room.

For codimension one choose a smaller box at each crossing, leaving a positive margin to its sides. Bound the transverse-to-normal speed ratio there by a finite constant, using transversality. The continued chord drifts by at most that constant times the restored thickness. Outside the boxes its finite unfolded segments and intended hits persist by the ordinary strict inequalities. Shrinking a single ambient neighborhood makes every entry displacement, change of gate endpoints and transverse drift smaller than its allotted margin. It works for neighboring points *along* the face as well as approaches from its interior side.

For the induction step, group consecutive parallel missing layers at the same crossing. Their drift bound uses their **sum** of thicknesses. Restore one coordinate with less than a fixed fraction of the available margin and retain the smaller boxes for the other coordinates. Crossings of other stacks use disjoint local boxes along the itinerary. Equivalently choose one radius in advance so that the sum of all c drift bounds and endpoint perturbations is below the smallest margin. This proves the induction uniformly for every subset of coordinates restored and every order, including highly unequal thicknesses. Relative interiors of faces alone are not compact; the construction is local at each point, and the finite-subcover step applies only after the higher-codimension points are also handled. The induction stops at a corner contact, an empty common overlap, a mark trapped in a disappearing layer, or a required reflection there, unless other geometry gives a certificate.

## An effective radius for a given interior or transparent-face certificate

Use the sup norm of the normalized gap coordinates in the product of closed simplices. Form a finite list of strict polynomial sign checks for the proposed witness at its center z. For an interior itinerary unfold its wall copies. Test transversal intersection with each designated open edge by intersection parameters strictly between its endpoints, in strict order along the chord. For each closed leg and each nonincident edge of its copy, choose a separating line at z; retain the strict signs for both endpoints of the leg and both endpoints of the edge. Such a line exists because these are disjoint compact segments, and a rational separating line can be chosen by strictness. The inward departure and arrival signs at prescribed reflections are retained. Coordinates of unfolded marked images and wall endpoints are affine in the gaps. Leg endpoints are rational functions obtained by intersection with the supporting lines. Clear denominators after including their nonzero signs among the tests. This produces polynomials p with p(z)>0. Signs can be verified by exact arithmetic at rational or algebraic centers, or with supplied rigorous lower bounds at a real center.

For a transparent stack use the same unfolded chord (no new reflection in the stack). At each constituent interface intersect the chord with its supporting line and require the intersection coordinate to lie strictly inside a chosen common open subinterval at z. Also check the endpoints of that subinterval strictly inside the relevant component of each layer. These tests have strict slack even when all those interface levels coincide at z. Keep transversality and separation from other events. Within that stack the interface ordering is automatic from positive gaps and the axial sign; omit differences of coincident event times from the strict list. The segment between consecutive intersections remains in the common rectangular passage. This supplies explicit tests for the transparent-stack hypotheses above, also for multiple seam encounters in different unfolded copies. It assumes stable endpoints and no disappearing reflection or intersecting perpendicular seams at the crossings.

For each resulting polynomial p(v)=Σ cₘ vᵐ put B(p)=Σ |cₘ|·|m|, with |m| the total degree. On the unit cube the sum of absolute first partials is bounded by B(p). A certified ambient radius is therefore

    ρ = min(1, min over p of p(z)/(2 max(1,B(p)))).

All sign checks retain at least half their slack on that relative ball, uniformly over nearby face points and all positive resolutions therein. If a strict check on positive realizations factors into positive gap factors times a polynomial with strict slack on the face, discard those factors before computing ρ. The radius is conditional on the listed itinerary and clearances; it cannot manufacture them for an exceptional pair. This makes the local certificate quantitative without implying an effective finite cover of all charts.

## Hand test: all ratios in the offset staircase

Use the special two-gain slice, keeping the widths and unit end lengths fixed in the original coordinates. Its real compactification is the triangle of barycentric parameters [1:x:y] normalized by their sum for x,y≥0; [0:b:c] is the edge at infinity. This is a slice of the full marked order chart. Only the real extension has a filled triangle; the integer-gain set alone is discrete at finite gains.

Let D=4+x+y. The direct chord works precisely for open middle-gate crossings y<2x+2 and x<2y+2. This contains the entire √2 sequence, including t=15, where d213's strict parent family misses. The previous two-scale theorem also supplies a passing parent for min(x,y)≥6 and max(x,y)>3(4+min(x,y))²−(4+min(x,y)) on the lattice. These two results alone leave the x^(3/2) regime. [Previous bounds](l1-selection-obstruction.md) · [d213 review](../../illumination-generalization-2026-11-06/fable/directions-2026-11-09/d213-review-marked-selection/review.md)

**Reflected construction.** Rotate the room by 180 degrees and reverse the pair if necessary, so x≤y is the lower gain. Set α=(x+2)/D, hence 0<α≤1/2. Choose a positive integer k congruent to 3 or 5 modulo 6 with

    1 < kα < 2.

Such a k always exists. The intervals (1/k,2/k) for k=3,5,9,11,15,17,… overlap consecutively because each successive k is strictly less than twice its predecessor. Their union is (0,2/3). Take slope s=k/D, so 0<s<2/(x+2)≤1. Launch up and right from abscissa 2. At the first interface the abscissa is 2+s in (1,3). At the middle interface it is 2+kα in (3,4). Both legs are straight inside their tiers.

In the long tier [3,6], unfold side reflections. It suffices to aim at developed abscissa 2+k at the target level. Its fold in [3,6] is 5: the two image classes of 5 are 5 and 1 modulo 6. At the last interface, one axial unit before the target, the folded abscissa is 5−s or 5+s. Either lies in (4,6), and the final unit leg stays inside the top tier and reaches 5. Every intermediate side reflection is on an open vertical wall. The only possible tier-end corner hits have been excluded by the three strict overlaps. This proves illumination of the specified pair for **every** finite real x,y≥0.

In particular the two vertices at infinity with only one dominant gain have full relative-neighborhood certificates by this variable-k construction. Along y=⌊x^(3/2)⌋ one can choose k of order √x. There is no surviving ratio gap for this pair in this slice. The construction illustrates why a face certificate may need arbitrarily many reflections as its face is approached. It neither supplies a passing single-band parent nor covers every pair or every width assignment in the full staircase chart.

**First open case for L1 coverage.** In the general chart the interior exceptional-pair exclusion is already missing before compactness can be invoked. Independently, the first unsupported face step is a collapse without a regular common-overlap crossing or another uniform replacement construction. The staircase solves that problem for its named pair. No new census follows from the compactness argument.


## A nondirect test at area 126 and at the irrational face

Keep the same four tiers, but move the lower mark from abscissa 2 to **1**; keep its height one, and keep the upper mark at abscissa 5 one unit below the roof. These are both strictly interior. In the following computation a,b,c are barycentric coordinates proportional to (1,x,y), so a+b+c=1 and the normalized axial travel between marks is D=4a+b+c. The three interface times are a, 2a+b and D−a. This radius calculation is in the specified two-gain slice.

At gains (15,21) the direct middle crossing has abscissa 1+4·17/40=27/10, outside (3,4). Instead use the image of the target across the long tier’s wall at abscissa 6: that image is at 7. The transverse displacement is 6. At the first, middle and last interfaces the physical abscissae are respectively **23/20, 71/20 and 103/20**. They lie in (1,3), (3,4) and (4,6). The ray hits x=6 once inside the third tier and then reaches the target. In particular this seed meets no corner. This is a reducible, nondirect pair, so the theorem must discharge it on the lift side, regardless of how small the terminal base F is.

In fact the same proof applies on the open region

    b+c−2a>0,       c−b>0,       2a+2b−c>0.

The first inequality gives 6a/D<1, so the first crossing is at 1+6a/D and the last at 5+6a/D after reflection. The other two give 3<1+6(2a+b)/D<4. The direct abscissa 1+4(2a+b)/D is strictly below 3 throughout this region. These tests allow a=0 as a neighborhood center: the positive factor a makes the first crossing strictly to the right of the mouth endpoint for every positive realization, however small the end tier. A regular path in the collapsed room at that corner is unnecessary.

A relative open ball preserving all three signs has radius

    min{(b+c−2a)/4, (c−b)/2, (2a+2b−c)/5}.

This uses the exact coefficient norms of the linear tests. At (1,15,21)/37 the radius is **11/185**. At the face center (0,1,√2)/(1+√2) it is **(2−√2)/(5(1+√2))**. Thus this is also a uniform neighborhood of nearby face ratios, with actual corner clearance allowed to tend to zero as the end tier disappears. The original lower mark at abscissa 2 is directly visible for the entire √2 sequence; this replacement avoids that discharge. For this new pair even the x^(3/2) regime eventually has a construction: put α=(x+2)/(4+x+y) and take k≡0 or 4 modulo 6 in (2/α,3/α). When α<1/4 this interval has length greater than four, the maximum spacing in these image classes. For x≥1 the slope k/(4+x+y)<3/(x+2)≤1. The middle crossing lies in (3,4); the last approaches the target at 5±k/(4+x+y) inside (4,6).
