Advances: L1 — obtaining a legal chord from a common open cylinder (CC) for a same-class interior lattice pair.

**Result.** No fixed finite list of rational directions guarantees CC, even for normalized, factor-free BBBB rooms with four reflex corners and a nondirect same-parity pair. The obstruction below includes both axes and works for any prescribed slope-height cutoff. It leaves open a direction chosen as a function of the dimensions. CC in that unrestricted form would give a legal chord immediately: lift the two points in the open cylinder to its Euclidean strip and join them there.

### Exact axis test

Use the four-copy translation unfolding with all physical corners marked, including convex ones. Slice the physical room horizontally. In a regular horizontal band, each connected slice interval (l,r) with unchanged endpoints produces a periodic strip: the ray travels between its two vertical ends, with unfolded circumference 2(r−l). Continue the strip through successive levels as long as the two endpoints stay the same and the leaf meets no marked vertex. Wall changes in *other* components of the same slice do not stop this continuation. A corner on the leaf cuts it, even when the mark itself is strictly interior. The reverse construction by folding shows that the resulting maximal open rectangles, swept out by the slice intervals, are precisely the projections of the open horizontal cylinders. Sheet reflections allow the two marks to be lifted to the same cylinder whenever both lie in one such rectangle. Exchange x and y for the vertical test.

Thus on each axis there are two ways to fail: at least one mark is on a critical leaf, or both marks lie in regular strips but in different ones. Both axes fail when each has one of these obstructions. D72's regular-row test is the same check in square coordinates. For an oblique direction the cuts are entire trajectories through marked vertices; counting reflexes alone does not locate those trajectories. D84 gives a different sufficient test when two exchanged cylinders and their paired rim intervals meet its hypotheses. It proves lighting even for certain boundary pairs, without placing those marks inside an open cylinder. ### An obstruction to every finite low-slope menu

Given any finite list, write its nonvertical directions as (p,q) in primitive integer coordinates with p>0. Choose D a positive multiple of 6 lcm{p} (take D=6 for an empty list). Use two chambers [−2D,0] × [−1,2] and [1,5] × [−1,2], joined by [0,1] × [0,1]. Take the interior of the closed union and put S=(−D,0), T=(2,0). They are interior, same-parity, and their direct chord meets the bottom neck corners. The four strictly inset shoulders give r=4, W=12. The wall lengths include a unit horizontal and a unit vertical length, giving axis normalization. All four parities occur in the interior of the left chamber.

This is also BBBB in D72's stronger sense. The area is n=6D+13, coprime to 6. In a normalized room the reviewed quotient budget gives kZ≤r for any proper block system, where k is the block size and the nontrivial quotient has Z≥1 zeros. Hence k would be 2, 3, or 4 and would divide n, which is impossible. With Q classes excluded by the interior points, every canonical factor is B. For **every** listed nonvertical direction and every lift of S, follow the leaf toward its first contact with x=0. The horizontal travel is D if initially rightward and 3D if initially leftward (one smooth reflection at x=−2D). The chamber's vertical fold has period 6. At travel D and 3D the unfolded vertical displacement is an integer multiple of 6, for either vertical sign. Thus the contact at x=0 is exactly (0,0), a reflex corner. All previous floor and ceiling hits take place on smooth walls, and an earlier outer-wall hit has ordinate zero. The source leaf is critical, so no lift of S lies inside a cylinder in any listed nonvertical direction. For the vertical direction the two marks are in different chamber strips, separated by a neck whose vertical interval is only (0,1). They cannot share a vertical cylinder either.

The **same physical reflex** therefore spoils arbitrarily many prescribed slopes with only four reflexes in the room. In particular, taking D=12 eliminates the axes and the signed (1,1), (1,2), (2,1) tests together. Enlarging the prescribed list changes D. The order of these quantifiers does not produce one room failing all rational directions. No common cylinder for the entire variable-D family is established here.
