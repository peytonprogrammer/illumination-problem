# Genuine cylinder avoidance and the two torus degrees

**Result for review.** Cut cylinders only at forbidden corner lifts. There is no genus-and-mark-count bound on the relative-period degree of general square-tiled translation surfaces satisfying even all-lift darkness. A branched-cover construction over the *named R22* gives an explicit obstruction. For an actual orthogonal or mixed 45° room, by contrast, the index between absolute and corner-relative periods divides four. Thus any unbounded-degree room counterexample would need unbounded **absolute** torus degree. This does not decide whether such rooms exist at W≤20.

### Which cylinder test detects a legal shot?

Track the endpoint lifts without cutting at them. If one endpoint is in an open cylinder and the other in its completion, or their occurrences are on opposite rims of the same cylinder, the straight open chord of the developed strip suffices. Add one further allowed incidence: both lifts strictly inside one open saddle connection of the corner critical graph. The intervening subsegment then provides a legal shot. It matters that this means a single open edge between consecutive forbidden sites; lying on the same rim does not suffice.

For rational marks on a square-tiled unfolding, these tests combined over all rational directions are *equivalent* to illumination. Indeed take the direction of a legal connecting segment. Its displacement in developed coordinates is rational and nonzero. The directional decomposition is periodic. A trajectory on an interior closed leaf stays there. Otherwise its regular starting point is on the critical graph; until the next forbidden corner it runs inside a single open edge. In particular the second endpoint lies on that same edge. The converse is the elementary chord or subsegment construction above. This equivalence explains the limits of adding rim tests: failure of all the augmented incidences is exactly darkness. The original, weaker corner-only incidence exclusion is also inherited by genuinely dark pairs.

### A fixed-genus obstruction even with true darkness

Take the ordinary 60-square translation unfolding X of R22. Its 22 physical corner lifts are 13 regular convex lifts and nine zeros of order two. Its genus is 10. Retain its four source and four target lifts. In a Euclidean unit-square chart choose two regular rational branch values in the open square separated by the horizontal segment (1/N,0), N≥4; choose a fixed rational starting position with enough clearance and no endpoint label on the slit. Glue two copies of X crosswise along the slit. The result X_N is a connected translation double cover, branched just at the two tips. Both new ramification points are genuine order-one zeros. The surface is square-tiled after a uniform clearing of denominators. This is a translation-surface test of the proposed genus-only inference, with no claim that X_N is a room unfolding.

Take as forbidden sites F_N the 44 preimages of the old corners and the two new zeros. Track separately the eight preimages of source lifts and eight of target lifts. Riemann–Hurwitz gives genus 20, with 46 corner/branch sites and 16 endpoint labels for every N. A legal connection upstairs projects by local translation on its open part to one downstairs: every preimage of an old forbidden corner is forbidden. Thus every source–target lift pair is genuinely dark for all N, using cylinders cut only at F_N. In fact d118 shows the R22 pair remains dark over all lift choices even if only true zeros are forbidden.

In the unscaled charts area(X_N)=120. The lifted slit is a relative period (1/N,0) between forbidden sites. A physical vertical edge of R22 of fixed length h lifts to a relative edge of period (0,h), since the slit lies in the open square. The lattice Λ_rel of corner/branch relative periods therefore has covolume at most h/N. Its torus degree is at least 120N/h. Clearing denominators leaves this degree invariant. On the other hand Λ_abs(X_N)⊆Λ_abs(X)⊆ℤ², since every closed curve projects to a closed curve and has the same integral. Its absolute torus degree is at most 120. Thus it is the relative index that diverges here. This example defeats a proof from corner-only cylinder avoidance, fixed genus and fixed mark count alone; it does not refute a theorem using the physical room hypotheses. ### The half-turn controls the relative index in rooms

In the canonical translation unfolding of a room with walls at multiples of π/4, the group of copy symmetries contains the central half-turn ι of derivative −I (for either pure orthogonal orientation use its four-copy unfolding). It fixes each physical corner lift individually. Indeed copies at a corner are identified under the subgroup generated by its two incident reflections. Their product is a nontrivial rotation by a multiple of π/2 at a genuine turn; that cyclic subgroup contains −I. Centrality puts −I in every conjugate corner stabilizer, including corners with multiple lifts.

Let F consist of all physical corner lifts, with no endpoints in this relative set. If a is a relative path chain in H₁(X,F;ℤ), its relative boundary is fixed pointwise by ι. Thus a−ιa is absolute and its period is twice the period of a. In the common plane,

    2Λ_rel ⊆ Λ_abs ⊆ Λ_rel,
    I=[Λ_rel:Λ_abs] ∈ {1,2,4},
    d_rel=I d_abs.

The last equality follows by dividing the same area by the two covolumes. The order-one zeros in the auxiliary cover are another way to see the difference: the genuine mixed room orders allowed by d135 are even. The index argument works for the real room class regardless of the number of convex vertices that have to be labelled.

For scalar-primitive mixed rooms area(P)=c I d_abs/8, with c=1 or 2 as in rigidity-area.md. To force area≤20 the exact absolute-degree targets are therefore 160/I at c=1 and 80/I at c=2. Sufficient bounds without prior knowledge of I are 40 and 20 respectively. These are targets, not established upper bounds. Bounding the number of corner-graph edges or cylinders controls their count while leaving their metric lengths free. The unresolved issue is whether the half-turn and the *actual placement* of its corner-fixed points prevent large absolute degree when every lift pair avoids the corner-only incidences. D137's tangent rank gives no such restriction. No search or cusp enumeration was run.
