Advances: L1 — Test of whether primitive monodromy supplies straight connections in d152's normalized three-chamber family.

Throughout take X=Y=3k+1, k=35j, j≥1, and assume the primitivity conclusion under d199 review. S,T are arbitrary distinct interior lattice marks. Monodromy acts on a regular fiber of the pillowcase cover, while the marks lie above the four branch values. Any argument transporting between these two descriptions must control the approach to the marked fibers.

**Direct tests in the actual geometry.** Place the chambers at [iD,iD+3] × [0,3], i=0,1,2, with D=X+3=3k+4. Consecutive chambers are joined along 1<y<2. Thus the interior lattice marks are precisely (iD+u,v) with u,v∈{1,2}. There are no lattice heights strictly inside a unit corridor, and an integral mouth endpoint is a corner. The following connections are uniform in k.

* Two marks in the same chamber are joined by their segment. For different chambers and different rows, the open segment stays strictly inside 1<y<2, which runs the full length of the room.
* For adjacent chambers and marks in the same row, aim at the opposite edge of the corridor at their horizontal midpoint and reflect there. The midpoint is strictly within the intervening corridor since D≥22 and the offsets are 1 or 2. The two halves stay strictly between the two corridor heights except at their endpoints and the one smooth-wall bounce. Reflection in y=3/2 exchanges the two rows.
* For the outer chambers in the same row with left offset u=2 and right offset v=1, first unfold across the left outer wall x=0. In the unfolded picture the endpoints have abscissas −2 and 2D+1, and their midpoint D−1/2 lies inside the first corridor. The same opposite-edge bounce works. At x=0 the folded ray reflects strictly between heights 1 and 2 on a smooth wall. Thus even one horizontal reversal can remove a midpoint obstruction when the offsets allow it.

**First remaining pair and an obstruction for every horizontally monotone path.** Take S=(1,1), T=(2D+1,1). Suppose a legal ray joined them without reversing its horizontal velocity. It crosses both middle-chamber mouths, at x=D and x=D+3, strictly between heights 1 and 2. In the full unfolding its x midpoint is x=D+1; its y midpoint is integral because the source and all reflected target images have odd integral y-coordinate. Refolding at integral horizontal walls preserves integrality. Thus, in the middle chamber, unfolding only across y=0 and y=3, its straight line has integer ordinate m at x=D+1.

Write a,b for the ordinates of that same line at the two mouths. At either mouth the folded height lies in (1,2). Reflection in either horizontal chamber wall and translation by 6 preserve the condition “residue modulo 3 in (1,2)”. Hence both a and b must satisfy it. But linear interpolation gives b=3m−2a. Writing a=3t+1+ε with 0<ε<1 gives b congruent to 1−2ε modulo 3, whose residue lies outside (1,2). This contradiction excludes all horizontally monotone shots, simultaneously at every j and every slope. It does not exclude a path reflecting from a vertical wall; in that case the horizontal midpoint need no longer be one third of the way across the middle chamber.

**What primitivity would still have to supply.** For this pair, a connecting path now specifically requires at least one horizontal reversal, with both corridor apertures cleared on the same unfolded straight line. Primitivity gives a way to disprove a proposed block: if a nonsingleton proper subset of sheets is proposed, some group element takes it to an overlapping, unequal subset. The element is realized by continuation along a loop avoiding the branch sites. Neither the definition of primitivity nor the midpoint computation turns that loop into a legal connecting segment with the required reflection. In particular the congruence above attaches to a specified order of two mouth crossings; it does not show that the same exclusion persists under continuation around each branch point. Taking the group-invariant closure of excluded pairs need not preserve exclusion by straight rays. This is the first failed implication. Full illumination and a nontrivial block forced by darkness both remain unproved.

**Congruence-class follow-on.** All three positive constructions and the monotone obstruction apply without the assumption k=35j, including k≡1 mod 5 or mod 7. The arithmetic under review permits size five in the first class, seven in the second, and either size on their overlap. It asserts existence of neither. For the explicit remaining pair the geometric reversal problem already occurs in the primitive subfamily; permitting an additional block size does not construct a reversal. This is the extent of the extension. No denominator or height cutoff was used.
