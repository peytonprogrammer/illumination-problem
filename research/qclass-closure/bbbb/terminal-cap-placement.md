# Placing a final reversal without losing earlier doors

**Terminal-cap lemma.** Suppose a prescribed chain of rectangular passages takes a source to the open mouth of a last rectangle. On an open slope interval I, assume a fixed developed chart gets through every earlier window legally, including the final mouth, and enters with the required sheet. Measure transverse height in the last rectangle from its bottom. Its developed entry coordinate has the form β+A s, where A>0 is the total longitudinal travel to the mouth, s is the signed transverse slope and β is constant. Use the triangular fold of period 2H for a rectangle of height H. The last rectangle has depth L, solid lateral walls and a full solid far cap. Let the target have depth 0≤t<L and transverse height 0<u<H; if t=0 it must lie in the open mouth.

For a path which goes to the cap and returns to the target, put B=A+2L−t and C=A+L. For either chosen target sign σ the complete list of endpoint slopes in this chart is

    s_k = (σu + 2Hk − β)/B,     k integral, s_k in I.

A candidate is legal provided β+C s_k is outside Hℤ. This last test excludes both ends of the cap; it is exact when both ends are stopping corners. There is no further seam to check, since the return stops at depth t. Between these three events all lateral reflections are smooth. The criterion also works when the prefix chart unfolds earlier turns, provided it includes their wall guards.

**Two consecutive indices suffice.** The cap heights for adjacent k differ by 2HC/B. From A>0, t≥0 and L>t we have 1<2C/B<2. Therefore the difference is not an integer multiple of H: two adjacent candidates cannot both hit a cap end. In particular, if **B·length(I)>4H**, every interior target height has a legal single-cap placement on either prescribed target sign. Indeed the image interval under s↦β+Bs contains at least two consecutive members of σu+2Hℤ. Equality is not used because the windows are open. With rational C/B=m/n in lowest terms, bad k are either absent or one class modulo n/gcd(n,2). This proves a general cap test after any chart, without building a separate backward chart for the far-wall bounce. For a partially open cap the forbidden set contains whole intervals and the two-index conclusion no longer follows. **DB55, exact short-interval control.** Use S=(1,1) and T=(9,1). The saved path through the two mouths and around the right cap has s₀=−37/8. For s=s₀+δ the first height is 5/2+4δ; the second is 21/8+7δ on the continuing positive folding sheet. Both are in (2,3) throughout −5/56<δ<3/56. The left-chamber unfolded height differs from its physical mouth height by −20 and the corridor makes 14 folds at the central slope; continuity on the stated window preserves both signs. In last-chamber coordinates β=35, A=7, L=5, t=1, H=5, B=16 and C=12. For σ=+ and u=1 the developed cap height is (19+15k)/2. This can **never** be a multiple of 5, because 19+15k is 4 modulo 5. At k=−4 we get s=−37/8, cap height −41/2 (physical height 1/2) and developed target height −39 (physical height 1). Here B·length(I)=16/7, far below 4H=20. The exact indexed test succeeds where the uniform interval bound does not. The first mouth occurs at one quarter of developed travel after this turn instead of the blocked halfway time before it. This calculation selects no new direction. [Saved itinerary and signed heights](reversal.md)

**R22, why the natural top-cap turn cannot repair the monotone chain.** Keep S=(0,0), T=(2,2). Start upward, reach y=3 in the top arm, then return to T on its mouth y=2, allowing any number of lateral reflections but no earlier reversal of vertical velocity. The original halfway obstruction at y=1 is gone: this crossing is now at one quarter. However, the outward crossing at y=2 is now halfway through total longitudinal travel four. The developed endpoint abscissas are even for every choice of lateral images; at halfway the abscissa is integral, and folding preserves integrality. The outward top mouth is 1<x<3. Its only integral crossing is x=2, which is **T itself**. Truncating there would produce an upward-monotone shot from S to T, already excluded by the halfway test at y=1. Thus every candidate for this turned word fails before the cap. The argument uses the actual open mouth, not an assumption that the cap has no legal contact. It exemplifies a general exclusion: if an earlier forced lattice hit in an open door can only be the target and the truncated itinerary cannot reach it, the longer itinerary cannot repair it. **Status.** The two-rectangle bound is constructive. A chain prefix supporting a sufficiently broad legal interval remains compatible with a full terminal cap by the lemma above. Neither room height nor a small wall count guarantees that prefix interval, and a turn can move the halfway obstruction to a different, mandatory door. R22's full darkness proof still uses the global fold. The proposed W≤20 implication would require a route-selection theorem for the remaining charts, including returns through intermediate doors. It does not follow from the cap lemma. No search was run.

**D157 review.** The cap test is conditional on a common prefix interval. For a turned prefix, wall guards require each crossing to lie on its open physical door and each bounce on a solid smooth wall subsegment, with the intervening legs inside their actual rectangles and in the prescribed order. The top-arm R22 exclusion treats only its specified longitudinal itinerary.
