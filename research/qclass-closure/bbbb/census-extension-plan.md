Advances: L2 — finite-area census of strictly simple orthogonal lattice rooms with W ≤ 20.

**Decision requested.** Approve at most **area 21 across all words** as the first slice. It would close one full area and measure the cost of deciding all lattice pairs. Defer 22–30. With d9's unmodified generation strategy, the second band alone projects to about 23 quadrillion fixed polyominoes visited. This document is a design. No enumeration, shooting, orbit decision or benchmark was run.

### Pipeline and coverage

1. Reuse d9's sharded Redelmeier generation of edge-connected unit-cell sets. For the requested area, reject diagonal pinch masks, holes (Euler characteristic must be one), and more than 20 maximal walls (count lattice vertices incident to one or three cells). Translate to the bounding-box origin and keep the least cell key among the eight lattice symmetries. Retain integral stretches as distinct rooms: dividing axis lengths can send a lattice mark to a nonlattice mark. Set the wall limit to 20 in the driver; it currently passes 22. The enumerator's MAXN is 24; resize and check buffers, counters, row masks and verifier limits before planning a run through 30. Do not prune a partial polyomino merely for exceeding 20 walls: later cells can remove corners.
2. Count every canonical room. Mark each vertex incident to four cells and test every unordered pair of distinct marks, including different parities. Rooms with fewer than two marks finish vacuously. **Change both producer and consumer:** d9's phase-one emitter drops rooms unless one parity contains two marks, and its shooter groups by parity. Reuse the all-class pair handling from d9 Part A, omitting the real-point cylinder/grid tasks. STATE summarizes the area-20 baseline; the d9/d29 phase-one logs specifically document same-class coverage. Before reporting a cumulative all-pair theorem through 21 or 30, reconcile the historical different-class coverage through 20. A new exact-area result can stand separately. 3. Use bounded exact shooting as a positive filter and independently replay each hit with the plane tracer. Send every unresolved pair to the finite modular-cover orbit: unfold four reflected copies (4A squares), label mark lifts and forbidden corners, canonicalize the marked horizontal/vertical gluing permutations, and traverse the two integral shears and their inverses. Test horizontal connections on each marked cover; the orbit represents all primitive directions, including paths of multiple primitive steps. A hit proves LIT. Only complete orbit exhaustion without a hit proves DARK by this procedure. Retain incomplete or resource-limited cases as unresolved. Independently reproduce any DARK result using the second orbit implementation and d2 decider, with canonical cells and physical mark coordinates.
4. Require a completion record for each disjoint shard. Reconcile fixed-node and canonical-room counts by (area,W), the sum of binomial(interior marks,2) against verified-lit + confirmed-dark + unresolved, and the two parity subtotals. Before an extension, validate the modified generator against d9's stored area-20 counts and the pair logic against Part A's all-class tables. Keep the known W=22 dark pair as a separate positive control. Stream lit witnesses through the verifier and keep shard manifests, survivor records and deterministic replay data.

### Outcomes and cost reduction

- **Confirmed dark pair:** stop the extension. A strictly simple W ≤ 20 counterexample ends the proposed 22-wall minimality program. Preserve the independent verdicts and geometry. Claim smallest area only when all smaller eligible areas have complete coverage.
- **All pairs lit:** extend the certified finite range to the completed area, subject to the baseline scope above. This supplies evidence for a candidate N₀ and tests for a future tail theorem. It supplies no all-area cutoff. Any unfinished shard or orbit leaves its scope open.
- **Smaller alternative:** area 21 restricted to ledger-OPEN words yields a counterexample hunt if even the full area is too costly. Record the ledger snapshot and canonical word rule. PARTIAL words still have uncovered assignments, so an OPEN-only pass cannot certify area 21. Filtering after generation saves pair work only; dividing room counts by the fraction of ledger words would misprice the slice.

**Largest projected aggregate cost:** generating fixed polyominoes that fail the wall or symmetry filter. A single survivor orbit can still dominate memory. To cut generation, design a generator of simple boundaries for W ≤ 20 directly, by canonical turn word and positive integral edge lengths with closure, area and simplicity constraints. At area A every edge length is ≤A, so the parameter domain is finite. Canonicalize the final cell key across symmetries and boundary starting positions. Before substituting it for d9, require equality of canonical key sets at small areas and agreement with the stored (area,W) counts through 20. This is a proposed optimization with unmeasured performance.

**First open L2 case:** exact area 21, W ≤ 20, interior lattice pairs in rooms outside proved all-area classes, in particular BBBB rooms. No extension has been authorized or executed here.
