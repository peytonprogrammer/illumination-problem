Advances: L1 — the local clearance lemma at a pinned interior mark is area-uniform. Every such pin has the same free local geometry. The first failed implication is the existence of a global itinerary that arrives on a line through the specified pin. A dark and a directly lit realization of the **same R22 marked chart** distinguish that obstruction from any local crossing type. I obtain no B(r) for r≤8.

### The local classification

Let X and Y be the sorted distinct wall coordinates, refined by the mark coordinates. A pin p=(Xᵢ,Yⱼ) has both coordinates on wall levels, but p is a strictly interior point of the physical room. Write δₚ for the minimum of its four adjacent refined gaps, left, right, below and above. They all exist. Then

    p + (−δₚ,δₚ)² ⊂ interior(P).

Indeed a vertical wall on another abscissa is at least δₚ away in x. A vertical wall on Xᵢ cannot contain p; its nearer endpoint has a different wall ordinate and is at least δₚ away in y. The same argument holds for horizontal walls. The open square is connected, contains p and misses the boundary. Equivalently all four open grid cells incident to p are occupied. The supporting lines crossing at p are coordinate references, and neither carries a wall *there*.

For completeness, the four quadrant occupancies at a grid intersection of a strictly simple orthogonal room give the following local types up to axial symmetry: zero occupied (exterior), one (convex), two adjacent (smooth wall), three (reflex), four (interior). Two opposite occupied sectors alone would pinch the boundary. Only the four-sector type is admissible for either mark. This classification is for actual positive realizations. A collapsed limit can identify several levels, marks or walls and requires separate face data. If the least gap is g, both pins have free neighborhoods of radius g in the sup norm, with **every** incoming bearing locally legal. There is no exceptional local type among the interior pins.

### Why a free square does not give a connecting direction

Fix an unfolded itinerary prior to arrival. It determines a developed target image p*. For a nonzero displacement from the developed source s, the only possible direction for this itinerary is the ray along p*−s. An interval of slopes can reach the free square without any of its lines through p* satisfying the upstream gates. Once inside the square the ray has no wall on which to turn. For example, in coordinates with required longitudinal travel D>0 and transverse displacement Δ, a persistence interval (l,u) gives an exact hit iff Dl<Δ<Du, in addition to itinerary legality. Enlarging the free square relaxes the test for entering the square but does not remove this scalar equality of aim. A family of distinct target images may help, but their positions and spacings depend on the whole unfolding.

D214 claim 1 bounds a window for a **given rational seed**, by 2/(bH) in its slope notation. Claim 6 succeeds by proving that a swept interval contains an allowed residue for a particular family. Neither gives a window containing a target image for every pinned pair. D215 supplies a perturbation radius once a regular itinerary or a transparent-face certificate is present. Finiteness of dark pairs in each fixed room does not, by itself, exclude a specified exceptional pair, even if nearby marks can move. Thus restricting the project to pins is a possible research focus; the reviewed compactness theorem alone does not prove every other specified pair lit. ### What an absolute gap bound would mean

On the class as worded, with all integral realizations of each chart allowed, the proposed bound is equivalent to **absence of any pinned dark pair in scope**. If (P,S,T) is one such pair, dilation by any positive integer t preserves the chart, pinning, strict simplicity, wall count and darkness. Inverse dilation carries any putative witness back to the original. Every refined gap is multiplied by t. Taking t>B(r)/g contradicts any finite absolute bound. This argument stays in the same nondegenerate ratio chart. Conversely, if there are no dark pairs in the class, the conditional bound holds vacuously.

If the intended domain is only the representative with the gcd of the gaps divided out on each axis, dilation is excluded. For pinned marks the wall and refined levels agree. A primitive-gap bound on these representatives is a different statement, and local clearance gives no upper bound on their minimum gap. Even if such a bound is proved, *one* small gap does not imply small area or a legal lift from a compressed parent. D214 claim 7 characterizes irreducibility by **every** refined gap being one. A reducible room still needs the witness-transfer step; shrinking a unit gap to zero can change the chart. ### R22 control: same marked chart, opposite lighting outcomes

In the manuscript coordinates both S=(0,0) and T=(2,2) are pinned. For S, the supporting line x=0 carries the external vertical edge between y=−2 and −1, and y=0 carries an external horizontal edge over 3<x<5. For T, x=2 carries an external vertical edge below y=−1, and y=2 carries an external horizontal edge over 0<x<1. Both marks themselves sit in open passages. They have the four-sector interior type just proved. The shared seams in the three-copy construction are not interior mirrors. [R22 cells and seams](../../illumination-publication/repository/preprints/twenty-two-wall/manuscript.tex)

Here is a symbolic comparison preserving even the marked wall order and wall incidences. Dilate R22 by t≥1. The pair (0,0),(2t,2t) remains dark, with least refined gap t. Now leave all abscissas and all wall ordinates at or below t unchanged, and increase all ordinates at or above 2t by 2, including the ordinate of T. Use the order-preserving linear interpolation between these levels. This is a deformation to a new room, not a billiard conjugacy. It preserves strict simplicity, the exact marked chart, the pin types and the parity of both marks. Its least refined gap is still t.

The deformed pair (0,0),(2t,2t+2) is **directly lit**. In the first positive tier, 0<y<t, its ray is in the left interval (−t,t). At y=t its abscissa is t²/(t+1), strictly between 0 and t, so it crosses the tier interface inside the overlap. In the next tier t<y<2t+2 the two reflected staircase copies meet along an open seam and their combined interval is (0,4t). The segment stays inside it and reaches the interior target. In the undeformed room the same direct-chord test gives x=t at the interface, exactly the reflex corner. Excluding **every reflected itinerary** in the undeformed room requires the staircase parity fold and seam-reversal argument. None of those obstructions occurs in the pin neighborhoods. As t grows the normalized ratios of the lit rooms approach the dark ratio, with the same local pin data on both sides of this comparison. Thus even the complete order type plus the fact that every gap is large cannot identify an endpoint crossing type that blocks R22. The example has r=9 throughout. It gives the requested control, not a counterexample at r≤8.

### Two-scale test when the macro chord grazes remote corners

There is a uniform local test at a *remote reflex graze* once a candidate chord is specified. Fix a positive marked chart. Suppose the macro direct segment is nonaxial and its open part is interior except for finitely many isolated corner contacts. At each such contact the two open half-legs occupy opposite quadrants. A convex corner has only one occupied quadrant and cannot occur under this hypothesis. A reflex has three. Of the two ways to pass its vertex, one cuts through the missing quadrant and the other passes through the third occupied quadrant. Equality hits the forbidden vertex. These are all the micro cases under the stated transverse, no-wall-following hypothesis.

For an affine scaling family write the displacement from source to target as tv+w and from source to the corresponding corner as tc+e. Assume the offsets are fixed and the chart is preserved for large t. At a macro graze det(v,c)=0, so the exact side test is the sign of

    det(tv+w,tc+e) = t [det(v,e)+det(w,c)] + det(w,e).

Choose the sign orientation separately at each reflex by the location of its missing quadrant. The chord clears all the grazes for every sufficiently large t iff the first nonzero coefficient, in descending order, has the allowed sign at **each** corner. Two zero coefficients give a persistent corner hit. To prove sufficiency, take disjoint small squares about the finitely many grazes in macro coordinates. Outside the squares the segment has positive clearance from every wall, which persists after division by t. Inside each square the directions converge to the nonaxial macro direction and the corner stays longitudinally between the endpoints. The determinant selects precisely which quadrant joins the two half-legs. Necessity follows in the same square at the first forbidden sign. The same test works on a fixed developed itinerary for grazes away from the prescribed reflections, provided all prescribed hits stay strictly inside their walls and transverse.

For the R22 comparison the developed displacement is (2t,2t+2) and the remote corner is (t,t) relative to S. The determinant is −2t, the allowed sign; in the exact dilation it vanishes identically. This calculation explains why the bounded offset repairs the direct chord. The endpoint pin type is unchanged. For several grazes the *same* endpoint displacement enters all the determinants, so showing that each corner separately has a legal side does not give a consistent chord. This lemma tests a supplied macro chord and offsets; it cannot create a macro itinerary for a specified dark pair.

**First open case.** In a positive eight-reflex chart with a specified nondirect pinned pair, one needs an itinerary whose developed target image lies strictly inside all upstream gates. For axis-primitive representatives one could instead try to force a small gap from those *global* gate constraints and then prove transfer at that gap. There is no remaining clearance condition at the pin itself. This note proves neither an in-scope dark family nor a bound on the primitive gaps; it uses no staircase-strip search.

### D225 extension. Exact sign patterns for the two end bounces

D225 uses “pin” for a corner contact of a specified limiting chord. Such contacts can occur far from either pinned mark. Its five types describe continuations of a chosen winding. In its positive transverse coordinate, P0 accepts either strict sign, P+ accepts minus, P− accepts plus, and R accepts the sign entering the opening (plus in its diagram). S is an endpoint clearance test. In particular the same spatial corner can have different P types for different windings. The following calculation is independent of the pending review of the staircase closure. [D225, Step 4](../../illumination-generalization-2026-11-06/fable/directions-2026-11-09/d225-attack-pinned-staircase/note.md)

**Sign-pattern theorem.** Fix the developed transverse endpoint images and a longitudinally monotone passage between the end tiers. Orient the developed transverse coordinate so its total increment K is positive. Measure each gate sign in this common coordinate, reversing a folded physical sign when its unfolding reverses orientation. Write D for the longitudinal increment, and α for a gate's fraction of D. For e,f in {0,1}, the two end reflections replace the longitudinal endpoints 0,D by −a,D+b, where a=2e d₀ and b=2f d₃. Linear interpolation gives the *exact* change in transverse crossing coordinate

    K [(Dα+a)/(D+a+b) − α] = K [a(1−α)−bα]/(D+a+b).

Thus at exact grazes the attainable patterns are precisely the zero vector, all plus, all minus, and plus below θ=d₀/(d₀+d₃) followed by minus above θ (zero at θ). This list concerns crossing displacements. To certify a legal path, a row of the list must pass every gate, its continuation or residue test, and the end clearances. Even an ordered (+,−) demand needs the actual θ strictly between those pins. An ordered (−,+) demand is impossible at *every* scale. This is stronger than a large-room asymptotic and applies even if the end distances can be chosen arbitrarily. Fixed nonzero initial gate offsets must be added to the displayed displacement before testing.

**A six-reflex realization of the forbidden order.** Let m≥1, t=10m+1. The open transverse intervals on successive horizontal tiers are as follows. Take the interior of the union of the closed tier rectangles so the overlaps are genuine passages.

| Longitudinal range | Transverse interval |
|---|---|
| (−m,0) | (−2t−1,t) |
| (0,t) | (−t,t) |
| (t,3t) | (0,4t) |
| (3t,4t) | (3t,6t) |
| (4t,4t+m) | (3t,7t) |

Every adjacent overlap has positive width. The two sides are staircase graphs, and each endpoint change at an interface contributes one reflex, giving 1+2+2+1=6. Both S=(0,0) and T=(4t,4t) lie strictly inside overlaps. Their abscissas are the two wall supports of the middle tier. The marks add no levels. The minimum refined gap is m, and the axis gap gcds are both one (the horizontal gaps include t and t+1, the vertical ones m and t). Thus neither a size threshold nor primitive normalization removes this example.

The unbounced chord has transverse coordinate equal to its longitudinal coordinate. At height t it needs to enter (0,t) strictly to the **left** of t. At 3t it needs to enter (3t,4t) strictly to the **right** of 3t. These are exact grazes at fractions 1/4 and 3/4. With both bounces the threshold is 1/2 and both signs are wrong. Each single bounce fails one gate. The floor and roof are available locally: the positive transverse slope after either or both bounces is at most one, so the displacement up to return to the endpoint height is at most 2m<t, inside the respective end passage. The obstruction occurs at the two separate, single-component gates. This refutes the proposed universal sign repair for a fixed developed chord even below the eight-reflex budget. Lighting this pair by a different winding or an interior reflection is a separate question.

**Multiple gates and the extent of the classification.** For a fixed developed monotone passage, list the open intervals of each slab in its developed copy. Connect consecutive slab intervals by an edge precisely where they have open overlap, and label the edge by that overlap. For a proposed line, keep an edge only when its interface abscissa lies strictly inside the label. A chain from the source interval to the target interval is necessary and sufficient for this straight traversal: between successive interfaces both crossings belong to the same interval, so convexity keeps the intervening segment inside its slab. For marks on interfaces start and finish in the incident interval containing the interior mark on the side of travel. Prescribed regular reflections can be treated by developing their copies and testing the wall hits separately. In this model the four sign choices above can be tested with the full edge labels, even when the interface has several gates. The exact residual *for this passage and move* consists of those for which none of the four choices has a valid chain and regular end legs. Testing each gate separately would lose the shared slab-component condition.

This gives a named additional type, **multiple gate components at one interface**. Even one reflex graze with no allowed continuation for the prescribed winding has the empty allowed-sign set. A wall-following limit or an axial reversal within the passage also falls outside the isolated transverse-graze hypothesis. The five labels and the sign-pattern theorem exhaust neither such itineraries nor the choices of winding of an entire room. For transverse monotone passages the interval-chain test handles the components exactly without a bound on r.

**R22 check.** In the coordinates above, the interface at y=1 has two openings (0,1) and (3,4) into the same upper slab (0,4). Immediately below it the source leg occupies (−1,1), while the other lower component is (3,5). The direct ray has x=1 there and hits the endpoint of the only opening incident to its component. Thus R22 has the multiple-component type and a strict gate failure on the direct itinerary. Its reviewed global darkness uses the fold for all reflected itineraries. The local calculation supplies no basis for attributing that darkness to an ordered pair of opposite sign demands.

**First open implication for L1.** Already at six reflexes the two end bounces cannot repair the ordered (−,+) grazes of a supplied winding, with both marks pinned and every gap arbitrarily large. To deduce a bounded gap from darkness one still needs to produce another legal developed itinerary in this situation, or rule out darkness by a global argument. The example is an obstruction to the move, not a pinned dark family. No explicit B(r) follows.
