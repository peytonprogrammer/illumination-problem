Advances: L1 — the proposed uniform lighting tail for a room with a long separating neck.

The question is whether at most eight reflex corners force a return region that a prescribed pair can use.

**Stopped at the corner-budget step.** No absolute C follows. There are two separate obstructions to this proof.

**1. The end with fewer corners has the same guaranteed budget at eight and nine.** Restrict first to a separating rectangular neck strictly inset at both ends. Its four reflex shoulders are distinct. Let p and q count the other reflex vertices on its two sides. Then p+q=r−4. For r=8 the sum is four, and for r=9 it is five. Both bounds give min(p,q)≤2. In particular a method using only the fact that one end has at most two further reflex vertices has no threshold at eight. The sharp information lost at nine concerns the *other* end: if both ends need at least two further vertices, (2,2) exhausts the budget at eight, while (2,3) is possible arithmetically at nine. No implication from failure of pair-level return to the need for that fifth further vertex is proved. A neck with only two reflex shoulders gives the same issue with residual sums six and seven.

In fact even six reflex corners allow arbitrarily long strictly inset unit necks with no d201 comparison pocket anywhere. Take the end polygon with successive vertices (0,0),(2,0),(2,3),(1,3),(1,2),(0,2). Open its wall x=2 at 1<y<2, attach a neck of any integral length L≥3, and put a reflected copy at the far end. Each end has one step and the neck has four shoulders. The point (1,1) and its reflected copy are interior marks. Every boundary wall except the longitudinal neck walls has length at most two. A full-mouth rectangular pocket of width at least three and depth at least one needs a roof of length at least three and a solid perpendicular flank extending inward at each roof endpoint. A proper subsegment of a straight wall cannot supply both flanks. Each long neck wall has two reflex endpoints, with no such flanks. This rules out the comparison pocket in either orientation for every L. It gives no dark-pair conclusion.

**2. A local return is weaker than a return usable by a pair.** A shallow strip under a topmost wall supplies a local rectangular return at either wall count. For the reviewed band itinerary a ray of speed ratio c returns with phase a+2hc modulo 2w. Writing U,V for the two mark images, g for band height and M=U+V, the legal entry must lie in the open interval (max(0,M−w,U), min(w,M)) and in a progression of spacing wg/(g+h). Both exterior legs must be clear. Counting reflex vertices gives neither leg nor a point of that progression in the interval. In R22 the corner-only odd–odd fiber excludes the wide, full-mouth pocket, as d201 proves. The six-reflex construction shows that absence of this pocket cannot distinguish R22. A narrower or nonrectangular return region would need its own pair-access and phase-matching theorem.

**Quantifier check.** For the stated class of all lattice rooms, without a primitive-scale restriction, uniform dilation by a positive integer m preserves wall count, incidence and specular paths, and sends interior lattice marks to interior lattice marks. A connecting path for mS,mT scales back to one for S,T, including corner avoidance. Any positive-length neck can therefore be made longer than a proposed C while keeping the same lighting question for the chosen pair. Consequently the proposed absolute-C theorem is equivalent, on this class, to illumination of every lattice pair in every neck-bearing room with W≤20, even at neck length one. This scaling observation does not apply within a class required to stay primitively normalized. D152 locates its uniform-tail counterexample at W=22. It supplies no W≤20 dark pair.

**First open implication.** For a prescribed pair across a separating neck, obtain compatible regular mouth phases at one slope from the two ends, or supply a more general connecting itinerary, using a fact valid at eight reflex corners that can fail at nine. This already remains necessary in the absence of a wide full-mouth pocket. There is no proof here of that eight-versus-nine step. The early-stop clause applies. Independently, a bound on every neck length alone cannot bound room area: rectangles have no reflex shoulders and can have arbitrarily large area.
