# A uniform unit tail would hold from the first length

**Verdict.** For real interior marks on the dilation-closed class of integral rooms, a cutoff uniform over the other lengths would imply invariance for *every* positive corridor length, including length one. This is stronger than d110's slice-wise tail. A strictly simple normalized 20-wall room can realize two separating corridors whose reviewed gluing thresholds depend on each other in both directions. That gives a counterexample to the proposed induction using those thresholds. It gives no dark pair and no disproof of uniformity for the actual darkness predicate. I have no unconditional K(20).

## Scaling removes a purported uniform cutoff

For telescoping assume admissibility throughout the real interval [L,L+1], so every dilated intermediate room is strictly simple. For an orthogonal supporting-line gap this follows by an increasing piecewise linear change of one coordinate.

Let P(B,L) have a fixed ordered wall pattern, sealed chamber data B and a separating corridor of length L. Assume every positive integral intermediate length under consideration is admissible and the class is closed under positive integral dilations. Write D for existence of two distinct dark real interior points. Suppose D(B,t)=D(B,t+1) for every t≥K, with the same K for all B of this type. Fix **any** positive L. Choose an integer m with mL≥K. Then

    D(B,L) = D(mB,mL) = D(mB,mL+m) = D(B,L+1).

The outside equalities use similarity: dilating a room preserves every reflected path and corner contact in both directions. The middle equality is m applications of the uniform tail, keeping *all other enlarged dimensions* fixed, including door width. The same argument works for a uniform one-way shortening implication and for a predicate restricted to real cross-chamber marks where scaling preserves that restriction. Thus uniformity for this class is equivalent to a tail starting at **1**. This is a conditional value, not a proved cutoff. Any two lengths in a connected admissible slice with different room-darkness verdicts refute *every* uniform cutoff by dilation and telescoping. A change in the verdict for just one direction does not suffice.

If the same uniform statement applied to **every independent supporting-line gap**, all positive gap vectors in the same cell of shape space would have identical D: change the gaps one at a time. In the orthogonal case the representative with all gaps one has bounding box at most (r+1)×(r+1), since at most r+2 vertical and r+2 horizontal edges occur. At W=20 this bounds its area by 81. A theorem only for separating corridors does not give that conclusion: capped arms and chamber gaps need separate arguments. Mixed diagonal walls also impose constraints between the gaps.

**Domain caveats.** A nontrivial integral dilation leaves the wall-gcd-one class. If the hypothesis is imposed *only* on normalized rooms, the telescoping argument above is unavailable at its first endpoint. And with the existential **lattice-mark** predicate, scaling up may introduce dark marks at previously nonintegral positions. One cannot identify D_lat(P) and D_lat(mP) by similarity alone. If a proposed uniform unit tail holds for D_lat over all integral rooms, the telescoping argument still makes the *rational-mark* predicate length-invariant: choose m to clear the denominators of a rational dark witness at one endpoint and to reach K, transfer lattice darkness along the enlarged slice, and scale the resulting witness down to rational marks at the other endpoint. Reverse the roles for the opposite implication. None of these arguments proves preservation of same-parity lattice darkness under shortening in normalized rooms.

## Two interacting separating corridors at exactly 20 walls

Place three 3×3 square chambers on the same baseline, with their consecutive vertical faces separated by positive integral distances X,Y. Join the chambers by rectangles of those lengths over 1≤y≤2. The intended room is the interior of the union of the five closed rectangles. The two ends of each of the four open mouths give eight distinct reflexes. The outer boundary is a single simple orthogonal polygon, with W=2·8+4=20 and area 27+X+Y. Take X=Y=3k+1. Some horizontal edges have length 3 and others length 3k+1; vertical edges include 1. Thus the rooms are normalized. For k≥6 the area is >64, and d69's Q/proper-factor area bounds give BBBB. This is a symbolic geometry construction, not an enumeration. [Area criteria](../../illumination-generalization-2026-11-06/fable/directions-2026-11-09/d69-bbbb/README.md)

Cut out corridor X. The larger sealed sub-room has area 18+Y and horizontal extent 6+Y. Cutting out Y instead gives area 18+X and extent 6+X. D124 **already** handles arbitrary sealed lattice sub-rooms for opposite interior marks. At width one its sufficient all-real bound on X is

    X ≥ 36 [1 + 2 F_(12(18+Y))]²,

and the bound on Y interchanges X and Y. At X=Y neither can initiate induction, however large the common length. Even the hypothetical extension of d112's rectangular L>4d with d taken as the maximum depth of the entire sub-room would demand X>4(6+Y) or Y>4(6+X). Both fail. Inductively knowing the sealed sub-room is lit gives no prescribed first-hit position and direction at its mouth. Also a cross-pair lemma alone cannot control a pair in the *same* sub-room whose sealed path reflects at the removed mouth. These are two independent gaps in the proposed substitution. [Reviewed arbitrary-chamber theorem](effective-chamber.md) · [Rectangular result](resonant-gluing.md)

This realizes mutually dependent **sufficient thresholds** in a strictly simple room. It does not realize the truth table D(X,Y)=[X=Y]. Indeed points on the horizontal line y=3/2 have a clear shot through both corridors at every X,Y. Whether *some other* pair is dark in this layout is not decided by this example. If all W≤20 rooms are lit, D is identically false and K=1 works trivially.
