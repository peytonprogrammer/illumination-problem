Advances: L1 — Closing the right cap gives an eight-wall exterior. First-hit states agree before the first mouth reflection. Ordinary illumination in that exterior gives no prescribed first-hit phase.

Use the two preceding shrink notes' chart, marks and cap notation. Both marks are strictly left of the mouth M at x=x0, y0<y<y1. Write h=y1−y0, w=n+3 and a=x0−x2=n+2. After clipping at x0, the two top slabs have the same horizontal interval (x2,x0) and merge. The three remaining slabs, bottom to top, have intervals (x3,x4), (x3,x0), (x2,x0). Their boundary has eight walls (two reflex corners). M is part of the right wall of the closed exterior E.

**Exact correspondence.** For r=p/q>0 reduced, start in any of the four signed directions with absolute slope r. Stop at the first contact with the designated mouth segment, before reflecting there. Use regular transverse contacts in its relative interior; stop separately on corner or mouth-endpoint events. The open and closed trajectories coincide until that stop: between true wall impacts they are the same straight line, with the same reflection law at each preceding impact. Conversely each such stopped closed trajectory gives the required open prefix. To test a phase one must specify the incoming vertical sign as well as the folded height. A later visit after reflecting at M does not qualify. Time reversal gives the corresponding assertion for the last exterior leg to T.

At fixed r there are at most four first phases from S and four reverse first phases from T, since the initial directions determine the trajectories. For integral marks, each phase lies in (1/q)Z modulo 2h: in an unfolding all mouth images have integral abscissa, so the ordinate at the crossing lies in (1/q)Z; folding and translating by y0 preserve that grid. This describes the sets as at most four individual residues modulo 2hq after multiplication by q. It gives no closure under addition of 2sp. In particular a nonempty first-hit set of size at most four cannot be closed under that shift if its order hq/gcd(hq,sp) exceeds four. The eight-wall illumination theorem quantifies over directions and interior point pairs. It does not specify a direction, a first strike on a designated boundary segment, or its velocity. L2 also has an area cutoff; the closed exteriors in this family have unbounded area.

**Both one-visit implications can fail at the same slope.** The following symbolic subfamily separates the intersection statement in the question from full darkness. Take arbitrary n ≥ 3 and arbitrary h ≥ 8 divisible by four. The lower vertical gaps may be any positive integers subject to normalization. Set S=(x2+1,y0+2), T=(x2+1,y0+3) and q=2w+2a−4; use r=1/q. Both marks are strictly inside the upper exterior strip. This choice is compatible with primitive rank-one vertical wall data by fixing the first vertical gap at one.

From either mark, for either vertical sign, a rightward start reaches M after horizontal travel a−1. A leftward start reflects at x2 and reaches M after a+1. These are all four first histories. Their vertical changes have magnitude at most (a+1)/q<1. The same statement applies in reverse from T. Relative to the mouth bottom, the two possible S-entry heights for each sign ε are 2+ε(a±1)/q. Through a cap of any of the three widths d, travel 2d changes the unfolded height by ε2d/q. The whole excursion stays strictly between floor and roof: (a+1+2w)/q<2 since 2w+3a>9. Thus the cap does not reverse the vertical velocity.

A one-visit connection therefore requires upward motion and total horizontal travel q, since the vertical rise is exactly one. By cutting at the first and last regular mouth hits, this is also sufficient. The three available travel totals are

    2d + 2a − 2,    2d + 2a,    2d + 2a + 2.

For d=w the smallest is q+2, so the one-visit intersection is empty. For d=w−1 the smallest is q; for d=w−2 the middle total is q. Both shortened intersections are nonempty. The prefix, excursion and reversed suffix meet away from corners by the strip bounds above. This disproves the fixed-slope one-visit implication for the two cap shrinks, even in the specified parity class. It is a family of lit pairs (the marks can see each other vertically), so it does not disprove the conditional assertion starting from a dark pair. Its use is to isolate exactly what an induction using only that intersection would lose.

**Two or more visits.** Let C_d be the partial translation θ ↦ θ+2dr, defined on legal regular cap excursions. From an outgoing phase let E_r follow the ray in the common exterior to its next mouth entry. Equivalently, start in the closed exterior immediately after reflection from M and stop just before the next reflection on M. E_r is partial because of singular or missing returns. The phase keeps the vertical sign and the side of the section fixes the horizontal sign, so this is a deterministic map independent of d. The exact exit set after k ≥ 1 regular visits is

    X_1(d)=C_d(A_S),    X_{k+1}(d)=C_d(E_r(X_k(d))).

Images omit undefined states. A path ending at T with that many visits exists precisely when X_k(d) meets B_T: split or concatenate at the regular crossings. If a path reaches T earlier it can also be stopped earlier. Handle tangencies or mouth-endpoint events separately; this formula concerns paths with regular visits only. The lattice phase grid is preserved: at integral abscissae all subsequent unfolded ordinates still lie in (1/q)Z. Thus at most 2hq distinct exit residues occur for one slope, and repeating an exit repeats its deterministic continuation. This proves the iteration formula without a search. It gives no comparison between widths: C_d is applied before every exterior return, and E_r need not identify its different inputs.

**First open case.** For an actually dark pair one knows avoidance of B_T by every legal path in the full room, simultaneously across slopes and visit counts. The subfamily above shows why retaining just one slope and one visit loses too much to choose either cap shrink. Transferring the simultaneous avoidance, or choosing another gap that preserves darkness, is still open even with an eight-wall exterior. No general corner-budget conclusion or inherited dark pair follows.
