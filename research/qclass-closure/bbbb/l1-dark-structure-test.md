Advances: L1 — Testing whether darkness forces a proper block of sheets in the d152 three-chamber family.

Fix X=Y=3k+1, k≥6, and assume two distinct interior lattice marks S,T are dark. The room has 20 walls, eight reflex corners and a regular point in every parity fiber. Choose a regular basepoint b of the pillowcase B away from the four branch sites. Let Ω be the sheets of Q above b and G the transitive monodromy group on Ω. The transitivity follows from connectedness of the punctured double. The marks themselves project to pillowcase sites, so identifying them directly with two elements of Ω would already require a choice of approach germs and transport.

**Family-specific constraint.** D152 gives physical area 27+X+Y for the three-chamber construction (d152 review, §3). The flat double has twice this area and the standard pillowcase of periods 2ℤ² has area 2. Thus the sheet count is N=29+6k, coprime to 6. Any block size d divides N since a transitive group permutes equal-sized blocks. In particular R22's size-three blocks are impossible throughout this test family.

There is a stronger restriction. Any nontrivial block system gives a proper intermediate cover by the covering-space correspondence over the punctured base and completion at the sites. D154 §2 shows that a normalized proper factor has a downstairs zero and that all d lifts of that zero are distinct reflex corners. Hence d≤8 here. Combining this with d | N leaves only d=5 or 7. The first requires k≡1 mod 5; the second requires k≡1 mod 7. These are necessary conditions, not constructions. For instance **throughout k=35j, j≥1, the monodromy is primitive**: no proper nontrivial block size is possible. This uses no darkness hypothesis. On that entire subfamily, deriving a partition from an assumed dark pair would already prove illumination for every interior lattice pair by contradiction. On the two remaining congruence classes any admissible d permits at most one downstairs 3π point, by the same disjoint-preimage count.

**Attempt to get a block from the marks.** Fix approach germs and transport to a common regular fiber when comparing sheets. Darkness excludes every straight, corner-avoiding connection between the marked lifts. To pass to monodromy one would need to show that a relation extracted from these exclusions survives simultaneous continuation by each branch loop and separates Ω into proper equivalence classes. Prepending or appending a branch loop gives a topological path, but generally introduces bends. Darkness does not exclude that path, and straightening it while avoiding the forbidden corners is precisely an extra geometric assertion.

Even granting an invariant graph of forbidden sheet pairs would not suffice. Its connected components are blocks, but they might form a single component. Abstractly, translation on a prime number p≥5 of labels preserves the cycle graph joining labels at difference ±1; the graph distinguishes pairs while the action is primitive. This is a group-theoretic illustration of the missing inference, not a proposed billiard realization. A sufficient geometric property would be a monodromy-invariant relation whose generated components are proper and contain at least three sheets. Neither darkness as stated nor the three-chamber hypotheses establish it.

**Verdict and stopping point.** No identified property of the dark pair rules out primitive monodromy. The first open case is already the primitive subfamily k=35j: one must prove that every two distinct interior lattice marks there admit a legal straight connection in the unfolding, or derive a geometric consequence of darkness incompatible with primitivity. The first unsupported step is promoting the missing straight connections to a proper monodromy equivalence relation. Assuming a proper factor here would trigger the early-stop condition and, on this subfamily, contradict the sheet-degree restriction. No partition follows and the conditional three-zero follow-on is not triggered.
