# Adding a terrace while keeping a two-reflection illumination proof

## A criterion in the plane

Orient a strictly simple orthogonal room P so it is contained in [0,A] × [0,H]. Assume P is closed under moving southwest: with (x,y) it contains [0,x] × [0,y]. Its bottom and left sides are then full physical walls. Assume further that it contains the closed triangle with vertices (0,0),(A,0),(0,H). The upper staircase may have arbitrarily many corners. **Every pair of distinct real interior marks is connected by a direct shot or by a shot with exactly two reflections, on the left and bottom walls.**

Write S=(a,b), T=(c,d), all coordinates positive. If ad=bc the chord is radial and direct, by southwest closure. Otherwise develop the line from (a,b) to (−c,−d) and fold by absolute value in each coordinate. The two zero crossings are distinct. The physical contact on the vertical axis is (0,|ad−bc|/(a+c)), and the contact on the horizontal axis is (|ad−bc|/(b+d),0). The ordinates and abscissas of those contacts are strictly less than H and A, respectively: each is strictly bounded by max(b,d) or max(a,c). Both hits are strictly positive and therefore on smooth physical walls.

Before the first contact both coordinates decrease from S. Backward from T they also decrease to the last contact. These legs stay inside by southwest closure and interiority of the starting mark (a small northeast perturbation of the mark is still inside). Between contacts the chord joins two strictly interior axis intercepts. Its open part satisfies x/A+y/H<1 and x,y>0, so it stays in the interior of the triangle. The folds are ordinary transversal reflections. In particular a step corner on the triangle's hypotenuse causes no intermediate collision.

The triangle test is sharp for this particular choice of developed images, if paired with the option of a direct shot. In a closed southwest staircase missing some of the triangle, a relatively open portion of its hypotenuse lies outside P; otherwise southwest closure would give the triangle. Take interior marks tending to (0,H) and (A,0). Their direct chords tend to the hypotenuse; so do the middle legs of the two-fold shots by the intercept formulas. Both eventually meet the open exterior. Other wall words may still light the pair, as they do for all six-wall L rooms.

## One new terrace

Start with a staircase satisfying the criterion. Add an entire rectangular bottom layer [0,A′] × [−h,0], with A′>A, and measure y from the new floor. The old bottom wall is partly removed. The proof uses the **new** floor. Its new triangle at the joining height h has width A′H/(H+h). Thus the check for inheriting the triangle from the old triangle is

    (A′−A)H ≤ Ah.

Above the join the two triangle widths decrease linearly to zero at the same height, so the check at the join suffices. Below it the new rectangle supplies the triangle. Southwest closure persists. This adds one reflex and two maximal walls. The criterion is sufficient for inheriting the old triangle; a particular staircase may have additional clearance. A second way to add a step is to enlarge a patch of the upper staircase while keeping southwest closure and both intercepts fixed. That preserves triangle containment by inclusion.

For example, for any integer m≥2 take m horizontal layers of widths m,m−1,…,1 from bottom to top, and unit heights. The construction from m−1 to m adds width and height one and meets the joining inequality with equality. One can give the bottom layer any integer height h≥1 at the last step. At m=9 this gives **W=20, r=8, area 9h+36**, and edge-length gcd one on each axis (both retain unit steps). For every h≥4 the area exceeds 64, so these normalized rooms are BBBB by the project's sufficient area test. Every real interior pair has the same two-reflection bound throughout this unbounded family. No classification in the smaller area range is needed. ## Tests required by d161

* **Three-step staircase.** D161's six cells are precisely the m=3 room; they contain x+y≤3 in the positive quadrant. For (1/4,11/4) and (11/4,1/4), the direct chord hits (1,2). The prescribed ray instead reflects at (0,5/2) and (5/2,0). The result for arbitrary positive dimensions of the eight-wall staircase already follows from the eight-wall manuscript. The terrace criterion gives a reflection bound and an induction beyond eight walls.
* **S-tetromino.** Its lower row is [0,2] and its upper row is [1,3]. No corner has both perpendicular full supporting walls; the triangle induction fails at its first hypothesis even though every axial section is connected. It does admit an attachment from an L: start with the two rows [0,2] and [1,2] and extend the upper row on the right to [1,3]. More generally extend one terminal row of such a two-row L by any length while keeping the open overlap positive. The result is still the union of two rectangles sharing their full common side interval. D157 proves illumination for lattice marks, and the eight-wall construction covers real dimensions and real interior marks, using the slab along the direction of the row. This certifies this step but gives no induction for arbitrary further rows with shifting left endpoints. As a check, on the S the chord (1/2,1/2) to (3/2,3/2) hits the reflex at (1,1); reflecting at (3/4,0) clears the mouth at (5/4,1) and reaches the target. [Two-rectangle construction](doorway-word-bound.md) · [Eight-wall proof](../../illumination-publication/repository/preprints/eight-wall/manuscript.tex)
* **R22 and BBBB scope.** Southwest closure forces connected sections along both axes. R22 fails that test. The S fails the anchoring test earlier than disconnection; a southwest L with a very deep notch can retain the anchor but lose the triangle. DB55, a lit 12-wall BBBB room, has a disconnected horizontal section and is outside the terrace class. Thus the theorem proves a W=20 BBBB family, not the universal wall bound. [DB55 and R22 controls](parallel-mouth-combs.md)

The remaining geometric question for this construction is whether alternating anchors can handle a staircase with genuine offsets on both sides. A useful target is a three-row offset extension of the S, where the two-rectangle theorem ceases to apply and no single southwest triangle need exist.
