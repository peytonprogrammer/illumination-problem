Advances: L1 replacement — per-pattern decidability. The reflection bound and a uniform Presburger encoding are distinct claims.

## A zero-reflection obstruction to the proposed logical reduction

In the one-neck word LLLLRRLLLLRR let m≥2 and u≥1 be integers. Take the left chamber [−u−1,0] × [−m−1,3], the neck [0,m] × [0,2], and the right chamber [m,m+2] × [−1,3]. Take the interior of their closed union. All four shoulders are strictly inset. Put S=(−u,−m) and T=(m+1,1). Every edge length and mark coordinate is affine in (m,u).

The open segment ST is legal exactly when u>m(m+1). Its ordinate at the first mouth is (u−m(m+1))/(u+m+1). The line rises towards T, so if this ordinate is positive both mouths are crossed strictly between 0 and 1 and chamber convexity handles the rest. Equality hits the reflex corner (0,0), and a negative ordinate misses the first aperture. This is a symbolic family with the itinerary fixed to zero reflections.

Consequently even the uniform predicate “at most k reflections suffice” on this word cannot be Presburger-definable with free dimensions, marks and k. Specializing k=0 and the affine data above would define u>m(m+1). Taking the least admissible u for each m would define the graph of m²+m+1. A single-valued unary Presburger function is eventually affine on each class of a fixed finite modulus, which rules this out. Equivalently the range of those minima has unbounded successive gaps and is infinite, whereas every unary Presburger set is ultimately periodic.

Thus a linear witness bound, **even if true**, does not by itself supply the claimed Presburger sentence by encoding bounded paths. This leaves open a different encoding of unrestricted illumination, for instance one that eliminates the zero-reflection distinction entirely. The counterfamily only separates zero from positive reflection counts. It supplies no superlinear shortest-path lower bound. D159's gate inequalities already have this variable-times-variable problem for the unique chord. D203's groups have variable moduli 2BⱼD and their phase advances involve dⱼn. They give exact tests after specialization of the dimensions. D159's caveat about artificial gate endpoints also matters when testing all paths: an endpoint of a cut can be a legal smooth wall point.

## A finite witness bound for the one-neck word, and for arbitrary words

The following weaker growth bound applies to all strictly simple orthogonal lattice rooms. Its proof explains precisely what a finite-state argument can buy without assuming a slope denominator. Let A be the integer area and N=4A. For distinct interior lattice marks, every lit pair has a legal path with at most

    2N · 2^F + 2 reflections,       F=(N!)² 8^(4N).

Here no automaton or decision procedure is run. The magnitude of F is incidental.

**Unfold and mark.** Glue the four reflected copies of the room along corresponding sides to obtain the translation surface for the billiard. It is tiled by N unit squares and maps by coordinates modulo the integer lattice to the square torus. Every physical wall corner, including each convex corner at which the unfolded metric is smooth, is forbidden. Color its lifts accordingly, and distinguish all lifts of S and all lifts of T by two additional flags. Every such point is a vertex above the origin of the torus. Straight segments from a source lift to a target lift avoiding the forbidden set in their interior project to legal paths, and billiard paths lift to such segments. A segment starting in the interior cannot first become tangent to a straight boundary side at a smooth hit. Axis directions are allowed. This model does not discard smooth hits at artificial cuts.

**Finite orbit under integral changes of basis.** Apply any determinant-one integral linear transformation simultaneously to the base torus and to the translation charts, carrying the flags along. The resulting surface is again an N-sheeted translation cover away from the origin. Pulling back the standard open unit square and its open edges gives a new square tiling: there are N square interiors, and right and top neighbors are two permutations of N labels. Vertex identifications are forced by these gluings. Three binary flags at each of at most 4N corner slots suffice to recover all the marked subsets. Hence there are at most F isomorphism classes of these marked covers over the coordinate torus. Integral basis changes act invertibly on those classes. This count concerns the geometric surfaces and their marks. The physical wall decomposition need not be preserved during a basis change because it is restored when the straight segment is pulled back.

**Short representative of a direction class.** A lit segment in the original unfolding has displacement k v for a primitive integer vector v and a positive integer k. A matrix in SL(2,ℤ) sends v to (1,0) by Bézout. Its transformed marked cover therefore admits a positive horizontal connection. Use the two elementary unit shears and their inverses, which generate SL(2,ℤ) by the Euclidean algorithm. In the finite orbit of the original marked cover, the same transformed class has a representative G of word length at most F−1: a shortest walk to it cannot repeat a vertex. A marked-cover isomorphism over the base preserves horizontal directions and the forbidden and endpoint sets, so the cover obtained with G also has a horizontal connection. This replacement is allowed to change the original slope.

**Short horizontal segment and pullback.** Every eastward horizontal unit step between vertices follows a positively oriented square edge. There are N such edge germs, counting the bottom edge of each square once. At a regular unblocked vertex horizontal continuation is unique. All singular vertices are blocked. A shortest horizontal connection between the two marked sets therefore uses at most N steps: if it repeats an oriented edge, the intervening loop can be deleted with the same continuation. Pulling this connection back gives displacement j G⁻¹(1,0) with j≤N. Each generator or inverse increases the vector 1-norm by at most a factor of two. In the original unfolding the total coordinate variation is at most N·2^(F−1). For a non-axis direction each physical wall reflection crosses an integral horizontal or vertical coordinate line in the developed plane, so their number is bounded by the total variation plus two. For an axis direction only transverse lines can cause a smooth reflection, and the same bound applies. This proves the displayed bound with slack.

This argument extends to all words, independently of the corner budget. It bounds reflections as a function of perimeter too, since area is at most the product of the bounding-box dimensions. Its dependence on perimeter is far too large for the proposed linear estimate. Counting basis-change states loses at F, and even a short word in shears can have exponentially large matrix entries. This gives no negative result about the true shortest path.

## What remains at the first word

For the one-neck word, let |ℓ| be the sum of the twelve lengths (equivalent up to word constants to their maximum). The first open quantitative case is a lit pair in opposite end rectangles, with the chamber widths, heights, aperture offsets and neck dimensions all variable. There is no proof here that a path exists using O(|ℓ|) reflections, and no family here forces more. Pairs strictly inside the same rectangle have a zero-reflection witness by convexity. A fixed-slope example with many visits cannot give the needed lower bound, since a new path may use another slope.

At a unit neck the oriented height at slope p/q changes over one transit of horizontal length L by Lp/q modulo 2. The translation has order 2q/gcd(Lp,2q) for coprime p,q, which has no uniform bound even with L fixed. This is the order of the phase translation, not a claim that a ray actually makes that many visits. A repeated exact directed state permits cycle deletion, but a repeated wall label or coarse phase class does not identify the same mouth height. The missing estimate is a cheap change of slope preserving endpoint access, or a different direct bound on the shortest itinerary. Per-pattern decidability via the proposed Presburger step additionally needs an independent logical reduction. No decisions were run.

### A linear witness on the entire quadratic-threshold family

In fact every pair S,T specified in the first section has a path with at most 2m+4 reflections, uniformly in u. Thus even an explicit linear bound for *all* these marked inputs coexists with the non-Presburger zero-reflection predicate.

Write w=u+1. Take the least d=u+2kw≥m+2 with k a nonnegative integer. This is horizontal travel from S to a rightward hit at the left mouth after k round trips in the left rectangle. Put D=d+m+1, the horizontal travel to T after continuing through the neck. Choose r in (m/d,(m+2)/d) such that −m+rD is odd integral. This is possible since the allowed values of r are spaced by 2/D<2/d. In particular 0<r<1.

Launch up and right with slope r. At travel d its height is strictly between zero and two. All earlier right-wall hits, if any, are below zero: the last is at d−2w, and (m+2)(d−2w)≤md because d≤w(m+2). For k>0 this bound follows from d<m+2+2w and m(w−1)≥2. Earlier left-wall hits lie on the solid outer wall. The whole chamber leg stays strictly between its floor and roof. The neck then folds the height into [0,2]. At travel D the virtual folded height is one because the unfolded ordinate is odd. The final unit of horizontal travel stays strictly inside that band since r<1. It therefore runs legally inside the right chamber without using a virtual neck reflection. Both neck-end crossings are regular. The chamber contributes 2k reflections, at most m+3 since k is minimal and w≥2. In the neck, vertical travel is rm<m and reflections are separated by two vertical units, giving at most m+1 more. The construction covers this symbolic family only. Its key simplification is the target at the neck mid-height and one unit beyond the far mouth, so both odd target phases work with equal spacing and the last leg always clears.
