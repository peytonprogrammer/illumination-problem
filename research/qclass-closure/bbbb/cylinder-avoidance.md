# Endpoint-marked cylinder avoidance gives no degree bound

**Verdict.** With the source and target lifts included among the points removed to form the cylinders, every pair satisfies the proposed avoidance condition. It has no finite degree bound even for W=5, r=0, genus 3 and the same 24 distinguished points in the count at every size. This refutes the implication from avoidance. The counterexample pairs are lit.

**Why every marked endpoint is on the boundary.** Let F contain all corner lifts and all endpoint lifts. A marked cylinder is an open annulus foliated by closed geodesics avoiding F. Its interior is disjoint from F by definition. In a periodic direction a regular leaf through an added regular mark becomes part of the critical graph: if it was a closed leaf inside an old cylinder, cutting there produces two rims. If it was already on the critical graph, it stays there. In particular, applying this cut to a label and then observing that the label is on a rim gives no information about its affine orbit or its visibility. A large-area open cylinder may omit every one of the labels regardless of how large its area is.

**Fixed-genus, fixed-mark counterexample in the named d135 chamfered square.** Use exactly the primitive specialization in [rigidity-area.md](rigidity-area.md), taking q=6j, j≥1. The side is 6q, the removed right isosceles corner has leg 2q+1. Use d135's marks (q,q) and (3q,2q). They are distinct, strictly interior and of the same parity. The segment is legal by convexity. The three right-angle vertices have two regular lifts each. The two 135° vertices each have one lift of order two. The unfolding thus has genus 3, eight distinct corner lifts and sixteen distinct endpoint lifts. In the convention counting all zeros and marks once, n=24. If n counts only regular labels the corresponding fixed number is 22.

The corner-period lattice has covolume c=1 by the axial edge lengths 6q and 4q−1, which are coprime for these q. The degree is

    d = 8 area(P) = 288q² − 4(2q+1)².

It tends to infinity while all the avoidance hypotheses hold in every rational direction. Already q=6 gives d=9692. Multiplication of the same marked polygon by 1+i rotates it by 45° and scales by √2. It permutes the allowed wall directions, preserves visibility and the cylinder incidence, and takes the corner-period lattice to the index-two checkerboard lattice. The transformed wall content is still one, since that lattice contains (1,1). The area and covolume both double, and the degree over the corner-period torus stays d. Thus the counterexample beats both the c=1 target of 160 and the c=2 target of 80, without changing genus or mark count. These calculations use a named family only and require no direction sweep.

**R22 control.** With the endpoints removed to define cylinders, its lifts are on the critical graph in every rational direction for the same definitional reason. R22 is orthogonal and has four lifts per interior endpoint. More substantially, use the manuscript's regular cylinders, formed by removing only the lifts of polygon vertices. In each saved direction (4,7) and (7,4) there are two such cylinders exchanged by the half-turn. If any source lift were in an open cylinder C, some lift of the target would lie in its closure (exchange by the half-turn if needed). The connecting chord in the completed strip has its open part in C and would light the pair. Reversing source and target gives the same conclusion for all target lifts. In these two directions both endpoints really are on the corner critical graph, even before adding them as cuts. This identifies the boundary mechanism there; the argument makes no classification of the corner-only cylinders in the other directions. [R22 manuscript, two-cylinder lemma and certificates](../../illumination-publication/repository/preprints/twenty-two-wall/manuscript.tex)

**Corrected question.** Keep endpoint labels as passengers tracked under affine maps, and cut only at the physical corner lifts. A sufficient lighting certificate is a common completed cylinder with one lift in its interior, or with the two regular lifts on opposite rims. Both statements follow by taking the open chord in a developed strip. Consequently a dark pair excludes both possibilities in every rational direction. At a given integral seed, this corrected predicate can be decided finitely: the subgroup fixing all endpoint and corner labels has finite index in the integral torus group in corner-period lattice coordinates (for rational endpoint images, first stabilize their finite torsion grid); transfer the cylinder/rim incidence from one direction per cusp of this subgroup. Labels never act as cuts in this computation. A bound on the number of cusps alone counts cases to check at a seed; the missing theorem would have to force a successful corner-only incidence at large degree, or otherwise bound the degrees of seeds failing all the incidence tests. Neither such a theorem nor a contrary example to this corrected predicate follows here. No search was run.
