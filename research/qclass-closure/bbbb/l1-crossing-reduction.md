## Stopping-rule result

Fix the first edge east and read LLLRLLLRLR as the turns after successive edges. The edge directions are E,N,W,S,W,S,E,N,E,N. Up to translation, the vertices in order are

    (x0,y0) (x1,y0) (x1,y1) (x2,y1) (x2,y2)
    (x3,y2) (x3,y3) (x4,y3) (x4,y4) (x0,y4).

Take every order and tie of the five line coordinates on each axis compatible with the indicated positive edge lengths and strict simplicity. This parametrizes all lengths: tracing any such boundary recovers the displayed coordinates, and consecutive differences recover its lengths. Insert the two marks in the orders as in d182. Thus each axis has at most four positive wall gaps, irrespective of how marks subdivide them.

For each chart C, let a_i and b_j be its consecutive *wall* gaps. Let M_ij be one if the open grid cell (i,j) is inside and zero otherwise. The area is exactly Σ M_ij a_i b_j by disjoint interiors. Under d187's finite-flat input, use its retained dark products U × V in marked coordinates. On each factor take a positive integral representative minimizing the largest wall gap after summing its marked subgaps, as in d187. Independent choice on the two factors is justified by the product property. If those minima are m(U), m(V), the product contains a dark witness of area at most (Σ M_ij)m(U)m(V) ≤ 16m(U)m(V). This bounds a witness, not the areas of all realizations. Ties only reduce the number of wall gaps. This calculation covers the word symbolically without enumerating its realizations.

**Where the number is lost.** Even granting finite-flat, d187 supplies no effective chart-uniform height H(C) for the factor equations, hence no numerical maximum G(C) of these minima. The W=10 substitution changes 81 to at most 16, but leaves G(C) unevaluated. Even a hypothetical G(C)=2 would give 64 with the coarse box estimate. It does not bound a full defining system, uniformly over its successive seeds. Fixed-template finiteness in d189–d192 also leaves the constants free when the template varies. No census extension is justified.

## A partial crossing reduction

Work in a fixed d182 orthogonal chart, passing to a subsequence to fix bounded gaps and order the growth scales. Form a bipartite graph on the diverging horizontal and vertical coordinate gaps. Join a pair when their product cell is interior. For marked subdivisions use the inherited occupancy of the wall cell. The graph is read directly from C and the growth support. An edge records an occupied rectangle growing in both dimensions, independently of the relative scale order. With no edge the growing strips have disjoint interiors in the room. This is a geometric dichotomy, not yet a transfer theorem.

* With no edge, apply d184 only where a seed and its disjoint-transfer hypotheses hold, including fixed transverse periods where required. Its exceptional affine slices still require treatment. Lack of a seed, failure of the period or itinerary hypotheses, and unresolved exceptional slices belong in the residual.
* With an edge, the reviewed explicit area bound applies when the crossing has the d193–d194 anchored, normalized overlap representation with its aperture and terminal-approach hypotheses, and the marked pair satisfies a covered placement: the lower-wing case with the required roof, or the inside-column extension with the solid opposite side or H ≥ b. A bound for a fixed template or fixed stretches counts as a numerical bound on a ray only if its required constants are uniform there.
* Remaining occupied cases include a growing part of the template or independent offset outside the anchored reduction, multiple occupied crossings without a simultaneous reduction, missing terminal approach (including grounded targets or repeated terminal traversals outside the theorem), and H < b with two open contacts without a proved return. These tests may overlap. They apply whether perpendicular scales are comparable or one dominates. An edge alone gives none of the anchoring, approach, or uniformity hypotheses.

These tests give a conservative residual by retaining every branch that fails a cited hypothesis. I have no proof that every occupied chart admits one of the reviewed normal forms, or that this residual can be reduced to a list of just the exceptional gate placements.

## Follow-on attempt and invariant

There is no specified measure on integer rays or on the union of chart strata. Use dimension of the real gap cone as a proxy. A full-dimensional stress case already occurs in the ten-wall word: order both axes by indices 3,4,2,0,1. The four horizontal slabs from bottom to top have x-intervals (x3,x4), (x3,x0), (x2,x0), (x2,x1). Their overlaps at consecutive heights have positive length, and their boundary is exactly the ten-edge walk above. All eight wall gaps can vary independently. With all gaps growing comparably, every occupied cell is a growing crossing, and one's neighbouring occupied cells can grow at the same scale. There is no fixed-size template supplied by selecting one such cell. This is a full-dimensional family of candidate geometries; nothing proves a dark locus of positive measure in it.

Use dimension of the current rational linear space E of axis gaps as the candidate invariant. If a new seed in E supplies a normal h annihilating the dark factor U but nonzero on E, replacing E by E ∩ ker h keeps U and strictly lowers dimension by one. This is exact by rank-nullity. On d184 exceptional affine slices the analogous invariant is affine dimension when the new exceptional equation is independent. Cutting a gate alone supplies no such equation.

At the rank-one end of this attempt, dimension ceases to control witness size. In the displayed chart the primitive positive wall-gap vector (n,n+1,n+2,n+3) has minimum possible largest gap n+3 on its rational line, although all coordinates are comparable and (1,-2,1,0) is a bounded normal. This symbolic family demonstrates precisely the missing arithmetic estimate. It is not asserted to be a dark factor. D188 gives no uniform next independent normal for this situation. D195's strip inequality is an exact test at one speed and does not supply that normal either.

**First open case and proposed change of route.** Already on a full-dimensional occupied growth cone for W=10, exclude retained dark products whose primitive positive representatives have large area, or show that any darkness there forces a different dark product with a small representative. A sufficient geometric lemma would extract independent bounded-height normals at each proper factor using seeds with uniformly controlled periods and clearance, including the final rank-one factor. A weaker, more directly targeted route is to bound the minimum of the exact bilinear cell-area form on at least one retained product whenever any exists. That target avoids demanding small representatives of every product. Neither estimate is proved. The stopping rule fires here: no numerical N0, no general decreasing crossing step, and no further strip-return iteration.
