# A sharp 22-wall minimum at area 15 with arbitrary real marks

**Updated result, November 19.** The [all-area proof for arbitrary real endpoints](twenty/README.md) establishes the sharp 22-wall minimum throughout the corner-only parity class. The earlier calculation below remains part of its evidence.

**Computer-assisted result.** Fix the unit square grid. Consider strictly simple orthogonal rooms made of exactly 15 grid squares. Assume some coordinate parity class contains only polygon corners among its grid points in the closed room. Every such room with fewer than 22 walls illuminates every pair of distinct interior points, including irrational points. The 15-square room in [the manuscript](../illumination-publication/repository/preprints/twenty-two-wall/manuscript.pdf) satisfies the parity condition and has a dark pair. Thus the minimum in this family is exactly 22.

The [earlier theorem](README.md) allowed all areas in this parity class and required lattice marks. The new calculation removes that requirement at the area of the manuscript example. Arbitrary real side lengths and rooms outside the parity condition remain open.

## How the continuous problem was checked

1. Enumerate the qualifying polyominoes through 20 walls, including rooms with zero or one interior grid point. In the block enumerator remove just the minimum-two-interior-points filter. Independently enumerate connected polyominoes by adding one cell at a time, then filter by area, topology, wall count, and parity. Canonicalizing each room under translation and the eight square symmetries gives identical sets. The wall counts at 12, 14, 16, 18, 20 walls are 13, 327, 3,413, 18,966, 55,006 respectively. Other wall counts below 22 contribute zero.
2. Use the existing [cylinder lemma and algorithm](../illumination-generalization-2026-11-06/fable/directions-2026-11-09/d13-cylinder-cover/theory.md). A source lift in an open cylinder can join any regular target lift in its closure by a straight segment in the unrolled strip. In the room this is a legal billiard path. Each rational direction certifies whole regions of endpoint positions. Source geometry tracks relatively open faces, edges, and points. For each source stratum the calculation encloses still-uncovered targets by closed polygons with exact rational vertices.
3. For the harder rooms, use reciprocity. If a source bucket A has remaining possible targets in B but no surviving source in B has a possible target in A, that bucket pair contains no dark pair. The implementation bounds each polygon by all half-grid buckets intersecting its coordinate bounding box and drops a source record only when all reverse possibilities have disappeared. For an expensive direction it can discard the entire attempted refinement, restoring the previous state. This only forgoes a potential certificate.
4. Six rooms required running the calculation separately on each unordered pair of tiles. Each interior source belongs to one tile by assigning shared open edges and grid vertices consistently. Each target is allowed its entire closed tile. Both orders are included. Restricting to closed target tiles preserves the positive-area test because a point in a tile outside a finite union of closed illuminated sets has an open relative neighborhood of positive area there. All 720 tile pairs cleared. The original 22-wall room run through the same program retained its known pair, (1,3) and (3,5) in the translated input coordinates.
5. Accept a continuum run only when its remaining source strata are isolated points and every mutually uncovered pair consists of lattice points. Every one of the 15,571 rooms whose accepted result retained lattice pairs matches a room by exact cell coordinates in the prior census. That census has already replayed a legal path for every distinct interior lattice pair. The accepted continuum calculations leave no other cases.

## Checks and reproduction

The [audit](offgrid/results/audit.txt) matches the two censuses shape by shape, the exact input and output room sets at every pass, and all 120 tile pairs in each of the final six rooms. Run `python3 offgrid/audit_area15.py` from this directory. The compressed inputs and outcome records are in [offgrid/results](offgrid/results). To recompute the geometric tests, run `bash offgrid/replay_area15.sh /path/to/fresh-directory`. To replay the separate area enumerator, generate its source using `python3 offgrid/brute15_input.py /tmp/brute15.c`, compile it, and run with arguments `15 20 1 0 rooms counts`.

A second tile-pair run with the unmodified cylinder predicates and original direction order, with undefined-behavior checks enabled, completed 708 of 720 tile pairs within its resource limit. The optimized calculation cleared all 720. The two programs share their cylinder geometry. The independent enumeration and the previous independent lattice-path replay check different parts of the argument. A separate end-to-end implementation of the continuum proof is still desirable before publication.

## Other areas and the 18-wall result

The original continuum checker closed the two named 28-square, 20-wall test rooms [cov4181](offgrid/cov4181.log) and [cov4269](offgrid/cov4269.log), with zero residual pairs. At exactly 18 walls, across all areas allowed by the parity condition, the initial calculation cleared 141,885 of 141,887 rooms. The [18-wall audit](offgrid/results/eighteen-audit.txt) records its two exceptions. An [endpoint-dependent shear certificate](two18/README.md) subsequently closed both, completing the all-real result for exactly 18 walls.

A separate [doorway calculation](offgrid/doorway.md) gives restrictions on endpoint depths for real side lengths, using cap retracing and reflected doorway windows. The area-15 result does not depend on that argument.
