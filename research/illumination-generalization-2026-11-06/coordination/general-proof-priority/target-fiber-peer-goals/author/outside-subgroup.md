# A genuine diagonal affine map outside the failed subgroup, and its endpoint limitation

This is a hand proof of map existence, not a full-target-fiber seed theorem. It also proves that the explicit safe diagonal twist constructed here fixes the complete branch fiber, including every endpoint in the accepted odd-d obstruction family. Thus merely obtaining an outside-subgroup map does not repair coverage.

No direction, room, ray or orbit is searched. No new discovery clock or numerical check is used.

## 1. General integral-L diagonal cylinder construction

Let M be any integral L unfolding, and choose an integral square torus through which its translation covering factors. For the accepted odd-d family use the exact primitive torus R²/(4Z²), with unique branch value c=(2,2). Let its side be b (b=4 for that family), and let c denote the branch value.

Cut the base torus along the closed diagonal circle through c, in direction e=(1,1)/sqrt(2). The complement is an open cylinder with core length ell=b sqrt(2) and transverse width w=b/sqrt(2), measured along n=(-1,1)/sqrt(2). It contains no branch value. Each component of its inverse image is an ordinary finite cylinder cover: transverse width w and core length m ell for a positive integer m.

Across a cut preimage without a cone, neighboring strips may merge into a larger maximal diagonal cylinder. Merging preserves the primitive core circumference: an interface identification is a homeomorphism of core circles, so the covering degrees m on its two sides agree. Consequently each maximal diagonal cylinder has length m_j ell and width v_j w, with positive integers m_j,v_j.

There are finitely many strips because the torus cover has finite degree. An all-regular cycle of strips would form a whole torus component, open and closed in M and containing no cone. Connectedness and the actual cone exclude such a component. Therefore the merging chains terminate at saddle connections through the genuine cone, giving finitely many maximal cylinders.

Choose L=lcm_j m_j. On each maximal cylinder take transverse coordinate eta from its lower boundary and longitudinal coordinate xi modulo m_j ell. Define

    P(xi,eta)=(xi+2L eta, eta).

At eta=0 this is identity. At the other boundary eta=v_j w, its longitudinal displacement divided by circumference is

    (2L v_j w)/(m_j ell)=L v_j/m_j,

an integer because ell=2w. Thus it is identity also on that boundary. The cylinder maps paste across every saddle connection. They have a common derivative, extend at the cone, and have inverses obtained by changing the sign of L. Hence P is a genuine affine homeomorphism of the actual surface.

In xy coordinates,

    DP = I + 2L e n^t
       = [[1-L,L],[-L,1+L]].

It is a nontrivial orientation-preserving unipotent, fixing exactly the diagonal line. The accepted genus-two centrality lemma makes P preserve every physical corner lift Sigma, including regular convex corners. Alternatively the boundary gluing construction already preserves the unique true cone; centrality supplies the regular marked corners.

No claim is made that L is the smallest possible shear parameter or that this construction has a bounded reflection description.

## 2. Why this map lies outside the accepted obstruction subgroup

For the odd-d family, put lambda=d(2d+1)>=21. The accepted full coordinate stabilizers are generated by G and the two anchored twists with derivatives

    H=[[1,lambda],[0,1]], V=[[1,0],[lambda,1]].

The diagonal and copy symmetries form a finite signed-permutation group N. It normalizes K=<H,V> at the map level, as already independently accepted.

Use projective coordinate s=x/y, including infinity. The derivative actions are

    H^a(s)=s+a lambda,
    V^a(s)=1/(a lambda+1/s)

for nonzero integers a, with the usual projective conventions. Let X be |s|>1 and Y be |s|<1. Since lambda>2, every nonzero H power sends Y into X, and every nonzero V power sends X into Y.

Starting from s=1, H^a sends it strictly into X and V^a sends it strictly into Y. An alternating reduced nonempty word therefore sends 1 into X or Y; it never has absolute value 1. This is also a proof that no nonempty reduced word is the identity. Signed permutations act by s->plus/minus s or plus/minus 1/s and preserve the condition |s|!=1.

Thus an element nk of the accepted subgroup that fixes s=1 must have empty reduced k. It is a finite symmetry n. No finite-order matrix is a nontrivial unipotent. The map P in section 1 fixes s=1 and has a nontrivial unipotent derivative, so it is OUTSIDE that entire subgroup.

This proves that genuine affine maps outside the failed coordinate subgroup exist on every member of the obstruction family. No finite-index Veech-group theorem or enumeration is needed.

## 3. Exact action of this safe twist on the entire branch fiber

Let z belong to pi inverse(c). If it lies on a maximal-cylinder boundary, the map P fixes it pointwise. Otherwise, within its cylinder its transverse offset from the boundary is r w for an integer r, 0<r<v_j: both the boundary and z project onto the cut diagonal circle through c.

The shear displacement divided by the cylinder circumference is now

    (2L r w)/(m_j ell)=L r/m_j,

again an integer. Hence P fixes EVERY z in pi inverse(c) individually, including the complete four physical lifts of any endpoint with base quotient c.

In the odd-d obstruction family, p and all gq are in pi inverse(c). Therefore the particular P constructed in section 1 fixes all those exact points. For g!=1 they remain in distinct copy interiors. For g=1 their straight segment remains the original reflex-hitting segment. P and every power of P fail the seed gate for this family despite lying outside the old subgroup.

Indeed adjoining this P to the pointwise endpoint-fixing twist subgroup K still gives a subgroup fixing Gp and Gq pointwise. Its conjugates under the original copy/diagonal symmetries fix those fibers too, because those symmetries preserve their union. This extension likewise does not produce a seed. No assertion is made about all conjugates by the FULL affine group.

## 4. What a smaller genuine diagonal twist would have to achieve

For a simultaneous boundary-fixing twist with common shear tau, exact gluing requires

    tau v_j/(2m_j) in Z for every j.

For z at transverse offset r w in cylinder j, it fixes z exactly when

    tau r/(2m_j) in Z.

These are derived from the same actual cylinder phases, not a projected-copy choice. A smaller compatible twist can move a branch-fiber point only if the second integrality condition fails while all first conditions hold. A point on a genuine saddle-connection boundary is fixed by every boundary-fixing twist of this foliation.

The safe choice tau=2L deliberately satisfies the second condition for every branch-fiber point and is therefore incapable of the desired endpoint motion. Existence of a rational-direction parabolic, or abstract existence of maps outside the old subgroup, has NO demonstrated force toward endpoint coverage on its own.

To continue this mechanism, one must prove that some actual cylinder decomposition and compatible twist moves the original ordered fibers into a same-copy regular seed, with their exact labels. We have NOT proved that a minimally compatible diagonal twist moves p or any gq, still less that it gives a seed. We do not choose or compute a cylinder orbit here. The section is an exact prerequisite and a precise limitation, not another coverage formula.

## Scope

Proved: an actual global Sigma-preserving diagonal map exists outside the entire failed coordinate subgroup for the infinite native obstruction family. Also proved: the specified safe lift fixes all relevant endpoints and therefore fails that family's seed criterion.

Not proved: the full affine-group gate for native L rooms, its negation for all four lifts, any dark qualifying pair, or the full r<=8 illumination objective. No ten-wall expansion follows. This result prevents confusing outside-subgroup map existence with the missing ordered-fiber availability.
