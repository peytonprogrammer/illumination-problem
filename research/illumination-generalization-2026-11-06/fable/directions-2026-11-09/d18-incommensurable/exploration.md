# Incommensurable rooms: what published theory gives

Date: 2026-11-10. Direction d18. Theory and literature only; no searches, no
scripts. Scope narrowed by the coordinator: tasks 1, 2, 5 in full, task 3 for
generic lengths, task 4 for the L-shaped room, task 6 in one paragraph.

Status labels: **PROVED** (proved in this file, given the cited results),
**READ** (cited, statement read in the arXiv source), **MEMORY** (cited from
memory, not re-read), **CONJ** (conjectural or only sketched).

## Results

- **Theorem B (generic orthogonal rooms).** If the edge lengths satisfy the
  explicit condition G below (true for Lebesgue-almost every room of each
  shape), every two distinct non-vertex points are lit. Each interior point is
  also lit from itself, and each vertex is lit from each non-vertex point.
  PROVED from READ results plus one MEMORY result (Wright's field of
  definition). The almost-every version needs READ results only.
- **Theorem L (L-shaped rooms, r = 1).** In every incommensurable L-shaped
  room, every two distinct points are lit, vertices included. PROVED from one
  READ theorem (Apisa). Agrees with d15 Theorem 1.
- **Theorem C (every incommensurable orthogonal room).** Dark interior pairs
  lie in E x E for a finite set E, or on fold lines of a smaller room that P
  covers. The second case is empty when a + b >= r + 3 (defined below). PROVED
  from READ results.
- **Not obtained:** "no dark pair in every incommensurable room" for r >= 2.
  It needs orbit closures and periodic points for special lengths. See
  [What remains](#what-remains).

## 1. The unfolding

Let P be a strictly simple orthogonal polygon with r reflex corners and r + 4
convex corners. Let G = {1, h, v, iota} be the Klein four-group generated by
the reflections in the two axes; iota is rotation by pi.

- X(P) is 4 copies gP, g in G, glued along walls. A convex corner lifts to 1
  regular point (4 x pi/2 = 2 pi). A reflex corner lifts to 1 cone point of
  angle 6 pi. So X(P) lies in H(2^r), with r + 4 regular marked points, and
  has genus g = r + 1. PROVED.
- iota acts on X(P) with derivative -1. X(P)/iota is the double DP of P, a
  sphere in Q(1^r, -1^(r+4)). So X(P) is hyperelliptic, iota is the
  hyperelliptic involution, and its 2r + 4 fixed points are exactly the vertex
  lifts. h and v act as anti-holomorphic reflections. PROVED.
- Let L be the locus of holonomy double covers of Q(1^r, -1^(r+4)). L is a
  closed GL(2, R)-invariant locus of complex dimension 2r + 2 = 2g. Every
  zero is fixed by iota, so L has no relative deformations and rank g. For
  r = 1, L = H(2). PROVED (dimension formula 2g - 2 + n: MEMORY).
- **Period coordinates.** Number the edges e_1, ..., e_(2g+2) around P, odd
  ones horizontal. Each edge joins two branch points, so it lifts to a closed
  curve c_j with period +-2 l_j (j odd) or +-2 i l_j (j even), where l_j is
  the edge length. The classes c_1, ..., c_2g form a basis of H_1(X; Z); the
  two relations are the closing conditions. (Chain basis of a hyperelliptic
  curve: MEMORY, standard.) So rooms of one shape form an open subset of
  R^g x (iR)^g in the chart C^2g of L: a totally real slice of full
  dimension. PROVED.

**Torus covers.** Let A and B be the Q-spans of the horizontal and vertical
edge lengths, a = dim A, b = dim B, each between 1 and r + 1.

- X(P) is a torus cover iff its absolute periods form a lattice iff
  a = b = 1. PROVED, from the period description. Relative periods are
  half-periods because zeros are iota-fixed.
- This is the task's "commensurable". The two units can be incommensurable
  with each other: diag(s, t) is in GL(2, R).
- Apisa-Wright Proposition 3.11 (READ): a rational polygon unfolds to a torus
  cover iff it is Gaussian, Eisenstein, or rectangle-tiled. Their k = 2 case
  ends with "relative periods lie in Z[ia] for some real number a", so
  rectangle-tiled means one a x b rectangle with a/b arbitrary. Yes, different
  units are allowed.
- Their no-slit convention (connected boundary, no slits) is used once: every
  zero is fixed by a non-trivial power of the rotation. Strictly simple rooms
  satisfy it. PROVED.

**pi/4 rooms.** Let n_k be the number of corners of angle k pi/4, and suppose
some odd k occurs. The group is D_4 and X(P) has 8 copies.

- Odd k: 1 lift, cone angle 2 pi k. k = 2: 2 regular lifts. k = 6: 2 lifts of
  angle 6 pi. So X(P) lies in H(2^(n_3 + 2 n_6), 4^(n_5), 6^(n_7)) with
  genus 1 + n_3 + 2 n_6 + 2 n_5 + 3 n_7. PROVED. Check: the 45-45-90 triangle
  gives genus 1; the right trapezoid (2, 2, 1, 3) gives H(2).
- The rotation T of order 4 has T* omega = i omega. X/T is DP with a quartic
  differential. X/T^2 lies in Q(-1^(n_1 + 2 n_2), 1^(n_3 + 2 n_6), 3^(n_5),
  5^(n_7)) and is not a sphere in general. PROVED.
- X(P) is a torus cover iff P is Gaussian, that is, similar to a polygon with
  vertices in Z[i] (Proposition 3.11 and its proof, READ). A diagonal wall
  forces one common unit.

## 2. The theorems to apply

All from Apisa-Wright, arXiv:1708.03411 (READ in the TeX source, last version
of January 2021), unless marked.

- **Setting.** x_1, x_2 on a translation surface are finitely blocked if a
  finite set B meets every straight segment from x_1 to x_2. Points can be
  equal and can be zeros. Non-illumination is the case where B is empty. For
  polygons, paths can't pass through vertices.
- **M-periodic point.** For an orbit closure M, a 1-point marking over M of
  the same dimension as M. Periodicity is relative to M, the orbit closure of
  the surface in question.
- **Theorem 1.2 (Eskin-Filip-Wright, as quoted there).** M has infinitely many
  periodic points iff M consists of torus covers. EFW itself not read.
- **X_min and Q_min.** Standing assumption: (X, omega) isn't a torus cover.
  Moeller (quoted): a unique translation cover pi_X: X -> X_min of minimal
  genus exists, and every translation cover from X factors it. Lemma 3.3:
  X_min has at most one involution j negating omega. Q_min = X_min/j if X
  covers some strict half-translation surface, else Q_min = X_min.
  pi_Q: X -> Q_min is the composite.
- **Lemma 3.1.** If j* omega = -omega, then p and j(p) are finitely blocked by
  the fixed points of j: the midpoint of each segment is j-fixed.
- **Theorem 3.6.** If x_1 and x_2 are finitely blocked on (X, omega), then
  either both are M-periodic points or zeros, with M the orbit closure of
  (X, omega), or pi_Q(x_1) = pi_Q(x_2). This is an "only if". Their remark
  after the blocking-set theorem says the converse fails for two periodic
  points and for two points with pi_X(x_1) = pi_X(x_2).
- **Blocking-set theorem (label T:blockingsets).** For a minimal blocking set
  B of non-periodic x_1, x_2: if pi_X(x_1) = pi_X(x_2), B contains no periodic
  point.
- **Theorem 1.4.** Let Q be a stratum component other than Q(-1^4),
  Q(2, -1^2), Q(2^2), and Q~ its locus of double covers with involution J. If
  Q isn't hyperelliptic, the only Q~-periodic points are the fixed points of
  J. If Q is hyperelliptic, add the fixed points of the hyperelliptic
  involution. Genus-0 strata are covered: Q(1, -1^5) is a base case of the
  induction, and the proof treats genus-0 strata as non-hyperelliptic.
- **Theorem 1.1 and Theorem 3.10 (k-differentials).** Non-torus-cover
  polygons: each point is finitely blocked from finitely many points. If some
  angle isn't a multiple of pi/2, only finitely many pairs are finitely
  blocked.
- **Lelievre-Monteil-Weiss, arXiv:1407.2975 (READ).** Theorem 1: M is a torus
  cover iff every pair is finitely blocked iff an open set of pairs is.
  Theorem 2: each point fails to illuminate finitely many points; the dark
  set in M x M is a finite set plus finitely many embedded translation
  surfaces that cover M finitely on both sides. Corollary 3: the polygon
  version.
- **Wolecki, arXiv:1905.09358, Theorem 1.2 (READ).** In a rational polygon
  with connected interior, only finitely many pairs are dark, torus covers
  included. Pairs with blocking cardinality at most n are finite for each n
  iff the unfolding isn't a torus cover.
- **Sharper results.** Apisa, arXiv:1710.05505 (READ): on a genus-2 surface
  that isn't a torus cover, the only periodic points are Weierstrass points,
  plus two golden points on the golden eigenform locus. Its finite blocking
  theorem (label T3): a zero is finitely blocked from no other point, and a
  regular point only from its image under the hyperelliptic involution.
  Apisa-Wright, "High rank invariant subvarieties" (MEMORY): rank at least
  g/2 + 1 forces a stratum component or a hyperelliptic locus.

## 3. Orbit closure for generic lengths

**Condition G.** The lengths of g of the g + 1 horizontal edges are linearly
independent over the field K of real algebraic numbers. The same holds for
the vertical edges.

**Proposition 1.** Under G, the orbit closure of X(P) is the component of L
that contains it. For each shape, the rooms that fail this lie in countably
many proper linear subspaces of the length space R^g x R^g. PROVED, given:

- orbit closures are affine invariant submanifolds, and countably many exist
  (Eskin-Mirzakhani-Mohammadi, READ as quoted in LMW);
- their linear equations have coefficients in a real number field (Wright,
  field of definition, MEMORY). The almost-every form doesn't need this.

Proof. Let M be the orbit closure; M lies in L. If dim M < dim L, a real
linear equation sum a_j z_j = 0 holds at X(P) with a not zero. In the chart
of section 1, its real part is a relation among horizontal lengths and its
imaginary part a relation among vertical lengths. G excludes both. So M is
open and closed in the component.

Remark. Q-independence isn't enough. L-shaped Veech tables with quadratic
irrational sides (McMullen, Calta; MEMORY) have a = b = 2 and a closed orbit.

**Proposition 2.** If a + b >= r + 3, then X_min = X(P) and
Q_min = X(P)/iota = DP. In particular this holds under G. PROVED. Proof: a
cover X -> Y of degree at least 2 has genus(Y) <= (g + 1)/2. The classes of
Re omega and Im omega lie in the rational subspace pi* H^1(Y), of dimension
at most g + 1 = r + 2. Their rational hulls are independent and have
dimensions a and b. The involution on X_min is unique, so it is iota.

## 4. The exceptional set

**Room dictionary.** PROVED. A billiard path from p to q with no vertex
contact is a segment on X(P) from one lift of p to some lift g q, g in G,
that avoids the vertex lifts. So if (p, q) is dark, a fixed lift p~ is
finitely blocked from every g q~ by the regular vertex lifts.

**Theorem B.** Let P satisfy G, or only: the orbit closure of X(P) is a
component of L. Then:

1. distinct non-vertex points p, q (interior or on walls) are lit;
2. an interior point p is lit from itself;
3. a vertex and a non-vertex point are joined by a path with no other vertex
   contact.

Proof. By Theorem 1.4 the periodic points of L are the iota-fixed points:
the vertex lifts. By Proposition 2, pi_Q is the quotient by iota. By Theorem
3.6, a finitely blocked pair (x_1, x_2) with x_1 not a vertex lift has
x_2 in {x_1, iota x_1}. For (1) and (3), g q~ in {p~, iota p~} gives q = p.
For (2), take g = h: h p~ isn't p~ or iota p~ when p is interior.

So for generic rooms the exceptional points are vertices only. No interior
point and no wall point is exceptional. Vertex-vertex pairs aren't decided.
The pair (p~, iota p~) is always finitely blocked (Lemma 3.1); it projects to
p = q and costs nothing.

**Theorem C (every incommensurable orthogonal room).** Let M be the orbit
closure of X(P). Let E be the set of points of P with an M-periodic lift or a
vertex; E is finite (Theorem 1.2). G descends to X_min, compatibly with
pi_X (the argument of Apisa-Wright Remark 3.4, applied to h and v). So h
induces a reflection R of Q_min. If interior points p != q are dark, then
either p and q are both in E, or pi_Q(p~) = pi_Q(q~) and this point is fixed
by R. The second case needs X_min != X(P), so it is empty when
a + b >= r + 3. PROVED.

In the second case P is tiled by reflected copies of a smaller table
Q_min/R, and p, q are corresponding points on fold lines. Such a pair isn't
automatically dark. Vertex lifts are periodic, so the blocking-set theorem
forces an empty minimal blocking set: darkness would mean no segment at all
joins the lifts, a monodromy condition on the cover X -> X_min. Not decided.

**L-shaped room, complete.** r = 1, X(P) in H(2), genus 2.

- Commensurable (a = b = 1): torus cover, outside this file. d15 Theorem 1
  proves all interior pairs lit there too.
- Incommensurable: X(P) isn't a torus cover, so it is primitive in Apisa's
  sense. By his theorem T3, x_1 is finitely blocked from x_2 only if
  x_2 = iota x_1 and x_1 is regular. For p != q, no lift g q~ equals iota p~.
  For p = q a vertex-free loop exists when p is interior (g = h).
- **Theorem L.** In an incommensurable L-shaped room every two distinct
  points are lit, vertices included, and each interior point is lit from
  itself. PROVED from T3 (READ).
- Sub-cases inside T3, for the record. Orbit closure H(2): Theorem B applies.
  Closed orbit (non-arithmetic Veech, quadratic lengths): periodic points are
  Weierstrass points (Moeller, quoted by Apisa). X(P) has one double zero, so
  it isn't on the golden locus, which lies in H(1,1).
- Consistency: d15 proves the same for all real lengths by elementary means.
  No conflict at r <= 2.

## 5. The statement

**Strongest supportable theorem.** Theorems B, L, and C, as stated in section
4 and summarized in [Results](#results).

### What remains

For "no dark interior pair in every incommensurable orthogonal room":

1. **Orbit closures for special lengths, r >= 2.** Classify the affine
   invariant submanifolds M of L that contain a room surface. Open in
   general. The high-rank theorem (MEMORY) would give M = L when
   rank(M) >= (r + 3)/2; the hypothesis must be checked from the lengths.
2. **Periodic points of each such M.** Show that none is the lift of an
   interior point, or list them. Known only for M = L (Theorem 1.4) and
   genus 2. Rank-1 closed orbits in genus >= 3 have no general answer.
3. **Periodic pairs.** Theorem 3.6 allows two periodic points to be blocked;
   a direct check per pair is needed, as in Apisa's genus-2 proof.
4. **Fold-line pairs** when X_min != X(P): decide the monodromy condition.
5. A possible tool, not carried out (CONJ): a periodic point in a horizontal
   cylinder must sit at a height that is a Q-combination of cylinder moduli.

**pi/4 rooms.** Theorem 3.10's proof (READ) gives more than the orthogonal
case, for every non-Gaussian room with an odd corner: a point is finitely
blocked from nothing unless its lifts are M-periodic or map to points of
X_min fixed by a power of the induced rotation. So no fold-line case occurs,
and the exceptional set is finite. For generic lengths the orbit closure is
the full double-cover locus of the quadratic stratum of X/T^2. (Sketch: the
rooms are a totally real slice of the i-eigenspace of T*, and a real
subspace containing it contains the conjugate eigenspace. CONJ until written
out.) Then Theorem 1.4 gives periodic points = vertex lifts when that stratum
isn't hyperelliptic. X_min = X for generic pi/4 rooms wasn't checked.

**Irrational-angle polygons.** Not touched. Every result here needs a finite
reflection group, a compact translation surface, and the GL(2, R) orbit
closure theorems.

## 6. Limiting argument for lattice rooms

It gives nothing. Being lit is an open condition in (lengths, p, q), so the
dark set is closed: a limit of dark triples is dark. The useful direction
would be the opposite one, and it fails: lattice rooms are limits of generic
rooms, which Theorem B shows fully lit, yet a lattice room can be dark (the
project's 22-wall room). The one consequence is negative: every dark pair of
an orthogonal room disappears under a generic change of edge lengths, so no
dark pair is stable in an open set of lengths.

## Weakest step

Proposition 1 as an explicit condition rests on Wright's field-of-definition
theorem, cited from memory. The almost-every version avoids it. Next weakest:
Theorem C's descent of h and v to X_min adapts a remark written for
holomorphic symmetries; the adaptation is three lines and wasn't checked by a
second reader.
