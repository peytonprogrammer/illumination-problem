# d34: stage 1 of the 22-wall minimum for (1,3,4) rooms

Date: 2026-11-12. Approved by Daniel on 2026-11-12. Compute used: about 16 CPU minutes, 61 MB of disk, at most 2 processes.

## Result

- **W = 18 is closed.** Every strictly simple (1,3,4) room with 18 walls and a pair of interior A points with at most 10 extra tiles is lit. By the ceiling F <= 42 (`../d32-fold-minimum/astra-r3.md`), no dark room has W = 18.
- **W = 19 is closed.** The same holds through 12 extra tiles, and the ceiling is F <= 44. So no dark room has W = 19.
- Together with d33 (W = 17), no dark (1,3,4) room has W <= 19.
- Candidates: none. Every interior A pair in every room with W <= 19 has an exact path. No pair needed the midpoint search.
- Profile cross-check: no violations in 51,772 rooms with W <= 21 (k <= 12), or in 97,984 rooms (k <= 13).

Correctness rests on the d7-review enumerator and tracer, the two prunes below, and the astra-r3 ceilings.

## Coverage invariant (task 1)

Claim: for each k, the scan evaluates every strictly simple room `Q = St(S) u St(T) u K` with `|K| <= k` whose stars share no C-tip, up to the symmetries of St(S).

1. **Placement.** The tile universe is the orbit of one tile under the group generated by reflections in its sides. That group acts transitively on tiles and preserves the universe. So any interior A point P of a room can be moved to the origin S.
2. **T range.** `Room.analyze` requires an edge-connected tile set, and a strictly simple room is one. A shortest dual path from St(S) to St(T) therefore uses at most k tiles of K. So some tile of St(T) lies within reflection distance k + 1 of St(S). `t_candidates` collects every A vertex at that distance and canonicalizes it under the 16 symmetries of St(S).
3. **Sets.** Redelmeier enumeration on the tile graph, with St(S) as root and St(T) contracted to one node, reaches every connected set exactly once. A pruned branch loses only sets that contain the current set and draw further tiles from the current untried list. Each prune rejects a branch only when every such set is invalid or has W > Wmax:
   - pairwise compatibility, which holds for every pair of tiles in a valid room;
   - `|K| <= k`, and the dual distance bound for reaching St(T);
   - **B-point prune** (new): every B point is a corner, because its angle is `3j pi/8` and never `pi` or `2pi`. The set of B points only grows, so `#B > Wmax` holds for every completion. Through k = 13 it never fired;
   - **finalized-corner prune** (new): tiles popped from the untried list are excluded for the whole branch. Suppose every edge-neighbour of the fan at an A or C vertex v is in the room, excluded, or incompatible with St(S) u St(T). Then no valid completion adds a tile at v: a second fan at v is a pinch, and joining it would need an excluded tile. So an angle other than `pi` and `2pi` at v gives a corner in every completion. Prune when `#B + #finalized corners > Wmax`.
4. **Final filter.** Each set is checked by `Room.analyze`: edge-to-edge, one boundary cycle, no pinch, boundary angles below `2pi`, interior angles `2pi`. Only final rooms are filtered by W. No prune uses intermediate wall counts or holes, as astra-r3 advises.
5. **R2.1 skip.** If St(S) and St(T) share a C-tip X, the four tiles at X form the convex rhombus S B T B'. The open segment ST meets only the interior point X, so S sees T. Dropping T = 2c loses only placements of lit pairs. A dark pair (P, Q) shares no C-tip, so its room is still reached with P = S and Q = T.
6. **Closing W.** The stars of distinct A points share no tile, so a dark W = 18 room has `F - 32 <= 10` extras relative to its dark pair. Every interior A pair of each kept room is searched, so the dark pair would be tested.

## Profile cross-check (task 3)

`profile34.py` computes b, nu_j, A-corner fans, p, s, m, t, I, f, F, and the boundary AB, AC, and BC edge counts. It checks every astra-r3 condition:

- the boxed identity, `F = 16I + 8f + K`, and `W = b + a + p + m`;
- `b >= 16`, `nu_1 <= b - 16`, the BA-spoke bound, and the I = 3 rules;
- the C-incidence and Euler equations, and `s + t + m >= 16`;
- the boundary-edge ranges and parities, plus a direct match of the three boundary edge counts;
- the ceiling, and membership in the three W = 18, F > 40 families.

Because the astra-r3 profile list is defined by exactly these conditions, passing them is the same as matching a listed profile.

- No room violates any condition.
- Maximum F is 40, 42, and 44 at W = 17, 18, and 19, so all three ceilings are attained.
- W = 18 rooms realize 9 profiles. All three F > 40 families occur: (b 17, A (2), F 42) in 88 rooms, (b 16, A (1,1), F 42) in 2, and (b 16, A (1), p 1, F 41) in 4. All 135 rooms are lit.
- W = 19 rooms realize 27 profiles. No room with W <= 21 has I = 3.

## Projected cost for W = 20 (k <= 16) and W = 21 (k <= 18)

These figures are estimates. Sets evaluated grow by a factor of 2.4, 2.2, and 1.9 per step from k = 10 to 13, so I assume a factor of 1.8 to 2.2.

- **W = 20, k <= 16, Wmax = 20.** The k = 13 enumeration took 113 s (587,023 sets), so k = 16 needs 11 to 20 CPU minutes. W = 20 had 12,881 rooms at k = 13. At 25 ms per room, plus deeper midpoint searches for hard rooms, path search adds about 10 to 40 minutes. Total: about 0.5 to 1 CPU hour, which fits one run under the current caps.
- **W = 21, k <= 18, Wmax = 21.** Enumeration needs 344 s times 19 to 52, about 2 to 5 CPU hours. W = 21 rooms grew 2.1x from k = 12 to 13, so expect 0.5M to 2M rooms. Path search costs 4 to 25 CPU hours.
- Before the W = 21 run, `scan34.py` needs two changes: search rooms as they arrive instead of holding them in memory, and write tiles only for candidates and the hardest rooms. Splitting T across processes is already supported (`part nparts` arguments).
