# Effective portal access for arbitrary lattice chambers

**Verdict for review.** There is an explicit corridor threshold for every pair of interior lattice marks in opposite, otherwise arbitrary, orthogonal lattice chambers. A second bound covers all real interior cross pairs, and cap doubling gives an explicit whole-room theorem for a fixed junction with one long arm. The proof controls one common interval of slopes across all corner switches by constructing it directly. It is exponential in the chamber area. It gives neither a universal area cutoff nor a synchronized deficit for the BBBB core. The large-rectangle argument below identifies what an area estimate would still need even if first-door access were cheap.

## Explicit cross-pair theorem

Seal the vertical mouths of two fixed, strictly simple, connected lattice chambers. The mouths have the same positive integral width w and lattice endpoints. Joining them with a straight empty rectangular corridor of length L must give a strictly simple room with physical long sides. Put m=4 max(area A, area B), F₀=0, F₁=1, Fₖ₊₂=Fₖ₊₁+Fₖ. Define

    T = 1+F_(3m−1).            L ≥ 4w T² suffices.

The conclusion is illumination of **every cross-chamber interior lattice pair**, with one corridor traversal. There is no parity, reflex-count, door alignment or chamber monotonicity requirement. The chamber area excludes the connecting corridor. This is a worse threshold than d112 for rectangular chambers. Its gain over the arbitrary-chamber compactness theorem is a numerical bound uniform in chamber shape at fixed lattice area.

### A finite-time portal hit without testing directions

The four-copy translation unfolding of one sealed chamber has M=4 area(chamber) unit squares. R and U are its permutations moving across the right and top edges of a square. They generate a transitive group: the translation surface is connected by paths crossing square edges away from vertices. A positive-x, positive-y ray in developed coordinates starts at the lower-left corner of a square incident to the chosen lattice mark. Choose an inward physical sector; any signed launch works for an interior mark. Count a hit when the ray crosses a vertical unit edge over the sealed portal. At least one square has such a right edge. This is checked on the cover, so crossing also specifies the physical sheet and direction.

Set φ=(1+√5)/2, α=1/φ². In unit horizontal step k, starting at abscissa k≥0 relative to the mark, the ray goes up one row before crossing right exactly when the letter floor((k+1)α)−floor(kα) is one. For a zero the state changes by R and the tested edge is the initial square's right edge. For a one it changes by RU and the tested edge is the right edge of the square reached by U. There are no lattice contacts at positive integer abscissas. A wall hit at noninteger abscissa is on an open horizontal edge.

The first letter is zero. The tail is the infinite Fibonacci word beginning with W₀=0, W₁=01, Wₖ₊₂=Wₖ₊₁Wₖ. Here is a check of the phase of the word, needed because the start is a lattice point. In the tail (number positions starting at one) the m-th one occurs at floor(mφ²). The complementary positions are floor(mφ): the cumulative counts through index j are floor((j+1)/φ²) and floor((j+1)/φ), whose sum is j since the two arguments are irrational and sum to j+1. Under substitution 0→01, 1→0, the m-th zero of the tail, at position floor(mφ), produces a one at position floor(mφ)+m=floor(mφ²). Thus the tail is fixed by the substitution; its prefixes are Wₖ and |Wₖ|=Fₖ₊₂.

For a block Wₖ let gₖ be its resulting permutation and Pₖ the set of all initial squares from which reading the block hits a portal edge at least once. These are abstract finite sets; no permutation table or trajectory is required. Composition gives

    Pₖ₊₂ = Pₖ₊₁ ∪ gₖ₊₁⁻¹ Pₖ,       gₖ₊₂ = gₖ gₖ₊₁.

The Pₖ are nested because the blocks are prefixes. P₀ is nonempty. If four consecutive sets from Pᵢ through Pᵢ₊₃ are equal to P, the recurrence gives invariance under gᵢ₊₁ and gᵢ₊₂: inverse images are subsets with the same cardinality. Every consecutive pair of block permutations generates the same group, since either earlier generator can be recovered after the replacement (g,h)→(h,gh). At the start g₀=R and g₁=(RU)R, so the group contains R and U. Transitivity forces P to be the entire set. Thus each run of three steps increases |Pₖ| by at least one until it is full, and P_(3(M−1)) already contains every square. Allowing the initial zero step gives a first portal hit by horizontal travel 1+F_(3M−1)≤T. The construction applies to both chambers at the *same* α, including the leg traced backward from the target.

### Stability and exact gluing

A change of slope in (0,1) can change the finite unit-square cutting word through step T only by crossing a rational p/q with 1≤q≤T. For p/q in [0,1], the polynomial x²−3x+1 and the conjugate α′=(3+√5)/2 give

    |α−p/q| = |p²−3pq+q²| / (q²|α′−p/q|) > 1/(3q²).

The closed interval α±1/(4T²) therefore lies in (0,1) and preserves the two first-hit words, all earlier reflections, and the vertical unit edges of the two portal hits. Neither hit is an endpoint. The signed phase mismatch in the corridor has slope L+a+b on this interval, where a,b>0 are the horizontal travels of the two legs. Therefore its range has length (L+a+b)/(2T²)>2w when L≥4wT², and contains a multiple of 2w in its interior. Folding gives a legal path. This is the same phase equation proved in the [corridor note](README.md). The argument makes no monotonicity assertion across different words. In [d113's saved room](general-gluing.md) the first-hit mismatch jumps upward from 130/33 to 136/33 across a required residue. That jump persists along the saved stretches and shows why the direct d112 proof cannot simply be used in arbitrary chambers.

## Uniform extension to real marks, and a whole-room consequence

**All-real cross-pair bound (for review).** With the same chamber data and m as above, put k=3(m−1), N=2|Wₖ₊₁|=2F_(3m), and H=N+1. Then L≥36wH² lights every interior real cross pair. No denominator of either mark enters.

First, every length-N factor of the Fibonacci word contains Wₖ. Indeed the infinite word decomposes into substituted letter blocks Wₖ₊₁ or Wₖ, both beginning with Wₖ, and of length at most N/2. A window of length N can discard the partial block at its start and still contain the next full block. For the rotation of slope α, every word of length N realized at an intercept away from boundary ties occurs in the Fibonacci tail: the first N letters depend only on the intercept interval between the points −jα mod 1 (0≤j≤N), and irrational rotation visits every such open interval. For slopes s in the closed interval I=α±1/(12H²), these N+1 points stay distinct and keep their cyclic order. By the quadratic estimate above there is no fraction of denominator at most N between s and α. Vary the intercept continuously inside a corresponding interval while varying the slope; each of the N floor increments stays constant. Thus **every** length-N word without a tie for s∈I contains Wₖ. The permutation proof applies at whatever square that block starts in.

For an arbitrary interior mark let h∈(0,1] be the horizontal distance to its first positive vertical grid crossing on the chosen developed sheet. Launch in positive developed coordinates. Through horizontal distance h+N the critical slopes are those where the ray goes through a grid vertex at a crossing h+j, 0≤j≤N. Exclude them even for harmless artificial cell vertices. Earlier truncation at an actual portal is allowed. If the mark starts on a grid line, choose the starting square by the initial velocity; a contact at time zero is not counted. At slope α the transverse positions at those crossings, modulo one, are pairwise more than 1/(3H) apart: for 1≤q≤N use the preceding estimate on the closest integer to qα. Across I each position moves by at most 1/(12H). Hence at most one of the positions can reach an integer anywhere in I, and it can do so at at most one slope. This argument is independent of both starting coordinates.

Remove the at most two exceptional slopes for the two legs together. Some component has width at least |I|/3=1/(18H²). Both first hits take place by time H on it: after the first vertical crossing each surviving leg reads N tie-free letters and therefore includes a full hit-forcing Wₖ. The two words, first hit edges and travel distances are constant across this component. Since a+b>0, the phase range has length strictly greater than 2w at the stated threshold even for open endpoints. This proves the all-real theorem. The same estimate works for one leg with a specified target height inside a strip: use the one-door phase hs+φ(s)−target, with h now the target depth and derivative h+a. 

**Capped arm.** Attach one full-mouth lattice rectangular arm of width w and length ℓ to a fixed sealed lattice junction J, with the simplicity and cap-projection hypotheses in the [cap-doubling result](degree-gluing.md). Set m=4 area(J). A sufficient explicit whole-room threshold is ℓ≥36w[1+2F_(3m)]². For marks both in J use opposite copies and the all-real theorem with passage length 2ℓ. For one mark in the arm, including on the open mouth, the reflected target depth is at least ℓ and the one-door estimate applies. Marks both in the arm see one another. The cap-projection lemma handles a crossing at the old cap endpoint by retracing and truncating the excursion. This removes the unspecified common-interval constants in that fixed-junction result. It does not cover four arms growing together at this bound.

## Large rectangles and the synchronized count

With r≤8 a rectangular decomposition has a piece of area at least n/9. Its shorter side need not grow. In the saved odd crosses every axis-aligned rectangle contained in the room has at least one side of length ≤4: an x interval and a y interval each extending outside [−2,2] would contain a product point outside the cross. The genuine two-mouth corridor is absent; even the artificial-cut estimate gives L/d≤1. The separate cap argument already lights the crosses. They disprove the proposed geometric dichotomy, not illumination. [Cross and corridor obstruction](general-gluing.md) · [Cap placement](cross-placement.md)

There is a local consequence of d104 stronger than a whole-room lower count. At n≥120 for a hypothetical dark pair the union U of marked off-diagonal components has valency d≥n−8. Its complement *within all off-diagonal components* has valency n−1−d≤7. The exact intersection budget gives at most 2(n−1−d)≤14 regular coincidences in that complement over the entire room. Thus for **any** collection of regular lattice lifts on Q, all but at most 14 of its coincidences belong to U. For an integral u-by-v interior rectangle (allow its boundary to touch the room boundary), the off-class sites strictly inside it alone contribute at least

    2[(u−1)(v−1)−⌊u/2⌋⌊v/2⌋]−14

candidates on U. The factor two counts physical interior lifts on Q. The source parity occupies at most the displayed product of floors. A negative lower bound gives no information. [Intersection budget](midpoint-necessity.md) · [Large-area valency](large-dark-profile.md)

By contrast a fixed pair of external access itineraries followed by rectangular reflections produces at most **16 physical midpoint positions inside that rectangle**, or at most 32 lifts on Q. In local coordinates fix the two developed endpoints (sₓ,sᵧ),(tₓ,tᵧ) determined by the two outside words. All further reflection images have abscissa ±tₓ+2ku and ordinate ±tᵧ+2jv. A developed midpoint abscissa is (sₓ±tₓ)/2+ku, with at most four values after folding modulo 2u and reflecting; the ordinate gives at most four more. Restricting actual wall or portal contacts only reduces the count. This concerns paths whose midpoint occurs in the central rectangular part and whose outside words, including incoming sheets, are fixed. It permits unbounded bounces inside. More generally B choices of such word pairs give at most 32B lifts by this argument. A single successful path suffices to light the pair; this observation concerns the attempt to obtain a *linear number* of synchronized midpoints by counting windings in one chamber. The area and the valency count supply candidate locations. They do not supply the external itineraries realizing even one of them.

## Effective consequence and census scale

There are no constants ε,K for a whole-core deficit proved here and no universal n₀(r). A chamber-dependent length threshold leaves pairs on the same side, rooms without a separating corridor, and rooms whose chamber areas grow with L. Reducing the Fibonacci threshold to a polynomial or to 4d would still leave the latter structural and positional issues. In the direct inequality, a uniform explicit deficit H_sync≤(3/2−ε)n+K would give n≤(K+32)/ε for n≥120. That formula by itself still includes a residual census below 120.

D102 replayed 3,933,192 *existing* witnesses in 72.5 CPU seconds, about 54,251 witnesses/s. At that rate R rooms with p already-supplied witnesses per room would need about 5.12×10⁻⁹ Rp CPU-hours for replay alone. At d73's extrapolated 10¹¹–10¹² rooms for areas 21–36 this is about 512–5,120 CPU-hours even in the illustrative one-witness-per-room scenario. D73's own sample estimates use shooting and enumeration with different pipelines; neither extrapolation sizes the residual after a theorem-based filter. There is no justified run budget through a nonexistent n₀.
