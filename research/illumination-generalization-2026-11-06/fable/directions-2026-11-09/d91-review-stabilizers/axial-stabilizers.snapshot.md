# Two axial stabilizers together can have bounded orbits

**Proposition for review.** Let Q be the room double of the previously studied cross with arm half-width 2 and arm extent b, for any odd b≥7. Let G be its orientation-preserving affine group and K its projective derivative kernel. Let A be the group generated by the **full** horizontal-direction stabilizer and the **full** vertical-direction stabilizer in G. For every lattice point lift z,

    A · z = K · z,       |K · z| ≤ 4.

Consequently even arbitrary products of elements from these two cusp stabilizers cannot give a growing suborbit in either orientation of the nondirect same-parity pair (6,0),(0,6). This pair escapes the kernel-orbit and central-fixed-point exceptions. The room has r=4 and area 16b−16; its marked valency is at least n−8 for b≥9. The pair is known lit with an off-class midpoint. ## Computing the action on the rows

Cut Q horizontally at y=±2. The middle horizontal cylinder has circumference 4b and height 4. Each outer cylinder has circumference 8 and height b−2. These are the maximal horizontal bands with the corner trajectories removed: each regular horizontal leaf goes once out and back over its row, and the band interfaces contain corner trajectories. At either central rim the two open intervals leading to the adjacent arm have length 4 each, one on each room copy. On the central circumference their centers are separated by 2b. The cap of an outer cylinder, at y=b or −b, has two convex corners half a circumference apart.

A horizontal-direction stabilizer element must take the middle cylinder to itself. Indeed all horizontal circumferences scale by the same positive horizontal expansion, and a permutation of the finite set of cylinders forces that expansion to be one. The middle circumference is unique. Area preservation and positive orientation now give projective derivative represented by the horizontal shear with parameter λ. On the middle cylinder an element may exchange its two ends. Compose with the kernel lift of reflection in the horizontal axis to undo that exchange. With bottom and top individually preserved, the element is a rotation at each central rim. Such a rotation preserves the pair of attachment intervals, so it is either 0 or 2b modulo 4b. The kernel lift of reflection in the vertical axis switches the room copies and translates central abscissas by 2b. Compose with it if necessary so that the bottom rim action is zero. The remaining map has

    4λ ∈ 2b Z.

In cylinder coordinates take the physical y-coordinate and an abscissa increasing along the front copy and decreasing along the back copy. The normalized map on the central band translates the circle by λ(y+2). On the upper arm's bottom the shift is either 0 or 4 modulo 8. To see this explicitly, central rotation 0 fixes both attachments pointwise. Central rotation 2b sends a front point of physical abscissa x to a back point of abscissa −x, which is rotation by 4 on the width-four doubled row. At the outer cap the map must preserve its pair of convex corners, so the shift there is again 0 or 4. Subtraction on the height b−2 gives

    (b−2)λ ∈ 4 Z.

Write λ=bj/2 using the first condition. Since b(b−2) is odd, the second gives 8|j. Thus λ is a multiple of 4b. This divisibility is needed for every horizontal element, including primitive parabolics; bounding a chosen large power alone would give no restriction on the full subgroup.

At integer y in the middle band the normalized map therefore fixes the point. At integer y in either outer band its rotation is 0 or 4 modulo 8: use the induced shift on the adjacent middle rim and add the shear times an integral vertical distance. Rotation by 4 on these rows acts exactly as the kernel lift of reflection in the vertical axis. The formulas extend to the rims, by their continuous parameterizations. Thus at every lattice lift the original element agrees with *some* member of K. The member may depend on the point; we have made no global kernel assertion about the element. The same reasoning with x and y exchanged applies in the vertical direction. The kernel preserves the lattice and is contained in both stabilizers. Hence each stabilizer preserves each K-orbit of lattice points setwise, as does the group they generate. This proves equality of orbits, including for mixed words.

## What this excludes

The kernel here consists of the four coordinate sign symmetries, lifted with positive orientation. Neither mark is the center, and no kernel element sends either mark to a lift of the other: the two physical kernel orbits are {(6,0),(−6,0)} and {(0,6),(0,−6)}. Every chord between these orbits has midpoint (±3,±3) outside the cross. Thus allowing independent axial orbit choices for both marks still cannot expose a direct chord. The legal shot in the cited note comes from different geometry.

This refutes a two-way lower bound using the two axial cusp stabilizers alone, even after the kernel exceptions in stabilizer-orbits.md have been removed. It does not bound the full affine stabilizer: conjugating an axial cusp by an element outside A can introduce a genuinely different direction. Any route-B proof relying on orbit growth for these marks must use an element outside A. No cusp, direction, room or word search was performed.
