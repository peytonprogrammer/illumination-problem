# Which shadow can bound the area?

**Verdict for review.** For ordinary endpoint illumination on the room double there is a uniform finite bound for each fixed reflex count, by a fixed-stratum argument. It gives no numerical constant. The d82 area reduction requires a different bound, for simultaneous straight paths on the marked ordered-pair cover. The projection of a hidden midpoint can be directly visible from the source. Moreover, the genus needed upstairs grows at least linearly in the marked valency, already in an archived family with four physical reflex corners. I have neither an explicit budget nor a counterexample to the synchronized budget.

## Two different endpoint problems

Write Q for the double and U for d82's marked union of normalized off-diagonal components of Q ×_B Q. On Q a legal path from a source lift to m suffices. On U one must start at a compatible germ over the ordered marks (S,T), reach the coincidence (m,m), and avoid physical corners in **both** projections along the open path. At a regular coincidence the two tracks then join into a legal shot. Darkness makes all such coincidences unreachable from the allowed starts *upstairs*. It makes no such assertion about the projections m downstairs. Each regular m has at most one coincidence germ; multiplicity of projection at m is not the issue.

Two exact controls distinguish these predicates.

* In cov4181 take S=(2,3), T=(4,3), m=(4,2). The open chord from S to m stays in the row of cells with 2<y<3, crossing x=3 at height 5/2. It avoids every vertex. From T the vertical unit segment to m is also interior. Either copy of these chords works on Q. Yet the two coincidence lifts at physical m lie among d66/d82's seven unrealized off-class coincidences. The relative timing and directions of the two legs matter. ## A uniform finiteness theorem downstairs

**Proposition.** For each r there exists B(r)<∞ bounding the number of distinct regular targets hidden from a single source on Q, uniformly over area and room geometry. The same bound works for physical targets. Here and below the source itself is excluded. No lattice assumption on the marks is needed.

Pass to the four-copy translation unfolding M. Its r reflex lifts are order-two zeros and its r+4 convex lifts are regular points marked as forbidden. Thus its marked stratum depends only on r. Fix any source lift. Every hidden Q target has all of its M lifts hidden from that lift, since projection of a legal M segment is legal. This also bounds physical hidden targets.

Here is the uniformity step. In the stratum with those fixed forbidden labels and two further distinct regular labels x,y, let D consist of surfaces where x cannot illuminate y. The latter two labels denote endpoints, outside the forbidden set. D is closed relative to this stratum: a legal segment has a compact developed neighborhood along the segment, represented if necessary by several overlapping flow boxes; its straight connection persists when the charts and endpoints vary slightly. D is invariant under the diagonal action of GL⁺(2,R). By Eskin–Mirzakhani–Mohammadi it is a finite union of affine invariant submanifolds, and by Filip these are algebraic. These results apply to marked strata; one can take finite level structure to remove automorphism identifications. [EMM](https://arxiv.org/abs/1305.3015) · [Filip, Theorem 5.4](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/2016/183-2/p05) · [Marked-stratum formulation, §2](https://msp.org/gt/2020/24-1/gt-v24-n1-p05-s.pdf)

The forget-y map on D is consequently an algebraic morphism of finite type. Each geometric fiber is finite by Lelièvre–Monteil–Weiss, with the convex lifts retained as order-zero forbidden marks. The numbers of points in the fibers of a quasi-finite morphism of finite type over a complex variety are uniformly bounded: stratify the base into finitely many locally closed pieces over which the finite sheets have constant generic degrees, or use Zariski's Main Theorem to embed the source in a finite scheme over the base and bound its module generators on a finite affine cover. With level structure a fiber counts actual target points. Taking the maximum over the finitely many stratum components proves the proposition. This is a deduction from the cited results; their pointwise finiteness statement alone does not give uniformity. [LMW, Theorem 2 and marked-point conventions in §2](https://www.math.tau.ac.il/~barakw/papers/illumination.pdf)

In particular a fixed-r family with unbounded *true ordinary* shadows cannot kill this proposition. Neither the number of components of D nor the degrees in the forgetful map are evaluated here. For 1≤r≤8 one could take the maximum of B(r)/r abstractly. That gives no explicit C and cannot be substituted into d82.

## Why the same uniformity proof fails upstairs

**Genus lemma.** Let R count all regular coincidences in d82's marked union of total projection valency d. If k is the number of its components containing a regular coincidence, their genera satisfy

    sum of genera ≥ R/2 − k ≥ d − r − k,       k ≤ 16.

Indeed coordinate swap extends to the normalization. At a regular coincidence the distinct-sheet germ in a common local coordinate is y=−x. Swap fixes its origin and preserves its component. Swap on that component is nonidentity since its generic ordered coordinates are distinct. If g is its genus, h its quotient genus, and F the number of fixed points, Riemann–Hurwitz gives F=2g+2−4h. Thus g ≥ F/2−1, and each of this component's regular coincidences contributes one fixed point. Sum, use d82's R=2d−J with J≤2r, and note that the marked union is selected by at most sixteen initial sheet pairs. This proof needs no assumption about components with no regular coincidences.

For the single marked DB55 component at n=53 the archived R=96 gives g≥47. More strongly, in the already studied normalized DB55 stretches of length 451+80j the same-parity marked pair is nondirect at every j and r=4. The reviewed d81 bound gives d≥2n/3 there. Thus even the **largest genus of a coincidence-bearing component** is unbounded in this fixed-r example (divide the sum bound by at most sixteen). These rooms illustrate genus growth, not growth of the hidden set. On passing from a half-translation surface to its orientation cover the genus cannot decrease. One cannot apply a finiteness bound for a fixed stratum upstairs. Also, intermediate points mapping to physical corners in either projection must remain forbidden, including when they are regular upstairs. ## What would suffice

For a hypothetical dark pair the synchronized hidden count equals R_other. An explicit estimate H_sync ≤ αn+K with α<5/6 and known K already gives n ≤ (16+K)/(5/6−α) at n≥49. In the reviewed n≥120 range d≥n−8 gives the stronger lower bound R_other≥2n−32−floor(n/2), so **any explicit slope below 3/2** with a known additive constant suffices there. Even a proof directly yielding one reachable off-class coincidence would settle these large-area same-parity cases. None of the ordinary-shadow or genus arguments above supplies such an estimate.

A different possible bridge uses genuine hidden endpoints downstairs: on Q, every affine automorphism preserving the forbidden corners and fixing a given source lift carries a dark target lift to another dark target lift. Its orbit therefore has size at most B(r). To turn that into an area reduction requires a lower bound growing with n for this *affine stabilizer orbit*, or a classification of bounded-orbit exceptions. The sheet-monodromy valency d used in d81 is not this orbit size. No such lower bound or classification is proved here.

**Decision.** The fixed-r ordinary budget exists non-effectively. The explicit synchronized shadow budget remains open. A census cutoff from ordinary shadows would require a separate transfer theorem.
