# Wolecki reply (2026-11-13) and Monteil slides

Source: Daniel's email thread with Amit Wolecki (reply 2026-11-13) and the
attached Monteil talk slides "Finite blocking property and illumination in
billiards and translation surfaces" (59 slides; local copy in
`.context/attachments/XD2yJS/Monteil-TransparentBlocking.pdf`).

## Facts
- Monteil's unpublished 22-sided example: built from reflected copies of
  the isosceles right triangle (45-degree walls), similar to Tokarsky's
  classic room, and it has a boundary self-intersection (Wolecki). It
  appears to be slide 4 (diagonal-walled room with a small square where
  the boundary touches itself). So it isn't strictly simple.
- Slide 5 shows a 20/19-sided example with "further relaxation" of the
  polygon requirements: a slit (wall segment ending inside the room).
  Outside the project target (strictly simple; slits out of scope).
- Our 22-wall room is strictly simple and orthogonal. Wolecki calls the
  example and the lattice argument "very nice".
- Alternative proof (Wolecki): cut the 5-square staircase into reflected
  isosceles right triangles so that a and b are unfoldings of the same
  acute corner; a trajectory a -> b would be a trajectory from an acute
  corner to itself, which Tokarsky's lemma (and the generalization in
  the appendix of Wolecki's "Illumination in Rational Billiards") rules
  out. Coordinator check: in the 2x2 "Union Jack" triangle tiling,
  even-even and odd-odd lattice points are 45-degree vertices of two
  orbits; a and b are both even-even, so they fold to the same acute
  corner. Same mechanism as our fold-back lemma.
- Slides cover history (Strauss, Penrose, Klee, Tokarsky 26, Castro 24),
  HST 2008, Lelievre-Monteil-Weiss 2015 finiteness, and "torus branched
  cover iff finite blocking". Nothing on minimum wall counts.

## Bearing on the minimality programme
None directly. Confirms the strictly simple / orthogonal distinction and
that no minimality result appears in this material.
