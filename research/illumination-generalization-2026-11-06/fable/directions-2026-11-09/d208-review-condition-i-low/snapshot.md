Advances: L1 — condition (i) for normalized rooms below area 49, with a separate degree bound for the translated pillowcase problem.

**Result.** At every prime area in the requested interval (23, 29, 31, 37, 41, 43, 47), the same-class marked degree is exactly n−1, even without the nondirect hypothesis. The twisted degree is at least n−1 at all six of those primes above 23. A uniform bound for either marked degree in the full interval is 4; there are stronger bounds below for specific residual actions. The desired two-thirds bound remains open at composite area 22. No room or direction enumeration was used.

### Where 49 enters

Write zₓ for the number of reflex corners of physical class x. In the primitive case d81 §2 proves either G contains Aₙ or n≤6zₓ for **each** positive zₓ. The nonexceptional fixed-point-ratio estimate is applied to gₓ², which moves exactly 3zₓ sheets. D81 treats the exceptional product actions separately before drawing that dichotomy. At 49 the latter alternative is impossible because zₓ≤8. If two classes contain reflex corners, the smaller has at most four, so 25 suffices in that case.

In the imprimitive case choose a maximal block system of block size k and degree m=n/k on blocks. Normalization gives kZ≤8 for the total number Z of quotient zeros. D81 §3 makes the block action alternating-containing using Jordan's theorem or Theorem G, then proves transitivity on ordered external sheet pairs via the matching or spare-block lemma. An external marked pair gives d≥n−k≥2n/3; if all eight distinct sheets are in one block, d79 gives direct visibility. The cutoff is the first integer beyond several different obstacles. In particular at 48, (k,m)=(2,24) with four quotient zeros at one pole and (4,12) with two at one pole are equality cases of the block-action test m>6z. At (8,6) the spare-block proof requires m≥7. Divisibility matters: no multiple of four between 49 and 51 or of eight between 49 and 55 needs its row's larger threshold. At 22, k≤8 and m≥3 leave only k=2,m=11 in the imprimitive case. The residual quotient multiplicities are z=2,3,4 at the least positive pole multiplicity. ### A prime-degree improvement

Use Burnside's classical prime-degree theorem: a transitive permutation group of prime degree p is doubly transitive or is permutation-isomorphic to a subgroup of AGL(1,p). A normalized rectangle has area one, so a room here has a reflex class. In the affine alternative its nonidentity element gₓ² of order three fixes at most one sheet. For p=23 there is no such element at all, since 3 does not divide 23·22. For p≥29 it fixes p−3zₓ≥p−24>1. Thus G is doubly transitive. Every ordered pair of distinct sheets lies in one orbit. The marked union contains such a pair and excludes the diagonal, proving d=p−1.

Write cₐ=vₐ+3rₐ for the corner contribution at the source pole. For a dark same-class pair the hidden off-class count H satisfies H≥max(0,2d−2r−(n−cₐ)/2). At these primes it therefore satisfies **H≥2p−18−⌊p/2⌋**, already 17 at p=23. An algebraic count does not ensure a legal midpoint.

### What still holds at composite degrees

Each of the four source incident sheets is paired with all four target incident sheets in the marked union. Both projection fibers have size d on generic sheets, so d≥4. If d=4, those sixteen pairs exhaust both the incoming and outgoing degrees there. They form an isolated complete bipartite component on four sheets per side. Transitivity sends it to components covering the entire first coordinate, so 4 divides n. In particular d≥5 when 4 does not divide n. This argument uses occurrence of all local marked branches, without assuming an external orbit. Substitution in the preceding H inequality can give only H≥0 in the unclassified cases.

There is a better estimate for k=2,m=11 at n=22. The quotient has a zero, hence an element of order three. Burnside at degree 11 excludes the affine alternative since 3 does not divide 11·10. Its action on blocks is doubly transitive. There is an external marked pair (two sheets cannot contain all eight). The setwise stabilizer of a source block is transitive on the ten other blocks, so its two sheets together have targets in all ten. Moving the source block into position need not fix the source sheet. The point stabilizer of a sheet has index two in its block stabilizer, hence is normal there. Its orbits on the ten other blocks are either one of size ten or two of size five. In the former case d≥10; in the latter d≥5. In either case the sixteen marked incidences and block transitivity alone give no proof of d≥15. The same two-thirds threshold is also unresolved for primitive n=22 groups outside d81's sufficient cases.

### Transferring to the twisted curve

Over a common regular base point, let L≤G₁×G₂ be the joint monodromy of f and τ∘f, where τ translates by the nonzero parity difference. Both projections of L are surjective; the two permutation groups are conjugate copies of the original monodromy after choosing sheet labels. The marked union contains a four-by-four set here too: transport toward the source pole along one short regular arc, with its translated arc at the target pole. Each of the two physical lifts per mark contributes two nearby sheets. Taking every branch through every pair of lifts includes all sixteen combinations. Thus the unconditional 4 bound, improved to 5 when 4 does not divide n, transfers verbatim. On a dark pair **Hₑ=2dₑ** by the twisted intersection count, so at least 8 candidates are hidden (at least 10 in the latter case). All these candidates are regular. The same degree bound gives different hidden counts in the two constructions. [Midpoint budget](midpoint-necessity.md) · [Twisted intersection count, pending d207 review](l1-bbbb-area-bound.md)

Two sufficient conditions give a much stronger transfer.

1. **Primitive action and unpaired reflex support.** Suppose some pole x has zₓ>0 but z at x+e is zero. The square of the peripheral loop then acts as (u,1) with u≠1. The kernel on the second coordinate projects to a nontrivial normal subgroup of the first primitive group. Its orbits form a block system, hence it is transitive there. Together with surjectivity in the second coordinate this makes L transitive on all n² ordered pairs. Thus dₑ=n.
2. **Natural alternating or symmetric action, n≥7.** If either projection kernel is nontrivial the same argument applies. Otherwise L is the graph of an isomorphism. For these natural actions every automorphism is induced by relabeling the n letters (the exceptional degree six is outside the interval). The graph then has two orbits on pairs, a matching and its complement. A four-by-four marked set cannot fit in a matching. Hence dₑ≥n−1.

For each prime p≥29 either d81 already gives G⊇Aₚ or it forces every reflex into a single class (two positive classes would give p≤24). In the second case every nonzero e moves that class to a reflex-free class. This proves the claimed transfer at all six primes, with Hₑ≥2p−2 under darkness. At p=23 the only primitive nonalternating case left by this argument with paired reflex support has two classes with four reflexes each, exchanged by e. Double transitivity of either factor alone does not identify the orbits of a subdirect product. In that remaining prime case there is still a lower bound of seven: in the graph case an element of order 23 acts as a full cycle in both coordinates. Label each cycle by ℤ/23 so that it advances by one. For the two four-element marked sets A,B the diagonal cyclic action on A×B meets |B−A| distinct orbits, each with one target per source. Cauchy–Davenport gives |B−A|≥7, so dₑ≥7 and Hₑ≥14 under darkness. At n=22 the imprimitive factor may also have an intransitive nontrivial normal subgroup. These are genuine gaps in the transfer proof; no dark examples or degree counterexamples are asserted.

**First open case.** A normalized nondirect same-class pair at n=22 still needs d≥15 or a different lighting proof. In the imprimitive residual the first missing implication is control of sheet orbits above the eleven blocks. For different-parity marks at n=22 the first missing implication is a large orbit in the joint, translated action containing marked sheets. Neither gap is an L2 census result.
