# d61: the fold-converse lemma for the equal-images branch

Date: 2026-11-18. Named rooms only (cross12, dumb12, R22, P6). The runs use
exact permutation arithmetic, one process at a time. Tags: **PROVED** (argument
here), **COMPUTED** (exact check here), **GUESSED** (judgment), **CITED**
(proved or checked elsewhere).

## Answer

- **The right H is per state, not a group.** Fix a state of the pure-braid
  orbit and a tail arc `alpha: a -> x`. Light between two points with equal
  images in N is generated by at most two partial involutions. These are the
  local monodromies of `Q -> N` at the two pole ends of the live component of
  `u`, restricted to their 2-cycles. If the live component is a cycle, the
  generator is instead the monodromy of `Q -> N` around it (PROVED, Lemma FC).
- **The lit side is exact.** `S~` and `T~` are x-lit if and only if, in some
  state, they lie in one orbit of this partial dihedral (or cyclic) action.
  The check covers 992,988 (state, arc, lift pair) instances on the four
  rooms, interior and wall marks, plus the cross12 tower step. It found 0
  disagreements with ground truth (COMPUTED).
- **No group works.** The monodromy group of `Q -> N` is transitive on the
  fibre (PROVED). Using full local permutations, which ignores corner
  avoidance, lights dark pairs on R22 and P6 (COMPUTED). x-lit isn't transitive
  (CITED, d14 section 4.3).
- **The certificate direction is open, and it isn't a statement about the
  minimal factor N.** By d10 R3, it's equivalent to a "realization lemma" about
  one canonical factor `N_x = Q / B_x`, not the minimal N (PROVED reduction).
  Every x-dark row in the tests has a certificate (COMPUTED, 0 exceptions). For
  `Q = DP` at a minimal N, none of those certificates lives in N itself.
- **Reduction.** If proved, the realization lemma plus the closed fold side
  (d46, d52, d56) gives the W <= 20 orthogonal target. `N_x` ranges over a
  finite set: degree at most `4r + 4 <= 36` (PROVED). The cover `Q -> N_x`
  has unbounded degree, so the lemma itself doesn't become a finite check.

## 1. Setting

`Q -> N -> B`, with `N = Q / Pi` for a block system `Pi`. `Q` is the double
`DP` of a room, or an intermediate factor (the tower step). It is a genus-0
pillowcase cover with cycle lengths 1, 2, 3. Points over a corner are cycles
of `g_y`: pole, regular, or zero. A point of N is **live** when a regular
point of `Q` lies over it (d10).

A state is a basis in the pure-braid orbit. In each state, the tail arc
`alpha: a -> x` gives the bipartite graph `Gamma_alpha(Q)`. A light path is a
walk in `Gamma_alpha(Q)` whose interior vertices are regular. Room corners are
exactly the non-regular points, so this is corner avoidance.

`S~ != T~` are regular points of `Q` over `a` with `phi(S~) = phi(T~) = u`.
`F_u` is the fibre of `Q -> N` over `u`. `K = K_N(alpha, u)` is the component
of `u` in the subgraph of `Gamma_alpha(N)` induced on live vertices.

## 2. Lemma FC (PROVED)

**Lemma FC.** Fix a state and an arc `alpha: a -> x`.

1. `K` is a path or a cycle. If it's a cycle, every vertex of `K` is
   N-regular.
2. **Cycle case.** Let `mu` be the monodromy of `Q -> N` around the closed
   curve `K`, acting on `F_u`. Then `S~` and `T~` are joined along `alpha` if
   and only if `T~` is in the `<mu>`-orbit of `S~`.
3. **Path case.** An end of `K` is either a live pole `p` or a wall: an
   N-regular vertex whose next vertex isn't live. For each live-pole end `p`,
   let `L_p` be the local monodromy of `Q -> N` at `p`. Transport `L_p` to
   `F_u` along `K`, which is unbranched there by Fact L. Restrict it to its
   2-cycles. The result `tau_p` is a fixed-point-free partial involution of
   `F_u`. If `u` is itself a pole, `tau_u` is `g_a` on the block of `u`,
   restricted to 2-cycles. Then `S~` and `T~` are joined along `alpha` if and
   only if an alternating word in the (at most two) `tau_p` carries `S~` to
   `T~`.
4. **Image of a light path.** The image in N is a closed walk at `u` inside
   `K`. It reverses only at live-pole ends of `K`. Otherwise it runs around
   the cycle `K`. It never meets a point over an N-zero.
5. **Wraps enclose branching.** If `mu` moves `S~`, both discs of the sphere
   N bounded by `K` contain a pole of N over which `Q -> N` is branched.
6. **Corner avoidance.** Replacing each `tau_p` by the full `L_p` changes the
   verdict only through 3-cycles of `L_p`. These are `Q`-zeros with `e = 3`
   over an N-pole. Fixed points of `L_p` are `Q`-poles. They act as self-loops
   and connect nothing.

Hypotheses and where they're used:

| Hypothesis | Used in |
| --- | --- |
| Cycle lengths at most 3 | Fact L: over N-regular points `Q` is regular with `e = 1`; over N-zeros `Q` is a zero. Parts 1, 3, 4, 6. |
| Genus 0 (`Q`, so N) | Jordan curve in part 5 |
| `S~`, `T~` regular | Endpoints of a light path |
| N minimal | Not used. The lemma holds for every factor. |

**Proof.**

1. Live vertices are regular (degree 2 in `Gamma_alpha(N)`) or live poles
   (degree 1). Zeros are never live. So `K` has maximum degree 2. A pole in
   `K` is an end, so a cycle has only regular vertices.
2. A light path from `S~` maps into `K` (d10 Lemma 1, projection).
3. Over an N-regular vertex, the `Q`-vertex has `e = 1`. Its two sheets lie in
   the two distinct blocks of the N-vertex, so the image walk passes straight
   through. Over a live pole `p`, the `Q`-vertex reached is a pole, a regular
   point (`e = 2`), or a zero (`e = 3`). Only `e = 2` continues the path. Its
   two sheets lie in the one block of `p`, so the image walk reverses. Between
   visits to `F_u`, the `Q`-walk is determined by its starting sheet. That
   gives the excursion maps `tau_p` and `mu`. The light path through a fibre
   point over `u` continues exactly when that point is regular. This holds
   automatically when `u` is N-regular. These facts prove parts 2, 3, and 4.
4. Part 5 is the last step of the Cut proof in d10 Theorem U. If one disc
   contains no branch value of `Q -> N`, then `Q -> N` is unbranched over the
   closed disc. The lift of `K` through `S~` is then closed, so `mu` fixes
   `S~`. By Fact L, branch values are N-poles.
5. Part 6: a fixed sheet of `L_p` returns the excursion on the sheet it left
   from, so the edge is a self-loop. Only a 3-cycle adds an edge that the
   restricted map lacks.

**Special cases (PROVED from FC).**

- **Degree 2.** `F_u = {S~, T~}`. Live poles are exactly the branch values. So
  `S~ ~ T~` along `alpha` if and only if `K` has a pole end or is an odd
  cycle. This is d14 Lemma D2 and Corollary D3 (CITED). FC extends them to
  every block size.
- **Parity factor at x** (no regular N-point over x, for example R22's N5).
  `K` is `u` plus its at most two live x-neighbours. With `beta` the block of
  `u` toward `p_1` and `beta' = g_a beta`:
  - `tau_1` is `g_x` on `beta`, restricted to 2-cycles.
  - `tau_2` is `g_x` on `beta'`, restricted to 2-cycles.

  A light path between equal-image points never leaves the sheets of
  `beta` and `beta'`.

**The d60 suggested statement, made precise.** "Lit with equal images implies
the N-image is a closed N-path through N-poles" is PROVED by parts 4 and 5,
with two corrections:

- "Through" means one of two things. Either the walk reverses at a live-pole
  end of `K`, or it wraps a cycle that has branch values on both sides.
- "Or zeros" is vacuous. A light path never meets a point over an N-zero.

The converse needs the orbit condition in parts 2 and 3. A closed walk at `u`
lights the pair only if its partial monodromy carries `S~` to `T~`.

## 3. Why the right H is not a group

- **Group generated by all pole loops.** `Mon(Q -> N)` is transitive on `F_u`
  because `Q` is connected (PROVED). When N is minimal it is primitive,
  because intermediate covers are block systems of `G` refining `Pi`. Every
  x-dark equal-image row below refutes "lit iff same orbit" for it: 16
  interior rows on cross12's minimal N6, and 4 on R22's N5 with wall marks.
- **Full local permutations per state (`Hfull`).** These ignore corner
  avoidance at the ends of `K` and at `u`. The verdict is wrong on P6 (12 of
  12 rows at B), R22 interior (8 of 12 at B), and R22 with wall marks (20 of 45
  at B). It's never wrong at a proper factor of these rooms (COMPUTED). By FC
  part 6, the difference is light passing through a reflex corner.
- **Groupoid generated over all states.** x-lit is a union over states of
  orbit relations, and it isn't transitive. d14 section 4.3 (CITED, abstract
  covers, n = 10) has `s_1 ~ s_3 ~ s_2` with `s_1`, `s_2` not x-lit. Two
  transports from different states compose to a closed loop that no single
  straight path realizes.

So H is the family `{D_alpha}`. Each `D_alpha = <tau_{p_1}, tau_{p_2}>`
(partial) or `<mu_K>`, one per state and arc. The pair is x-lit if and only if
some `D_alpha` joins `S~` to `T~`.

## 5. Proof status of the two directions

### Same orbit implies lit (PROVED)

This is Lemma FC parts 2 and 3. For a fixed state the joining path is
explicit. It needs no minimality and no certificate hypothesis.

### Certificate implies different orbits in every state (PROVED)

This is Theorem U (d10), restated through FC:

- Par separates the images in a factor `N'`.
- Cut means N has no live pole over `a` or `x` (no `tau` at all), and every
  wrap is trivial.
- Rig makes `K` the same for every state.

### Different orbits in every state implies a certificate (OPEN)

This is the direction that would close the target. What it needs:

**Reduction to `B_x` (PROVED from d10 R3).** Par(x) holds for some factor
exactly when `B_x` separates `S~`, `T~`. So "x-dark implies a certificate at
x" is equivalent to the following.

**Realization lemma (OPEN, COMPUTED on the named rooms).** Let
`N_x = Q / B_x`. Let `S~ != T~` be regular a-points with one image in `N_x`.
Suppose neither Rig(x) (via `R_y`, `y` not in `{a, x}`) nor Cut(x) holds. Then
in some state of the pure-braid orbit:

- The 2-cycles of `g_x` and `g_a` inside the blocks of `u` connect `S~` to
  `T~`.

By the parity-factor case of FC, this is a single straight path. Data: 202
rows with `B_x` not separating are x-lit, and 56 are x-dark. All 56 are Cut.

Notes on this reduction:

- `B_x` is state-independent. Pure-braid moves conjugate `g_x` inside `G`,
  so induced cycle types and block systems don't change. So `N_x` is
  canonical.
- The minimal factor N in d60's statement is the wrong object. For `Q = DP`
  its certificates live in other factors, and `N_x` needn't be minimal.
- For `N_x = B`, the realization lemma is the per-class form of d14 Lemma H.
  This is consistent with d14's remark that FH\* at `N = B` is Lemma H.

**What Fact L and Theorem U supply.** They supply all of the following:

- Lemma FC.
- The soundness direction.
- The reduction to `N_x`.
- The finiteness of the possible `N_x` (below).

They don't supply the realization step. That step must turn a closure
statement into a single straight path. The closure is that `S~ ≡ T~` under
the `G`-closure of the x-bounce pairing, which is what `B_x` is. d14's
non-transitivity example shows that no argument can stay inside the relation
"lit in some state". It has to use the pure-braid dynamics of the states.
Lemma H's twist technique (d14 Lemma H1) is the only tool so far of that type
(GUESSED).

## 6. Does the lemma reduce the W <= 20 target?

**If the realization lemma is proved for orthogonal lattice rooms, the W <= 20
target follows from completed work.**

1. A dark pair is x-dark for b, c, and d.
2. The realization lemma with R3 gives a certificate at each corner.
3. d46, d52, and d56 show no W <= 20 room has a pair certified dark over any
   pillowcase. That covers lattice and rational marks.
4. d16 passes from rational to real marks.

So the realization lemma is the whole remaining content of the lit side. It
is the per-class D2 of d10 section 4, localized to the blocks of `u` in
`N_x`.

**Finite part (PROVED).** When Par(x) fails, `N_x` has no regular point over
x. So:

- `deg N_x = P_x + 3 Z_x`, where `P_x` and `Z_x` count poles and zeros over
  x.
- Gauss-Bonnet on the genus-0 surface `N_x` gives `P - Z = 4`.
- By Fact L, zeros of `N_x` have only zeros above them, so `Z(N_x) <= r`.

Hence `deg N_x <= (r + 4) + 3 r_x <= 4r + 4 <= 36` at `r <= 8`. Up to the
pure-braid action, `N_x` ranges over a finite set. These are the d52 fold
targets.

**Infinite part.** The cover `Q -> N_x` has unbounded degree. It's branched
only over the at most `r + 4` poles of `N_x`, with local monodromy made of
2-cycles and at most `r` 3-cycles. So the realization lemma doesn't become a
finite check (GUESSED that no simple bound exists). A finite reduction would
need a bound on the block size of `B_x` for rooms with a candidate dark
pair. Alternatively, it would need a Theorem-R-style argument when
`Mon(Q -> N_x)` is large. A transposition plus primitivity gives the full
symmetric group (Jordan), which is the d14 setting.
