# Accumulation of primitive lattice dark pairs

**Result for review.** Conditional on the reviewed d89 dark-locus theorem and its d80 inputs, unbounded area for dark same-parity interior lattice pairs at r≤8 would already occur in rooms of **one fixed integer height**, after exchanging the axes if necessary. Moreover, the rescaled marked rooms can approach a strictly simple rational room with distinct interior marks, or a nearby room whose unfolding is not a torus cover. Both limits can be chosen on the same dark line of physical rooms. The fixed height depends on the hypothetical examples. This neither exhibits a dark room nor bounds its area.

## What is closed

Fix a cyclic turn type, label the walls, and translate one vertex to the origin. For compactness independently scale the bounding-box width and height to one. Positive diagonal maps preserve billiard legality because they commute with both coordinate sign reflections. This normalization is for shape space; for counting lattice rooms use primitive wall lengths separately on each axis (gcd one). In a primitive lattice room with bounding-box dimensions X,Y, the two scaled coordinate denominators divide X,Y respectively. There is no common denominator automatically comparable to the cell area n. Bounded X,Y give only finitely many labeled integer rooms and lattice marks.

In the space U of strictly simple rooms with two distinct interior marks, lighting is open: unfold one finite legal path and retain its ordered, transverse contacts, every contact in the open edge and every open segment clear of unused edges. These conditions persist. Thus the physical dark set D is closed **relative to U**. In the compact chart closure define the persistent set by

    P∞ = intersection over N of closure of {primitive dark marked lattice rooms of area ≥ N}.

Use all incident labeled charts, or take their disjoint closures. Nonempty tails for arbitrarily large N imply P∞ is nonempty by compactness. A point in P∞∩U is physically dark. A pinch, an erased wall, a mark at a corner or a coincident pair in P∞ is only a boundary limit; closedness gives no billiard assertion for the erased or degenerate room. Persistence is stronger than membership in D: a single isolated dark rational shape contributes nothing by subdividing its grid, which leaves its primitive area unchanged. ## A regular replacement for an arbitrary escaping sequence

Assume unbounded primitive area with a dark same-parity lattice pair. Fix r and labeled combinatorics on a subsequence. Use d89's minimal-locus construction on **these** examples: it only requires an unbounded collection and one can retain the original mark and parity condition when selecting the seeds. Its output is a conical rational marked affine invariant locus N in the four-cross-pair dark set, stable under the physical mirror with label exchange. It has complex dimension 2k, k≥2, and the forgetful projection has zero tangent kernel. There is a smooth physical arithmetic seed from the collection: the intersection with the mirror image and the singular/self-intersection locus are handled by the same minimality argument as in d89. We use the conical locus before area normalization. No assumption that the **original** sequence has a smooth limit is needed. [D89 proof and dependencies](dark-locus.md) · [Independent review](../../illumination-generalization-2026-11-06/fable/directions-2026-11-09/d89-review-dark-locus/review.md)

The d80 input used by d89 is the exclusion of irrational dark marks in a strictly simple normalized lattice seed with r≤8 and n≥120. Its chain includes corrected d16, the low-valency and three-sheet results, and uniqueness of the largest partition at n≥120. D89's referee explicitly recalled EMM, Filip, EFW, Wright and Apisa–Wright from memory, including the marked-stratum conventions; d115 did not independently check that literature. The later [primary-source audit](generic-line.md) distinguishes the theorems actually checked and their scopes. The fixed-height deduction uses d89's minimality, mirror-stable smooth branch, injective forgetful tangent, rationality and rank lower bound, not its upper rank bound or D(r). [D115 review](../../illumination-generalization-2026-11-06/fable/directions-2026-11-09/d115-review-accum/review.md)

**1. The real branch really parametrizes physical rooms.** Near the seed choose a marked period chart on the labeled quadratic double, using the orientation cover and its prescribed sign data. Complex conjugation together with the mirror and exchange of the two source labels and the two target labels gives a real structure. The physical parameter map sends signed horizontal/vertical wall lengths and the two marks to periods. It is real linear with rational coefficients after fixing homology paths: copy transitions change signs, and edge and endpoint displacements are differences of coordinates. The map is injective on its tangent space: periods of individual walls recover their signed lengths on chosen lifts, and relative periods to the marked points recover their two coordinates, with the initial vertex fixed. Regular preimages of convex corners are retained in this description as marked branch points of the orientation cover.

Here is the dimension check needed for the converse local identification. The physical unmarked edge parameters have W−2=2r+2 real degrees of freedom (two closure equations); the two interior marks add four. The labeled genus-zero quadratic stratum has complex dimension 2r+2, and its four distinct regular labels add four. Its real fixed germ at the seed has real dimension 2r+6. Thus the injective physical parameter map is locally open in that fixed germ. Strict simplicity and interiority persist. One must allow nonincident parallel walls initially at equal coordinates to separate; freezing their weak order at such a tie could remove physical tangent directions. It follows that the smooth N-fixed branch gives an open subset of a **rational real linear plane E of dimension 2k** in physical coordinates. Rationality uses d89's rational period equations and the rational physical parameter map. Every configuration there has all four lifted pairs dark and hence the physical pair dark.

**2. There is a deformation on one axis that changes shape.** Split physical variables into horizontal and vertical wall and mark coordinates. The plane E splits into its pure horizontal and pure vertical parts: small positive diagonal stretches preserve the labeled dark locus N by GL invariance and keep physical tuples physical. At every point of an open subset of E, differentiating a horizontal stretch gives its horizontal projection in E. By linearity the horizontal projection preserves E; subtraction gives the vertical projection. The two pure radial stretches account for only two dimensions. Since 2k≥4, on at least one axis there is a rational vector v independent of the stretch on that axis. Its **wall** component is not proportional to the seed's wall vector: otherwise subtract that multiple of the radial stretch to get a nonzero mark-only tangent, contrary to d89's zero kernel. Rotate the room if necessary and call this the horizontal axis. Clear denominators of v, including the two horizontal mark velocities. For all sufficiently small |t| the room z(t)=z₀+t v is physical, is dark, and has unchanged vertical walls and marked ordinates.

**3. Recover primitive lattice rooms without losing the pair.** Let e be the vector of signed horizontal wall lengths of the original integer seed, and u the integer horizontal wall part of v. We have gcd(e)=1. Choose two entries with nonzero minor M=eᵢuⱼ−eⱼuᵢ. Put D=2|M|. Given a small rational parameter p/q with q prime, q∤D, p a multiple of D and gcd(p,q)=1, stretch z(p/q) horizontally by q. Its horizontal wall vector is qe+pu; the mark coordinates transform by the same integral rule. The unchanged vertical lengths retain gcd one. If a prime divides all horizontal wall entries, taking minors with u and e makes it divide qM and pM, hence M. It then divides p and does not divide q, contradicting gcd(e)=1. So the new room is already primitive. The horizontal difference of the marks is q times their original even difference plus p times an integer, hence even. The vertical difference stays even.

Let ε>0 be a physical dark radius after clearing the denominators of v. Taking p=D and q→∞ gives normalized marked rooms tending to the arithmetic seed in bounding-box coordinates. More generally, around any |t| sufficiently small (restrict to |t|<min(ε,1/2)), choose p a multiple of D with p/q→t and 0<|p|<q; at zero use the previous choice. Thus every point of the line segment is a persistent limit. Areas grow like q times the positive area of z(t), while integer heights are fixed. At irrational t the ratio (eᵢ+t uᵢ)/(eⱼ+t uⱼ) is irrational by the nonzero minor. The orientation unfolding has horizontal absolute periods twice the two wall displacements (follow a wall and the reverse of its half-turn image). Two incommensurable parallel periods cannot lie in a torus period lattice. These are genuine non-torus limits even though their containing marked locus has rational defining equations. At large area the primitive rooms are BBBB by [d69 §1](../../illumination-generalization-2026-11-06/fable/directions-2026-11-09/d69-bbbb/README.md).

This proves a conditional equivalence: unbounded same-parity lattice darkness exists **if and only if** some normalized same-parity dark lattice seed admits a rational one-axis dark deformation with nonproportional wall motion and integral mark velocities after rescaling the parameter. The reverse implication is just the elementary minor argument. An isolated finite-area dark example would not supply that deformation. The reduction permits replacing the sequence; it does not classify every boundary subsequence of the original rooms.

**Continuation through the physical component.** Let I be the connected parameter interval containing the seed on which the labeled turn type persists, the polygon remains strictly simple with positive wall lengths, and both marks remain distinct and interior. In each marked period chart z(t) is affine (developed relative displacements are signed linear combinations of the physical coordinates). Start with the known open dark segment in N. If an endpoint of its maximal interval of continuation lay inside I, closedness of N would put its image in N. Near that point N is a finite union of linear sheets; one contains a sequence of distinct approaching parameters from the known interval. An affine equation vanishing on such a sequence vanishes identically on the line in that chart. Hence this sheet extends the interval through the endpoint, a contradiction. This proves z(I)⊂N⊂D₄ even across harmless changes of coordinate order. The proof uses an accumulating interval; mere closedness at an isolated point would give no continuation. No claim is made at an invalid endpoint of I.

## Status of the three kinds of limit

**(a) Degenerate shapes or limiting marks.** Two rectangular chambers with separating length L>4d light all real pairs even if the door shrinks. A fixed arbitrary junction and noncollapsing mouth with a sufficiently long cap also light all pairs. These results exclude sequences satisfying the actual inequalities or uniformity hypotheses. A disappearing indentation, several simultaneous pinches, changing junctions with collapsing doors, or boundary marks without a controlled excursion remain possible limits in the compactification. Regular coinciding interior marks are locally excluded by a chord. The replacement theorem means a proof of boundedness need not dispose of every possible original degeneration individually. [Rectangular theorem](resonant-gluing.md) · [Cap and notch cases](degree-gluing.md) · [Reflex limit](thick-thin.md)

**(b) Non-torus limits.** Lelièvre–Monteil–Weiss Theorem 2 gives finitely many hidden targets for a fixed source. It does not identify each target as an involution-fixed point. On a non-torus-cover translation surface Apisa–Wright Theorem 3.6 gives two alternatives for a finitely blocked pair: periodic points or zeros, or identification on the minimal quadratic quotient. The involution classification in Theorem 1.4 concerns the orientation doubles over specified full quadratic strata. A proper rank-2–5 orbit locus of the room double is exactly where d89 leaves periodic markings unclassified. The relevant physical condition includes both mirror lifts at each end and all forbidden corners. Kronecker excludes the documented rectangular cross-chamber configurations with compatible travels, but irrationality of two wall lengths elsewhere does not provide such a geometry. The theorem above produces non-torus persistent points with **all vertical levels fixed and integral**. Thus it suffices to rule out an entire dark interval of this restricted kind, rather than classify arbitrary non-arithmetic rooms. [LMW, Theorem 2](https://msp.org/gt/2016/20-3/gt-v20-n3-p11-s.pdf) · [Apisa–Wright, Theorems 1.4 and 3.6](https://public.websites.umich.edu/~alexmw/ApisaWright.pdf) · [Kronecker hypotheses](thick-thin.md)

**(c) Arithmetic interior limits.** Openness excludes a lit marked limit, including any interior limit covered by a whole-room all-real certificate. An exact grid refinement of a fixed marked room gives no growth after primitive normalization. Nearby arithmetic points can instead be transverse deformations: the construction qe+pu has growing primitive area while its projectivized shape approaches e, with the same fixed vertical coordinates. No single corridor or rigid retained chamber is required. Thus fixed denominator of the *limit* is compatible with unbounded denominators along the sequence. Conversely, a proof that an arithmetic dark seed admits no nontrivial one-axis dark interval would, with d89, exclude unbounded area even if the original sequence escaped exclusively through degenerations. This is the crux shared by (b) and (c).

## A lighting test on the deformation line

There is a direct attack on (b) once the line in (c) is available. Suppose the two marks are joined in height order by m consecutive full horizontal runs, each bounded by actual vertical walls on its open height range, with positive open overlaps at the intermediate levels. The vertical travels d₁,…,dₘ (partial travels in the end runs) are fixed positive rationals. Work on a subinterval of the deformation parameter on which this description persists. Let Pⱼ(t) be twice the width of run j, a positive rational affine function. If the Pⱼ are **pairwise nonproportional as functions**, the pair is lit at every transcendental t in that interval. For two runs every irrational t suffices. A single run gives a chord. Thus any dark deformation line having such a chain must have a repeated proportional-width class somewhere in the chain. This handles a resonant rational seed by lighting a different member of its required dark interval.

To prove it, use the [unfolded column placement formula](placement.md) with x and y exchanged. Put X=Σdⱼ and Aᵢ=Σ_{j≤i}dⱼ. The attainable crossing coordinates on the prescribed positive sheets form a translate of Gℤᵐ in ℝ^(m−1), where

    Gᵢⱼ = (Aᵢ/X − 1_{j≤i})Pⱼ(t).

Any point in the product of open overlaps gives a legal shot. Subtract consecutive prefix equations: G has full row rank and its real kernel is spanned by (dⱼ/Pⱼ(t))ⱼ. By the character criterion for closed subgroups of Euclidean space, Gℤᵐ fails to be dense exactly when some nonzero ξ has Gᵀξ integral. Since the image of Gᵀ is the orthogonal complement of the kernel, this is equivalent to an integer relation Σkⱼdⱼ/Pⱼ(t)=0 with k≠0. Equivalently one may take m−1 linearly independent generator columns as a lattice basis and apply the torus form of Kronecker to the last column.

For pairwise nonproportional rational affine Pⱼ, the reciprocal functions are rationally linearly independent: each nonconstant denominator has its own simple pole, and at most one denominator is constant. A nonzero integer relation therefore yields a nonzero rational polynomial on clearing denominators and cannot hold at transcendental t. For two runs the polynomial has degree at most one. Density now gives an exact lattice point in the open overlap product. No bound on the required windings is needed. The hypothesis is geometric: endpoints at the same height, turns with no monotone chain, and runs whose widths change proportionally are not excluded. In particular this is not a proof that every one-axis dark line is impossible.

## What would have to be counted

No upper bound on primitive area has been proved. Even qualitative exclusion of all unbounded sequences gives an existential finite maximum, not a value to feed a census. If an effective bound N were supplied, each primitive cell room of area ≤N has width and height ≤N. At W=2m≤20 use ≤m vertical and ≤m horizontal supporting lines, m^(2m) possible weak-rank assignments before filtering, and at most 2m−2 positive coordinate gaps of size ≤N. This gives the deliberately loose room count Σ_{m=2}^{10}m^(2m)N^(2m−2), followed by the actual simple-room, primitive and BBBB filters. Each room has at most N interior lattice sites (assign the upper-right incident unit cell to each), so at most N(N−1)/2 unordered pairs before parity filtering. Arbitrary real marks require a separate continuum certificate. The fixed-height replacement supplies neither a universal height nor N; no compute run is justified by this estimate.

**Next theorem target.** With all horizontal wall positions and both marked abscissas affine in one parameter, and all ordinates fixed integers, prove that at least one parameter in any nonradial rational interval lights the marked pair. One admissible parameter suffices to contradict an interval of darkness. The remaining issue is the existence of that witness on a line constrained by the marked orbit locus, not the existence of a limit.
