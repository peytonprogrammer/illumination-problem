# Which folds can reach the d64 enumeration?

For distinct same-parity interior lattice marks, d64 applies as soon as **any** class has a proper canonical factor. A geometric fold recovered from affine point blocks need not have this property. Even two complete unramified fibers containing both physical mirror pairs can occur at unbounded area with all four canonical factors trivial. In the smaller subcase with primitive sheet monodromy one can exclude the entire projective-derivative-kernel explanation for bounded same-parity suborbits. The implication from physical darkness to a canonical factor remains open.

## The extra condition on a geometric fold

Write π:Q→B for the pillowcase map, n for its degree, g_x for inertia at pole x, and Π for a sheet partition defining a proper intermediate cover Q→N→B. A regular point over x is a two-cycle upstairs. The canonical partition B_x is the finest monodromy-invariant partition identifying the two entries of **every** such cycle. Thus the following are equivalent:

1. B_x refines Π, allowing equality.
2. Every two-cycle of g_x has both sheets in one Π-block.
3. N has no regular point over x.

Indeed a regular point on N is a two-cycle of the induced permutation on blocks. Each sheet above it has local degree divisible by two over B, hence exactly two since the only upstairs degrees are 1, 2 and 3. Its cycle crosses between those two blocks. Conversely a two-cycle crossing blocks induces precisely such a regular point. If any upstairs two-cycle exists over x, B_x is nondiscrete; if Π is proper and (2) holds, B_x is also nonuniversal. This puts the room in d64 even if N is smaller than N_x. Recognition from the two source-class fibers in d93 supplies no such condition at any other parity. There is a quantitative obstruction. Let k=deg(Q→N), m=n/k and z be the number of zeros of N. There are z+4 simple poles on the sphere N. Each zero consumes k distinct reflex preimages, so kz≤r. At any base pole x, writing p_x,z_x,u_x for the counts of poles, zeros and regular points on N gives m=p_x+3z_x+2u_x. Exactly k u_x regular upstairs points cross Π-blocks there. Consequently at **each** parity at least max(0,(n−4r−4k)/2) upstairs transpositions cross. This counts points separately for the specified Π. Corrected normalization excludes intermediate pillowcases, whence z≥1 and k≤r. In particular at n>8r every proper intermediate cover has crossing transpositions at every parity, recovering d63's size barrier and quantifying its failure of (2). ## A fold satisfying the two-fiber hypothesis in the BBBB core

Use the previously studied odd cross with b≥7, r=4, n=16b−16. The projective derivative kernel K has order four, the positive lifts of the physical sign changes. Each acts over the identity of B. Indeed its derivative on the double is ±I, and its physical action preserves lattice parity. In the base model (R²/2Z²)/± the induced map is translation by a two-torsion point; fixing even one pole makes that translation zero. Thus Q→Q/K→B is a geometric factor. The quotient map has degree four.

For S=(6,0) and T=(0,6), the two K-orbits are disjoint and contain both mirror lifts of their respective mark. Each contains four distinct points: the axial reflection fixing the physical mark exchanges its halves, and the half-turn sends it to a different physical point. Thus the quotient images are distinct regular points, each with a full unramified fiber. All these points lie over the same parity. Normality of K in the affine group makes the K-orbits an invariant point partition (restriction to an individual affine orbit gives blocks). This gives exactly the geometric two-fiber property without any d64 factor: the degree-four quotient is proper, n>8r, and every parity has regular upstairs points in the central square. Therefore every B_x is universal. This argument uses the K-orbits; it does not assert cubic dark orbitals in the cross. The seed pair is lit. It refutes the implication from this geometric configuration alone, leaving open an additional implication using darkness. ## The case of no proper intermediate cover at all

Suppose n>2 and sheet monodromy is primitive. The subgroup of K preserving a specified parity is trivial. Indeed the induced action of K on the four poles is by translations as above, so fixing one fixes the entire base pointwise. A nonidentity such kernel element is an involution by d91. Its quotient would be an intermediate map of degrees 2 and n/2 (or the involution is already impossible if n is odd). This contradicts primitivity. For n>3 all deck transformations are trivial: on the sheets a nonidentity centralizing permutation acts freely, and its cyclic orbits are blocks; if it is transitive its centralizer is cyclic, forcing primitive cyclic monodromy of prime degree. In the latter case nonidentity branch permutations are full prime cycles, impossible for n>3 since local degrees are at most three. The involution argument suffices for all assertions about K, including n=3.

Two consequences for an interior lattice source:

* An axial reflection through the mark preserves its parity and would lift to a nonidentity element of K. Thus no lattice mark in a primitive room of degree >2 lies on a horizontal or vertical physical symmetry axis. No kernel element carries a lift to a different lift of the same parity, including its own mirror.
* For the pure affine group P fixing the four base pole labels, the projective derivative is faithful. Indeed affine maps descend to B by the integral relative periods and integral corner images; the induced map on its poles is the derivative modulo 2 followed by a translation. Its triviality gives derivative in Γ(2) modulo ±I, and trivial projective derivative in P is the just-excluded kernel. In particular, if a pure affine element sends one lift to the other, it must have a nontrivial derivative.

For clarity the decisive index in P is [P_s:P_s∩ιP_sι], the pure mirror suborbit, since ι normalizes P. It is at most the full mirror suborbit, hence bounded at both ends under physical darkness. Even index one only says the two **point stabilizer subgroups** agree. It gives an equivariant matching of their affine orbits. It gives no automorphism over B or monodromy-invariant relation on sheets. In fact the latter interpretation as fibers of size k, 1<k<n, is impossible in the primitive case: every attempted d93 closure merging two distinct sheets generates the universal partition. This is the first failing step even after the axis explanation has been eliminated. It does not contradict a bounded index in P.

**Remaining geometric question.** In the primitive branch, can a bounded-index mirror matching (or higher-degree affine relation) persist at both ends of a physically dark four-lift pair when every sheet identification propagates to all sheets? One needs an implication using straight paths, for example that such a matching, when realized by two common hidden neighbors, continues across the four base pole loops as a proper sheet relation. Distinct states of d61 cannot simply be concatenated. In the imprimitive BBBB branch a continuation proof would further need to force the collapse of every two-cycle at one parity, or prove illumination by another argument. The cross separates these obligations. No new room, direction, word, or ray search was used.
