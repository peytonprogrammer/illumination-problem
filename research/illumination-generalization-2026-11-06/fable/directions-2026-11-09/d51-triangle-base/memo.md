# d51: fold certificates over the (2,4,4) triangle base

Fable subagent, 2026-11-15. Author-proved in one session. No referee yet.

Tags: **PROVED** (argument written here), **CITED** (with the source),
**GUESSED** (estimate or belief, not proved). "Ran" marks the one script.

## Verdict

**(c), with a narrow theorem (a') inside it.**

- Par and Rig extend to the (2,4,4) base `O_h` with sound proofs
  (Theorem U45). Cut doesn't: no sound Cut over a three-cone-point base is
  established here either.
- Riemann-Hurwitz gives a sharp inequality: `t_X <= 2 delta - 2`. Here
  `t_X` counts regular points of `DR` over the certificate's ramified class
  `X`, and `delta` is the degree of `DR` over the certificate factor. All
  named dark rooms meet it with equality.
- For normalized rooms, `delta` is bounded only by corner counts. The
  resulting tile bound at `W <= 21` is `N <= 160` (A/B pairs, different-class
  pairs) and `N <= 282` (C pairs). The d11 census stops at 32 and 24. The
  gap is a factor of 5 and 12.
- Theorem (a') closes two subcases with the census: Tokarsky-type rooms
  (`k = 0`) at every `W <= 23`, and A/B pairs with Par at both targets and
  `delta_B + delta_C <= 4` at `W <= 21`.
- The exact obstruction is section 5.2. A finite check exists (section 5.3)
  but is out of reach (estimate at least `10^15` rooms, GUESSED).

## 0. Conventions

- `R` is a strictly simple lattice room with axis-parallel and 45-degree
  walls. `W` is its number of corners. `Q = DR` is its double.
- A corner of interior angle `k pi/4` has `k` in `{1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 7}`.
- `T_h` is the triangle `(0,0) (1,0) (1,1)`, legs 1. Its reflection group
  `W_h` has mirrors `x in Z`, `y in Z`, `x +- y in 2Z`. Its chambers are
  d11's canonical45 tiles. `N` is the number of tiles of `R`.
- Lattice classes, as in d11: A = (even, even), B = (odd, odd), C = mixed.
- `O_h = R^2 / W_h^+ = D(T_h)`. A and B are its order-4 cone points
  (angle `pi/2`). C is its order-2 point (angle `pi`).
- `o_X` is the order of class `X`: 4 for A and B, 2 for C.

## 1. Setup

### 1.1 Cone angles of DR (PROVED)

A corner of angle `k pi/4` becomes a cone point of angle `k pi/2`.

| Corner angle | 45 | 90 | 135 | 225 | 270 | 315 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cone angle in `Q` | `pi/2` | `pi` | `3pi/2` | `5pi/2` | `3pi` | `7pi/2` |
| Curvature in units of `pi/2` | 3 | 2 | 1 | -1 | -2 | -3 |

Interior points and flat boundary points are regular (`2 pi`). The
curvatures sum to 8, which is the exterior-angle sum of `R`.

### 1.2 Holonomy (PROVED)

Products of two wall reflections give the holonomy of `Q`.

- Walls in both families (axis and diagonal): holonomy `Z/4`. Call `R`
  **mixed**.
- Only axis walls: holonomy `+-1`. This is the orthogonal theory.
- Only diagonal walls: holonomy `+-1`. All vertices then lie in one coset of
  `D = {x + y even}`, so `R` is an orthogonal room on the rotated lattice
  `D`. Lattice points of the other coset are square centres there.

The rest of this memo treats mixed rooms.

### 1.3 Which bases (PROVED)

A base is a closed flat orientable orbifold `O` with a branched local
isometry `Q -> O`.

1. The holonomy of `Q` embeds in that of `O`. Flat orbifolds have holonomy
   `1`, `Z/2`, `Z/3`, `Z/4` or `Z/6`. Only `Z/4` contains `Z/4`. So `O` is
   a (2,4,4) orbifold `R^2 / Gamma` with `Gamma` of type p4.
2. The front face `R` is simply connected, so its map to `O` lifts to an
   isometry into `R^2`. Normalize it to the identity. The back face then
   has chart `rho_e` modulo `Gamma` near each wall `e`. So
   `rho_e rho_f` lies in `Gamma` for all walls `e`, `f`. Hence
   `W_R^+ <= Gamma`, where `W_R` is generated by the wall reflections.
3. Conversely, every p4 group `Gamma >= W_R^+` gives a base.

`W_R` is a discrete reflection group with point group `D_4`, so it is the
affine group of a 45-45-90 triangle `T_R`. The walls of `R` are mirrors of
`W_R`, so `R` is a union of copies of `T_R`. The finest base is
`O_R = D(T_R)`.

Lattice rooms come in two types.

- **Type h.** All diagonal walls lie on lines `x +- y = c` with one parity
  of `c`. After a translation `R` is a union of canonical45 tiles.
- **Type q.** Both parities occur. Then `R` is a union of quarter-squares,
  and `z -> (1+i) z` maps it to a canonical45 room. Lattice marks go to A
  and B points.

So every mixed lattice room is, up to similarity, a canonical45 room with
marks at A, B or C points. This is the d11 census class (CITED,
`d11-census-45/README.md`, "Tiling and room definition").

Rotation centres of `Gamma = <L, J>`, with `L = Z u + Z iu` and `J` a
quarter turn about `c`: order 4 at `c + L` and `c + (u + iu)/2 + L`; order 2
at `c + u/2 + L` and `c + iu/2 + L`. For `O_h`, `L = 2 Z^2` and `c = 0`.

### 1.4 Normalized (definition)

`R` is **normalized** when `W_R = W_h`, that is, `T_R = T_h`. Equivalently,
the translation subgroup of `W_R` is `2 Z^2`.

- A non-normalized room is a union of copies of a larger triangle. The
  similarity `T_R -> T_h` maps it to a normalized room, and lattice marks
  go to rational marks. This is the analogue of d47's rational marks.
- Lemma Z below is the analogue of Proposition R1.

**Lemma Z (PROVED).** Let `R` be normalized and let `M` be a factor of
`Q -> O_h` with `M != O_h`. Then `M` has a **rigid point**: a point over A
or B with local degree `e_M` in `{3, 5, 6, 7}`, or a point over C with
`e_M = 3`.

Proof. Suppose not. Local degrees over A and B lie in `{1, 2, 4}` and over
C in `{1, 2}`. Every cone angle of `M` is then `2 pi / j`, so `M` is a good
flat orbifold `R^2 / Gamma'`. It covers `O_h` as an orbifold, so `Gamma'` is
conjugate into `W_h^+`. The lifting argument of 1.3, applied to `Q -> M`,
gives `W_R^+ <= Gamma' <= W_h^+`. Normalization forces equality, so
`deg(M -> O_h) = 1`.

`e_M <= 7` because a point of `Q` has `e_Q <= 7` and `e_Q = e' e_M`
(Fact L). Over C, `e_Q <= 3`.

## 2. Certificates

### 2.1 Sheets and monodromy (CITED, d11 "N_min and k")

Sheets are tiles. `kappa_E` crosses the tile edge of type `E` (AB, AC, BC)
and fixes the tile at a wall. `g_A = kappa_AC kappa_AB`,
`g_B = kappa_BC kappa_AB`, `g_C = kappa_AC kappa_BC`. Points of `Q` over
class `X` are the cycles of `g_X`; the local degree is the cycle length.
An interior lattice point gives two cycles of length `o_X`. A flat boundary
point gives one. A corner gives one cycle of length `k` (A, B) or `k/2` (C).

For a factor `M = Q / Pi`: `delta = deg(Q -> M)`, `d = deg(M -> O_h)`,
`N = delta d`. By Fact L, `e_Q = e' e_M` with `e'` the local degree of
`Q -> M`.

A point `p` of `M` over `X` is **regular** when `e_M(p) = o_X`. It is
**live** when some regular point of `Q` lies over it.

### 2.2 Light paths and saddle connections (PROVED)

A light path from `sigma` passes only regular points of `Q`. Its image in
`O_h` is a geodesic from `c_s` that reverses at every cone point. So it runs
back and forth along one saddle connection `gamma` from `c_s` to a cone
point `x`.

- **No loops at order-4 points.** A lift of a saddle connection from an
  order-4 centre to its own orbit has a cone point at its midpoint (1.3
  list). So from A the targets are B and C. From B they are A and C.
- **Loops at the order-2 point exist.** From `(1,0)` in direction `(1,1)`
  the first lattice point is `(2,1)`, again class C.
- Saddle connections in `O_h` need not be simple. In the pillowcase `P_L`
  of 2.5 a saddle connection in direction `(p, q)` is half of a simple
  closed torus geodesic, and `-1` maps it to the other half, so it is
  simple. In `O_h` it is identified with its quarter-turn image, direction
  `(-q, p)`. On the torus `R^2 / 2Z^2` those two closed geodesics meet
  `p^2 + q^2` times, so for `pq != 0` the image in `O_h` crosses itself.

### 2.3 Transit lemma (PROVED)

Let `q` be regular in `Q`, over `p` in `M`, over a cone point of order `o`.
The two germs of `gamma`-lifts at `q` in opposite directions map to opposite
germs at `p` if `p` is regular. They map to the same germ if `p` is not.

Proof. `e' = o / e_M`, and `Q -> M` is `theta -> theta mod 2 pi / e'` near
`q`. Opposite germs differ by `pi`, which is `0 mod 2 pi / e'` exactly when
`e'` is even. With `o = 4`, `e_M` is in `{1, 2, 4}`; with `o = 2`, in
`{1, 2}`. So `e'` is even exactly when `p` is not regular.

In sheet terms, the lift of the base edge `c_s x` labelled by sheet `beta`
joins the `g_{c_s}`-cycle and the `g_x`-cycle of `beta`. The opposite germ
at a regular point of order `o` is `g^{o/2} beta`.

**Trails.** An M-trail follows lifts of `gamma`. At a regular vertex it
continues on the opposite germ. At a live non-regular vertex it returns on
the same edge. At a dead vertex it stops. `K_M(gamma, u)` is the set of
vertices over `c_s` that M-trails from `u` reach.

**Lemma 1' (projection, PROVED).** The image of a light trail of `Q` from
`sigma` is an M-trail from `phi(sigma)`. So `phi` maps the light set of
`sigma` into `K_M(gamma, phi(sigma))`. This follows from 2.3, and it needs
neither genus 0 nor simple arcs.

### 2.4 Definitions and Theorem U45

Fix a lift pair `(sigma, tau)` over `c_s`, `c_t`. A factor `M` gives:

- **Par(x).** `c_s = c_t`, `x != c_s`, `phi(sigma) != phi(tau)`, and `M`
  has no regular point over `x`.
- **Rig(x).** `x != c_s`. `y` is the third cone point, `y` not in
  `{c_s, x}`. `M -> O_h` has identity monodromy at `y`, and `phi(tau)` is
  outside `K_M(gamma_0, phi(sigma))` for the base edge `gamma_0 = c_s x`.

Targets: for `c_s = c_t` in {A, B}, the two other cone points. For
`c_s = c_t = C`, the points A and B and the **loop target** at C. For
`c_s != c_t`, the point `c_t` only.

**Theorem U45 (PROVED).** If every lift pair has a certificate at every
target, the pair is dark. For the loop target, the certificate is a
Theorem U certificate on the pillowcase lift (2.5).

Proof.

- Par. `M` has no regular vertex over `x`, so every M-trail returns from `x`
  along the edge it came on. The only vertex over `c_s` that it reaches is
  `phi(sigma)`. Lemma 1' finishes.
- Rig. `M` is unbranched over `y`, so `M` minus the fibres over `c_s` and `x`
  covers the sphere minus two points. Blow those up to boundary circles: an
  annulus, where all arcs between the two boundaries are homotopic with
  ends sliding. Sliding an end rotates every germ at that end by the same
  step. Opposite-germ pairing survives a uniform rotation. So the labelled
  trail structure is the same for every arc, simple or not. Lemma 1'
  finishes.

Soundness doesn't use genus 0, so it also holds for the slit room and for
weakly simple rooms.

### 2.5 C pairs and the loop target (PROVED)

`P_L = R^2 / <2 Z^2, -1>` is the unit pillowcase B. It double covers `O_h`.
A and B have one preimage each (local degree 2). C splits into the corners
`m1 = (1,0)` and `m2 = (0,1)`. A loop at C lifts to a saddle connection
`m1 -> m2`.

Let `Q' = Q x_{O_h} P_L`. It is connected because the holonomy of `Q` maps
onto `Z/4 / {+-1}`. `Q' -> Q` has degree 2. It branches at the corners with
odd `k`, so `Q'` has positive genus in general.

A C mark has two lifts in `Q`. Each splits into lifts over `m1` and `m2`. A
loop light path from `sigma` lifts to a path from `sigma1` (over `m1`) to
`tau1` or `tau2`. So the loop target is certified when Theorem U gives a
certificate over `P_L` at target `m2` for both:

- `(sigma1, tau1)`, same corner: Par or Rig.
- `(sigma1, tau2)`, different corners: Rig with identity at the A-corner or
  the B-corner.

d10's proofs of Par and Rig never use the genus of `Q` (Fact L, Lemma 1,
isotopy in the base). So they apply to `Q' -> P_L` (PROVED, by reading
d10 section 2.2).

**Consequence (PROVED).** Par over `O_h` at targets A and B doesn't certify
a C pair. This explains d11's finding that `N_min` separates about 14.9
million lit C pairs (159,403 + 14,770,425; CITED, d11 answer (d)). `N_min` is the join `B_A v B_B`. It
says nothing about the loop target.

### 2.6 Cut (decided: no sound Cut over O_h here)

d10's Cut proof uses the arc between the "other two corners". `O_h` has only
one other point, `y`.

- **Tree lemma (PROVED).** Suppose `M -> O_h` has a single cycle at `y` and
  `gamma` is simple. Then `O_h - gamma` is a disc containing `y`, and its
  preimage in `M` is one disc. So `Gamma_gamma(M)` is a tree. Under the Cut
  hypothesis (every regular point of `Q` over `c_s` and `x` maps to a regular
  point), every vertex an M-trail meets is regular. M-trails are then
  non-backtracking walks in a tree. They never revisit `phi(sigma)`, so
  `phi(tau) = phi(sigma)` with `tau != sigma` is unreachable.
- **Gap.** Saddle connections of `O_h` are not simple in general (2.2). For a
  non-simple arc, the lift graph is defined by a conjugated monodromy and
  can be disconnected with a cycle. Single-cycle monodromy at `y` doesn't
  make homotopies across `y` harmless.

So a Cut over `O_h` is sound only for simple saddle connections. That isn't
a certificate. d48's statement stands: nothing in the programme defines a
sound Cut over a three-cone-point base. Theorem U's Cut over `P_L` applies
to `Q'` when the factor `N'` has genus 0 (d10's proof needs `N'` to be a
sphere). The area bound below covers Par and Rig only.

### 2.7 Canonical partitions (PROVED; R3 analogue)

- `B_x` is the finest block system containing `{s, g_x^2 s}` for every
  `s` in a 4-cycle of `g_x` (`o_x = 4`), or `{s, g_C s}` for every `s` in a
  2-cycle of `g_C`.
  - A point of `M` over `x` with `e_M = o_x` comes only from a regular
    point of `Q` with `e' = 1`, because `o_x` divides no other `e_Q`.
  - So `M` has no regular point over `x` exactly when `Pi >= B_x`. Par(x)
    holds for some factor exactly when `B_x` separates the lift pair.
- `R_y` is the finest block system merging every cycle of `g_y`. Rig(x)
  with identity at `y` holds for some factor exactly when it holds for
  `Q / R_y` (Lemma 1' applied to `Q / R_y -> M`).
- `B_x` and `R_y` are `kappa`-invariant. `kappa_E g_x kappa_E = g_x^{-1}`
  maps the generating pairs of `B_x` to generating pairs. The third `kappa`
  differs from these by an element of `G`. This is d10 R3(3) verbatim.
  `check51.py` confirms invariance on every partition it computed.

d11's `N_min` for an A pair is `B_B v B_C`, a common Par factor.
**Tokarsky's lemma** is Par with `M = Q`: when no B or C point is
transparent, `Q` itself has no regular point over B or C.

## 3. Area bound

### 3.1 Two identities (PROVED)

`t_X` is the number of regular points of `Q` over `X`: twice the interior
`X`-points plus the flat boundary `X`-points. `K_X` is the sum of `k` over
corners of class `X`. For C corners `k` counts tiles, which is 1 or 3.

- **Fibre identity.** `N = 4 t_A + K_A = 4 t_B + K_B = 2 t_C + K_C`. Each
  is `deg(Q -> O_h)` summed over one fibre.
- **Angle identity.** `K_A + K_B + 2 K_C = 4W - 8`. This is the
  exterior-angle sum.

Consequences:

- `N = t_A + t_B + t_C + W - 2`.
- For an A pair: `3N = 4 (t_B + t_C) + 4W - 8 - K_A`.
- For a C pair: `N = 2 (t_A + t_B) + 2W - 4 - K_C`.

### 3.2 Ramified class (PROVED)

Each certificate has a **ramified class** `X` whose regular points all
ramify in `Q -> M`:

| Certificate | `X` | Local degree `e'` at regular points over `X` | Bound |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Par(x) | `x` | 2 or 4 | `t_X <= 2 delta - 2` |
| Rig, identity at `y` of order 4 | `y` | 4 | `3 t_X <= 2 delta - 2` |
| Rig, identity at `y = C` | C | 2 | `t_X <= 2 delta - 2` |

Proof. `Q` and `M` are spheres, so `sum (e' - 1) = 2 delta - 2`. Each regular
point over `X` contributes at least `e' - 1`.

**Theorem B1 (PROVED; no normalization).**
`N <= o_X t_X + K_X <= o_X (2 delta - 2) + K_X`, with the Rig/order-4 row
divided by 3.

### 3.3 Bounding delta (PROVED for normalized rooms)

Every preimage of a rigid point is a corner of angle 135, 225, 270 or 315
degrees. At a 3-point (`e_M = 3`), `e'` is 1 or 2. At a zero, `e' = 1`. Let
`P` be the number of corners of these four angles, so `P <= W`. Let `nu`
be the number of rigid points (`nu >= 1` by Lemma Z).

Riemann-Hurwitz, with the rigid fibres counted separately, gives
`t_X + sum_p (delta - #pre(p)) <= 2 delta - 2`.

- `nu >= 2`: `t_X <= P - 2`.
- `nu = 1`: `delta <= 2 #pre(p)`, so `t_X <= 3P - 2`. If `p` is a zero,
  `t_X <= 2P - 2`.
- Rig with identity at an order-4 point: `3 t_X <= 3P - 2`.

**Theorem B2 (PROVED).** Let `R` be normalized, strictly simple, with a
lattice pair certified dark by Par and Rig over `O_h`. Then:

- A/B pairs and different-class pairs: `N <= 8W - 8`.
- C pairs (Par or Rig at both A and B): `N <= 14W - 12`.

Proof. Use `P <= W`, `K_C <= 2W - 4`, `K_A, K_B <= 4W - 8`. For an A pair,
every combination of certificates bounds `t_C <= 3P - 2` or
`t_B <= P - 1`. Then `N = 2 t_C + K_C <= 8W - 8`, or
`N = 4 t_B + K_B <= 8W - 12`. For a C pair, Par at both gives
`N = 2 (t_A + t_B) + 2W - 4 - K_C <= 12P - 12 + 2W <= 14W - 12`. Any Rig gives
`<= 8W - 12`. For different-class pairs, Rig gives `t_C <= 3P - 2` or
`t_B <= P - 1`.

**At W <= 21:** `N <= 160` tiles (A/B and different-class pairs) and
`N <= 282` tiles (C pairs). The census covers `N <= 32` and `N <= 24`.

### 3.4 Comparison with d36

d36 bounds `n <= delta (4z + 1)` with `delta z <= r`, so `n <= 5r`. The
pillowcase has only zeros as rigid points. Here 3-points (angle `3 pi/2`,
positive curvature) are also rigid. A zero-free factor can carry them:
the double of the quadrilateral `(0,0) (2,0) (2,1) (1,1)` has angles
`pi/2, pi, pi, 3pi/2`. Such factors allow `delta` up to `2P`. Porting the
d36 accounting (Gauss-Bonnet on `M`, `d <= 8 + 10 nu`) gives a worse bound,
`N <= 8 delta + 10 P`.

### 3.5 Theorem (a') (PROVED from CITED census)

The census used: d11, A/B pairs, every strictly simple canonical45 room
with `N <= 32` and `W <= 24`. Findings: all pairs at `W <= 21` are lit, and
no pair with `W <= 23` and `N <= 32` has `k = 0`.

1. **Tokarsky type.** No strictly simple lattice room with `W <= 23` has an
   A/B pair with `k = 0`. Proof: `t_B = t_C = 0` and 3.1 give
   `N = (4W - 8 - K_A)/3 <= 28`. At `W = 24` the bound is 29, so d11's 12
   clean rooms are all of them.
2. **Small-delta Par.** No strictly simple lattice room with `W <= 21` has
   an A/B pair certified by Par at both targets with `delta_B + delta_C <= 4`.
   Proof: 3.1 and 3.2 give `3N <= 8 (delta_B + delta_C) - 24 + 4W - K_A`,
   so `3N <= 92` and `N <= 30`. Soundness and the census give a
   contradiction. (`delta_B + delta_C = 5` needs `K_A >= 4` to reach 32.)

Neither needs normalization. Type q rooms enter through the map `(1+i)`
(1.3), which preserves `W`. Both are unreviewed.

Findings:

- **No named dark room is excluded.** Theorem B1 holds with equality on
  every certificate. On the 22-wall room's Par(C), `t_C = 4 = 2 delta - 2`.
  So the Riemann-Hurwitz step is sharp, and all the loss is in bounding
  `delta`.
- All four lift pairs separate in each listed case. Rig over `R_C` fails
  for the 22-wall room (`R_C` is a pillowcase, `d = 2`).
- The 22-wall room is orthogonal, so it isn't normalized in the 45-degree

  each (`nu = 3`, `P = 9`). The 3.3 bound gives `t_C <= 7`; the true value
  is 4.
- The slit tip is a C point with `e_Q = 4`. That breaks the `e_Q <= 3`
  assumption used in Lemma Z and 3.3, but not Theorem U45 or B1.
- Theorem B2 is not checked on these rooms as a hypothesis test, because
  none is both mixed-normalized and at `W <= 21`. The bound holds for
  them trivially.

## 5. Conclusion

### 5.1 What is proved

- Par and Rig over `O_h` are sound for every lattice mark type (U45), with
  the loop target handled on the pillowcase lift.
- Canonical partitions `B_x` and `R_y` exist and are fold-compatible.
- The tile bound `N <= 8W - 8` (A/B) or `14W - 12` (C) holds for certified
  dark normalized mixed rooms.
- Theorem (a') holds.

### 5.2 The obstruction (exact statement)

To bring Par/Rig-certified darkness at `W <= 21` into the d11 census, you
need, for an A/B pair:

    t_B + t_C <= 5 + K_A / 4,    equivalently N <= 32,

and for a C pair, `N <= 24`. The proved control is `t_X <= 2 delta - 2`,
which is sharp, with `delta <= 2P` from a single rigid point. Two features
block a better bound:

1. **Positive-curvature rigid points.** A factor with no zero can still be
   non-orbifold through `3 pi/2` points. A 3-point's preimages are 135- and
   270-degree corners, so `delta` scales with that corner count, not with
   the reflex budget.
2. **A single rigid point.** With `nu = 1`, Riemann-Hurwitz leaves
   `delta - #pre(p)` free. A lemma "`nu >= 2`" would give `t_X <= P - 2`
   and roughly halve the bound. It still wouldn't reach 32.

What would close it: a geometric lemma, in the style of d39's seam
argument, bounding `delta` for `kappa`-invariant factors. One such fact is
proved here: a `kappa`-invariant factor has at most one conjugate pair of
off-seam singular points. An off-seam point of `M` has only interior,
hence regular, preimages, each with `e' = o / e_M >= 2`. Its fibre costs
`delta (1 - e_M / o) >= delta / 2` of ramification. A conjugate pair costs
at least `delta`, so two pairs exceed `2 delta - 2`. This lemma was not
converted into a `delta` bound.

### 5.3 The finite check, if wanted (spec; not run)

## 6. Mixed scope: one statement for the target class

**Statement (combining CITED and PROVED parts).** Let `R` be a strictly
simple room on the square lattice, with axis-parallel and 45-degree walls,
`W <= 21`, and a pair of interior lattice points. Then the pair isn't
certified dark, except possibly in these residues:

- **Orthogonal (`W <= 20`, since `W` is even).** Certificates are Par, Cut
  and Rig over pillowcases `P_Lambda`. Covered by d46 together with
  d47-d50. Residues: D20, and non-lattice rational marks over index-2 and
  index-4 bases. Both are finite checks that weren't run (CITED,
  `d50-review-d49/review.md` section 7, via `STATE.md`).
- **All-diagonal.** These are orthogonal rooms on the lattice `D`. Marks in
  the vertex coset are lattice marks there and fall under the orthogonal
  result. Marks in the other coset are square centres, that is,
  half-integer marks over an even-index base. They fall in the
  non-lattice residue above (GUESSED that d49/d50's scope applies after
  rotating by 45 degrees; not checked).
- **Mixed, normalized.** Certificates are Par and Rig over `O_h` (this memo).
  Residue: `32 < N <= 160` for A/B and different-class pairs, `24 < N <= 282`
  for C pairs, minus Theorem (a').
- **Mixed, not normalized.** Lattice marks become rational marks of the
  normalized presentation. Lemma Z fails because orbifold factors
  `O_R -> O_h` exist. Untreated residue (analogue of d47's G1).
- **Mixed, Cut-type darkness.** No sound Cut over `O_h` is defined (2.6).
  Untreated.

Hypotheses, all of them:

1. Strictly simple, vertices on `Z^2`, walls in the 8 directions.
2. Marks are interior lattice points. Different-class pairs are included
   for mixed rooms.
3. "Certified" means certified by the listed certificate types. Conjecture
   U* (dark implies certified) is open in every setting.
4. Normalized, for the mixed tile bound.
5. The censuses are correct: d9, d11 (A/B to 32 tiles, C to 24), d46, d42.
6. The unreviewed d47-d50 scope claims hold as their referees amended them.
