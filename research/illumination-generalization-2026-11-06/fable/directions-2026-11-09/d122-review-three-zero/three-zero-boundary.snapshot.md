# Three zeros and the boundary of a rank-two locus

**Decision.** The three-zero classification remains open in this argument. A putative minimal example in the unmarked stratum Q(1³,−1⁷) must have rank two, relative dimension zero and field of definition ℚ. There is a concrete restriction on an isolated zero–pole collapse of such a locus. If its marked boundary divisor is cylinder rigid, the unmarked limiting surface must have just one orbit of cylinders under the orientation involution in *every* periodic direction. This restriction concerns a particular boundary type. It does not assert that every locus has that type.

## What Apisa's proof needs

Use the orientation cover, with its involution J and with regular Weierstrass points unmarked. For z simple zeros there are 2z+4 branch points, the genus is z+1, and the Abelian stratum is H(2^z). The quadratic sphere locus has dimension 2z+2 and no relative tangent kernel: all zeros upstairs are individually fixed by J, so the anti-invariant relative kernel vanishes. Its codimension in the Abelian stratum is z−1. This is the parameter in Apisa's proof, not the codimension of the unknown invariant subvariety in the sphere locus. [Apisa, Lemma 2.1 and §4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2110.07540)

At z=3 the high-rank theorem applies from rank three (genus four). On a minimal orientation surface the unique negating involution identifies the holonomy-double alternative with the given double, as in d119. A stratum component is too large. For the remaining rank two, Wright's field-degree bound gives degree at most two; equality makes it algebraically primitive. Apisa–Aulicino's classification places their only such genus-four example in H(6), whereas we are in H(2,2,2). Thus the rational-field restriction holds even without assuming d89. For the physical question, d89 already supplied rationality. Minimality matters here: d105 excludes a canonical corner-only factor and the BBBB cross has a noncanonical quotient. [High-rank theorem](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/2023/198-2/p04) · [Wright](https://arxiv.org/abs/1210.4806) · [Apisa–Aulicino, Theorems 1.1–1.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.10273) · [Minimal-quotient reduction](minimal-quotient-rigidity.md)

Apisa's Proposition 4.3 classifies positive-relative-dimension, rank-one cylinder-rigid loci in ambient hyperelliptic codimension at most one, with marked quotient data and a cylinder-preimage condition for later reconstruction. Here is a specific failure in extending its proof. Under Definition 2.6, cutting the J-exchanged pairs of parallel cylinders gives a tree with vertex weights kᵢ+1 summing to z. At z≤2 there is at most one edge. Lemma 2.12 uses the fact that the component on the specified side of a cut has codimension zero and at most one incident edge relation. This forces a *whole common boundary* for two exchanged cylinders meeting along a saddle connection under its full-support rel hypothesis. Lemma 4.4 uses that conclusion in the bordering case and uses the unique exchanged pair in the nonbordering analysis (Sublemma 4.5). At z=3 a path of three weight-one vertices is allowed. The middle piece has two incident edge relations in Lemma 2.8. One cut can leave a codimension-one piece. The one-edge inference no longer follows. Proposition 2.13 bounds boundary codimension by the original codimension and allows products; it does not give a strict drop. Apisa's §§5–6 use the covering and cylinder-preimage conclusions to reconstruct the interior. Merely proving the boundary is a torus cover would give less. [Apisa, §§2, 4–6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2110.07540)

## What the proposed induction actually produces

Take an isolated collision of one simple zero and one pole, with all other singularities distinct, nonzero limiting area, and connected normalization. Downstairs their orders sum to zero. Upstairs the normalization loses one handle and the regular collision position downstairs has a pair of J-exchanged lifts. Assume that retaining this one collision position gives a boundary divisor of the unmarked rank-two locus in the marked partial compactification. This is an explicit hypothesis on the degeneration.

The divisor has complex dimension three. Positive area forces rank at least one and dimension three forces rank at most one. Its relative dimension is therefore one. Forget the collision position. The unmarked lower sphere locus has zero relative kernel, so the image has dimension two: projection preserves absolute periods, and its kernel on the divisor is precisely the one relative dimension. In particular the collision point is locally free to move on a fixed unmarked surface. The connected-boundary tangent formula intersects the rational tangent with the annihilator of integral vanishing cycles; hence the surviving absolute plane is rational. Its integral period values lie in a rank-two lattice (choose a rational basis and clear denominators; positive area gives real independence of the two complex generators). Integration maps the normalization to the resulting torus. This proves a torus-cover conclusion for this boundary type at every z. At z=3 the unmarked normalization lies over Q(1²,−1⁶), but it has rank **one**, outside Theorem 1.5. [Mirzakhani–Wright, connected-boundary theorem](https://arxiv.org/abs/1508.01446)

**Free collision mark lemma (for review).** Suppose this marked boundary is cylinder rigid. In each periodic direction on its unmarked surface the maximal cylinders form one J-orbit.

*Proof.* Work on the labeled branch on the orientation double. The marking consists of p and Jp. Local freedom extends to generic positions in any chosen open cylinder on the fixed unmarked surface: the fiber of the closed marked locus in the connected regular position space (with fixed points removed) is analytic, and contains an open set, hence is the whole space there. Suppose there are two cylinder orbits. Place p at a generic small height y in a cylinder of height H in one orbit. A cylinder in the other orbit retains constant positive height. If J exchanges two cylinders in the chosen orbit, cutting their closed leaves at the marks produces cylinders of heights y and H−y. If J preserves the chosen cylinder, J exchanges its rims; the marked leaves cut it into heights y, H−2y, y. Circumferences stay fixed while y varies. Thus the constant height and the two distinct affine height functions are pairwise nonproportional on an open interval. They give three disjoint horizontal subequivalence classes in a cylinder-rigid locus: subequivalent cylinders have locally constant modulus ratios. But the number of disjoint subequivalence classes is at most rank plus rel, here two. Contradiction. This uses cylinder rigidity, not just complete periodicity. A single J-orbit consists of one fixed cylinder or two exchanged cylinders. [Apisa, proof of Lemma 3.4 and §4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2110.07540)

**Single missing lemma for this approach (open).** Every minimal rational rank-two locus in Q(1³,−1⁷) admits a cylinder-rigid boundary divisor formed by one isolated zero–pole collision whose unmarked normalization has a periodic direction with at least two J-orbits of cylinders.

The free collision mark lemma would exclude such a locus immediately. The missing lemma has two checkable geometric requirements on the *same* degeneration. An abstract surgery in the ambient stratum need not remain in the locus. A disconnected collapse, a simultaneous collision, or a branch marking several related positions can evade this argument. No implication from an expected dimension of 4−z to the existence of this divisor is known here. That count would need an upper bound on the dimension of every incidence component, including components with dependent equations.

## Named controls

The saved horizontal direction already tests the cylinder obstruction without a direction sweep. In a four-copy orientation unfolding, J maps a horizontal orbit to one with the same physical height and the same physical run. Thus two maximal cylinders separated by genuine corner leaves and projecting to different runs cannot be J partners. Test the hypothetical *free conjugate marking over the closed orbit* of each named square-tiled surface. These are controls for the lemma, not proposed normalizations in the three-zero stratum.

The existing controls verify the second requirement in concrete cylinder geometries; none establishes the first requirement for an unknown minimal locus. **Next move.** Determine whether a rank-two locus in the three-zero stratum must admit a connected degeneration with just one conjugate regular marking. This is the point at which the new obstruction could be applied. Without it, neither three zeros nor the induction through eight zeros is closed.
