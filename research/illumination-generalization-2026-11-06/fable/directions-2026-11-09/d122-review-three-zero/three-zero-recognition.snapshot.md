# Two matchings that force the three-sheet cover

**Conditional theorem for review.** On a rational rank-two locus in Q(1³,−1⁷), two synchronized matchings that form an embedded alternating hexagon force a degree-three unramified cover on the orientation double. This gives a sufficient topological configuration for reconstructing the cover after several arcs collapse together. The open step is to produce that configuration on a hypothetical minimal locus. No regular endpoint markings enter the theorem.

## Hypothesis in homology

Work at a smooth generic surface on the unmarked locus. Write X for the genus-four orientation cover, J for its involution, A for the abelian-surface quotient of Jac(X) defined by the **saturated** rational rank-four absolute subsystem, and j for X → Jac(X) → A based at a Weierstrass point. Thus j generates A, the map on integral first homology is onto, and ω is pulled back from a one-form α on A. These are the unmarked inputs of [the rank-two reduction](rank-two-periodic.md). Every Weierstrass point maps into A[2].

On the sphere distinguish the three zeros Z₁,Z₂,Z₃ and three poles P₁,P₂,P₃. Use the same labels for their unique lifts. Suppose there are two triples of simple arcs

    aᵢ : Zᵢ → Pᵢ,            bᵢ : Zᵢ → Pᵢ₊₁       (indices modulo three).

Their six interiors are disjoint from one another and from the branch points. For each arc c choose one lift γ from endpoint to endpoint. Its double δ(c)=γ−Jγ is an integral closed cycle, defined up to overall sign. **Synchronization** means that signs can be chosen such that all three j₊δ(aᵢ) agree in H₁(A,ℤ), and all three j₊δ(bᵢ) agree there; the two common values may differ. Here j₊ denotes the homology pushforward. Equivalently the cycles in each triple, after those signs, evaluate equally on the entire absolute tangent space. This is stronger than simultaneous vanishing on one boundary divisor: proportional restrictions might have different coefficients. The arcs can be topological, so they need not be saddle connections on the same flat surface.

## Why the two triples suffice

**1. The zeros have the same image.** In the Betti description of A[2], the image difference between the endpoints of c is j₊δ(c) modulo two, using the identification λ mod 2 ↦ λ/2 mod the integral period lattice. This follows by integrating anti-invariant forms on γ−Jγ. Write zᵢ=j(Zᵢ), pᵢ=j(Pᵢ). Thus zᵢ+pᵢ=c and zᵢ+pᵢ₊₁=d for fixed c,d in A[2]. Adding the three equations pᵢ+pᵢ₊₁=c+d gives c+d=0. All three pᵢ coincide, and all three zᵢ coincide.

**2. The intersection form bounds the image curve.** In H=H₁(X,ℤ) put K=ker j₊. It is primitive of rank four. The two consecutive differences of the a-cycles and the two consecutive differences of the b-cycles all lie in K. Within either triple the intersection numbers vanish. Let Cᵢⱼ be the oriented intersection of δ(aᵢ) and δ(bⱼ). It is ±1 at a common endpoint and zero otherwise. Indeed lifting disjoint incident arcs through the simple branch coordinate u↦u² produces two curves crossing once; the arcs may be straightened near the endpoint. The support of C is a six-cycle. Every 2-by-2 minor is ±1: each corresponding two-row, two-column subgraph has exactly one perfect matching.

Let E have columns (1,−1,0) and (0,1,−1). The four differences have skew intersection form with off-diagonal block F=EᵀCE. By the minors formula det F is a sum of nine terms, each ±1 (all maximal minors of E are ±1). Thus 1≤|det F|≤9. As a separate arithmetic check, evaluating the 64 possible sign arrays gives absolute determinants 1 (30 arrays), 3 (26 arrays), 5 (6 arrays), 9 (2 arrays). In particular the differences span a finite-index sublattice of K. The absolute Pfaffian of K is at most 9: passing from the full lattice to a sublattice multiplies its Pfaffian by the index.

Let D=j₊[X] be the curve cycle, with its mapping multiplicity. Then D²/2 equals the absolute Pfaffian of K. Here are the integral details. Poincaré duality identifies j*H¹(A,ℤ) with K-perpendicular in H because j₊ is onto. Primitivity and unimodularity identify the discriminant orders of K and its perpendicular: H/(K⊕K-perpendicular) maps isomorphically to either discriminant group by pairing and restriction. On H¹(A,ℤ) the restricted cup form is (u,v) ↦ ∫_D u∧v. In an oriented integral basis of the four-torus its absolute Pfaffian is |D²|/2 by exterior algebra. The generating effective cycle has positive square. Consequently D²/2≤9, independently of the degree of a square tiling.

**3. A birational image would need more genus.** Suppose j is birational onto its image B. Then D=B and adjunction on A gives pₐ(B)=1+B²/2≤10. Its normalization X has genus four, so the total singularity contribution is at most six. Yet the three distinct zeros found in step 1 give three branches at one point with pairwise intersection multiplicity at least three. To see this take linear local coordinates (x,y) on A with dy=α. At each zero, y−y₀ has order three. In an odd parameter at a Weierstrass point both centered coordinates are odd, since j intertwines J and negation. A singular branch thus has multiplicity at least three. A smooth branch has x as parameter and its graph agrees with y=y₀ through order two. Either way each pair contributes at least three. The delta invariant of a reduced plane curve germ is the sum of the individual branch deltas and the pairwise intersection multiplicities (successively separate the branches in the normalization exact sequence). The one point contributes at least nine, a contradiction. [Adjunction and normalization genus formula](https://mreid.warwick.ac.uk/surf/Reid_Park_City_chapters.pdf)

**4. The cover has degree three.** Write Y for the normalization of the image and k for the degree X→Y. Its genus is at least two because the image generates A. Riemann–Hurwitz and k≥2 force genus(Y)=2 and k≤3. The induced map Jac(Y)→A is an isomorphism: both have dimension two and the map is onto on integral homology since its composite from X is onto. Thus Y embeds in A up to translation. All three zeros lie in a single fiber of X→Y, forcing k≥3. Riemann–Hurwitz now gives zero ramification. The pulled-back form is ω, so this is a flat cover. The hyperelliptic involutions intertwine by negation on A, giving the predicted downstairs degree-three quadratic cover. In particular the actual Pfaffian is nine (pullback multiplies the genus-two cup form by three). [Downstairs ramification profile](three-zero-divisor.md)

## What this changes for the Apisa extension

The proof reconstructs the cover from signed integral period relations and intersection numbers; no labeling of all sheets around the remaining poles is required. It also gives a diagnostic: if a proposed pair of coupled collapses yields |det F|<9 under these hypotheses, that configuration cannot occur. The argument is fiberwise, and fixed homology relations on the full tangent persist on the marked chart.

The boundary theorem rules out the isolated zero–pole collision divisor of the specified type. It does not imply that the surviving degenerations yield even one synchronized triple. For the second triple one needs the matching to change by a three-cycle, with disjoint representatives after transport to the same sphere. A common vanishing equation supplies neither unit coefficients nor those intersection numbers. Establishing these conditions, or obtaining comparable control on K from other coupled degenerations, remains the classification problem. No room or direction search was used. No bound on primitive area follows.
