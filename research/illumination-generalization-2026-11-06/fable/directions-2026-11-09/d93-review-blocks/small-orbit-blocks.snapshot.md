# What a bounded mirror suborbit forces

**Decision.** Darkness at fixed r forces bounded mirror suborbits at both endpoints by d86/d92, without an explicit numerical budget. Small mirror suborbits give restrictions on the *finite permutation action* beyond those of the single-large-suborbit test. At e=2 there is an exact cycle classification. In the cubic, single-orbital case seen in R22, physical darkness forces a system of blocks of size three or four, unless the entire point orbit is that small. Neither conclusion alone constructs a quotient of the room double. R22's two triples can be recovered without inferring darkness from a failed finite search.

Use the full orientation-preserving affine group G on Q and its normalizing mirror ι. Write Ω=G s and Ω′=G ιs for a lattice lift s. They have the same finite size N by conjugacy. The groups below acting on finite sets always mean their **faithful permutation images** when an order is asserted. ## Mirror suborbit graph, including the case of distinct absolute orbits

Use separate left and right copies of Ω and Ω′, even if the sets coincide. Join gs to gιs for every g, with duplicate edges removed. The graph is edge-transitive under G, and both parts have degree e=e(S), by orbit-stabilizer and equality of part sizes. Its components induce an invariant partition on each part.

* At e=1 the graph is an equivariant matching. In particular, the stabilizers of the two paired lifts agree: inclusion and equal finite index give equality. This need not be a physical symmetry.
* At e=2 the components are even cycles of a single length 2h, with h≥2. If there is more than one component they give a nontrivial block system of size h on Ω. In the connected case the faithful left action, for N≥3, is the natural dihedral action on N cyclically ordered points, of order 2N. Indeed the automorphism group of the 2N-cycle preserving its two parts has order 2N and the edge-transitive image must have order at least 2N. Its left restriction is faithful for N≥3. Consequently a primitive connected case with N≥3 requires N prime, since residue classes modulo any proper divisor give blocks. This also applies when the mirror partner lies in a different absolute orbit.

There is a useful restriction for any bounded e≥2. The distinct other left vertices at distance two from s form a nonempty G_s-invariant set of size at most e(e−1). The induced group on Ω therefore has a non-diagonal suborbit of size d≤e(e−1). In a primitive action the Sims theorem bounds the order of the **induced** point stabilizer by a function of d alone. Taking the maximum of that function up to e(e−1) gives the following dichotomy: along a sequence with bounded e≥2 and growing induced stabilizer orders, the left actions are eventually imprimitive (when the orders tend to infinity). Equivalently, for primitive actions with growing stabilizer orders and e≥2, the mirror suborbits must grow. This requires no assumption about the sheet monodromy or double transitivity. It supplies no cutoff in terms of area. An infinite affine stabilizer can have tiny image on Ω, and the e=1 matching supplies no distance-two point. [Cameron–Praeger–Saxl–Seitz, Theorem 1](https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/document?doi=d7050073e631c694738f87d32d8b224fe63610a9&repid=rep1&type=pdf)

## The cubic dark-pair theorem

Take distinct interior marks S,T and their four Q lifts. Suppose the single G-orbit of the edge (s,t), in the bipartite graph on copies of Gs and Gt, contains **all four** edges between the two mirror pairs, and its degrees on both sides are three. This is stronger than just having e(S)=e(T)=2. Then every connected component is either K₃,₃ or the graph of the ordinary cube. The alternatives cannot mix because G is transitive on edges. If one seed edge is dark, all edges are dark; the graph need not exhaust all the dark targets. The classification also applies to an orbital of lit pairs.

Here is an elementary proof. The seed mirror pairs give a square. At any vertex x the stabilizer acts transitively on its three neighbors, hence also on the three unordered pairs of neighbors. The number c of *additional* common neighbors of any such pair, besides x, is therefore constant on each side and lies in {1,2}: positivity follows by transitivity from the square. It is the same on both sides by counting squares per edge: that number equals 2c from either end. If c=2, the three neighbors a,b,c′ of a left vertex have identical three-element neighbor sets (each pair shares three). This saturates a K₃,₃ component.

Suppose the additional common neighbor is unique for each pair. No two left vertices can have identical neighbor sets. Otherwise the transitive left action partitions that part into equal twin classes of size at least two. Each right vertex's three neighbors are a union of such classes, so the class size must be three. This gives a K₃,₃ component and contradicts uniqueness. Thus the extra neighbors u_ab, u_ac′, u_bc′ are three distinct vertices: an equality would give a twin of x. At a, the pair u_ab,u_ac′ must have another common right neighbor. It cannot be b or c′, since that would give another common neighbor of b and c′ besides x and u_bc′. Hence their third right neighbors agree. Applying the same reasoning at b shows that u_bc′ shares this third neighbor. These four vertices on each side have all their degrees accounted for and give the cube.

Accordingly each part of a component consists of three or four points. A larger orbit has a proper G-invariant point partition. For the cubic pattern a primitive orbit of degree >4 is therefore ruled out explicitly, independently of B(r).

## R22 and the meaning of special position

D92 gives H(s)={t,ιt,w_t} and H(t)={s,ιs,w_s} among regular lattice lifts, with w_t the wall point (2,−2) and w_s the wall point (4,0). These sets are stabilizer orbits. The already proved darkness of (S,T) is essential to that verdict: equivariance gives the lower inclusion, and the saved legal witnesses exclude all other lattice lifts. Group actions by themselves also propagate *visibility* and cannot choose the sign of the seed.

The size-three neighborhood of s in the orbital is mirror-invariant, as is the one of t. Reflection therefore gives the same three incident edges from ιs and from s. To justify using reflection on the orbital, its reflected seed (ιs,ιt) belongs to the orbital: e.g. take a g fixing t with gs=ιs, giving (ιs,t); its three neighbors at ιs are hidden and are contained in the three mirror images of H(s), hence exhaust that set. ## Exact recognition of an intermediate cover from two point blocks

There is an if-and-only-if test for the geometric interpretation of two *given* blocks A,B of k distinct regular Q points over the same base pole a, with A,B disjoint. The interpretation tested is that they are two complete, unramified fibers of the first map in a factorization Q → N → pillowcase, with first degree k and distinct regular images on N. It takes at most 2^(k−1) closures under the four known permutations, independently of the number of directions.

Represent each point of A by its two incident sheets (a 2-cycle of g_a). Choose one sheet per cycle. Fix the first choice, since applying g_a reverses all choices and gives the same invariant partition. Form the finest sheet-monodromy-invariant equivalence relation merging the k chosen sheets. Reject if its parts have size other than k. In the remaining partition check that the k cycles of A all join the same two **distinct** blocks and that those of B all join another common pair of distinct blocks, a different pair from the first. Accept precisely when these checks pass.

Necessity: if the factor exists, g_a interchanges the two incident blocks over the regular image of A. Choosing the sheets in one of them selects its entire k-element block. The generated equivalence is contained in the factor partition, and by transitivity all its parts have size at least k, hence they are that partition. Sufficiency: a monodromy block system yields the intermediate cover. On the two selected blocks g_a induces a transposition, so the intermediate image is regular. Each specified point has local degree two on Q, hence local degree one for the first map by multiplicativity. The k distinct unramified preimages exhaust the fiber. The same argument verifies B. This tests a preassigned geometric conclusion; it is not an assumption that every affine point block passes.

**Exact R22 result.** Starting only with the two triples identified above and the archived sheet permutations, the A cycles are (0,4), (1,3), (12,14); the B cycles are (8,11), (9,10), (6,7). In lexicographic order the four choices beginning with 0 generate block sizes **15, 3, 15, 15**. The sole surviving choice (0,1,14) yields five blocks; the A cycles all join block indices {0,2} and the B cycles {3,4}, indexing the sorted blocks from zero. Thus the affine triples **do** reconstruct a unique degree-three intermediate factor here, without supplying the staircase map to the test. For a cube component with size-four parts the corresponding test takes at most eight closures. This is a check on the named R22 data, not a room or direction search. **Open geometric step.** A block here is a set of regular point lifts permuted as a set system by affine maps. A pillowcase intermediate cover instead needs a block of *sheets* preserved by continuation around the base poles. The graph proofs establish the first property; they give no such continuation across the room. To derive the r≤8 conclusion one must either promote these finite point blocks to geometric fibers, exclude primitive small-stabilizer and matching cases, or light the remaining configurations directly. Cubic saturation is verified in R22 and is not implied for general dark pairs. No discovery search or new family was used.
