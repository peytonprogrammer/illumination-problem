# Which reflex corners must stop the light?

**Joint working note, for independent review.** For a dark pair in a square-tiled room, there is a criterion for when one specified physical reflex is indispensable as a stop. It uses the rational directions in which that corner sees each mark, up to the affine symmetries of the marked unfolding. It handles physically straight passage through a reflex, a condition arbitrary affine maps need not preserve. In R34 the criterion forces three particular stops. Eight stops cannot even trap the source at its first lattice step in that room. The minimum number of stops in R34 remains unknown.

## Removing the convex-corner labels for ordinary physical darkness

Use the four-copy translation unfolding X of a strictly simple orthogonal room. A reflex gives a zero of angle 6π and a convex vertex gives a regular point. For distinct interior S,T a legal physical shot exists if and only if some pair of lifts is connected in X by a straight segment avoiding the true zeros in its interior. This equivalence is for existence over the lift choices.

Indeed, an interior crossing of a convex-corner lift folds to exact retracing: the two perpendicular wall reflections have product −I. Project any such connecting segment. Trim it from the last occurrence of S before its first occurrence of T. At the first remaining reversal the ray retraces the full preceding part back to S, encountering no new physical point. This contradicts the trimming. Hence the trimmed path is legal. Axial travel along a boundary cannot start on a smooth wall from an interior mark and tangent velocity; prior to the first convex encounter the ordinary path lies in the interior except for transverse bounces. Unfolding proves the reverse implication. This extends the reversal argument of the staircase proof. It need not preserve the original pair of endpoint lifts on trimming. In particular the ordinary translation-surface illumination theorem applies to physical darkness over all lift choices; its finiteness conclusion furnishes no nine-corner bound. ## Exact criterion for an indispensable corner

For this section the room and marks have lattice coordinates. Choose the group of lifts of integral linear torus maps fixing the square-tiled cover and fixing its finite fiber over the grid vertex pointwise. Its derivative Γ has finite index in SL₂(ℤ): there are finitely many labeled covers of the given degree (encoded by the two edge permutations), and labeling the finite vertex fiber again costs finite index. This choice preserves every corner and every marked lift. Call a Γ-orbit of *unoriented rational directions* a cusp. For a reflex C let V_C(S) be the cusps represented by an open straight leg from its zero to any lift of S, allowing the zero as start and avoiding every physical corner lift thereafter. Define V_C(T) the same way. These are actual sight lines. Arbitrary sheet permutations do not certify membership. [Square-tiled Veech groups](https://doi.org/10.1080/10586458.2004.10504555)

**Criterion.** Suppose S,T are physically dark. Every set of reflex stops blocking all relaxed routes must include C if and only if V_C(S) and V_C(T) intersect. Convex vertices are forbidden and an unchosen reflex admits only straight physical continuation with both open legs inside the room.

*Necessity.* If C is indispensable, stopping every other reflex leaves a route. Its arms up to the first and back to the last C contain no other corner. Every segment of the physical route has the same component magnitudes of velocity. For multiple visits the two arms can be unfolded independently in room copies that give them the same unoriented developed direction. Lattice endpoints and integer mirror lines make the direction rational. Thus they give a common cusp.

*Sufficiency.* Use an affine map fixing all labels on one leg to align its developed line with that of the other. If necessary apply the involution with derivative −I, which fixes C, preserves the forbidden set and permutes lifts of each physical mark. We may now take the two *outward* directions at C to differ by π in the developed plane. On the cone circle of length 6π, the positive angular gap is π, 3π or 5π. The four physical copies are consecutive sectors of width 3π/2. Folding the angular coordinate into the physical wedge is a triangle wave of period 3π. In the 3π case the two legs therefore start down the very same physical ray. Ordinary billiard uniqueness until the first corner puts the nearer mark on the leg to the farther, giving a legal shot. This case is impossible for a dark pair.

In the other cases the short angular arc is π. We can apply one common affine map to fit this short arc inside one physical sector of width 3π/2. Here is why. The stabilizer acts minimally on the cone-direction circle. First Γ acts minimally on the projective line: powers of the parabolic fixing each rational direction belong to Γ and their iterates send any different direction arbitrarily close to that rational direction. Rational directions are dense. The oriented direction circle, and in turn the 6π link, are connected finite cyclic covers. The relevant affine actions commute with their deck rotations. For the link, a lift of the angular derivative satisfies F(θ+2π)=F(θ)+2π in lifted real coordinates. Likewise F(θ+π)=F(θ)+π by antipodality and monotonicity. A minimal closed invariant set projects onto the base circle. Its finitely many deck translates cover the upstairs circle, and two minimal sets are equal or disjoint. Connectedness forces the minimal set to be the whole circle. One can thus move the starting prong into the open interval of positions that put the entire short arc strictly inside one copy. The two open arms remain corner-free, and in that copy their projections continue physically straight through C. They give a route with sole stop C. This proof uses a stabilizer of labels, not a claim that arbitrary affine maps preserve the physical relaxation rule.

This test is finite in principle for a *fixed* marked room: choose one direction per cusp and follow the rays from C to their first forbidden corner, recording mark lifts on the way. Rational rays from a lattice zero on an origami have finite such runs (the finite sequence of lattice germs is reversible before absorption). Test all germs over each unoriented direction. It is enough to detect marks on different prongs in the same cusp. A single prong containing both already gives illumination. A large labeled cusp orbit can make this test expensive. It gives no a priori lower bound on the number of corners with a shared cusp. Moreover a blocking set can need extra stops for routes through several reflexes even when no individual one of those stops is indispensable.

## What can be done exactly in R34

In the archived 19-cell room take S=(2,0), T=(6,0). The strip [1,7]×[−1,0] is filled and its two vertical ends are wall segments. In strip coordinate u=x−1 the top marks have coordinates 1 and 5. The three image pairs (−1,5), (1,5), (1,7) fold back to these marks. Their means are 2,3,4. From each corresponding bottom midpoint draw the two segments **up** to the indicated top images. They reach the two physical marks without an intermediate corner: the whole open segment lies within the open height band, and any strip-end bounce is smooth. Their horizontal displacements have opposite signs and equal magnitude; their vertical displacements both equal one. Choosing the unfolding copy for one leg reflected vertically (negating its vertical velocity) makes the developed directions opposite. The physical prongs differ, excluding the 3π case directly. The criterion therefore forces **(3,−1), (4,−1), (5,−1)** in every blocking set. A width-six strip supplies the certificates for all three. Independently, ten reflexes are directly accessible from S at its first primitive lattice step: its four axial neighbors, (1,1), and (j,−1) for j=3,…,7. The last five follow from the same filled strip. These ten are distinct three-cell vertices, as checked against the named cell list. Fix the vertex fiber pointwise and use cone-link minimality to move each incoming direction into the open part of a passable quadrant at that reflex. Its opposite physical ray then starts inside the room. The transformed leg still has no intermediate grid-vertex lift, hence still reaches C at the first primitive step. Eight chosen stops cannot prevent *all* continuations at the first hit from S. This statement specifies no eventual target.

Finally each off-class has five reflexes and four convex vertices. The only even-even regular sites other than S,T are the smooth wall sites (4,0) and (8,−2). For a nonaxial primitive direction the physical states at lattice hits alternate between the marks’ class and one off-class. Folding each coordinate into the unit interval sends successive primitive intervals alternately to the same base arc and its reverse. At a smooth wall site the arriving and departing cell germs pair uniquely; at a transparent reflex only the diagonally opposite two of its three cells pair. Thus for this fixed arc the germ graph consists of paths and cycles (each germ has at most one arc and one local partner). A simple subpath from a source port to a target port uses each of the two smooth-site matches at most once, after trimming intermediate endpoint visits. It has at most six primitive intervals and three off-class visits. An axial ray cannot pass straight across a reflex with both open legs inside. This bounds the number of *relays*, not the size of the primitive velocity or the number of smooth reflections. Eight stops in R34 remain possible on this evidence.

## Decision

A corner being accessible from S is cheaper than a corner connecting S and T. R34 separates the statements quantitatively: ten accessible corners, three proved indispensable. The discriminating question is whether **the two endpoint sight-line cusp sets overlap at enough different reflexes**. Nine such reflexes would suffice to defeat eight chosen stops in R34. Fewer than nine would require testing the several-corner routes before declaring that eight suffice. An eight-stop blocker in this 34-wall control would refute the proposed general route while leaving minimum wall count open. Nine compulsory stops would give a second mechanism to compare with R22; it would still require a room-independent bound for the ≤20 goal.

I would put this criterion and the R34 strip calculation through independent review before spending compute. The targeted analytic check is whether the odd-x, even-y class in R34 (absent from the three certificates) supplies shared cusps. No discovery runs were made.
