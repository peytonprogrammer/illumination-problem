# Perpendicular doors and finitely many boundary obstructions

**Result for review.** A clear straight segment on a flat two-torus with finitely many forbidden points can be replaced by a clear segment between the same endpoints in any open angular interval. There is a bound on the winding index independent of the denominators of the forbidden points. This gives a large-rectangle gluing theorem for two perpendicular doors, allowing other doors on *all four* sides. For rational normalized center positions there is an explicit clearance estimate. For centers bounded away from the corners at the two designated doors there is a uniform computable threshold on compact aspect and access ranges. A coalescing corner is an exact obstruction to the limiting point argument. Neither result gives a cutoff for all BBBB rooms.

## Torus statement

Take the torus ℝ²/ℤ² and a finite set F, which may include the endpoints. Suppose a developed segment A to A+d avoids F in its open interior. For the result here take d≠0. Choose a primitive integer vector v whose direction is strictly inside the desired interval and det(v,d)≠0. Consider d+Kv for positive integers K. The transverse projection det(v,·) modulo one of the point at time t is independent of K. Each f in F thus has at most 1+ceil(|det(v,d)|) possible collision times 0<t<1. At each such time use an integral complementary coordinate which maps v to 1 (Bézout). Collision requires

    Kt = c mod 1.

K=0 is allowed for the whole segment and thus forbidden by none of these congruences. For irrational t each congruence rules out at most one integer K. At rational t it rules out at most one residue class modulo its reduced denominator.

Here is a uniform bound, useful because simply taking an lcm would depend on the point positions. Put N=|F| (1+ceil(|det(v,d)|)); for N=0 take any sufficiently large K. Begin with h=1. If a rational time of denominator b has 1<b/gcd(b,h)≤2N+1, multiply h by that quotient. This makes multiples of h permanently safe for that congruence: b divides h and K=0 was safe. After at most N updates h≤(2N+1)^N. Each congruence still active rules out at most one among any N+1 consecutive multiples of h. One survives. Given K₀ beyond which d+Kv is inside the angular interval, a surviving K lies between K₀ and K₀+(N+1)(2N+1)^N. This proof uses no distribution assumption about F and allows collinear obstacles outside the original segment.

## Back to a rectangle of width W and height H

Use normalized rectangle coordinates [0,1]², ρ=W/H. The folding torus has periods (2,0),(0,2); divide both coordinates by two when applying the preceding bound. Freeze every door at its center and forbid all their reflected images, plus the four corners. Start on the left and end on the top, after interchanging or reversing axes as needed. Suppose the designated centers lie in the open sides. Choose signed lifts A=(0,±a), B=(±b,±1), 0<a,b<1. Choose the sign of a to match the forward entry velocity and that of b to match the required exit velocity; choose the last coordinate of B on the same side of zero as the second coordinate of A. Both choices ±1 give the same top-wall phase modulo 2. The straight segment from A to B folds strictly into the physical rectangle between endpoints. In particular it misses every frozen boundary obstruction on all four sides, even if several centers coincide in the limit.

Suppose continuous first-access legs exist on a closed interval I of positive physical slope magnitudes, on the specified sheets. Choose v in a strictly smaller positive normalized slope interval inside ρI, off the line through A,B. The torus result supplies a developed displacement D=B−A+2Kv with both components positive, slope D_y/D_x strictly inside ρI, and every intermediate wall crossing clear of the frozen door centers and corners. Positivity ensures entrance from the left and arrival at the top with the required signs. This is the main difference from just matching the endpoint slope: it certifies the earlier crossings on *both* wall orientations.

For clarity, the following bound turns any such center connection into a usable finite-width statement. Let Δₓ=D_x, Δᵧ=D_y, τ=Δᵧ/Δₓ, τ_max=max(ρI), and let μ be the distance of τ to the two ends of ρI. Let η=min(a,1−a,b,1−b). Let c be the smallest normalized along-wall distance, at any intermediate crossing, to a corner or any frozen center on that wall (cap c at 1, use 1 for no crossings). All are strictly positive. Suppose every open door has physical width ≤w and put e=w/[2 min(W,H)]. A sufficient condition is

    e < min{ η/2, Δₓ/2, Δᵧ/2, μΔₓ/[2(1+τ_max)],
             c/[2+2 max(τ,1/τ)] }.

In fact the entry ordinate and exit abscissa vary from the centers by ≤e as s varies over I. The endpoint mismatch ρs Dₓ(s)−Dᵧ(s) has opposite signs at the ends of I by the μ inequality, and so has a root. At that root both components retain their signs. The endpoints' noninteger coordinates stay in their original grid intervals; the same intermediate integer wall lines lie between them. At a crossing of a fixed vertical line the ordinates of the old and new segments differ by ≤e(1+τ): interpolate the new endpoints at its crossing time, then compare with the old line at the same abscissa. The horizontal-wall version gives ≤e(1+1/τ). The extra e for each hole's half-width is covered by the stated c inequality. Thus neither an earlier exit nor a corner bounce invalidates the match.

**Explicit rational bound.** If all normalized door-center coordinates have a common denominator d, then c≥1/[d² max(1,Δₓ,Δᵧ)]. For example the crossing ordinate on a vertical integer line has denominator dividing d times the positive integer dΔₓ. All forbidden ordinates have denominator dividing d. A nonzero difference has absolute value at least 1/(d²Δₓ). The horizontal argument is identical. One may choose τ in the middle half of ρI in the torus argument, ensuring μ≥|ρI|/4. Bounding K by the formula above then bounds both displacements from above; choosing K₀ so both are at least 1 permits substitution of 1 in the lower displacement bounds. This is an explicit scale test when the normalized layout has controlled denominator, including square central chambers. In a general lattice room d can grow with W,H.

**Uniform computability away from corners.** Fix rational bounds 0<ρ_min≤ρ≤ρ_max, η≥η₀>0, a fixed bound on the number of doors, and rational 0<s_min≤min I≤max I≤s_max, |I|≥δ>0. The other frozen centers can range over entire closed sides. These parameters form a compact semialgebraic set (use separate charts for side and sign choices). The winding lemma gives a finite menu uniformly. For instance choose a denominator q with 1/q<ρ_min δ/16 and take two distinct fractions p/q in the middle quarter of ρI, then reduce them; one avoids parallelism to d. Both p and q are bounded by the input bounds. Bound N using these bounds and |(B−A)/2|≤1 in each coordinate. Choose a uniform K₀ so (B−A)+2Kv has both components ≥1 and its slope differs from v_y/v_x by less than ρ_min δ/8, by the quotient formula for two coordinates. The preceding index bound then gives integer component bounds for the whole winding menu.

For each menu member the strict clearance and slope conditions at e=0 are open around every configuration it certifies. This also follows from the finite crossing formulas: the two noninteger endpoint coordinates stay in their grid intervals. The configurations are covered by the menu. Compactness and monotonicity in e now give a common positive margin. An exact algorithm to obtain it from the rational input constants alone is to halve e from 1 until real quantifier elimination certifies that every configuration satisfies the displayed strict test for some menu member. Only finitely many integer grid lines need be included by the displacement bound. Absolute values, maxima and along-wall distances are finite rational inequalities after splitting signs and clearing positive denominators. Termination follows from the common margin. This is a computability result; no elimination was run, and it gives no practical numerical threshold for the whole-room problem. Exterior lattice chambers of bounded area and a single access door have uniform charts by [effective-chamber.md](effective-chamber.md).

## Exact failure at a corner and implications for n₀

If the two designated centers tend to their shared corner, the limiting problem on the unfolded torus is a segment from a two-torsion point to itself. Every nonconstant primitive winding passes through a different two-torsion point at its midpoint, and every nonprimitive winding already contains a primitive subloop. Those four points are precisely the rectangle corners. Thus the limiting problem has **no** corner-free return, despite the clear chord for every nondegenerate pair of perpendicular centers. A finite uniform menu with positive clearance cannot be continued to that limit. Finite-width illumination there is a different question. The same-side case can also have compulsory midpoint contacts on a vertical side; [large-rectangle-access.md](large-rectangle-access.md) gives the signed height condition.

The theorem discharges a class of actual pairs using a single synchronized connection. It supplies neither access through arbitrary attachments nor a bound on the area of the remaining normalized rooms. In particular the compact bounds on the designated centers and access interval are not consequences of r≤8. There is an exact test for the proposed local repair at the shared corner. In physical coordinates let the entry be distance A(s)>0 below the corner on the left wall and the required exit distance B(s)>0 to its right on the top wall. A passage avoiding both far walls and all earlier exits must be the direct chord, with physical velocity rightward and upward and sB(s)=A(s). Indeed x increases from the entry and cannot reverse without a far-wall hit. An earlier reflection at the top would send the path away with no way to reach it again. Conversely these velocity signs and the equality give the open chord. If none of the available paired access branches satisfies this equality, a connection must involve a far wall or an additional chamber visit. The unresolved effective estimate is for these finite-width near-corner configurations, where the limiting torus has no legal return.
