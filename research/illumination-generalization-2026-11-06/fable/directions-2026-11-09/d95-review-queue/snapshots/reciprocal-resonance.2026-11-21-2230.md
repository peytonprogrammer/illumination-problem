# When two orientation tests bound the rational height

This is a conditional coefficient bound for two projective parameters. It also identifies a redundancy that occurs if the vertical test just interchanges the same two widths and travels. It uses the necessary relations in [flat-resonance.md](flat-resonance.md), currently awaiting review.

Take two specified rational two-dimensional coordinate spaces, one on each axis, with fixed integral bases. Write their projective coordinates as X and Y in affine charts. The candidate factors in this lemma are rational **lines** in the respective spaces. A pair of supported two-term resonances, one for each orientation, after clearing width denominators has the form

    A(X)Y + B(X) = 0,     C(X)Y + D(X) = 0.

Indeed each horizontal term is a vertical travel times the other horizontal width. After transposing, each vertical term is a horizontal travel times the other vertical width. All four coefficients are integral affine polynomials. Assume each coefficient entry has absolute value at most J. For example, if the two coefficients of every participating linear width or travel form in its chosen basis have absolute value at most E and both resonance vectors have entries at most M, one can use J = 4ME² by expanding the two terms. This assumes an actual two-term identity in each direction. A relation on three bands need not contain such an identity.

**Bound.** Form the two univariate elimination determinants, the first AD−BC and the other obtained by exchanging X and Y and regrouping both equations. If both are nonzero polynomials, the numerator and denominator of each reduced rational coordinate have absolute value at most 4J². Each determinant has degree at most two. Its middle coefficient is bounded by 4J² and its outer coefficients by 2J². Both determinants vanish at a simultaneous solution. Remove any power of the coordinate and apply the rational-root theorem to the remaining polynomial, treating coordinate zero separately. An infinite projective coordinate already has height one. For a point at infinity swap coordinates in its projective chart first, or use the homogeneous determinants directly. Thus in the specified bases the primitive equations defining the two projective points have entries bounded by 4J². A conversion to the original gap coordinates must additionally account for the heights of the two ambient bases. This does not bound unknown ambient spaces.

**Redundancy test.** In the nondegenerate case the first relation prescribes a Möbius function Y=f(X) and the second X=g(Y). Independence is exactly the condition that the matrix for g composed with f is nonscalar. Otherwise the two relations give one graph. This is a literal loss of one equation under transposition: for positive widths u₁,u₂ and depths d₁,d₂ the identity d₁/u₁=d₂/u₂ and its reciprocal u₁/d₁=u₂/d₂ have the same cleared polynomial. With width and depth ratios both (N+1)/N the two orientation checks therefore admit unbounded primitive height even with unit resonance coefficients. This calculation concerns the arithmetic inputs to the tests. No dark room is asserted to realize those inputs. For degenerate linear fractional maps use the determinant test, which detects separately if a coordinate is free.

**Consequence for H.** In this two-parameter case a uniform placement clearance, bounded ambient bases and a genuinely independent second route identity would suffice. Merely rotating the test gives no independence guarantee. Higher-dimensional factors and resonance supports larger than two also remain open. A check on proposed data is symbolic: compute both determinants before estimating any clearance. The first unresolved geometric question even in the pencil case is whether a room admitting only dependent identities on these routes necessarily has a lit pair of the specified marks by another route. No room or direction search was used.

## A coefficient bound in the original gaps

There is a sufficient rank test that avoids supplying the two pencil bases. Suppose a collection of two-term identities holds on a dark rational product Vₓ×Vᵧ in the original fine-gap spaces of dimensions a,b≤11. Each cleared identity is a bilinear form xᵀCᵢy: the two summands are products of a width and a depth, each a sum of consecutive gaps. If the resonance weights have absolute value ≤M, every entry of Cᵢ has absolute value ≤2M. Flatten the Cᵢ to integral rows of length ab and let K be their common kernel. It contains Vₓ⊗Vᵧ.

Take the span X of *all columns* of matrices in K, and Y of all their rows. These contain Vₓ,Vᵧ whenever both factors are nonzero. If dim X=dim Vₓ and dim Y=dim Vᵧ then the containments are equalities. This is sufficient even if a chosen list of route equations has redundant rows. In particular equality holds if K=Vₓ⊗Vᵧ. Dimensions of the candidate factors need to be known or justified independently for the equality test.

Here is an explicit height bound under that condition. Let t be the row rank and Δ=ceil(max(1,2M√max(1,t))^t). Choose pivot columns of an independent row matrix. Setting each free coordinate in turn to the pivot determinant and all other free coordinates to zero gives, by Cramer's rule, integer spanning vectors of K with entries bounded by Δ. For rank zero use the coordinate vectors. Unflatten them and collect their columns and rows to span X and Y. For a factor of dimension j take j independent spanning vectors and repeat the pivot-minor construction on their transpose to obtain integral defining rows with entries ≤ceil((√j Δ)^j). For full-dimensional factors the defining list is empty. Thus this is an ambient-basis-free bound H under two explicit hypotheses: enough two-term identities to recover both marginal dimensions, and a uniform M. A single proportionality between two ratios can leave both marginal spaces full, as in the reciprocal example above. Neither dimension recovery nor a room-uniform M is presently established. The rank test is a way to identify precisely which directions in gap space a supplied collection fails to constrain.
