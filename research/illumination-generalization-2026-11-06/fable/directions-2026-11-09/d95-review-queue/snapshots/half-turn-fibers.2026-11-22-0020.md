# No absolute-degree bound from marked cylinder avoidance

**Theorem for review.** For every prime p≥7 there is a genus-two square-tiled cover of degree p over its own absolute-period torus, branched at just one value, with full symmetric sheet monodromy. With six forbidden points (the zero and five regular points), it has two distinct regular source and target points above one ordinary torus site that are genuinely dark. Thus they satisfy corner-only cylinder avoidance in every rational direction. There is also a genus-nine version of absolute degree 8p, with eight sources and eight targets, all 64 pairs dark and exactly 48 forbidden points. No proper intermediate translation cover of the base of bounded degree accounts for the latter examples as p varies. These are statements about general marked square-tiled surfaces. They do not provide a dark pair of distinct physical points in a W≤20 room.

## Prime degree, even with two monodromy generators

Here is a symbolic permutation construction; there is no group or trajectory search. Glue p unit squares by right and top permutations h=(0 1 … p−1) and v=(0 1), respectively. Connectedness follows from h. The commutator is the product of (1 2) and (0 1), hence a 3-cycle. Over the origin there is a single point of local degree three, all others unramified. Riemann–Hurwitz gives genus two with one double zero. Off the origin the map is a translation covering. The absolute periods are exactly ℤ²: otherwise integration modulo the smaller absolute lattice factors this prime-degree map through an unbranched torus cover of index p, making the genus-two surface map to that torus with degree one. This is impossible. In particular this example does not exploit a redundant choice of base. Conjugating v by powers of h gives all adjacent transpositions, so the group is S_p and has no nontrivial block system.

Use the genus-two hyperelliptic involution ι. Recall the short justification. The canonical bundle has degree two and two sections, with no base point (Riemann–Roch gives h⁰(K−x)=1). The canonical map is double onto the projective line; its involution has six fixed points by Riemann–Hurwitz. It acts by −1 on every holomorphic differential since an invariant one would descend to the sphere, also at a branch point by the local coordinate z↦z². Thus in translation charts its derivative is −I, and it fixes the unique zero. Declare these six fixed points forbidden. Anchoring torus integration at the zero, π(ιx)=−π(x), so all forbidden sites project to at most four torus points.

Take s=(1/2,1/2) on the unit torus. Its p preimages are regular. At most six are fixed by ι, so choose a nonfixed one x and set y=ιx. They are distinct, outside the forbidden set, and lie over the same unbranched site. If γ were a legal straight segment from x to y, reversing time and applying ι would give a second segment with the same start, velocity and length. Uniqueness through regular points forces the two parametrizations to agree. Their midpoint would be fixed by ι, contrary to legality. This works also for different base sites if desired: take a rational s of order greater than two and the target site −s.

In particular x and y share **no open corner-cut cylinder** in any rational direction; an interior strip chord would connect them. Yet for the specified s the horizontal leaf at height 1/2 follows the single p-cycle h through all sheets, including the source and target. That leaf hits fixed points and is a boundary after the required cuts. Labels for x,y are tracked only, never cut. Thus even at an unbranched base site the premise about geometric separation does not imply that two sheets never share a primitive-word cycle. Prime degree and S_p also directly exclude a proper intermediate cover in this example. This makes no assertion about the separate permutation problem in which raw cycle disjointness is imposed as a stronger hypothesis.

## Eight lifts over each endpoint, and the factor question

Let H=H₁(X;ℤ). The period map H→ℤ² is surjective with a free kernel of rank two and splits as an abelian group map. Choose a homomorphism χ:H→ℤ/8 surjective on that kernel and zero on a splitting. The induced connected cyclic unramified cover X'→X has degree eight and genus nine. The composite to the torus has degree 8p and is still branched only above the origin. The absolute periods upstairs remain ℤ²: each torus period is represented by a class in the splitting, hence by a based loop of χ-value zero that lifts closed. Mark as forbidden exactly the preimages of the six fixed points. The eight lifts of x and the eight lifts of y are distinct, regular and disjoint; each of the 64 pairs is dark by projection. The total count is 64 distinguished points including endpoints (48 if only forbidden points are counted). Thus a bound fails with either convention for n.

Suppose X'→Y→T is an intermediate connected holomorphic translation factorization. If genus(Y)≥2, Riemann–Hurwitz gives deg(X'→Y)≤(9−1)/(genus(Y)−1)≤8. Hence deg(Y→T)≥p. If genus(Y)=1, Y→T is unbranched, and the full absolute period lattice upstairs forces its index to be one. Genus zero cannot map nonconstantly to a torus. The explicit degree-eight upper map to X is an example of a factor whose *lower* degree grows with p. Thus the existence of blocks would not by itself bound the degree requested.

## What R22 uses and what survives for rooms

R22's separation uses a different mechanism. The d72 marked-origami replay found no common open cylinder for its two physical marks. The staircase proof gives a three-copy fold onto a five-square base, with parity there and retracing at seam endpoints. Independently d93 recovered the unique nontrivial block system of the **15-sheet room-double monodromy over the pillowcase**: five blocks of size three in a group of order 233,280. That is a different permutation action from either the 60-square translation unfolding or the abstract torus action above. For the physical symmetry half-turn in a room unfolding, x and ιx project to the **same** physical point. The abstract example makes distinct marked endpoints this way; the unramified lift supplies eight copies but has no asserted physical room reflection action. This is exactly the hypothesis missing from a general genus/mark/monodromy bound. No numerical area bound or enlargement of the W≤20 census follows. Any room-specific degree theorem must use the two complete, disjoint reflection-group orbits of lifts, with the forbidden set prescribed by the walls. The set of six fixed points in this construction suffices for one antipodal partner; whether *two disjoint physical orbits* can be mutually hidden at large degree is the open question.
