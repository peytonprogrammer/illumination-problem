## Exact criterion for clearing one joint without reversing height

Orient the room so horizontal sections are intervals. Consider marks at fixed levels in two adjacent open bands whose horizontal runs are (l,l+u) and (m,m+v). Write the marks' abscissas as l+ξ and m+η, their positive distances to the joint as a and b, and the open mouth as I, the intersection of the runs. Allow any number of side reflections. Set D=a+b, ω=(bl+am)/D, p=bξ/D and q=aη/D. A shot monotone in height through the open mouth exists exactly when I contains an element of

    ω ± p ± q + (2bu/D)ℤ + (2av/D)ℤ.

Indeed, develop both bands outward from the physical crossing. The endpoint images are independently l±ξ+2ku and m±η+2jv. Linear interpolation gives the formula. Conversely join those images and fold each half about its own sides starting from the crossing. There is no other interface or horizontal reflection before the marks. At a flush lip a legal simultaneous side reflection can provide an additional shot. At a staggered joint both lips are forbidden vertices, so the open test is exact for every height-monotone shot.

**All abscissas at the two levels.** If bu/(av) is irrational, the subgroup is dense and every pair clears. Otherwise let g be its positive generator, let J be I projected to the circle of circumference g, and let the two distinguished residues be ω and ω+g/2.

* If both residues are in J, every pair clears. The complement of J is connected and lies strictly between the distinguished residues on one side. Its reflection about ω is disjoint from it. The two choices ω±(p+q) are already enough.
* If either residue is outside the closure of J, a nonempty open set of placements fails. To approach ω with all four choices, let p,q tend to zero. To approach ω+g/2, let p tend to g/2 and q to zero. The allowed p range reaches g/2 in its closure since 2bu/D is a positive multiple of g.
* In the boundary case, both distinguished residues belong to the closure. Let H consist of those omitted by J. The closed complement of J intersects its reflection only in H: its interior contains neither fixed site, so it lies in a closed semicircle between them. Thus a placement fails exactly when **both** ω+p+q and ω+p−q lie in H modulo g. This forces p and q onto the quarter-period grid modulo g, since their sum and difference are half-integral multiples of g. Checking at most sixteen residue pairs against the open ranges 0<p<bu/D, 0<q<av/D settles the all-abscissas question. The punctured-circle case |I|=g is included.

**First obstruction to this construction.** The eight-wall S-tetromino has lower and upper runs (0,2) and (1,3) with joint y=1. At a=b=1/2 the period is 2 and the residue 1/2 misses the closed mouth. For S=(3/8,1/2), T=(11/8,3/2), the residues are 1/8, 1/2 and 7/8. A floor reflection at (5/8,0) followed by crossing at (9/8,1) lights T. D15 covers the word for every length. In a rectangle or six-wall L, adjacent unequal runs share a side. At such a flush joint, one can use the shared side: when the marks' direct or shared-side-reflected abscissas are interpolated, a choice of images of the mark in the narrower band brings the absolute crossing into the narrower run (equivalently use the reviewed flush-joint criterion). **An OPEN staircase word exhibits the same obstruction.** In the named ten-cell staircase S10, the successive horizontal runs from bottom to top are (2,4), (1,4), (0,3), (0,2), of unit height. Across y=2 take S=(5/4,3/2), T=(1/4,5/2). Here g=3, ω=1/2 and p=q=1/8. All four choices miss (1,3). The cap at (5/8,4) repairs the shot with initial direction (−1,4). Its ascending and descending legs remain in the indicated runs. No positive-width horizontal or vertical spine spans this room, as the two extreme sections in either sweep have disjoint interiors. The word is LLLRLRLLLRLR.

## How far a bank decomposition goes

In the connected-section orientation there are at most W/2−1=r+1 nonempty open bands: their bounding heights belong to horizontal edges, of which there are W/2. Consecutive runs have a common open interval by strict simplicity and interior connectedness. Partition the ordered runs into successive groups, greedily taking the longest prefix with a common open interval. Greedy minimizes the number of groups because every subinterval of a feasible group is feasible; moving a first cut later can only shorten or remove the later groups of another partition. Every nonfinal group contains at least two bands. This gives at most ceil((r+1)/2) groups, hence five at W≤20. Each has a spanning rectangular spine in the sweep direction. On either side the sets of heights attainable at increasing distance from that spine are nested unions of intervals, giving rectangular attachments nested on each bank. Boundary levels of adjacent groups remain transparent. This yields a chain of at most five two-bank halls, with no lower bound on the overlap of successive spines. It does not transfer a legal shot across their cuts.

Even in four bands, a bounded number of height reversals cannot repair the general construction. On the named st279 stretches the reviewed lower bound gives lit lattice pairs for which every route uses Ω(√m) horizontal-band passages. Between two reversals of vertical velocity a route visits at most four bands, so k reversals give at most 4(k+1) passages. This argument applies to that orientation; allowing a pair-dependent choice of orientation remains open. D164 also found legal paths for every one of the 4,646 DB55 and 127 eight-level-hall placements missed by its tested family. Those checks certify individual pairs. ## What is the smallest W ≤ 20 room this result doesn't cover?

A smallest OPEN word in the original target is LLLRLLLRLR, W=10. Its ledger polygon is (2,0), (4,0), (4,1), (3,1), (3,4), (0,4), (0,3), (1,3), (1,2), (2,2). The missing conclusion is illumination of every interior pair for every realization of the word.
