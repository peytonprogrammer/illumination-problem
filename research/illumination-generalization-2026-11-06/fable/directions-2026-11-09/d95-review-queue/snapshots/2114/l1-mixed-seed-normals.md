# What a mixed seed can detect

**Fixed-period lemma.** Use a rational product of ambient equation spaces E × F and an integral lit seed (b,d) in its positive chart. Suppose there are a compact slope interval J of positive length inside (0,∞), fixed periods P,P′>0 and a radius 0<ρ<1/2 with the following property. If all crossed coordinates of tu/P and τ/(uP′) are within ρ of integers for a common u in J, the enlarged placement (b+t,d+τ) is lit. Take nonnegative integral increments. D184 proves precisely such an implication for disjoint insertions with fixed transverse widths, using strict retained-gate margins and the endpoint slope correction. Choose ρ strictly inside its allowable tolerances in both orientations. Let k be the total number of crossed gap coordinates in both factors and put

    K = 1 + ceil(8k / (π² ρ^(k+1))).

For any rational dark product U × V inside E × F meeting the positive orthant, there is a character (h,g) with maximum entry bounded by K, supported on crossed coordinates, such that h annihilates U and g annihilates V, and **at least one** restriction h|E or g|F is nonzero. Zero components are allowed. For a nonrational factor the assertion applies to the rational span of its integral points when a positive integral point exists. The use of dark product flats in the room application is conditional on d132 and d134. The elementary implication here requires darkness under independent integral scaling before imposing a wall-gcd restriction.

**Proof.** Pick positive integral (z,ζ) in the product and put (t,τ)=(λz−b,λζ−d). For large integer λ the increments are nonnegative. The product of the d184 circle bumps has constant Fourier coefficient 1, nonnegative coefficients, and total absolute tail below 1/4 after truncation at K. With e(s)=exp(2πis), a retained term is the average on J of

    e(λ(αu+β/u)) · e(−(h·b)u/P−(g·d)/(uP′)),
    α=(h·z)/P,  β=(g·ζ)/P′.

The last factor is a fixed smooth amplitude. If β≠0 the phase has second derivative 2β/u³ bounded away from zero in absolute value on J. Removing an interval where the first derivative has magnitude below λ^(−1/2), then integrating by parts on the remaining intervals, gives O(λ^(−1/2)); monotonicity bounds the reciprocal-derivative variation. When β=0 and α≠0 one integration by parts gives O(λ^(−1)). Thus even a stationary point causes no persistent term. Modes annihilating E and F contribute their full nonnegative coefficient since the seed belongs to E × F. Unless another retained mode has α=β=0, the average is eventually positive, so some slope satisfies all the phase tolerances and the transfer lights a dark placement. This contradiction gives a character for each integral point. Clearing denominators and using density in the positive part of U × V gives a finite union of product kernels covering an open subset of this space. At least one kernel contains the entire space. [D184 transfer and Fourier tail](l1-gap-hyperplanes.md)

**The proposed model.** For U=V=span(1,m), a component annihilating a factor must be a multiple of (m,−1). When m>K both components would have to vanish. Thus a genuine fixed-period mixed transfer excludes this high-height product even though both seed slices miss it. For unequal slopes m₁,m₂ the conclusion is only min(m₁,m₂)≤K when the ambient factors are full. This distinction is sharp at the phase level: take b=(1,1), x ray (1,2), and h=(2,−1). For t=λ(1,2)−b, h·t=−1. On a J where dist(u/P,ℤ)>3ρ, the two x phases cannot both match, independently of every y parameter. A single seed can therefore detect one equation and say nothing about the other axis.

## Exact failure of the transfer hypothesis at an occupied crossing

In the reviewed st279 family let a be the travel up to the mouth, D the width of the lower box, and z the crossing abscissa in (0,1). At the integral same-parity seed (a,D)=(7,5), fix c=1, e=−1 and v=dz/dy=57/2. Both folded seam coordinates are exactly 1/2:

    (1+7v) mod 10 = (−1−v) mod 6 = 1/2.

This seed traverses the lower box sideways several times and rises through its full mark-to-mouth depth, so both cuts can be taken in the occupied lower rectangle, away from vertices and simultaneous cut contacts. Now take a=7+2n and D=3a. The horizontal and vertical increments from the seed are 16+6n and 2n. For any fixed periods, any fixed positive slope window and any positive tolerance, the two fixed-period phases match for every sufficiently large n. Indeed each nonconstant Fourier mode has leading coefficients 6h/P and 2g/P′, never both zero, and the preceding averaging proof applies.

Nevertheless **no direct ascending shot** crosses this seam to the target. The exact condition is that (0,a+1) contain a residue ±1−a or ±1−3a modulo gcd(2D,6a)=6a. Their least positive representatives are 5a±1 and 3a±1, all outside the window. This covers all direct horizontal windings, both launch signs and both target sheets. No primality assumption is needed. The changing lower period is 2D. For example the phase for vertical travel, expressed in side-wall periods at slope u=dy/dx, is a/(2Du)=1/(6u) along this ray. It does not oscillate faster with n. Replacing 2D by the seed period loses the actual side-wall exit phase. The gate is strictly cleared at the seed; tightening a fixed phase tolerance cannot cure the discrepancy. The d186 cap theorem lights the designated pair for a>93 by allowing a different route. Thus the counterexample addresses the proposed transfer of a seed itinerary; it does not refute an all-route illumination theorem. ## Number of seeds and the missing input

Where the transfer hypothesis holds on each successive ambient product, add each independent axis equation supplied and restrict that ambient factor to its kernel. For a specified dark product, at most codim(U)+codim(V) successful seeds recover both rational factors. This is at most 20 for a chart with at most 11 marked gaps per axis, since both positive factors have dimension at least one. This is a depth bound per product, not a total catalogue size. A lit seed must lie in the current ambient product and have a valid transfer there. A wholly dark ambient product would terminate that branch without another seed. If all usable K were bounded by K*, branching on all possible bounded axis rows would give at most 2(2K*+1)^11 children per node; empty or dependent rows can be discarded. This conditional recursion is compatible with d133's primitive feasibility test.

Effective seed bounds alone do not supply this geometric implication. Even once it holds on the necessary ambient products, an effective bound on the new seeds' tolerances is needed to bound K*, then the primitive minima and G(C).
