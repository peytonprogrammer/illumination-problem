Advances: L1 — Determines the exact frequency with which continued-fraction approximants sample successful marked cusps, conditional on their incidence. Positivity and the area cutoff remain open.

# Cylinder incidence at a prescribed pair

Fix a normalized orthogonal BBBB room in the primitive natural branch, distinct same-parity interior lattice marks, and their unordered-pair half-translation cover Y. A compatible start records the two physical positions with the required lift choices. A clean hit at a swap pole folds back to a legal shot between the marks. Conversely the midpoint of a legal shot supplies such a hit. [Marked poles](l1-natural-marked-poles.md) · [Midpoint necessity](midpoint-necessity.md)

**Two different direction tests.** For an unoriented primitive vector v let E(v) mean that some compatible start reaches a swap pole cleanly along v, allowing either sense and any positive integral multiple of the primitive step. Let R(v) mean that in the v-decomposition a compatible start is inside a clean cylinder with a swap pole on its rim, or lies on the opposite rim with a legal inward germ. Here a clean cylinder has no physical stop in its open interior. R gives a clean chord by unrolling the cylinder. The chord generally has a different direction from v. The DB55 prime stretch already separates incidence from modulus. At area 53 the horizontal pair cylinders with offsets 1 and 6 both have circumference 26 and height 1. The first has no swap pole on either rim and the second has one on both. Cylinder count grows without bound even at four reflex corners. [Cylinder construction and modular-fiber literature check](l1-swap-cylinders.md)

**Exact frequency on this fixed surface.** Choose a finite-index projective integral affine derivative group H preserving the covering data, stops, swap poles and compatible starts. Such a group exists because the integral group permutes finitely many isomorphism classes of covers of fixed degree over the punctured pillowcase and the additional markings are finite over the four sites. Use projective slope coordinates p/q for the vector (q,p), conjugating the group by the coordinate exchange. For either test F = E or R, for almost every real slope x the fraction of its first m ordinary continued-fraction convergents passing F tends to
     
    (sum of widths of marked cusps passing F) / [PSL₂(ℤ) : H].

Indeed a marking-preserving affine map carries every admissible launch, clean segment and marked endpoint to one with the same properties. It also carries clean cylinders and their rims to clean cylinders and their rims. Applying the inverse gives equivalence in a cusp. The formula follows by summing the Moeckel–Nakanishi theorem over finitely many cusps. It holds for noncongruence H. This frequency concerns the union over compatible launches in each approximating direction. It makes no assertion of uniform sampling of individual launch labels. [Fisher–Schmidt, Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1208.0131)

For a convergent p/q with next denominator q′, the triangle from 0 to (q,qx) to (q,p) has doubled area q|qx−p| < q/q′ ≤ 1. Its primitive side has no intermediate lattice point. Thus the d179 straightening proof identifies the terminal point on Y of a given lifted triangle, even if its edge labels change. The frequency formula tells us which marked direction cusps occur along the approximants. Straightening preserves the actual terminal point but does not change its type to a swap. [Straightening lemma](l1-swap-pole-averaging.md)

**An incidence test downstairs.** In the room double Q, take clean cylinders on opposite sides of the same open horizontal rim edge of length l. Choose a common developed orientation along that edge and arclength u in (0,l). Its cylinder coordinates are o₁+u and o₂+u. Suppose the chosen lifts of S and T are inside the respective cylinders at equal transverse distance h>0 from the edge, with abscissas x₁ and x₂ and integral circumferences C₁,C₂ in a common horizontal unit. A chord with midpoint on the open edge exists in this chart exactly when

    2u ≡ (x₁−o₁)+(x₂−o₂)  mod gcd(C₁,C₂)   for some 0<u<l.

Indeed the two developed horizontal travels must be equal. They are o₁+u−x₁+iC₁ and x₂−o₂−u+jC₂. Bézout gives the equivalence. Their common value divided by h is the required horizontal travel per unit transverse travel. Both open halves stay inside their cylinders. Consequently 2l>gcd(C₁,C₂) suffices for every pair of abscissas at that depth. At equality an endpoint-only solution can miss the open edge. A simultaneous horizontal shear fixing the seam pointwise moves the starts by opposite amounts at equal depth, preserving their sum. This explains exactly why iterating that shear cannot repair a miss in this chart. This is an equal-depth specialization of the [adjacent-cylinder construction](adjacent-cylinders.md).

**First open case.** In a primitive natural-action BBBB room of area above 20 with at most eight reflexes, can the compatible starts of a prescribed same-parity pair miss the swap poles in every marked cusp? The E frequency is positive exactly when the pair is lit. The narrower R and equal-depth tests suffice where they apply. No proof forces positive mass or supplies an N₀. No room, ray, direction or word search was run.
