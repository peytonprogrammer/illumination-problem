Advances: L1 — At four reflex corners the natural pair cover has unboundedly many maximal cylinders in one direction; cylinders of identical modulus can have opposite swap-pole incidence. Cylinder count and modulus alone cannot supply marked reachability.

# Cylinder chords to a swap pole on the unordered-pair cover

**Scope.** Y is the pillowcase (half-translation) cover on unordered pairs of sheets. One may pass to its translation orientation cover when invoking translation-surface results. The physical stop set upstairs includes metrically regular points with a corner in either projection. All claims here concern same-parity interior lattice marks. There is no unconditional area bound.

## When a cylinder does give a clean segment

Cut a periodic cylinder along any core-height leaves containing an ordinary forbidden point. Work in a resulting open annulus with clean interior. If a compatible start is in that annulus and a swap pole has a representative on its rim, a strip chord reaches the pole cleanly. The same holds if the start has an allowed representative on the opposite rim. In a horizontal integral unimodular chart the possible displacements are (d+kC,h), with C the circumference and h the nonzero transverse travel. For lattice data one can choose a primitive displacement exactly when gcd(d,C,h)=1. For each prime dividing h but not C, avoid the single bad k residue; for primes dividing both h and C, d is already nonzero modulo that prime. Apply CRT. Thus height-one opposite-rim chords automatically meet the primitive-address requirement of the straightening lemma. Here the chord itself is exact. In contrast, the core trajectory remains at one height and cannot flow out to a pole on a rim. A start on the critical graph need not have any open cylinder containing it.

## Same modulus, different rim data at arbitrarily large degree

Use the reviewed DB55 stretch at prime degree p≥53, corridor length ℓ=p−50, and let D=10+ℓ=p−40 be the full room width (odd). On the room double Q the through-band 2<y<3 is a clean horizontal cylinder, written (u mod 2D,t), t=y−2 in (0,1). One semicircle in u tracks the front chamber sheet from x=0 to D, the other its back from x=D to 0. The base pillowcase has horizontal period 2. For each integer 1≤j≤(D−1)/2, there is a horizontal annulus in Y parametrized by

    (u,t) ↦ {(u,t),(u+2j,t)}.

The coordinates project to the same base point. Offsets ±j give the same annulus; the listed offsets are inequivalent. Since D is odd, no half-period exchange identifies two u values in any one parametrization. The annuli all have circumference 2D and height 1. They are **maximal geometric cylinders**, even before cutting at ordinary forbidden points: each rim contains a configuration with first coordinate at the reflex corner (5,2) or (5,3). A local cycle on unordered sheet pairs involving a letter of a 3-cycle has length divisible by 3, including when both letters belong to that 3-cycle. Its cone angle on Y is at least 3π. A horizontal cylinder cannot extend past that rim. In particular Y has at least (p−41)/2 maximal horizontal cylinders at r=4, with circumference-to-height ratio 2(p−40). This refutes an r-only upper bound on either count or that ratio. The reciprocal height-to-circumference modulus tends to zero.

**Exact swap-pole incidence.** On each of the two rims the abscissas in the pair fold to the same physical x precisely when u and u+2j are opposite modulo 2D. The two possible physical positions are x=j and x=D−j. At either position the coordinates approach on opposite copies of Q. They agree as a regular point of Q exactly when the copies are identified on a smooth horizontal wall. That wall lies at 5<x<D−5 on each rim. The positions x=5,D−5 are reflex corners, and positions strictly inside either chamber are separate points of Q. Consequently:

- For j=1,…,5 neither rim contains a swap pole.
- For j=6,…,(D−1)/2 both rims contain swap poles. All these annuli have exactly the same dimensions as the first five.

Already at p=53, j=1 and j=6 give opposite answers with circumference 26 and height 1. The all-p proof uses the named room's boundary, with no direction enumeration. There are even compatible starts on the pole-free rim: the front-copy lifts of physical marks (1,2),(3,2) give the j=1 pair at t=0. Those marks are themselves directly visible. Other incident cylinders or other compatible starts are allowed; the negative result concerns inference from the cylinder data, not failure in every direction. The closure of a pole-free annulus is compact and disjoint from the finite swap-pole set, hence separated from it by a positive distance for that surface. Winding longer inside that annulus cannot produce a reciprocal-distance near approach to a swap pole.

## Affine orbit and the literature

An affine automorphism preserving the marked starts, the physical stop set and the swap-pole set preserves this incidence test under change of direction. If it preserves the latter two sets but moves a start, the test in the image direction must use the inverse image of the prescribed start in the reference decomposition. Replacing a fixed bad start-cylinder incidence by its simultaneous affine image keeps it bad. A different compatible start, another point of the inverse orbit, or an inequivalent cusp can still succeed. An arbitrary matrix in SL₂(ℤ) can change the surface; only a returning affine map gives transport on the original marked surface. Finite index supplies finitely many marked cusp types for each room, without forcing a successful type or a uniform index.

The project's [literature notes](../../illumination-generalization-2026-11-06/fable/directions-2026-11-09/d27-free-group-lit/notes.md) flag the arithmetic distinction. I checked [Hubert–Schmoll–Troubetzkoy, Theorems 2 and 5 and §8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0602394). Theorem 2 gives finiteness of hidden partners on a Veech translation surface. Its arithmetic proof for a periodic source can leave finitely many exceptional targets. Theorem 5's finite invariant-subspace conclusion is on the non-arithmetic side. Neither places a specified periodic start in an annulus incident to a specified kind of pole. Our marked starts and swap poles project to lattice sites and have finite orbits under the cover-preserving integral affine subgroup. Even granting finiteness after marking all physical stops, the entire finite swap set could fall within an unevaluated bound.

**Decision.** The marked incidence criterion is sufficient. Neither the r-only estimates nor automatic transport within a cusp establish it in every room. The first open case remains the marked same-parity natural-action BBBB branch above area 20. No N₀ follows. The remaining cylinder question is whether the *inverse orbit of the compatible starts* intersects the clean interior or opposite rim of a swap-incident cylinder among the marked cusp representatives.
