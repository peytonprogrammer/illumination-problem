# Parallel mouths and adding a rectangular arm

## Opposite mouths of a dumbbell

Take a straight rectangular corridor of width w and positive length 2b. A rectangular end chamber opens onto its entire left end. Use y=0 on the corridor centerline and x=0 at the left mouth. Suppose the other end chamber is its half-turn image about (b,0). For **every** interior chamber mark S=(−a,p), a>0, and its half-turn partner T, there is a legal shot through both mouths. Neither cap nor either end chamber needs to supply a reflection.

Aim from S at a developed image (b,kw) of the corridor center, where k is the nearest integer to −bp/(aw). The developed ordinate at the first mouth is e=(bp+akw)/(a+b), so |e|≤aw/(2(a+b))<w/2. Until that mouth the line is a chord of the end chamber. Fold only its continuation between the horizontal corridor walls. At x=b the physical ordinate is 0, as every kw folds to the center. Continue with the reversed half-turn image of this first half. The velocity agrees there because reversal and the derivative of the half-turn each negate it. The second mouth is crossed at height −e and all bounces are on smooth corridor walls. Horizontal shots are included. The same construction works with arbitrary end geometry whenever the first straight leg and its rotated copy lie in the end chambers. Symmetry prescribes only this paired-target result.

In DB55 the nondirect pair (4,1)–(9,4) has a=1, b=3/2, p=−3/2, w=1. The rule gives k=2, e=−1/10: the physical mouth ordinates are 12/5 and 13/5. The center is (13/2,5/2). The initial direction is (5,7). These endpoints have different lattice parity; the theorem covers real marks. The coordinator's same-parity control (1,1)–(9,1) is not a half-turn pair. Its saved right-cap path at slope −37/8 instead crosses the two mouths at 5/2 and 21/8 and reflects on the cap at height 1/2. An x-monotone shot cannot reach that target since its first door is at half-time and requires a noninteger ordinate. Thus a cap or another longitudinal reversal really is needed for some pairs in the very same room. [Exact cap calculation](terminal-cap-placement.md)

## Parallel mouths on one side of a hall

There is a separate **all-pairs** construction. Let the hall be [0,A] × [0,c], with solid floor and vertical sides. Attach rectangles above its roof. More rectangles may be stacked on them: each child's horizontal interval is contained in its parent's interval, the child's base lies on the parent's roof, and siblings have positive gaps. Keep the union strictly simple. The useful property is that, from a point in a rectangle, its entire horizontal interval stays available vertically down to the hall. Use interior marks throughout.

**Sufficient metric condition.** For each added rectangle of width u whose top is at height c+h, require

    8 A h ≤ c u.

Then every two distinct real interior marks are lit. A single reflection on the hall floor suffices apart from reflections on the hall's two vertical sides. No bounce in an attached rectangle is required. There is no bound on the number of rectangles or their number of levels. The condition can be applied pairwise using the actual depths and clearances below.

**Proof.** Two marks strictly below the roof inside the hall see each other. For each elevated mark independently choose the horizontal direction pointing toward the farther end of its containing rectangle. Available horizontal clearance is at least u/2 in that direction, all the way down to the hall. For a mark on an attachment seam use its interior upper interval when present; at y=c one moves directly into the hall. A mark strictly inside the hall imposes no sign restriction.

Write the physical marks as (x_s,y_s),(x_t,y_t), where y_s,y_t>0. Unfold at the floor; total vertical travel is D=y_s+y_t. Unfold the hall sides as well, using the usual triangular fold of period 2A in x. Start at X_s=±x_s and finish at X_t=±x_t+2jA. Choose the two signs independently so *forward* horizontal motion at the source and *backward* horizontal motion at the target point in the chosen inward directions. For a mark in the hall choose either sign. Interior x lies strictly between 0 and A. Choose j for the least positive travel E=X_t−X_s; then 0<E≤2A. Use either E or E+2A, giving slope |dx/dy|≤4A/D.

With at least one mark above the hall, D>c. In the leg from an elevated mark of height c+d down to its mouth, the horizontal displacement is at most 4Ad/D < 4Ah/c ≤ u/2. The leg stays inside the chosen interval for its whole descent, including intermediate levels, and clears the mouth strictly. Both end legs satisfy this estimate **at the same slope**. In the open hall, folding the intervening straight line is legal except possibly for a floor bounce at a vertical side.

Put f=y_s/D. The two candidates' developed floor abscissas differ by 2Af. If both were in Aℤ, then 0<f<1 forces f=1/2. In that case y_s=y_t and the marks have distinct abscissas. Their signed abscissas cannot sum to 0 modulo 2A: for opposite signs this would require equal abscissas, and for equal signs their unsigned sum is strictly between 0 and 2A. The floor abscissa (X_s+X_t)/2 is therefore not in Aℤ. At least one candidate always avoids a floor corner. All vertical-side bounces occur below the roof; the two strictly cleared descending legs ensure that no upper contact occurs at a roof corner. If both marks are on the roof level they are interior only over open mouths; use the same folded path, with end legs of length zero. If one lies below that level and the other on it, the straight hall chord suffices. Marks at equal roof height in the *same* mouth can also be joined directly. This handles the potential boundary case of a horizontal chord on a roof interrupted by a gap.

**Induction step.** Start with the empty hall. Add one rectangular arm to any available roof interval, including on an earlier arm, checking containment, strict simplicity and the displayed inequality only for the new rectangle. Every old folded path in the construction uses only the floor and the hall sides as mirrors, so opening an upper wall does not remove a required bounce. Old horizontal chords on an interior seam remain legal. For every new mark the same two choices of travel give simultaneous clearance against any other mark. Positive rational examples of arbitrary finite stacking depth exist by taking all layer heights sufficiently small after choosing nested positive intervals; scaling gives integral rooms. This is a metric class with an induction over attachments, not a theorem for every length assignment of its turn sequence.

**First failure of this construction.** A second bank of attachments through the reflecting floor can remove the required bounce. An overhanging child can also remove the guaranteed clear straight descent through the parent interval. A narrow tall arm violates the uniform displacement estimate; that failure alone does not imply darkness. The earlier [adjacent-arm theorem](adjacent-arm-illumination.md) independently handles arbitrarily deep full-side arms. Two different teeth in the present theorem can give a disconnected horizontal section, so R22's disconnected horizontal section alone is *not* an obstruction to illumination or to this induction.

In every orientation of a stacked hall its sections perpendicular to the floor are intervals and it has a full spanning floor. R22 has disconnected sections on both axes: y=1/2 has a separate component at x>4, and x=3/2 has a gap at −1<y<1. It has no orientation of this form. DB55 is a W=12 BBBB counterexample to coverage by the two hall theorems: horizontal slices disconnect, while neither the top nor the bottom wall spans the bounding width, ruling out the other orientation of the stacked hall. DB55 is already lit by other results in their respective scopes. Neither this comparison nor a failed slope proves a room lies outside every known illumination theorem. The general W≤20 BBBB conclusion remains open.

**Obstacle to general two-sided attachment.** To allow attachments on both sides, the floor contact must land on a solid interval as well as avoiding corner points. For the two candidates its physical coordinates are the folds of X_s+fE and X_s+f(E+2A). When a floor interval is open, both can be in that interval. A further usable travel or a different reflection wall must preserve clearance at both marks and place the contact on solid wall. This is the step needed before this attachment induction can handle two-sided profiles.

## A second attachment step on the opposite wall

A two-sided class also admits an induction. Put stacks on **both** horizontal sides of the same hall, with the nesting and separation rules above. On each side keep every root mouth inside [A/4,A/3] in physical x (either bank may independently use its mirror image). Strengthen the depth condition to 16Ah≤cu on each rectangle, measuring h outward from its own bank. Then every distinct interior pair is lit. Further arms on either bank or on an existing arm preserve the conclusion under these same checks. Counting closed mouth intervals as unusable also avoids all their lips.

For an opposite-bank pair, unfold the hall sides without a floor turn. The vertical travel D is at least c. Choose the inward endpoint signs as above and use 0<E≤2A. The two legs clear their mouths strictly. For one mark in a stack and one in the hall at distance at least c/4 from that stack's bank, the same direct traversal has D≥c/4 and slope ≤8A/c. The inward distance consumed above the hall is strictly less than u/2 (or equal only if every bound is an equality; positive stack depth makes D>c/4). A terminal hall mark on the opposite seam is approached from inside the hall. Marks exactly on a mouth may be assigned to their bank with depth zero.

It remains to treat a same-bank pair, or a stack mark and a hall mark within c/4 of its bank. Measure both heights y from the opposite bank. Both now lie between 3c/4 and 17c/16, since u≤A. Use the floor-turn construction with the two candidates E,E+2A. Clearance of both end legs follows as before since D≥3c/2. The circular distance between their developed floor contacts modulo 2A is 2A min(f,1−f), at least 24A/29: the smallest possible min(f,1−f) is (3/4)/(3/4+17/16)=12/29.

The forbidden floor coordinates modulo 2A lie in {0,A} together with the two signed copies of [A/4,A/3], or its mirror about A/2. Any two forbidden coordinates, at least one of which lies in a mouth interval, have circular distance at most 3A/4. This follows by comparing the interval with 0, with A, with itself and with its negative (maximum respectively A/3, 3A/4, A/12 and 2A/3 for the unmirrored choice). Since 24/29>3/4, the two candidates cannot both be forbidden this way. If both are corners, their distance forces f=1/2, already excluded for two corner hits by the equal-height argument. Thus one candidate bounces on actual solid wall. Two marks in the open hall are direct; for marks on the same bank seam use the same-bank argument rather than a horizontal shot along a broken roof.

This step identifies the relevant failure more narrowly: a second bank can be opened safely if it cannot catch both available reflected phases. Arbitrarily placed or deeper attachments need a different avoidance bound. Neither quantitative class forces a verdict for every W≤20 BBBB room.
