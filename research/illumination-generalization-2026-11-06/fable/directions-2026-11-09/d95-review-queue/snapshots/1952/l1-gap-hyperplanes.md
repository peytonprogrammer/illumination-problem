Advances: L1 — Gives an explicit lattice-seed threshold and confines failure of one seed to finitely many affine slices for parallel gaps and for perpendicular insertions with disjoint strips. Lit seeds on the exceptional slices suffice recursively.

# Effective descent from a lattice shot

Work in an ordered wall-and-mark chart. The marks are anchored on the retained pieces, all coordinates integral, and gaps are enlarged by nonnegative integers. Insert only at the designated cross-sections. A whole supporting-line insertion can enlarge several disjoint intervals. When only a particular separating corridor is to change, cut that corridor alone. Fix a nonaxial legal seed shot. Write X,Y for its total positive horizontal and vertical travels in the unfolding and s₀=Y/X=p/q in lowest terms. X,Y are positive integers. A parallel-to-axis seed simply lengthens through transverse cuts and can avoid parallel cuts.

## One ray and its margins

For vertical cuts choose each cut just to the right of an integer coordinate inside the closed marked gap, at distance 1/(2p) from that coordinate. This fits even a unit gap. At every crossing the unfolded cut abscissa is an integer plus or minus 1/(2p). Its ordinate is therefore an odd multiple of 1/(2q), at distance at least 1/(2q) from an integer. At an integer abscissa, a strictly cleared integer corner has transverse clearance at least 1/q. For a horizontal reflection the horizontal clearance to its endpoint multiplied by s₀ gives precisely this vertical clearance. Use actual wall or gate endpoints in the developed word. Artificial equalities at smooth points carry no strict corner test. Equivalently, within each retained copy cut the word at the recorded reflections and cuts. The inequalities for the line to clear the vertices and meet each open edge in order can change only by a vertex contact, a cut endpoint contact, or loss of transversality. At launch and arrival keep the prescribed interior point fixed. These observations give the safe common transverse margin μ=1/(2q). Arbitrarily preselected real cut abscissas need not have this margin.

Let M count cut crossings with their positive integral insertion weights, and let P be a common multiple of **all** crossed doubled strip heights. Under the d182 slope correction the error at a retained abscissa x after cumulative weight b is (x−bX/M)(s−s₀), including either side of a cut. Its magnitude is ≤XMP/[2(X+Mt)]. Therefore, for M>0,

    t > X P q − X/M

suffices (take t≥0 and positive corrected slope). The stated strict inequality already keeps the slope positive since Xs₀=Y≥1. With measured margin μ replace XPq by XP/(2μ); also require positive slope. The reviewed DBas cut has X=14,M=1,P=2,μ=1/7, giving t>84. The denominator-only choice gives t>182. If M=0 nothing changes. The ray contrapositive reaches any admissible earlier base: one lit witness there rules out arbitrarily distant dark members. ## Exact fixed-word test for two parallel gaps

For increments t₁,t₂, index the individual cut crossings by i, write j(i) for their gaps and Pᵢ for their doubled interval heights. For each choice of winding integers kᵢ on the prescribed exit sheets put

    s = (Y + Σ Pᵢkᵢ)/(X + Σ tⱼ₍ᵢ₎),     eᵢ = tⱼ₍ᵢ₎s − Pᵢkᵢ.

The seed's retained itinerary and signed crossing states are realizable exactly when some such s>0 satisfies their strict unfolded gate inequalities: in retained coordinates add x(s−s₀)+Σ(previous eᵢ) to the seed line, evaluating both banks of each cut at the same retained abscissa. Require both cut phases inside the recorded open intervals on the recorded sheets. Retain all avoidance inequalities for unused edges as in the original chart. This is necessary by cutting a legal path and sufficient by gluing the retained legs and folding the inserted strips. It tests a specified chart, excluding tangencies and cut-endpoint contacts. The endpoint equation is built in. A smaller test with one winding index per gap and a common P suffices for the theorem below. Offsets (n,n+c) then require cs close to Pℤ when the two errors must both be small; an unrestricted homogeneous approximation to the slope does not settle this condition.

## Finite exceptional slices

**Theorem.** Fix one seed at parameter b. Among integer parameters componentwise ≥b, that seed supplies legal shots everywhere except possibly finitely many proper affine hyperplanes. On an affine slice through b, the same statement holds with hyperplanes proper *in the slice*. All constants for a specified seed are computable. The theorem applies to arbitrarily many parallel gaps. It also applies to horizontal and vertical gaps together when the cross-sections of opposite orientation do not intersect in the occupied room and the inserted strips have fixed transverse widths. In particular the occupied-cell test in row 3 of the growth classification guarantees this condition. Uncrossed gaps are harmless. [Growth classification](l1-growth-modes.md)

**Phase correction.** Write t for the increment from b, and P for a common multiple of all needed transverse periods. On a small fixed positive slope interval J around s₀, seek a trial slope u with every crossed horizontal-length phase tⱼu and every vertical-length phase tⱼ/u within ε of Pℤ. Choose respective nearest multiples Kⱼ and Bⱼ (already in length units), and write Σₓ,Σᵧ for sums over crossings with multiplicity. The exact endpoint slope is

    s = (Y + Σₓ Kⱼ + Σᵧ tⱼ)/(X + Σₓ tⱼ + Σᵧ Bⱼ).

Indeed residual horizontal-length insertions give vertical errors tⱼs−Kⱼ. A vertical-length insertion gives a horizontal error tⱼ/s−Bⱼ, hence a vertical intercept error −tⱼ+sBⱼ. This proves the formula in the common positive development. For perpendicular cuts use horizontal coordinates at horizontal gates, dividing an intercept error by s. Because the inserted strips are disjoint, every retained piece is translated, and each inserted rectangle folds independently with its original transverse period.

Here are quantitative details to justify uniformity in t. Let N be the total number of crossings, restrict u to [s₀/2,2s₀], and take Nε<X/2. For denominator D in the formula, Bⱼ≥0 and

    D ≥ X/2 + Σₓ tⱼ + Σᵧ tⱼ/(2s₀).
    D |s−u| ≤ X |u−s₀| + N ε max(1,2s₀) = E.

Thus |s−u|≤2E/X, every corrected x-insertion error is at most ε+E, and for s≥s₀/4 every corrected y-insertion horizontal error is at most ε+16E/s₀ (use tⱼ/D≤2s₀). Shrinking J and ε keeps s positive and places all errors in any prescribed stability neighborhood, uniformly in every increment. Such a neighborhood is computable from the finite strict gates at the seed. This also justifies a purely local use of these formulas even for paths with many cut crossings.

**Why the exceptions are affine.** Use one torus coordinate per crossed gap. For k coordinates, convolve a uniform interval with itself to form a periodic triangular bump of integral one, supported within distance ρ<min(1/2,ε/P) of zero in each circle. Its one-dimensional Fourier coefficients are sinc²(πρh), including coefficient 1 at zero. Their sum is 1/ρ. The product bump has nonnegative coefficients of sum ρ^(−k). Truncate at |h|∞≤K so the omitted absolute sum is <1/4; for example the tail is at most 2k/[π² K ρ^(k+1)]. Average the bump along u∈J. Each retained character has phase 2π(Au+B/u)/P, where A=hₓ·tₓ and B=hᵧ·tᵧ are integers. Characters for which both forms vanish identically on the linear direction space of the chosen slice contribute their full nonnegative coefficients, including the constant 1.

For completeness, the normalized integral of each remaining character tends uniformly to zero as max(|A|,|B|)→∞. If |A|≥2|B|/min(J)² and A≠0, the first derivative has magnitude ≥π|A|/P and is monotone. Integration by parts bounds the unnormalized integral by 4P/(π|A|). Otherwise |B| controls the maximum and the absolute second derivative is at least λ=4π|B|/[P max(J)³]. Discard the interval where the first derivative has magnitude <√λ, of length ≤2/√λ. Integration by parts on the two remaining intervals bounds the whole integral by 10/√λ. These estimates divided by |J| give an explicit integer C making every normalized integral with max(|A|,|B|)>C smaller than ρ^k/4. The truncated average minus its tail is then positive unless a non-identically-zero character pair has both |A|,|B|≤C. Positivity supplies the trial slope above. In the exceptional case a form nonzero on the slice takes one of 2C+1 integer values, giving a proper affine slice. For two parallel gaps these exceptions are affine lines. The constants can be large; this is an upper cover of failures, not a claim that all its points fail.

**Finite versus effective seed coverage.** Within any integral affine slice and the nonnegative gap orthant, the set of lit parameters has finitely many componentwise minimal elements (Dickson's lemma). Each lit parameter dominates one of them: descend within the finite lower box. Pick one witness at each such base. Their upward orthants cover the lit set and the preceding theorem covers each orthant except for finitely many smaller-dimensional slices. Repeat on those slices inside the orthant, picking new minimal *lit* bases there. Dimension decreases strictly and the zero-dimensional leaves are single parameters. This proves that a finite catalogue of seeds with the indicated phase transfers covers **every lit parameter** for these fixed-width insertions. It does not assert that a dark parameter is lit, or that the minimal lit bases can be obtained from the area-20 database. Equivalently the lit set is semilinear on these integral gap families: off the finite slices the relevant orthants are covered, and on the slices the same induction applies. The finite-minima input is elementary: any infinite sequence in ℕᵏ has a subsequence nondecreasing in each coordinate, by applying the one-coordinate fact k times. A proposed finite catalogue can be used constructively without assuming that one knows all the minimal elements: verify coverage by the explicit orthants, the bounds on the exceptional slices, and seeds on those slices, ending in dimension zero.

**Effective universality test on a specified Presburger domain.** If the input domain of integral gap parameters is given by a Presburger formula (for example the whole cone, an affine slice, inequalities, and fixed endpoint parities), its componentwise minimal elements are effectively listable. Repeatedly pick a domain point outside the already covered upward orthants, minimize in its finite lower box, and test whether any point remains outside by linear integer arithmetic. The preceding finite-minima argument ensures termination. At each minimal base decide the fixed marked instance using the finite colored origami orbit and horizontal-germ test. A dark base is a counterexample. A lit base supplies a witness, hence the explicit finite list of exceptional slices in its orthant. Recurse on their intersections with the domain. Dimension goes down, including when a slice has several discrete minimal points. At dimension zero use the fixed-instance decision directly. This is a terminating decision of **universal illumination on that domain**, without knowing in advance which bases are lit. It is not an effective construction of the entire dark set by the same algorithm, since a dark base suffices to stop a universality test. Variable wall-gcd-one restrictions need their own domain-emptiness algorithm or a stronger unrestricted theorem. No assertion of practical complexity or new census is involved. The word crosses the first cut once and the second twice. Common P=40 covers all three widths 1, 5 and 4. ## What L2 buys

Area ≤20 is a source of seeds only within its verified room, normalization and mark scope. A lattice shot between integral marks has the stated q bound; a seed outside the target normalization still works if independently certified. On a ray with such a seed the computed T leaves only the admissible parameters up to T to certify, including any larger than the L2 ceiling. Finiteness alone cannot erase these exceptions. In two parallel gaps an L2 seed covers the complement of finitely many explicit lines in its upward cone. To close that cone one needs seeds at the admissible bases of the remaining line tails, plus the finite initial portions. An exceptional line need not pass through an L2 room. The missing uniform information is how large those bases and their witness spans and periods must be, or a structural lighting argument on the uncovered slices. The W≤20 restriction bounds the gap count and licenses the applicable L2 data. It does not bound X, the crossed periods, or the offset of an exceptional line by 20.

**First case still open.** Even with two parallel gaps, a residual affine line can begin above the census ceiling with no established lit seed. A finite catalogue exists for the lit part; completeness from L2 remains open. In row 4, an occupied crossing of two growing perpendicular slabs changes transverse periods and creates a growing rectangle at the intersection. The independent-strip phase equations above no longer describe the geometry there. There is no N₀(20) from this result.

**Smallest ledger word still open.** No all-length word closure follows. The W=10 staircase band LLLRLLLRLR has ledger vertices (2,0),(4,0),(4,1),(3,1),(3,4),(0,4),(0,3),(1,3),(1,2),(2,2).
