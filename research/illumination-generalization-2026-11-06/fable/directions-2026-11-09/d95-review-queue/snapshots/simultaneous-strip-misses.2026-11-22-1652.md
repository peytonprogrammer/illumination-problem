# Both translated strip windows can miss at ten walls

D173 uses a crossing beam plus endpoint corrections in a box of radius δ. Consider its common signed strip at the shared side of nested bands. For V1, V2, and every member of d174's infinite family, the same physical pair misses this box on both axes for all direct and cap choices. The misses have positive margin. This strengthens the homogeneous-character observation to an inhomogeneous obstruction for this construction. The calculation allows any number of intermediate bands with a common strip.

## Terminal projection

Use narrow-to-wide orientation, narrow width w, terminal width W, terminal abscissa z and terminal travel B=b or 2h−b. If P is the least positive shift restoring each full middle run, a necessary condition for the common-strip construction is

    dist(z, PB ℤ + 2W ℤ) ≤ w.

Indeed the signed terminal representative after subtracting the added shear is ±z−kPB modulo 2W. It must lie in the closed strip [−w,w]. Either target sign gives the same distance. A source cap can alter the source phase but leaves this projection intact. Using a translated constant beam with its correction interval still inside the strip cannot enlarge the terminal window. The same miss applies in reverse travel by path reversal of these constructed shots. This is not a necessary condition for unrestricted billiard illumination: a sloping reference line or an asymmetric endpoint correction can leave the common strip at the target while clearing the gates.

Take Cartesian coordinates with the common left wall and bottom floor at zero. After swapping axes, read from the original target at the narrow end. The transposed terminal abscissa is the original source ordinate y, the transposed target depth is w−x for original source abscissa x, and the transposed narrow width is h. Thus the two tests below use the same physical endpoints. Each ordered distance pair is for direct travel and terminal-cap travel.

All marks are interior, including at m=n=1. For V1 the original terminal increments are 18 and 162 modulo 18; the transposed increments are 16 and 64 modulo 16. For V2 they are 12 and 36 modulo 12, then 20 and 120 modulo 20. For the family they are 30 and 150 modulo 180m, then 20 and 60 modulo 80n. Both original progressions lie in 30ℤ, and 15 is halfway between consecutive multiples. Both transposed progressions lie in 20ℤ, and 10 is halfway. Zero belongs to all the progressions, proving the stated distances for every m,n. This uses only the named rooms and exact arithmetic.

## Consequence for the resonance question

Neither a short homogeneous relation nor simultaneous misses of these actual terminal windows force 22 walls. The second conclusion concerns the shared-side common-strip beam only. Whether every admissible beam in both orientations can miss remains open. For a nonconstant beam the base terminal abscissa changes; preserving the phase offset from the constant beam would discard precisely the quantity under investigation. No darkness claim or new positive Ferrers test follows.

D174 also establishes T1 directly by the prime-power test. For the combined denominator argument the necessary arithmetic fact is divisibility: the denominator of the sum divides the lcm of the two denominators. If it divides an lcm of two small integers, it is itself the lcm of two small divisors (intersect each via gcd with the sum denominator). Hence the review's lcm formulation subsumes both individual sufficient tests. [D174 review](../../illumination-generalization-2026-11-06/fable/directions-2026-11-09/d174-review-deepterm/review.md)
