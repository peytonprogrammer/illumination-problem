# How a normalized room can grow

Use maximal physical walls and translate the two minimum coordinates to zero. Pass to a subsequence of area tending to infinity. For a fixed W=2m there are m supporting edges in each direction. Record the weak order of the supporting lines on each axis, including ties, and their cyclic boundary incidences. There are finitely many such charts at W≤20. In one chart the room is exactly a fixed binary occupancy grid with positive consecutive-line gaps xᵢ,yⱼ. Its area is the sum of xᵢ yⱼ over occupied cells. Increasing coordinate changes separately in x and y preserve the boundary embedding, so every positive choice in the chart is geometrically valid. The separate wall gcd conditions select a subset of its integral points. These changes of coordinates describe shapes and carry no assumption about transporting shots.

**Subsequence classification.** There are at most 18 gaps. Each can be made constant or tending to infinity on a common subsequence, by integrality. Extract again so every pairwise gap ratio converges in [0,∞]. Positive finite limiting ratio defines an equivalence relation, and the classes are totally ordered by growth rate. At least one gap diverges. The finite labels are the chart, occupied cells, constant-versus-diverging flags and ordered partition into scale classes. The actual bounded lengths and positive limiting ratios are parameters, not finite labels. For marked lattice rooms one can insert both marks' coordinate lines first, allowing ties. This adds at most four gaps, and gives the same proof. The gcd test still uses physical walls. These are subsequence assertions, not an assertion that growth is eventually along a straight ray. In particular a scale of order t can carry a second, unbounded deviation of order √t from a proposed ray.

The following geometric cases are exhaustive, up to exchanging axes. They are read from the grid, independently of the turn word.

## First missing case: propagation across a thickening slab

**Theorem.** Fix an integral room and two distinct interior marks, even real ones. Insert vertical cuts in chosen open x-gaps, one per gap and away from the marks. Across cut j insert horizontal length aⱼ t in *each* occupied interval there, with positive integer weights aⱼ and t≥0. Retain each mark on its original piece. If the marks are lit in the base room, then they are lit for **every sufficiently large t**. The threshold depends on a single legal base shot. No restriction on the number of times it crosses the cuts is required. Horizontal cuts obey the transposed result. This improves the fixed-slope arithmetic progression for integral insertions. [Earlier progression](length-periodicity.md)

**Proof.** Choose the cut abscissas in the given gaps away from the finitely many wall contacts and vertical legs of the shot. The marked placements determine which side of a cut they occupy. A cut in a mark-to-wall subgap permits this choice even for marks inside the original thick slab. A shot missing all cuts is unchanged up to translation. A horizontal shot lengthens directly. In other cases choose unfolded coordinates with both velocity components positive. Let s₀>0 be the absolute vertical travel per unit horizontal travel, X the total horizontal travel of the base shot, and M the sum of aⱼ over its cut crossings, with repetitions. Let P be a common multiple of twice every crossed interval height, in lattice units.

For t>0 take k nearest to ts₀/P and set

    s = (Xs₀ + MkP)/(X + Mt),      e = ts − kP.

Then X(s−s₀)+Me=0 and

    |s−s₀| ≤ MP/[2(X+Mt)],      |e| ≤ XP/[2(X+Mt)].

Crossing an inserted rectangle at cut j adds aⱼts to unfolded vertical travel. Its aⱼkP part is a whole number of doubled heights, restoring both height and vertical sign, in either horizontal crossing sense. The remaining shift is aⱼe. In the retained pieces, after sealed horizontal travel x and cumulative crossing weight b, the unfolded height error is x(s−s₀)+be. Horizontal reflections preserve the magnitude of the corresponding physical error. One can justify the itinerary before assuming it: open the fixed base word and treat s and e as independent variables, inserting a signed phase shift aⱼe at each cut. At (s₀,0) the finite segment has only transverse smooth contacts, and its cuts lie strictly inside their intervals. These strict inequalities persist in an open neighborhood. The long inserted strips realize precisely those phase shifts, with only smooth reflections on their long walls. For all sufficiently large t our (s,e) is inside that neighborhood. At the target x=X and b=M, so the error vanishes exactly. The unchanged developed endpoint in the retained target piece is therefore reached. The argument applies to either parity or sheet and needs no enumeration. If η>0 is a verified admissible radius in both variables, X+Mt > P max(M,X)/(2η) suffices.

**Exact named control.** The saved DBas shot at L=4 has s₀=2/7, X=14, one crossing and P=2. Insert 100 units. The formula gives k=14 and s=16/57. The entry height is 107/57. The signed exit phase after 104 units of corridor is 18/57 modulo 2, so the exit height is 75/57 with upward velocity. Four more horizontal units reach the right cap at 139/57 and two more reach the target at height 3. All contacts are smooth. The fixed old slope would require the insertion to be a multiple of 7. **Consequence for one separating corridor.** In the reviewed d110 reduction for fixed lattice end chambers and width, choose a base integer L* beyond the opposite-end bound and beyond twice the collar depth. Only a finite list of end-relative lattice placements remains. If every residual placement has a witness there, taking the maximum of its transfer thresholds proves full lattice illumination at **every** sufficiently large integer length. For a same-parity-only seed use base lengths of the appropriate parity when needed. More generally for each residual placement its lighting status is eventually constant: if it is ever lit beyond the collar threshold, apply the theorem at that length, and otherwise it stays dark. Finiteness gives eventual constancy for the full residual list. This last statement is qualitative. It supplies neither a lit seed nor an effective bound for a placement with no known witness. **First open case.** For a single separating gap with arbitrary fixed ends, a residual same-end placement might have no lit seed at any admissible length. It now suffices to light that placement at *one* such length. An unbounded dark sequence in this mode forces that placement, after taking a subsequence, to be dark at every base length beyond the collar threshold. For a general parallel growth ray the same backward implication holds for anchored marks. Several independent lengths with drifting ratios can require incompatible phase corrections. Perpendicular insertions also involve the reciprocal slope. None of the uniform bounds on seed lengths, clearance or scale ratios required for N₀ follows from W≤20. Its role in the classification is the bound on the number of gaps, not a new illumination inequality.
