# A finite tangent-rigidity test with diagonal walls

**Result for review.** For a supplied integral axial or mixed axial/45° room with two distinct interior lattice marks, all first-order constraints arising from a route with one forbidden corner and a legal bypass on one side have a computable joint kernel. This uses every primitive direction at the seed, without a word-length cutoff. In a genuine mixed room, a kernel of dimension one in anchored fixed-direction coordinates proves local rigidity up to uniform scaling for dark flats through the seed. The test does not assert this dimension for every seed with W≤20.

### The constraint

Let X be the translation unfolding, Σ the labelled corner and endpoint lifts, H=H₁(X,Σ;ℚ), and h:H→ℚ² its period map in the original Cartesian frame. Reflections in the allowed directions have signed permutation linear parts and integral translations at lattice walls. Consequently development modulo ℤ² gives a torus cover branched at most over the origin, with Σ over that origin. Its square-tiled presentation may subdivide the original room copies. All physical corner lifts are forbidden, including regular lifts. Let F be the physical tangent space on the chosen fixed-direction cell, anchored at a vertex. For a variation v in F, write ηᵥ:H→ℝ² for the period derivative from the linear period chart.

Consider a straight incoming and outgoing leg through the nominated corner C with no other forbidden contact. In the cone link at C require that on one side the angular sector between the outward leg germs has angle exactly π. That side develops to a half-disk, so a small transverse perturbation to that side bypasses C. At a regular forbidden mark both sides work. Let a be the oriented source-to-corner relative class and b the corner-to-target class, oriented along travel. Their periods satisfy h(b)=ρ h(a) with ρ positive rational. Put z=b−ρa in ker h. On any two-sided deformation through the seed consisting entirely of dark pairs,
    
    det(h(a), ηᵥ(z)) = 0.

Indeed the derivative of det(hₜ(a),hₜ(b)) is exactly this expression. If nonzero, one sign of t moves the developed chord to the bypass side. Away from the corner the two compact leg interiors, with small disks at their endpoints removed, have corner-free tubular charts along the itinerary. Near C the same outgoing sector persists and the sign puts the chord in its legal smoothing; the unfolded chord then connects the chosen endpoint lifts. Self-overlaps of the developed charts cause no issue since one follows the parametrized legs. Passing another allowed endpoint label early also supplies a connection after truncation if needed. This is a necessary equality for a two-sided dark flat, irrespective of the sign chosen for the one-sided bypass. The proof works on the unfolding, including isolated simultaneous artificial cuts.

### Exhausting the constraint space by rational linear algebra

Take the subgroup of labelled affine automorphisms covering linear torus maps in SL₂(ℤ). Its derivative has finite index: at the fixed cover degree there are finitely many permutation pairs with the finite label data. The SL₂(ℤ) generators act by retiling this finite orbit. Schreier loops give generators for the derivative stabilizer, with a lifted affine map for each loop. Include the finite label-preserving deck kernel (enumerate permutations of the sheets). The action on H can be computed by mapping labelled relative edges and taking their relative homology classes. Equivalently any finite-index lift subgroup suffices if its own cusp representatives are used. [Origami stabilizer algorithm](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0401185)

Primitive integral directions split into finitely many orbits of this derivative group, represented via cosets in SL₂(ℤ): apply full-group coset representatives to the first coordinate vector. In each representative direction there are finitely many possible leg pairs. After integral change of basis make the direction horizontal. From a corner there are finitely many outgoing horizontal germs. In each sign follow the unique continuation until the next forbidden vertex. It terminates: the directed unit-edge states between forbidden sites form a finite partial injection; a ray launched at a forbidden site's outgoing sector cannot enter a disjoint periodic cycle since every regular directed germ has a unique predecessor. Equivalently cut the finite directed horizontal edge diagram at every forbidden vertex. Record every eligible mark met on a leg, retaining the relative edge chain. Pair opposite germs at the same corner when the link separation on one side is π. This permits multiple occurrences of the same physical label on different lifts. An orientation-preserving linear map carries an angular sector with opposite boundary rays and exactly a half-turn to one of the same kind (one turn around the circle of directions has degree one). The group preserves all labels. These finitely many templates and their group images therefore give every constraint of the stated kind.

Form W in ℚ²⊗ker h from the template vectors h(a)⊗z. Take the smallest invariant linear span under Dg⊗g_* for the finitely many affine generators and inverses, where Dg is the derivative. Iteratively adjoin images of a basis until dimension stabilizes. There can be at most 2 dim ker h strict increases. Since ρ is invariant under g, every orbit image has exactly the required tensor, so this span loses no linear constraints and adds none. The complete tangent test is the kernel on F of the linear functionals obtained from W by pairing u⊗z with det(u,ηᵥ(z)). This terminates at each finite seed. No enumeration was run.

### What would imply uniform rigidity

A sufficient representation-theoretic condition is W=ℚ²⊗ker h. Indeed it forces ηᵥ to kill ker h, hence ηᵥ=A h for a real two-by-two matrix A (the periods span the plane, since the surface has positive area). For a physical deformation the signed-copy symmetries act on h and ηᵥ by the same fixed reflections, so A commutes with every generating reflection. In mixed rooms, commuting with an axial reflection and a diagonal reflection forces A to be scalar; in axial rooms it forces A to be diagonal. Relative periods from an anchored vertex to every vertex and mark recover their velocities on a chosen room copy. Thus these are precisely the trivial deformations. Full tensor saturation is sufficient; the restriction to F can have trivial kernel modulo the symmetries even without saturation.

At a dark seed the local dark set is locally a finite union of whole flats on the order cell. Every flat through an interior point of that cell satisfies the kernel condition. Therefore trivial kernel modulo the physical symmetries proves local rigidity there. For an assertion across an order-cell boundary one must also check adjoining cell branches, or work in the larger open fixed-wall-direction parameter chart and use the tangent condition for two-sided branches there. The result does not bound the cover degree, the number of cusp templates, or the seed's denominator. In particular a deficient kernel is inconclusive: higher-order constraints and other corner mechanisms may still force rigidity. The outstanding structural question is whether, at a hypothetical dark seed with W≤20, these orbit rows separate every physical deformation except the trivial ones. This replaces the unsupported independent stretching in mixed rooms by an exact test on relative homology.

**Only the physical symmetry component matters.** Let Γ be the finite room reflection group acting on the full labelled set by permutations (it need not fix each lift separately). Project tensors by
     
    P(t) = (1/|Γ|) Σ_{k∈Γ} det(k) (k⊗k_*) t .

For any fixed-direction physical velocity, pairing (k⊗k_*)t multiplies the original pairing by det(k), by equivariance of η and the determinant. Hence t and P(t) give the same physical row. Thus the weaker condition P(W)=P(ℚ²⊗ker h) already gives the commutant conclusion. The finite affine-orbit span must be taken before this projection unless the generators are shown to commute with it. This is the exact symmetry component to target in a uniform spanning theorem.

The earlier fixed-angle R22 rank calculation is a consistency check on the *kind* of constraint. Its 147 saved witnesses already annihilate every extra two-sided fixed-angle motion. Its perturbation allowing the wall directions themselves to tilt lies outside F. R22 has 22 walls and gives no small-wall rank bound.
