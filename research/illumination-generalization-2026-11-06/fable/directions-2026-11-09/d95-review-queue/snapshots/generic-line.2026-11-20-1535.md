# What orbit-closure theory implies on the dark deformation line

**Verdict for review.** On the rational one-axis line from [accumulation.md](accumulation.md), every transcendental parameter gives a non-torus-cover surface whose orbit closure has field of definition **ℚ**. Conditional on d89 and the fixed-height reduction, the marked lifts are individually periodic for the actual unmarked orbit closure. The available classification does not exclude these periodic points. Indeed, even a four-reflex physical room can have a regular interior periodic point in precisely this arithmetic-field, non-torus, higher-rank setting. No area cutoff follows.

## The actual orbit closure on the line

Let X_t be the orientation (translation) double of the quadratic room double Q_t, and let M_t be its conical GL⁺ orbit closure in the Abelian stratum, before adding the source and target. Use an ordinary period chart small enough that the physical polygon is strictly simple and all marked lifts remain distinct. Periods on our line have the form z(t)=a+tb with a,b in ℚ[i] in each coordinate. Horizontal wall lengths include two with nonproportional coefficient vectors. At transcendental t their ratio is irrational. Their doubles are parallel absolute periods, so X_t cannot cover a flat torus by translations.

**Field lemma.** Wright's Theorem 1.1 says the field of definition of an affine invariant submanifold is the intersection of the holonomy fields of its surfaces and is a real number field. A local branch of M_t at X_t is cut out by homogeneous linear period equations over that number field. Substituting z(t) into any equation gives A+tB=0 with algebraic A,B. Transcendence forces A=B=0. Thus the branch contains a small segment of the line, in particular a rational parameter where all periods are rational complex numbers. The holonomy field there is ℚ (two independent periods exist by positive area). Wright's intersection formula gives k(M_t)=ℚ. Equivalently the surface holonomy field at t itself is contained in ℚ(t), whose only algebraic constants are rational. This argument uses the unnormalized cone, so area rescaling introduces no irrational coefficient into the line equations. [Wright, Theorem 1.1](https://msp.org/gt/2014/18-3/gt-v18-n3-p03-s.pdf)

The actual rank is at least two. In absolute period coordinates write z(t)=x₀+t v+i y. The real tangent coefficient space of M_t contains x₀+t v and y by diagonal stretching and the real-coefficient equations. It also contains v by the line lemma. These are three independent absolute vectors: evaluations on doubled horizontal walls distinguish x₀ and v by the nonzero minor in the construction, while a doubled vertical wall detects y independently. Hence the absolute projection has complex dimension at least three, and its dimension is twice the rank. Its rank is at most d89's ambient rank h by containment; equality with h is not needed and does not follow just by calling t transcendental. [Rank convention and high-rank theorem](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/2023/198-2/p04) · [D89 setting](dark-locus.md)

**Why individual periodicity does hold conditionally.** Work on the smooth d89 marked branch around the chosen seed and shorten the parameter interval if necessary. Its projection forgetting all regular labels has injective differential, including after projection to absolute cohomology. The simultaneously marked orbit closure through the parameter is contained in this branch locally (globally in its locus). Forgetting labels maps that orbit closure into M_t with dense image since it contains the unmarked orbit. Its image is algebraically constructible and its closure is M_t: the image lies in M_t, and Euclidean density implies Zariski density there. Its dimension therefore equals dim M_t. Injectivity on the local tangent gives equality of the marked and unmarked dimensions. Forget all but one regular label: its orbit-closure dimension is sandwiched between those same dimensions. This is individual M_t-periodicity in the dimension sense, for each lift. It uses the reviewed zero-relative-dimension result and the hypothetical line, not an implication from nonillumination alone. Finite orientation, corner, and level labels do not add dimensions. [D89 review](../../illumination-generalization-2026-11-06/fable/directions-2026-11-09/d89-review-dark-locus/review.md)

## A geometric check on the proposed interior-point exclusion

Use the [four-reflex cross geometry](every-direction.md), with horizontal bar [−(7+t),7+t]×[−2,2] and vertical bar [−2,2]×[−7,7] for |t| small. The center O is an interior point. The physical half-turn about O maps each of the four signed copies to itself and respects all wall gluings. It induces a holomorphic involution A of X_t with A*ω=−ω. Its fixed set consists exactly of the four lifts of O: any fixed point must project to a fixed physical point, and the four distinct interior lifts are each fixed. These lifts are exchanged by J, and O is not on the fixed seam of the physical mirror.

Each fixed lift is periodic for M_t. Indeed the data of an automorphism A with A*ω=−ω and a chosen fixed point add only finitely many choices on a fixed smooth genus-five surface. The involution persists along GL⁺ orbits since −I is central, and stays in the smooth automorphism incidence locus on taking the orbit closure. More explicitly, on the smooth genus≥2 moduli stack the automorphism scheme is finite over the base (proper diagonal and finite stabilizers); the equation on the form and the choice of a fixed marked point give a proper finite incidence over the smooth stratum. The identity cannot enter as a specialization since ω is nonzero. Thus the marked orbit closure has the same dimension as the unmarked one. Equivalently, O lies over a branch point of the half-translation quotient X_t/A.

The numerical range also matches the obstructing range. The field lemma and rank lower bound above apply to this rational line. A and J commute: the physical half-turn keeps sheet signs and J reverses both. AJ is a free translation involution (a fixed point would project to O, where it exchanges distinct sheets). Its quotient has genus three by Riemann–Hurwitz. The family and its orbit closure lie in this degree-two translation-cover locus: one can retain the finite automorphism data as above, with the free topological action locally constant in the smooth family. All absolute periods therefore vary in the pullback of a genus-three absolute cohomology space. Hence 2≤rank(M_t)≤3, below the genus-five high-rank threshold.

The rational specializations include primitive BBBB rooms approaching any chosen small t: take odd primes q and even p coprime to q with p/q→t, stretch x by q, and keep the other axis unchanged. Horizontal wall lengths have gcd gcd(4q,5q+p)=1 (odd 5q+p, coprime to q), and vertical gcd is one. Area eventually exceeds the d69 BBBB bound. Thus an interior periodic point and a proper quotient can both persist through BBBB specializations. At transcendental t there is no finite pillowcase sheet set on which to impose the four B_x conditions. Even at the arithmetic members, BBBB excludes only the specified *canonical* factors. [Canonical-factor distinction](canonical-transfer.md)

This control is about the proposed exclusion of **each** interior periodic point. It provides neither a dark pair nor two distinct dark physical points. Scaling a single point or duplicating its lifts cannot supply that missing example.

## Decision

The unresolved statement is that a proper rational-field locus in this rank range cannot support **two distinct physical interior periodic marks whose four mirror-lift cross pairs are all dark** along the physical line. A classification of the one-point markings on those particular loci, or a lighting theorem for such pairs, would address it. BBBB and the cited theorems do not currently provide either.

Finally, if a transcendental parameter on the hypothesized *whole dark line* were lit, that would contradict the line immediately, by definition. On an arbitrary unrelated line, relative closedness alone allows isolated algebraic dark parameters. The previous reduction would at most turn a proof excluding whole lines into a **qualitative** bound on primitive area of same-parity counterexamples. It would still require an effective area bound and finite verification (and separate treatment of other mark classes for the unrestricted goal). No such bound or finite check is obtained here.

## Every irrational parameter tests the same physical component

**Theorem for review.** Let I be a connected interval of strictly simple marked orthogonal rooms of fixed turn type, with positive wall lengths and distinct interior marks. Suppose all wall and mark ordinates are fixed rational numbers and every wall and mark abscissa is a rational affine function of t. Carry the corner labels and all lifts of the two marks. For any **irrational** t₀ in I, the conical orbit closure of the marked unfolding at t₀ contains the marked unfolding at every t in I. Consequently all irrational parameters in I have the same marked orbit closure. If one such parameter is dark, the whole physical component is dark. If any parameter is lit, every irrational parameter is lit and the possible dark parameters are a locally finite set of rationals in I.

This strengthens the transcendental-parameter argument and applies to general lines of this form, independently of d80. It uses no classification of periodic points. Here is a direct marked proof so the conclusion does not hinge on extending an unmarked field-of-definition statement to marked labels.

**1. Encode the transverse twists.** Near t₀, cut the translation unfolding along vertical lines through all physical vertex and mark abscissas, using all reflected copies. The order of the cuts is locally constant: equality of two rational affine abscissas at irrational t₀ is an identity. Inside each open physical slab a vertical ray oscillates between two fixed horizontal walls. On the translation unfolding each resulting open subcylinder has constant rational circumference cⱼ (twice the height of its run) and positive transverse width dⱼ(t)=aⱼ+bⱼt, with aⱼ,bⱼ rational. All marked points and cone points lie on the cuts. The extra cuts may subdivide a cylinder. Full vertical turns on any subdivided cylinder still fix both rims pointwise. Use the left-to-right orientation in the *translation chart* on each subcylinder; this automatically reverses the order of the cuts in reflected physical copies.

Under the global vertical shear (x,y)↦(x,y+s x), the relative shift from the left to the right rim of subcylinder j is s dⱼ(t₀) modulo cⱼ. These twist coordinates describe the sheared surface with its labels: use charts based separately at the left rims, retaining the original boundary identifications in intrinsic vertical coordinates. Changing one traversing vector by an integral multiple of i cⱼ changes only its winding around that cylinder and leaves the labeled surface isomorphic. At small twist values one can keep a single developed period chart.

**2. Obtain the transverse deformation in the orbit closure.** Choose a positive integer K clearing denominators so K aⱼ/cⱼ and K bⱼ/cⱼ are integers for all j. Given any small real η, take integers n with n t₀ modulo one tending to η/K modulo one. Irrational rotation of the circle gives such a sequence. At s=Kn the twist in every subcylinder tends, modulo cⱼ, to η bⱼ. Thus the marked orbit closure contains the whole local arc with traversing vectors dⱼ(t₀)+iη bⱼ, with the same rims and marked positions on those rims.

In local period coordinates this is z(t₀)+iηv, where v is precisely the derivative of the original physical line. Indeed changing t by h changes the very same traversing vectors by the real increments h bⱼ; the vertical boundary vectors do not change. Expressing a relative period by crossings of the cuts and pieces on their rims sums the same increments with the same signs. This also verifies the assertion for periods ending at a mark. Complex affine linearity now gives z(t₀)+hv locally in the orbit closure. More explicitly, among the finitely many local complex-linear sheets there is one containing an accumulating sequence on the imaginary arc; its equations then annihilate both iv and v. It is important that the orbit closure is taken in moduli, where full twists in step 1 are identified, before using a local period chart in this step.

**3. Continue without crossing a singular room.** The marked closure is closed in the smooth stratum and locally a finite union of affine sheets. At any interior endpoint of an interval already contained in it, one sheet contains infinitely many points of the approaching line and thus contains its continuation. This extends through the whole connected I. Applied in both directions between two irrational parameters this gives identical orbit closures. To transfer darkness, use the closed invariant condition on the unfolded surface that *all* source–target lift pairs lack a connection avoiding the labeled forbidden corner lifts. Its restriction to physical rooms is exactly billiard darkness. Finally, this closed invariant set is a finite union of affine loci. On a compact subinterval of I it intersects the line in only finitely many isolated points unless it contains a local segment, in which case it contains the whole physical component by the same continuation. If there is a lit parameter, the isolated dark points must be rational by the irrational-parameter conclusion. No bound on their number near the ends of I follows.

**Literature used in this theorem.** EMM Theorem 2.1 supplies affine invariant orbit closures on the unit-area Abelian stratum; use the cone for GL⁺. Its Definition 1.1 gives the proper immersion and linear period charts, which yield locally finite sheets. EMM Theorem 2.2, for a closed upper-triangular-invariant subset, is used for the local-finiteness assertion about the entire dark set. Lelièvre–Monteil–Weiss §§2.1–2.2 explicitly allow additional marked regular points in these EMM results. We retain them distinct and avoid leaving the stratum. Irrational rotation on ℝ/ℤ is the only density input. Independently, Wright's cylinder paper, Theorem 1.9, states that given a translation surface in an affine invariant submanifold and a direction containing cylinders, the affine field is contained in the field of their circumference ratios (ℚ in the one-cylinder case). Rotating the vertical direction to horizontal applies this to the unmarked unfolding: all its vertical circumferences are commensurable. This checks k(M_t)=ℚ also at algebraic irrational t. Neither Filip algebraicity nor the Apisa–Wright periodic-point theorems are needed for the new marked twist argument. [EMM, Definition 1.1 and Theorems 2.1–2.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1305.3015) · [LMW, §2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1407.2975) · [Wright, Theorem 1.9](https://public.websites.umich.edu/~alexmw/Cylinder.pdf)

**What still requires a proof.** For a d89 line, the reviewed argument already supplies a dark interval at its seed. Hence the entire physical component is dark under the counterexample hypothesis. A witness at any one parameter in that component (for example a quadratic irrational, or a rational specialization in a certified class) would contradict it. There is no argument here that every nonradial d89 line has such a specialization. A witness only in a pinched or self-contacting endpoint room does not qualify. No finite area cutoff or general W≤20 lighting theorem follows.
