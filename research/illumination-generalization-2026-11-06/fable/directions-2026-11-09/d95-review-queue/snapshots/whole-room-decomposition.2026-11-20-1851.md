# What a rectangular decomposition proves for the whole room

**Verdict for review.** Parallel cuts avoid perpendicular doors altogether. They give a tree of at most r+1 rectangles and, for any one piece, single-door access from each component of its complement. A sufficiently deep piece with one closed end lights the entire room, even if several chambers attach to the other end. A deep piece with openings at both ends reduces possible dark pairs to one end and its collar, subject to the far-wall test below. Large area alone forces neither of these scale inequalities. The normalized cov279 stretch gives a decomposition-independent obstruction to inferring them from area and r. There is no resulting universal illumination cutoff N(r).

## Decomposition at every area

At a reflex vertex extend the incident vertical edge into the interior until it first hits boundary. The open chord is interior. It splits the simple polygon into two simple polygons. At the chosen endpoint the old 270° angle becomes 90° and 180°. At an interior point of a horizontal wall the new angles are both 90°; if the chord ends at another reflex vertex it also removes that reflex. No convex vertex can receive a vertical chord through its open interior sector. Repeat on the remaining reflex vertices, suppressing straight vertices. The cuts stay on lattice lines with lattice endpoints. Parallel cut interiors do not cross, and cannot end transversely on an earlier cut. Thus each cut is a crosscut with its endpoints on the original boundary. Each new crosscut adds a face and one adjacency, and the dual graph is connected and a tree. There are at most r cuts. The final faces have four convex corners and no reflexes, so they are rectangles. The horizontal construction gives a second tree. This part requires only strict simplicity and n≥1.

Fix one vertical-sweep rectangle R=[0,L]×[0,H]. Its upper and lower sides are physical walls. All its doors lie on its vertical sides. Deleting R from the tree leaves components each joined to it by a single open segment. Closing just that segment gives a strictly simple lattice chamber: it is the region cut off by that crosscut before making its internal subdivisions. Internal subdivision lines play no role in its billiards. In particular two marks in the same attached component can both access the **same** door; there is no need to reflect off some other artificial edge. For a fixed decomposition the sequence of distinct pieces of a simple tree route is unique. A billiard can revisit pieces. Moving a cut changes the names of the pieces, not which physical crosscuts separate the marks.

## Whole-room bound with an unobstructed far wall

Let B≥1 bound the cell area of the largest component attached to R, put T=1+F_(12B−1), and let Z=4HT². Here F₀=0,F₁=1. Assume L≥Z. The lattice portal bound is independently reviewed. [Effective chamber theorem](effective-chamber.md) · [d124 review](../../illumination-generalization-2026-11-06/fable/directions-2026-11-09/d124-review-eff-chamber/review.md). Each exterior lattice mark has a legal first hit at its unique door for every slope in the *same* closed interval of width δ=1/(2T²) around the fixed irrational reference slope. The door can occupy just part of the rectangle side. Using a larger B only shortens the interval. All interior sheet signs at first access are known from the chosen branch. Count marks on open doors as marks of R.

For a fixed piece R, let k* be the maximum first full index over all attached chambers, each with its actual door to R. One may replace T by T*=1+F_(k*+2) and Z by 4H(T*)² everywhere below. Taking the maximum gives a single interval valid for every exterior mark in every attachment. In a test involving only two named attachments the maximum over those two suffices. In this partial-door assembly the modulus 2H comes from the receiving rectangle. Its entry doors may occupy different heights; this differs from a straight full-width corridor, whose mouths must share one height span.

**If one vertical side of R has no doors, every two distinct interior lattice marks of the whole room are lit.** Put all doors on the left. For two exterior marks trace both up to their first doors and reflect the target configuration across x=L. Between the two hit points the developed travel is 2L. In the signed transverse coordinate modulo 2H the mismatch has derivative 2L+a+b, with a,b the two first-hit travels. Its span exceeds 2H. A matching slope therefore connects the legs; there is no intermediate visit to any left door. The only intermediate vertical contact is the far wall. If it is a corner, the formal path reverses both velocity components and retraces. Truncate the out-and-back part to join the two distinct marks legally, as in the [cap projection lemma](degree-gluing.md). This argument also works when the two legs started in different attached components; if a corner retrace cannot connect those components, that exceptional contact cannot have occurred in the matched path.

For one exterior mark and a target in R at depth x from the left, use the reflected depth 2L−x≥L and the specified target ordinate. The one-leg mismatch span is (2L−x+a)δ>2H, and the same cap reasoning applies. This includes a target on an open door. For two marks of R the chord suffices unless they are on the same side and the joining segment includes physical wall. In that case both are on open left doors and the single open far-wall reflection at the average of their heights joins them. The assembly allows any number of partial doors on the near side and arbitrary lattice chambers behind them. No isolated mark placements remain in this case.

**Explicit conditional area cutoff.** If H≤D and all attachments have area ≤B, there are at most r attachments, so L H≥n−rB. Hence for the class just described it suffices that

    n ≥ rB + 4 D² (1+F_(12B−1))².

For r≤8 replace r by 8. If a family has a certified common first full index at most k₀ for every attachment, the same proof gives n≥rB+4D²(1+F_(k₀+2))². A first full index measured on one fixed layout alone supplies only its instance test. This bound concerns a room possessing that decomposition and those B,D. A rectangular room with no attachments is direct. If D and B are allowed to grow with n, the display is a test on the actual geometry, not an upper bound on counterexample area.

## Two-ended pieces and the residual

For the same R and B, when L≥Z the following finer reduction holds without assuming a closed end.

- Two exterior marks accessing opposite ends are lit. The signed mismatch derivative is L+a+b; there is no intermediate vertical contact. Narrower openings on either end do not affect the period 2H.
- A mark outside and one in R at depth x≥Z measured from its access end are lit by the one-leg equation with travel x. Marks in R whose open chord stays in the room are lit.
- For two exterior marks at the same end, a single far-wall reflection suffices if either parity of the middle crossing is safe throughout the common chart. Precisely, let f(s), g(s) be the two signed access heights in [−H,H], using the required forward target sign. At a root of 2Ls+f(s)−g(s)=2kH the middle height is (f(s)+g(s))/2+kH. The mismatch span exceeds 4H, so either parity of k can be prescribed. It suffices that one parity, after folding, misses the closed far doors and corners throughout the chart. In particular this can be checked by using the two door centers with an error of at most one quarter of the sum of their widths. For marks on disjoint doors on the same side of R the same test works with zero-length exterior legs and a free choice of their transverse signs; at exact span 4H use L>Z or check the desired roots directly.

Thus a dark pair for a long two-ended piece must be a same-end pair failing the far-wall test, or an exterior mark paired with a mark in its near collar of depth <Z (including a mouth). The same-side chord exception for two R marks is included as a same-end pair. This is a restriction on the *endpoint locations*, beyond just the shape of the room. A two-ended separating corridor with rectangular end chambers and full corridor-width openings has the sharper whole-room L>4d theorem, including the collar. [Reviewed rectangular case](resonant-gluing.md)

For a piece with both dimensions large, [opposite-door access](large-rectangle-access.md) supplies its additional scale test even if L<Z. Its fixed access interval is provided by the tree's single attached chambers. Same-end access retains a middle-wall obstruction. The [perpendicular-door test](rectangle-windings.md) can be used with other cuts that genuinely yield those openings, but neither canonical parallel decomposition ever *requires* such a pair of doors. Both orientations may be used and a pair discharged by either. Nothing in the rectangle count bounds the areas of both access components, the mark-to-door distances, or the fraction of the opposite wall occupied by other doors.

An explicit way to describe the remaining class for this assembly is to retain only nondirect marked rooms that survive **both** parallel decompositions: discard the whole room on any one-ended bound or applicable reviewed whole-room certificate; discard the pair on any eligible opposite-end, depth, or safe middle-wall bound above or the opposite-door scale test. For every remaining piece with L≥Z the pair has the same-end or near-collar positioning just proved. If there is no such piece, the room remains unless another certificate applies. One may also remove the named capped crosses by their all-pair result. This gives computable geometric exclusions, not a claim that the survivors are dark. The substantial additional residual is several growing pieces or growing access chambers. It is present before a shared-corner winding issue is reached.

**A cut-independent scale obstruction in cov279.** In the above primitive stretch the entire room is at most 6 high and 6q wide. The bottom block (0,2q)×(0,2) and top block (4q,6q)×(4,6) each have area 4q. No inscribed axis-aligned rectangle meets both in positive area: its product of coordinate intervals would include a bottom-height point with abscissa greater than 4q, outside the room. For *any* central piece of *any* decomposition into at most seven rectangles, the exterior therefore has area at least 4q. At least one other piece has area ≥4q/6. For a parallel cut tree the largest attached sealed chamber hence has area at least 2q/3, independent of which orientation or piece is chosen. The longest possible piece side is 6q. The all-attachment Fibonacci length threshold Z already exceeds that for the saved q≥16, since the transverse integral length is at least one and B≥ceil(2q/3). Both the small-attachment hypothesis and a central piece growing in both physical dimensions fail for unbounded n. This proves that a different decomposition cannot enforce the proposed uniform scale alternatives, even if the shared-corner issue is set aside. It does **not** claim that every possible geometric certificate fails; this very marked pair has a different exact certificate.

## Census consequence and next lemma

The unconditional threshold for a decomposition into r+1 pieces is N=1; the unconditional area cutoff for illumination is undetermined. The conditional formula above proves a whole-room tail for its class and cannot be substituted into a census for the complement. D102 verified 3,933,192 saved witnesses in 72.5 CPU s, about 54,251/s. Over d73's guessed 10¹¹–10¹² rooms even through area 36, a one-witness-per-room replay at that rate would cost roughly 510–5,100 CPU-hours, before enumeration and witness production. No finite run through a proven universal N can be recommended. [D102](../../illumination-generalization-2026-11-06/fable/directions-2026-11-09/d102-qclass-replay/review.md) · [D73](../../illumination-generalization-2026-11-06/fable/directions-2026-11-09/d73-bbbb-21-36/README.md)

The single bound to improve is the **common first-door access and phase placement bound for a marked pair across an unavoidable cut**, in terms of reflex count and local dimensions rather than total attachment area. In the cov279 stretch the actual crossing grid has spacing one inside a door of width three even while both sides grow. Any general bound needs to exploit that kind of arithmetic or other actual placement information. A smaller winding constant in a large central rectangle cannot help where no such rectangle exists.
