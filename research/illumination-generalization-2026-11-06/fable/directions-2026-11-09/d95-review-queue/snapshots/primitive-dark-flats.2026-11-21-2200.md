# Primitive marked rooms on a dark flat

**Result for review.** Conditional on the candidate finite-flat theorem, every maximal dark flat in a wall-and-mark order cell splits into independent horizontal and vertical factors. Given an integer basis for the rational part of each factor, there is a terminating test that either constructs a separately normalized lattice room with same-parity marks on the flat or certifies that none exists. No bound on the area needs to be supplied to this test. The outstanding effective input is a coefficient bound, or a complete list, for the factors. This note does not provide either.

## Independent axes

Use the gap coordinates of [parametric-orbits.md](parametric-orbits.md). The order cell is the product of positive orthants for the two axes, with at most eleven gap variables per axis at W≤20. Every map (x,y)↦(ax,by), a,b>0, commutes with reflections in axis-aligned walls, maps corners to corners and takes straight segments to straight segments. It preserves the physical dark set, and hence permutes its finite family of maximal supporting flats in the cell. The transformations vary continuously in a connected group. The image of any fixed flat in that finite family is therefore constant. Common dilation makes the flat L a linear subspace. For (x,y)∈L subtract (x,y) from (2x,y) to obtain (x,0)∈L, and similarly (0,y)∈L. Thus L=Lx×Ly. The rational spans of the integer points factor in the same way. This uses finite *global* flatness, the new input for review, rather than an effective classification of orbit closures.

## The one-axis primitive test

Let V be a supplied rational linear subspace in the one-axis gaps. Work in the lattice Λ of integral points of V satisfying the endpoint parity equation: the sum of the gaps between the two marked coordinate levels is even. Equal mark coordinates impose no condition. Let A send the fine gap vector to the gaps between consecutive distinct **wall** levels, by summing the constituent gaps. The extremal levels are walls because both marks are interior. The sum of the components of Az is the bounding span. Primitivity on this axis is exactly gcd(Az)=1. In fact every projected wall-edge displacement is a difference of wall levels, and following the boundary from a fixed vertex expresses every such level difference as a sum of edge displacements. The two lists generate the same ideal in ℤ. This is the per-axis normalization needed for the room problem.

Compute a basis of Λ by integer linear algebra and the image lattice Γ=AΛ. First test by rational linear feasibility whether V has a strictly positive vector. If not, reject. If so, a strictly positive vector exists in Λ by rational approximation, clearing denominators and doubling. For the remaining steps z>0 means all gaps are strictly positive.

* **Image rank zero:** impossible for z>0, since Az sums positive gaps.
* **Image rank one:** compute a generator g of Γ. Only the two wall vectors ±g could be primitive, and only when gcd(g)=1. For each positive choice test Az=±g, z∈Λ, z>0. This is finite: each constituent integer gap lies between one and the corresponding fixed wall gap. Rank one primitivity fixes the entire wall shape on this axis, including its scale.
* **Image rank at least two:** a primitive positive solution exists **iff** the first Smith invariant of Γ in its ambient wall-coordinate lattice is one. This invariant is just the gcd of all entries of any basis matrix of Γ.

Necessity in the last case is immediate since that gcd divides every coordinate of every wall vector. For sufficiency, Smith reduction with unimodular row and column operations supplies z₀∈Λ with a=Az₀ primitive (the ambient unimodular map takes the first Smith column to a primitive vector). Positivity is not required of z₀. Choose v∈Λ strictly positive such that b=Av is not proportional to a. This choice is effective: start with a positive lattice vector. If its image is proportional, add a basis vector of Λ with nonproportional image to a sufficiently large multiple of the positive vector. Choose any nonzero two-row minor of [a b], and call its absolute value M. Then

    z(t) = z₀ + t M v

is strictly positive for all sufficiently large positive integers t and belongs to Λ. Any prime dividing every entry of a+tMb divides the chosen minor, since det[a+tMb,b] on those rows equals det[a,b]. For primes dividing M the vector reduces to a modulo that prime, and a is primitive. No prime can divide every coordinate. Thus the entire positive tail is primitive. The first t making every gap positive can be taken directly from the coordinate inequalities. This argument avoids imposing a variable gcd in Presburger arithmetic.

Pair an accepted x vector and an accepted y vector. The product-flat property puts the resulting marked room in L, positivity puts it in the original cell, and the two built-in parity checks and both wall gcds hold. If both images have rank one there are only finitely many primitive marked possibilities on this flat. A rank-at-least-two accepted factor gives a full affine progression of primitive rooms with the other axis fixed. If both have rank at least two, either progression parameter can vary independently. In the one-axis progression all wall gaps in the varying axis grow and a horizontal (or vertical) slice through an occupied cell has positive fixed thickness, so area goes to infinity.

## An additional restriction at large primitive area

If a dark flat contains even one normalized integral room of area at least 120, the wall projection A is injective on each **real** factor Lx and Ly, conditional on the reviewed d80 exclusion of irrational dark marks. Otherwise hold the other axis fixed and perturb the gap coordinates of this axis a small real distance in a nonzero kernel direction. Wall levels stay fixed. At least one mark moves since wall and mark levels together recover every fine gap. Positivity, strict interiority, distinctness and darkness persist on this part of the flat. Some choice of the perturbation makes that mark irrational in the original normalized room, contrary to d80. This only uses d80 in the room where it applies. Hence on these flats the two marked coordinates per axis are linear functions of the wall levels on the projected wall subspace. On its rational part these functions have rational matrices in rational bases. Their denominators and coefficient sizes remain unbounded by the present proof. In particular an accepted higher-rank factor, through the primitive positive progression above, puts its product flat in this injective regime as soon as the other factor admits a primitive witness. ## What effectivity now requires

If an integer H bounds independent integer defining rows for the rational parts of the dark flats in the combined coordinates, the same H suffices on each axis: set the other variables to zero in the equations of the product space. There is an explicit terminating finite reduction for **primitive** inputs with H supplied:

1. For every nonempty order cell, enumerate one-axis matrices with at most eleven columns, at most as many independent rows as columns, and entries in [−H,H]. Include the empty matrix. Apply the one-axis primitive test to each kernel and save one positive witness from every successful test.
2. For that cell take the Cartesian product of the saved x and y witnesses. Every candidate is a normalized, same-parity marked lattice room. Check each by the individual-room decision procedure. There is no need to know *which* matrices describe dark spaces.
3. If a primitive dark example exists, the true pair of rational factor spaces occurs in the matrix list; their saved witnesses together are on that same dark flat and will detect darkness. Conversely every dark result is an actual counterexample.

For a one-axis cell of dimension d≤11 the crude matrix count is at most (d+1)(2H+1)^(d²), before removing duplicate kernels or rejecting negative cells. Thus there are at most 144(2H+1)^242 witness pairs per order cell. The primitive test constructs finite coordinates for each. Running it on that finite list would also give a computable area cutoff by taking the maximum constructed area. This is a conditional design, not a proposed run: qualitative semilinearity has supplied no numerical H.

**Effectivity audit.** The d130 reflection bounds concern the number of segments needed for a lit example. They give neither coefficients nor lower bounds on the shortest lattice point of a *dark* factor. Likewise non-Presburger supplied-direction residues do not exclude this factor test, which concerns the all-direction dark locus. Requiring independent normalization no longer adds a second unknown theorem once the rational factors are bounded.

**Next coefficient question.** It suffices to bound the integer equations of each maximal flat's one-axis rational span on at most eleven variables. Rank one requires a bound on the primitive wall-gap generator if a compatible positive lift exists. In higher rank the Smith and progression construction handles primitivity, but one still needs control of the wall ratios and of the marked-coordinate relations defining V. Counting the number of flats alone gives neither bound.
