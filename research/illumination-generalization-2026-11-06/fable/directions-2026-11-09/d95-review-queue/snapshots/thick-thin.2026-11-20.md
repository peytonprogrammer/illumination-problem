# A compact-cover reduction and a uniform shrinking-door theorem

**Verdict.** Openness gives an exact, conditional reduction to a finite cover of marked shape space. The missing inputs include coverage of the thick interior and uniform certificates on the actual degeneration faces. A collapsing doorway is one such face. For rectangular chambers there is a width-independent cross-pair theorem below, including a precise arithmetic exception. The exception includes the lattice core.

## The space to cover

Translate the bounding box to the origin and normalize its **larger side** to one. This preserves rational coordinates under rescaling a lattice room and gives diameter between one and √2. Literal Euclidean diameter one need not give rational coordinates or a polytope. Fix cyclic edge labels and the weak orders of the horizontal and vertical supporting lines. In gap coordinates these are finitely many bounded polyhedral charts, with the choice of maximal side fixed. Keep the strictly simple charts and include lower-dimensional valid charts with coincident supporting coordinates. Record which closed rectangular cells contain S,T. Take the closures of the valid marked charts; this is a finite compactification. Its extra points can have coincident marks, marks on walls, pinches, touching components, collapsed rooms or several gaps vanishing at different rates. No billiard law on those extra points is required.

A legal finite path between distinct interior marks persists as the shape and both marks vary. Reflect successive labeled copies, join the first mark to the developed last mark, and follow the same ordered crossings. Every crossing is transverse and in the open wall, every unused vertex has positive clearance along the relevant segment, and the endpoints are interior. The finitely many crossing inequalities persist. A direct chord strictly inside has the same property. For a fixed word this is a **semialgebraic** certificate in the joint variables: image coordinates are affine, and segment-edge intersection, order and interior conditions can be expressed using polynomial signs or quantified intersection parameters. The determinants need not be linear once shape and both endpoints vary.

**Conditional covering theorem.** Suppose every point of the added boundary admits a relative neighborhood on which all valid distinct interior configurations have an effective lighting certificate. A certificate may be a theorem choosing an unbounded itinerary as a function of the inputs. It must handle mixed and simultaneous degenerations, including the endpoint positions. Require verifiable domains, for example rational semialgebraic neighborhoods or boxes and proved bounds. Removing a finite union of such neighborhoods leaves a compact interior remainder. If every point of that remainder is lit, finitely many legal words cover it. Conversely an exact check that a proposed list of word domains covers the remainder proves lighting there. These two certificates, repeated over the finitely many charts, imply the W≤20 goal. The proof is the finite-subcover theorem, applied first to the boundary and then to the remainder. When all domains are rational semialgebraic, containment can be checked by real quantifier elimination. Enumerating finite word lists would terminate **if** the remainder is fully lit; compactness gives neither a word bound nor a decision procedure for a negative answer.

In particular, openness, thin tails and corner control alone do not prove lighting at an interior thick point. An edge contraction can use induction where a limiting witness survives with clearance from the entire collapsed feature; an indentation collapsing to a doubled segment can leave a slit across a path that was legal after erasing the slit. Pinches or width and length vanishing together need their own lifting arguments. A proof for opposite chambers also leaves pairs with both marks on the same side or in disappearing material. ## Endpoint faces

Near an interior diagonal point, two nearby marks see each other by a chord in a common disk. The same argument uses a convex half-disk at a smooth wall or a convex quadrant at a convex vertex, on a nondegenerating shape chart. For distinct limiting marks, boundary-to-boundary legs with strictly inward departures/arrivals and clearance at all intermediate contacts persist for interior perturbations in the appropriate endpoint sectors. Taking all sectors matters at a reflex corner.

In fact, a reflex corner has **no purely local all-pairs lemma**. In a small sector of angle 3π/2 take equal-radius points at angles ε and 3π/2−ε measured from one wall, with 0<ε<π/4. Unfolding across the two rays gives angle coordinates modulo 3π, with endpoint representatives ±ε and ±(3π/2−ε). Their least angular separation is 3π/2−2ε>π. A straight segment avoiding the origin sweeps less than π of continuous polar angle. Hence no legal path staying in this sector joins them, however small the radius. A global excursion could light them. This explains why treating the reflex diagonal by direct chords or by local corner reflections fails even for a fixed thick shape. The fixed-room shear certificates below are examples of the extra type of argument required.

## Two different uniform corridor limits

**Positive relative door width.** Measure each configuration in a common chamber scale d, for example the larger end-chamber diameter. Let the scaled *sealed chambers with their labeled open doors* vary in a compact family of strictly simple nondegenerate charts with noncollapsing doors. Assume the separating corridor construction is valid. There is a constant K for the family such that L/d≥K lights every pair of interior marks in opposite chambers.

Indeed the first-hit proof chooses a common generic slope for the two inward chamber rays, reaches both open doors in positive time, and avoids vertices. For the finitely many transverse collisions, varying the wall coordinates and door endpoints continuously preserves the word exactly as varying the marks does. On a relative neighborhood of the shape and marked starts there is therefore a common closed slope interval of positive width. Use closed cell charts and inward starts at boundary points as in the reviewed compactness proof. A finite subcover of the compact marked chamber family gives a minimum interval width δ>0. With scaled door width bounded above by w_max, K=2w_max/δ suffices by the phase derivative L/d+a+b, where the two travels are in the same units. This is a uniform theorem for real orthogonal chambers: rational angles suffice for the first-hit argument. It does not include a limiting **zero-width** marked door. A normalized corridor can have w/L→0 while L/d stays bounded and w/d→0. [Reviewed first-hit argument](README.md) · [Explicit rectangular bound](length-periodicity.md)

**Shrinking doors in rectangles.** Let the two end chambers have horizontal depths D₁,D₂ and heights H₁,H₂. Their vertical doors share any positive width w and may be placed anywhere on the facing sides where the assembly is valid. Let x,z be the marks' horizontal distances to their respective doors, strictly between zero and the chamber depths. Select

    a ∈ {x, 2D₁−x},       b ∈ {z, 2D₂−z}.

The second choice at either end means starting toward the outer vertical cap and reflecting there once. If L>|a−b| and H₁b/(H₂a) is irrational, the marks are lit **for every w**. The assertion is uniform in both mark heights and in the door placements.

To prove it, let s be positive absolute slope and prescribe upward vertical velocity at the source door in forward time and downward at the target door in backward time. In each rectangular vertical unfolding, visits to the door center on those respective branches have slope centers forming arithmetic progressions of steps 2H₁/a and 2H₂/b. One may launch with the desired vertical signs and choose the center images giving the desired terminal signs. As s varies, the accepted open slope windows have widths w/a and w/b. There is no earlier visit to the door wall on either chosen leg. Temporarily continue an exact outer-cap corner hit by the rectangular unfolding, and remove it below.

Choose a closed slope interval of width δ less than w/max(a,b) but greater than 2w/(L+a+b); the strict length condition is exactly what allows it. The two slope phases form a dense positive half-line on their product of circles by irrationality, so the centers can be matched within any positive tolerance in slope. Both open windows therefore contain a common closed interval of width δ. There the signed corridor mismatch is affine with derivative L+a+b, even across a formal cap-corner hit, by rectangular unfolding. Its range contains a multiple of 2w, giving a matching slope and strictly interior door crossings. Every corridor bounce is smooth.

An exact cap-corner hit needs care: deleting its parameter from an interval could delete the only matching root. Instead observe that the two rectangular reflections there reverse **both** velocity components. Formal continuation retraces the incoming leg exactly, including its horizontal-wall reflections, and revisits its starting mark before reaching the door. Start at this later visit and discard the excursion. The remaining leg travels toward the door with no intermediate vertical wall, so has no corner hit. Apply the same truncation at the target in backward time if needed. No forbidden reflection is retained, and neither signed door phase changes. This finishes the proof.

**Consequence for compact chamber families.** If L≥2 max(D₁,D₂), the length inequality holds strictly for all four choices. Failure of the irrationality test for every choice is equivalent to the three rationality conditions

    x/D₁ ∈ ℚ,      z/D₂ ∈ ℚ,      H₁D₂/(H₂D₁) ∈ ℚ.

Indeed, holding b fixed and comparing the two a choices gives (2D₁−x)/x rational, and similarly for z. Substituting into the direct/direct ratio gives the third condition. Conversely those three conditions make all four ratios rational. In particular, whenever the two rectangular aspect ratios have irrational quotient, **all real opposite-chamber pairs are lit**, uniformly as w→0. The same explicit inequality works over any compact family satisfying these hypotheses; no Diophantine approximation rate is needed because the witness may make arbitrarily many horizontal-wall reflections. Even for commensurable aspect ratios, a cross-pair with either horizontal offset an irrational fraction of its chamber depth is covered. This is an actual shrinking-door result for macroscopic separation. It does not cover shorter normalized passages or the triply rational placements, which include the marked lattice problem. The progression argument is the irrational counterpart of the [rational chain test](chain-criteria.md).

**Why finite words alone fail on a collapsing door.** Let the horizontal room span be B and the endpoint heights differ by Δ>0. Every cross-pair path traverses the corridor at least once. With Nₕ horizontal-wall and Nᵥ vertical-wall hits and slope magnitude s, total horizontal travel is at most (Nᵥ+1)B. A complete traversal contributes vertical travel Ls≤(Nₕ+1)w since each monotone vertical piece in the strip travels at most w. Thus

    (Nₕ+1)(Nᵥ+1) ≥ Δ L/(Bw).

Take fixed rectangles of incommensurable aspect ratios, fixed L≥2 max(D₁,D₂), fixed marks of distinct heights, and doors shrinking about smooth wall points. All those pairs are lit by the theorem, yet no finite list of reflection words can certify this sequence. Diameter normalization preserves the inequality. The chamber shapes even remain fixed. A neighborhood certificate at that face must allow unbounded itineraries or use another geometric invariant. This bound is stronger than observing that the old common-slope interval can shrink: it concerns every actual witness.

## What the evidence supports

The all-real assertion is plausible for W≤20 but remains open for arbitrary real side lengths. The [20-wall Q-class closure](../twenty/README.md) proves illumination for all real interior marks in its lattice-room class. The [20-wall two-bay theorem](convex-cylinder.md) gives BBBB examples at unbounded area with the same real-mark conclusion. [D74](../../illumination-generalization-2026-11-06/fable/directions-2026-11-09/d74-continuum-check/README.md) and [d100](../../illumination-generalization-2026-11-06/fable/directions-2026-11-09/d100-check-residual/README.md) check fixed shapes, including residual endpoint neighborhoods; varying the shape adds independent variables. The strictly simple orthogonal R22 room has a dark interior pair (0,0),(2,2) at W=22, in an ordinary nondegenerate shape. Wolecki also exhibits a 22-sided polygon with a non-illuminating pair. Neither supplies a W≤20 orthogonal counterexample. [R22 manuscript](../../illumination-publication/repository/preprints/twenty-two-wall/manuscript.tex) · [Wolecki, Fig. 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.09358)

If one restricts shape coordinates to rational values, scaling recovers lattice rooms; the endpoints of the all-real lattice-room goal must still be allowed arbitrary real values. For the lattice-mark subproblem all coordinates may be taken rational. Neither rational configuration space is compact. Density plus openness of lighting does not exclude an irrational dark limit, and proving every rational tuple lit does not itself give a finite open subcover. No counterexample to the stronger W≤20 assertion is established here.

The first unresolved thin case highlighted by the theorem is a collapsing door between commensurable rectangular chambers with both horizontal offsets rational fractions of their depths. Even there the necessary witness complexity can diverge. An effective neighborhood certificate must control the **placement** of the signed crossing phases in that resonant regime, or use a different mechanism. Merely shrinking the aperture inside the old compact family supplies no such certificate.
