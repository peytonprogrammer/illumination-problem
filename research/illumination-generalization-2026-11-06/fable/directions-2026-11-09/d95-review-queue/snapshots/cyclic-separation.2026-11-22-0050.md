# Can two primitive classes fail together?

**Verdict.** The two-class conjecture remains open. For any two reference classes, their regular-step products already have *transitive normal closure* on the sheets of a BBBB room. Thus a nonconstant monodromy-invariant quotient label preserved pointwise by those steps is impossible. The missing implication is from single-arc failure to the existence of that label. The weaker one-class proof encounters the same gap. A separate winding argument gives an exact geometric condition forcing two or three classes in one open cylinder.

## What one can prove about the proposed block

Work in the orthogonal pillowcase dictionary with four peripheral permutations A,B,C,D in a common marking, product identity. Each has cycle lengths 1,2,3. For X among them write u_X=X³, an involution allowing precisely the regular swaps. The source sheets of a physical mark over a form a u_A-invariant set. In an adapted marking the reachable endpoint sets for a single b-arc are tested by powers of u_A u_B. Varying the marking matters: a primitive class generally contains infinitely many arcs. [Single-arc test](deletion-invariance.md)

**Exact criterion over every slope.** Fix the source parity a. For a primitive vector v in class c, take an arc-adapted marking of the pillowcase double, with endpoint permutations Aᵥ,Dᵥ. In that *same marking* let Iᵥ,Jᵥ be the sheet sets incident to the two physical source and two physical target lifts, respectively. Put Lᵥ=Aᵥ³Dᵥ³. The pair is lit in class c exactly when

    for some primitive v ≡ c (mod 2) and some integer k, Lᵥᵏ Iᵥ meets Jᵥ.

A regular arc alternates the two swaps, and Iᵥ is invariant under Aᵥ³, so powers give exactly the legal endpoints for this v. Conversely a legal path between the lattice lifts develops in a primitive integer direction, and its successive primitive steps project alternately along the same pillowcase arc. This proves the stated equivalence. Two-class illumination asks for this condition in at least two distinct c. The exponents, directions and lift choices may differ between successes.

For a *fixed* room this is also a finite all-slope statement. Take the subgroup of Γ(2) admitting affine lifts that preserve the corner stops and fix the regular lifts of the marks pointwise. It has finite index: the action on finite-degree punctured covers has finite orbit, and on the cover the additional finite fiber data have finite orbit. One direction from each orbit of this subgroup on primitive unoriented directions in c suffices. Indeed the lift carries a regular segment to a regular segment in the new direction with the same marked endpoints; its inverse gives the converse. Finiteness follows since Γ(2) is transitive in each primitive parity class. No cusp enumeration or room-independent bound is needed for this equivalence. The transitivity theorem below concerns a *normal closure* and does not imply a hit in any one of these single-direction tests. [Cusp and arc dictionary](per-class-converse.md)

**Normal-closure theorem.** Let G be the sheet monodromy and let K be the normal closure in G of u_A u_B and u_A u_C. Its orbits form one block in any normalized BBBB orthogonal room. The same statement holds for either other choice of two target sites.

**Proof.** On the set of K-orbits the images of u_A,u_B,u_C are one involution u. It commutes with each of the images of A,B,C, since it is a power of each. Those three generate the quotient action by the product relation. Thus u is central. The canonical relation B_a is the orbit relation of the normal closure of u_A: the permutation u_A consists of exactly the regular transpositions, and taking all conjugates gives the finest invariant equivalence merging their endpoints. BBBB makes that normal closure transitive on the original sheets, hence on K-orbits. Its image is now just ⟨u⟩. A central involution acting transitively leaves at most two K-orbits. Two would give a degree-two sphere factor of Q. Universality at each site forces its peripheral permutation to move the two blocks (a trivial permutation would kill the whole corresponding regular-swap normal closure). Four transpositions give total ramification four, but a degree-two map from the intermediate sphere to the sphere has total ramification two. The intermediate is a sphere since it is covered by the sphere Q. Hence exactly one orbit remains. [Canonical relation](auxiliary-exclusion.md) · [Reviewed small-factor exclusion](exclusion-descent.md)

The conclusion also applies to a putative common separating partition: **if** its parts are fixed individually by both regular-step products and G permutes the parts, then it has only one part. Mere invariance as a partition permits permutation of the parts by the two products and does not give this conclusion. In particular connectivity of a cover alone allows many invariant partitions.

**Where the contradiction breaks.** Failure in two classes says that each individual cyclic orbit of the source avoids the target for every admissible arc in those classes. A union of such orbits need not be closed under applying the next cyclic action. Even closure under that next action does not imply closure under arbitrary G-conjugates with the endpoint labels held fixed. The block generated in the theorem above enforces *both* operations. Neither is a legal continuation of a fixed shot.

There is a physical control for this logical gap even with the quantifier over **all** arcs. In cov4181 the pair (2,3),(4,3) fails both c and d in every marking (d144). Since c has no regular points, u_C=1. Consequently the normal closure of just u_A u_C equals the normal closure of u_A, which is transitive since B_a is universal in this room. Thus two class failures really can coexist with this precise transitivity property on a strictly simple W=20 room. Its canonical degrees, in a,b,c,d order, are 1,1,28,1, so it leaves the BBBB conjecture open. Its proper four-sheet factor separates the marks by an arc-component argument; the two products do not both act trivially on its blocks. ## A geometric two-class theorem

Take two lifts in one open cylinder after cutting at all physical corners. In integral unimodular coordinates with horizontal core, let C be its circumference and (u,h) the developed displacement. Every (u+kC,h) is a legal chord. If h=0 only the core class occurs. Otherwise put t=v₂(C), b=v₂(h), with v₂(0)=∞ when applied to u. The **complete** primitive-class count among these chords is

| Condition | Classes in horizontal cylinder coordinates |
|---|---|
| min(v₂(u),b) < t | Only the primitive parity of (u,h) |
| 2ᵗ divides u and b=t | (0,1) and (1,1) |
| 2ᵗ divides u and b>t | All three |

Indeed in the first case dividing by the common power of two makes addition of kC invisible modulo two. In the other cases the odd part of C is invertible modulo every power of two. Choose k so that u+kC is zero or 2ᵇ modulo 2ᵇ⁺¹, giving the two classes with odd transverse component. When b>t, one can also make the longitudinal valuation exactly t, giving the horizontal class. Removing an odd common divisor preserves parity. The unimodular change permutes the three nonzero classes. For same-parity lifts C,u,h are even in the normalized orthogonal coordinates. In particular an open common cylinder of circumference 2 modulo 4, with distinct transverse heights, always proves the two-class theorem for that pair.

This yields a necessary condition on any counterexample to two-class illumination: in **every** open common cylinder and for **every** compatible lift choice, the heights coincide or the displacement has strictly smaller 2-adic content than the circumference. There is an additional consistency requirement: the singleton classes so obtained must agree in physical coordinates across all those cylinders.

In the nine saved d72 BBBB rooms, evaluating this formula on the one stored common-cylinder witness per pair gives exactly one/two/three classes for 661/84/2 pairs. Adding the stored straight-path witness gives 506/239/2. These are counts certified by those particular witnesses. D66/d69 already report that all 747 pairs are lit in all three primitive classes, and d147 independently obtains all three for all 574 pairs on the seven-unit cross. The earlier 272/272 result concerns the restricted d147 pencils; it is not the strongest physical evidence. ## One-class follow-on and factor degrees

With all three classes assumed to fail, adding u_A u_D to the normal closure above still gives one orbit. Thus taking the monodromy-invariant closure of legal step identifications cannot yield a separating sheet block even in the putative dark case. This rules out that construction; it gives no contradiction with darkness. A useful factor would have to retain the action *inside the arc graph of the quotient*, as cov4181 does. Its blocks may move under a regular-step product.

Reviewed coverage excludes proper outer degrees 2–4 in BBBB. For degree 5 the separate partial argument leaves the collapsed-mark configurations in U,U,Z,Z and U,V,V,Z; the possible physical areas are respectively 20 and 20,25,30,35,40. Area 20 is already closed for existence of one physical shot in the lattice census. The general degree-5 classwise claim remains unproved. No general reconnection theorem covers outer degrees ≥6. For a particular room of n sheets, a proper factor of outer degree m must divide n, with 2≤n/m≤r: the factor has a zero by the normalized-period argument, and that zero requires one upstairs reflex per inner sheet. The primitive case has no proper factor and remains open. The degree-24 and degree-48 factors of the named cross are settled for that room by d147. **Next question.** Can a physically lit pair in BBBB have the same singleton primitive parity on every available cylinder and every other legal shot? One exact restriction is that any integral-affine automorphism preserving the corner stops and both physical endpoint lift sets (allowing their exchange) must stabilize that parity modulo two: apply it to one legal chord, and use reversibility. A pair stabilizer containing a three-cycle modulo two therefore gives all three classes as soon as one class is lit. The built-in axial sign changes act trivially modulo two. The useful geometric target is two genuinely different cylinder displacement cosets with incompatible singleton parities. For a wholly dark pair, existence of even one open common cylinder would suffice and remains unproved.
