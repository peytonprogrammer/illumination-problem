# Parametric darkness and parametric directional visibility differ

**Candidate theorem for independent review.** Fix a cyclic orthogonal polygon type and distinct interior marks. In wall and mark coordinates, on each of finitely many relatively open rational polyhedral cells C, the set of dark real configurations is a finite union of sets C ∩ L with L a real affine flat. Consequently its restriction to integral coordinates, including a same-parity test, is Presburger definable. This is an existence theorem for the formula, not an extraction algorithm or a decision of emptiness for W≤20. It uses the reviewed d86/d116 marked-stratum inputs. A different conclusion holds with the direction supplied as an extra input: the exact directional relation already defines coprimality on a linear slice of the named cov279 rooms.

## 1. Geometry after eliminating the direction

Translate one room vertex to the origin, fix the axis directions, label all corners, and split by the weak orders of all x coordinates and all y coordinates of walls and marks. A retained cell specifies a strictly simple room, interior marks and their distinctness. These tests are constant on the cell because incidence with the axis-aligned grid is fixed. C is convex and relatively open in its affine span. Edge-length and coordinate presentations differ only by integral linear equalities. Work with all integral rooms; a primitive-normalization promise can be applied to the inputs. A separate gcd-one predicate on arbitrary tuples is not being asserted Presburger.

Let E be the locus in a translation stratum with its prescribed forbidden zeros and regular corner marks and two further distinct regular labels such that the two labels do not see one another. The legal segment persists locally, hence E is closed relative to the stratum and GL⁺-invariant. The project input is that E is a finite union of affine invariant loci, algebraic in the marked stratum and locally finite unions of linear branches in full (non-projectivized) period coordinates. Use EMM's closed-invariant-set theorem on the area-one slice and restore scaling, and Filip's algebraicity theorem. The two endpoint labels allow truncation if a path meets an endpoint early. On the orientation double take the intersection of the darkness conditions over **all** source and target lifts, forgetting the unused endpoint lifts in each test. Forbidden convex lifts are retained throughout. This is exactly physical darkness; the four-copy unfolding and folding preserve legal paths. [d86 proposition and corrected references](shadow-budget.md) · [Marked-stratum audit](generic-line.md) · [Filip, Theorem 5.4](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/2016/183-2/p05)

**Local linearity in C.** Glue the four signed copies of the physical room along edges. Subdivide them by the wall-and-mark grid and triangulate each occupied grid rectangle with a fixed diagonal. This is a topological marking over the cell and does not require declaring grid vertices forbidden. A relative-homology basis can be represented by paths along the subdivided edges. Each edge holonomy is an integral linear function of the wall and mark coordinates, with the sheet sign applied. Thus locally the period map in this marking is linear, including for the maps forgetting unused endpoint lifts. The pullback of E for each lift pair, and their finite intersection D, are locally finite unions of affine flats relative to C. This alone does not bound the number of flats near the boundary of C.

## 2. Global tameness in the physical coordinates

Here is an independent description of the *same* D that supplies the missing global input. Use the upper half-plane Schwarz–Christoffel representation of the polygon. Put infinity in the interior of a boundary edge by a real Möbius reparametrization. Let the finite ordered prevertices be a_i and set ε_i=+1 at a reflex corner and −1 at a convex corner. In the upper half-plane the derivative is

    f'(z) = c ∏ (z−a_i)^(ε_i/2).

Choose the analytic branches there. The sum of the exponents is −2, consistent with a regular boundary point at infinity. After multiplication by a nonzero complex constant and translation, vertex images give the polygon, and f(z_S), f(z_T) give its interior marks. The Schwarz–Christoffel theorem supplies parameters for every room in C. Conversely require the labelled boundary to be the specified simple polygon with the correct orientation. The primitive has continuous boundary extension including infinity, maps the edges monotonically, and has boundary winding one about each interior point. The argument principle gives the conformal bijection in this case. [Driscoll–Trefethen, Theorem 1.1, including the Möbius normalization](https://assets.cambridge.org/97805218/07265/excerpt/9780521807265_excerpt.pdf)

The graph of this coordinate presentation is definable with real parameters in the o-minimal structure ℝ_an,exp. To see this without any assertion about a global inverse period map, integrate from a fixed upper-half-plane point, e.g. i, along a straight segment to each prevertex and to z_S,z_T. Use real and imaginary parts. Each integrand on 0<t<1 is semialgebraic jointly in t and the parameters: choose the upper-half-plane square root factor by factor. The only endpoint singularity possible on these segments has exponent −1/2 and is integrable. Extend by zero outside the interval. Cluckers–Miller's stability-under-integration theorem gives constructible, hence ℝ_an,exp-definable, coordinate functions. Translating by the value at the chosen first vertex recovers the anchored coordinates. [Cluckers–Miller, Theorem 1.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/0911.4373) · [O-minimality of the ambient structure](https://www.math.ens.psl.eu/~zchatzid/papiers/o-min-21/DMM.pdf)

In these same parameters the translation unfolding and its marked fibers are algebraic data. The doubled sphere has quadratic differential c²∏(z−a_i)^ε_i dz²; Schwarz reflection across a horizontal or vertical edge gives the same continued quadratic differential (after fixing the orientation of the physical axes). Its orientation cover has a concrete smooth projective model obtained by completing

    v²=∏(z−a_i),       ω=c v dz / ∏_(ε_i=−1)(z−a_i).

At a convex prevertex ω is regular, and at a reflex prevertex it has order two. Branch points correspond to the labelled corner lifts. Each physical endpoint lifts over the two conjugate arguments z and its conjugate, with both choices of sheet. Conjugation is real algebraic. The family of curves, differentials and chosen sections on the finite root-choice cover defines an algebraic classifying map to the corresponding marked stratum (or work with its moduli stack and then take the coarse image). Thus testing membership in the algebraic E is definable in real and imaginary parts. No algebraic equations for E are known explicitly here. Finite lift intersections and projection through the definable Schwarz–Christoffel graph show that D is definable on C. Existence of a moduli presentation over the reals with real parameters suffices; this step does not invoke decidability of ℝ_an,exp.

## 3. Locally flat and globally definable gives finitely many flats

Let D be relatively closed in a relatively open convex cell C, definable in an o-minimal structure, and locally a finite union of whole relative affine-flat germs. A flat L supporting a local branch extends throughout L∩C. Indeed the set of points of L∩C with a relative L-neighborhood in D is open. At any limit of these points, closedness puts the point in D. A finite local union of flats covering an open patch of L must have one member containing L, since finitely many proper subspaces cannot cover a relatively open patch. This gives a neighborhood at the limit too. Connectedness of L∩C proves the extension.

Consider all inclusion-maximal affine flats L in aff(C) with nonempty L∩C contained in D. They cover D by the preceding extension and the finite bound on possible dimensions. They form a definable subset of the finite-dimensional space of affine flats: inclusion and the tests involving C and D are first-order real quantifiers. There are at most countably many. Cover C by countably many basis balls small enough for a finite local description (empty descriptions off D). For a maximal flat meeting such a ball, one local branch contains an open patch of that flat. Its own extension and maximality identify the two. Thus only finitely many maximal flats meet each ball. An o-minimal definable countable subset of Euclidean space is finite by cell decomposition. Use finitely many standard charts for the affine Grassmannians. This proves the claim globally on C, even when C approaches arbitrarily thin or multiply pinched rooms.

For any resulting flat L, L∩ℤ^k is empty or a translate of the subgroup dir(L)∩ℤ^k. A subgroup of ℤ^k has a finite integral basis, regardless of the field of definition of L. Existential integer coefficients of this basis give a Presburger formula. Intersect with C's rational linear conditions and endpoint parity and take the finite union. This argument concerns **total darkness**, after all directions have been accounted for by the marked locus.

**Follow-on and effective limit.** If there are f flats on C, an affine one-parameter family inside C is wholly dark or has at most f exceptional real parameters. On a ray extending to infinity the eventual Boolean period is therefore one (before imposing an input congruence). An exceptional parameter can be far out. The proof supplies neither f, the equations of the flats, the integral subgroup bases, nor the positions of the exceptions. Even one unknown flat in a cone can first meet the positive lattice at arbitrarily large coordinates, e.g. y=Mx. Per-instance decidability and qualitative semilinearity do not extract these data: even the uniformly decidable unary family whose member n records an algorithm's *first* halting step has empty-or-singleton semilinear fibers and no effective uniform threshold. This is an explanation of the inference gap, not a claim of undecidability for rooms. For a finite check the missing input is an effective description or bound for the rational spans of the lattice points on the physical dark branches, including which cells they occupy.

### A conditional numerical bound for the finite check

In gap coordinates C is a positive orthant: record the successive strictly positive differences between distinct x ranks and separately y ranks, anchoring the minimum in each axis at zero. At W≤20 there are at most twelve distinct coordinates on each axis after adding the two marks, hence at most d=22 gaps. Every maximal flat is linear in these coordinates. In fact positive simultaneous scaling acts on the finite family of maximal flats and preserves each one by continuity, so its affine offset is zero. For each flat use the rational subspace V spanned by its integer points. It has exactly the same integer points as L.

Suppose an effective H≥1 bounded the absolute entries in some independent integral defining rows for every such V, uniformly over the retained cells. Put Δ=ceil((√d H)^d). If V contains any strictly positive vector, intersect its nonnegative cone with the simplex whose coordinates sum to one. For each coordinate some vertex of this compact polytope has that coordinate positive. By Cramer's rule each vertex becomes integral on multiplication by its own positive denominator ≤Δ: the active matrix consists of the defining rows, unit rows and the simplex row, and Hadamard bounds its determinant. Select at most d such vertices covering all coordinates and sum their integral multiples. Doubling gives positive even integral gaps, each ≤2dΔ. Thus a dark lattice witness, if one exists in that cell on that flat, exists with both bounding-box dimensions ≤2d²Δ and area ≤4d⁴Δ². This is for unrestricted integral rooms with same-parity marks. It preserves the geometric statement under rescaling but does not enforce a primitive-gcd promise on the resulting marked lattice example. It gives a precise coefficient-height target for effectivity. No value of H follows from the qualitative arguments above.

## 4. Why a uniform Presburger acceleration retaining the direction cannot prove this

These tests eliminate the entire orbit for supplied direction (p,1), including all launch signs and arbitrarily long paths. The first component is positive and the direction is automatically primitive.

**cov279.** Put q=4u, u≡1 modulo three, u≥4. Fix S=(4,1), T=(12,1), and p=8v with v>0. The bottom rectangle has width 8u and height two; its door lies between 8u−3 and 8u. At every horizontal-wall height the developed abscissa is 4 modulo eight up to sign, which is still four after folding. No ray from S can hit an open door, its lips or a rectangle corner. It therefore follows the full rectangle flow forever. Returns to physical height one occur at every even integer time. In transverse phase modulo 16u the possible target hit is exactly

    16v j ≡ ±12 − (±4)     mod 16u.

The right sides are ±8 or ±16. The gcd on the left is 16 gcd(u,v), so the orbit connects iff gcd(u,v)=1. Integer solutions may be translated by a period to get positive time. At q=16, v=3 works at time two from abscissa 4, folding 52 to 12; v=4 leaves the same abscissa at all height-one returns. The all-direction pair itself has a horizontal chord. [Room data](thin-room.md)

**st279.** Take m a sufficiently large multiple of 16, S=(2,1), T=(6,1), p a positive multiple of four. Inside the bottom m-square every horizontal-wall contact folds to 2 modulo four, away from the opening (m−1,m) and all corners. With both choices of vertical launch sign, the height-one return times are 0,2,−2 modulo 2m. The transverse displacement must be ±4 or ±8 modulo 2m. For m≥32, the zero time residue cannot serve. The other two give p≡±2 or ±4 modulo m; since 4 divides p and m only ±4 remain. Hence the exact condition is p≡±4 modulo m. At m=32, p=36 works with initial negative horizontal sign and downward vertical sign in time two (start image −2, final image 70 folds to 6); p=40 fails. Again the two marks are directly visible without a directional restriction. [Room data](stabilizer-orbits.md)

Neither directional family is Presburger. After Presburger quantifier elimination a common positive eventual period P must work in the last variable for all fixed values of the other variables, even though the starting thresholds can vary. In the cov279 slice choose u=3P+1 and a sufficiently large v divisible by u. The two values v and v+P have different verdicts. In st279 take the variable p/4 and m/4 larger than 2P+4; the two isolated residues ±1 cannot be invariant under shift P. Consequently adding finitely many existential Presburger variables for crossing counts cannot encode even these fully decided directional orbits. The failure occurs with just one repeated circle translation. It is compatible with the qualitative whole-darkness theorem above and leaves its effectivity question open.
