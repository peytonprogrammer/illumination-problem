# Thin rooms admit periodic compression for fixed directions, but require unbounded directions

**Theorem.** Even at thickness five and four reflexes, there is no function of thickness and reflex count bounding the absolute primitive horizontal component of a lighting direction across a vertical door. This holds for distinct same-parity interior lattice marks in normalized BBBB rooms. It also excludes a universal finite menu of directions for the thin regime. The examples have a legal one-reflection path.

## A corner intercepts every direction of bounded horizontal component

Use the existing [width-U and width-(U+1) dumbbell](length-periodicity.md), with two height-five chambers, corridor length two, and corridor span 2<y<3. The left chamber is (0,U)×(0,5) and the right starts at x=U+2. Given B≥1 let M=lcm(1,…,B), a=10M and U=2a. Set

    S=(U−a,2),       T=(U+2+a,2).

The abscissas differ by 2a+2. Both points are interior. The room has τ=5 (its total height and an attained square side), only four reflexes, and wall-length gcd one on each axis because the horizontal wall lengths include U and U+1, and the vertical wall lengths include the full cap height five and the exposed height two. Its area is 10U+7, so the reviewed large-area classification at r=4 gives BBBB.

Take any nonvertical primitive vector (p,b) with 1≤|p|≤B. Before leaving the left chamber the first possible contact with the right end x=U occurs after horizontal travel a if launched right and 3a if launched left. The latter launch first reflects at x=0 after travel a. Since a is divisible by 10|p|, at every one of these events the transverse displacement is a multiple of 10, the period of the height-five chamber. For either transverse sign the physical height is exactly two. The left cap contact, when used, is regular. At x=U the contact is the lower doorway lip (U,2), a reflex corner. The path terminates. Horizontal launches have the same failure, and vertical launches stay in the left chamber. This covers every possible continuation or reversal because the obstruction happens at the first encounter with the separator. It permits arbitrary |b|.

Now launch right and upward with primitive direction (a+1,1). Both mouth crossings have height 3−1/(a+1). At x=U+1 the ray reaches the solid corridor ceiling at height three, reflects, and reaches T at height two after a further horizontal travel a+1. This proves legality at every width and gives an exact signed match. It also shows that the distance of the witness from the *upper* doorway lip tends to zero in physical units, although no lower bound on clearance for all possible witnesses follows. All graphs, junction geometry, and the number of reflections in the constructed path are fixed. The growing quantity proved necessary is the primitive horizontal component.

## What can actually be compressed

For a fixed primitive direction (p,b) with both components nonzero, cut the coordinate grid at all wall and mark coordinates. A horizontal gap means an interval of **x** coordinates between consecutive grid lines. If its length exceeds τ₀ in a room with τ≤τ₀, its occupied components are straight horizontal strips of integral transverse heights h≤τ₀. Every mark is outside the open gap. Changing that length by Δ preserves every traversal's signed exit point if Δ b/p ∈ 2hℤ for each such h. Thus a common sufficient integral period is 2|p| lcm(1,…,floor τ₀). The transposed period is 2|b| times the same lcm. Include a factor two as in these formulas to preserve endpoint parity. For a finite direction menu take common multiples along each axis. For axial directions ordinary chord visibility along coordinate lines is invariant under positive coordinate-gap changes.

Keep every short gap and reduce each longer gap by its period to a representative in (τ₀,τ₀+period]. This is simultaneous in the following sense. Throughout a long x gap every occupied component has total y extent ≤τ₀, so it contains no long y gap. Shortening long y gaps merely translates these components vertically. The transposed assertion holds as well. At a strip entrance the new ray has the same local signed state. Every intervening transverse reflection is on an actual smooth wall in the open strip. The exit has the original signed state, including the one-sided germ at a smooth simultaneous event. Induction over traversals transfers a finite legal path in either direction of the length change. Paths reaching a mark or turning at a gap boundary use the same local event there. The coordinate orders and all open intervals stay nondegenerate. The representative need not be normalized and should be retained as a marked integral room for this comparison.

With at most ten original supporting lines and two extra mark lines per axis, each bounding dimension of the representative is at most 11 times its maximum retained gap length. This is a finite reduction of **fixed-menu visibility** with no bound on the number of reflections. No census was run. The corner-intercept theorem proves why no width-independent fixed menu, however large, promotes this reduction to a proof of illumination at τ=5.

## Consequence for the general goal and the fat follow-on

A bounded junction can force both legs to use a direction with arbitrarily large primitive normal component. The residual problem is to select a width-dependent slope that places both signed states legally. A small final mismatch gcd can do this on a given placement, and the example has an especially simple variable slope. The proof leaves open the existence of such a placement on every tree with up to nine pieces. It also leaves open a bound on the number of reflections or on the *other* primitive component.

The same fixed-direction obstruction applies if one enlarges a room by a common integral similarity while retaining the scaled marks: τ grows and all legal directions are preserved. This observation concerns the unnormalized formulation only. For normalized fat rooms the earlier end-square stretches give a different obstruction to using the all-start depth bound: both the square side and the travel to its door from the other end grow linearly.
