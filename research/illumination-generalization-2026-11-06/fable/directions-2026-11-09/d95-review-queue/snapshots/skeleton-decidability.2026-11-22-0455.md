## Failure at word length zero

For a vertical gate at developed abscissa A with bounds l,u, measure heights from the source. The target vector (D,Y), D>0, crosses precisely when lD < AY < uD and 0<A<D. These are linear in the direction for fixed coordinates. With variable coordinates they are determinants.

In fact the direct-shot relation is **not Presburger** even on one orthogonal six-wall skeleton and distinct same-parity marks. Put the reflex at (0,0). Take the rectangle [−x−2,z+1] × [−2,y+1] with the open northeast quadrant cut out (take the interior of the resulting closed L). Put S=(−x,y), T=(z,−1), x,z,y positive odd integers. The two legs are in the left and lower arms. At abscissa zero their chord has height (zy−x)/(x+z). It is legal exactly when zy<x: equality hits the reflex, and positive height exits through the notch. Every parameter and mark is affine in x,y,z. On the affine slice z=y=2b+1, x=2c+1 the legal section is c>2b²+2b. If it were Presburger, the least legal c for each b would be Presburger too. Its graph c=2b²+2b+1 contains no infinite arithmetic progression, hence is not semilinear. This symbolic slice uses no discovery search. It concerns **a specified word**. The all-word illumination relation on this two-rectangle skeleton is universal by d157.

**Even the union through one reflection is nonsemilinear.** In the same L, take odd x≥3. A direct or single-reflection shot exists exactly when zy<3x. Reflect T in the bottom wall to (z,−3). At the notch line the developed ordinate lies strictly above −3, so its folded value is negative exactly when the developed ordinate is negative, equivalently zy<3x. The bounce occurs strictly between the outer vertical walls; if it is at the cut x-coordinate zero it is still a legal smooth-wall contact. Both legs stay inside because unfolded height decreases and stays below zero after crossing into the right arm (or folds there into [−2,0)). Conversely a left-wall bounce can clear the notch only if zy<x+4, by reflecting S to (−x−4,y). A right-wall bounce requires (z+2)y<x, by reflecting T. A top bounce requires z(y+2)<x, by reflecting S. Each condition implies zy<3x. The two notch walls cannot supply the only bounce since the marks are on opposite sides of each supporting line. These cases exhaust the six walls. With y=z=6b+3 and x=2c+1 the condition is c≥6b²+6b+2. Its graph of least c again has no infinite arithmetic progression. This obstruction already concerns a bounded existential choice of words allowing reflections. Chart endpoint coverage does not alter it.

## Which reflection bounds follow

* A length-independent N(σ) fails even on the fixed 16-wall cov279 skeleton. For q=12j+4 its specified lit pair requires R+1 ≥ √q/12. [Proof and reviewed inputs](reflection-complexity.md)
* This lower bound is compatible with a bound linear in perimeter. The reviewed two-rectangle upper bound 6HD+5 is quadratic when both spans grow. It is linear when one span stays bounded. No general linear bound follows here. [Two-rectangle construction](doorway-word-bound.md)
* There is an unconditional, very large **effective area-dependent** bound for the individual-room question. Form the four-copy translation unfolding with m=4 area unit tiles. Color the vertices by forbidden physical corner, lift of S, lift of T, or other. Shearing by the integral unit upper and lower shears and their inverses, followed by retiling over the unit torus, preserves the marked integer vertices. In a labeled tiling two neighbor permutations specify all gluings; the colors of the m lower-left corners encode all vertex colors. Hence there are at most B=(m!)²4^m isomorphism classes of such marked tilings. One can label each afresh to count them. No compatibility of labels between moves is needed.
* Every legal lattice shot develops to an integral vector k v for v primitive. An integral determinant-one change of basis carries v to the positive horizontal axis. Consequently a horizontal shot exists in some state of the marked shear orbit. Conversely each horizontal shot in a visited state pulls back to a legal shot. Forbidden vertices include physically convex corners. Nonvertex points are automatically regular throughout a unit horizontal step. Folding the pullback gives a legal physical path, as interior endpoints and corner avoidance exclude gliding along a physical wall.
* A reachable state has a simple path of length <B in the finite orbit graph, since the unit shears generate SL₂(ℤ). The inverse matrix of that path sends the horizontal unit vector to a vector of 1-norm at most 2^B, by doubling at each shear. There are m oriented right-going unit bottom edges (including distinct germs at singularities); following them deterministically from a source until a target or forbidden vertex gives a first hit within m steps whenever it gives one at all. A repeated oriented edge could be removed, or would cycle forever before a first hit. Thus a lit pair has an unfolded vector of 1-norm ≤m·2^B. Physical wall reflections occur at integer grid-line crossings and are bounded by that norm (for an axial shot count only transversal crossings). This is also a terminating per-input decision algorithm by finite orbit traversal. No traversal was run. The bound depends on both lengths and is far larger than linear.

For a fixed cell C of skeleton σ the desired integer sentence, **if an effective Presburger formula for darkness Φ_C were supplied**, is ¬∃(ℓ,S,T)[Valid_C(ℓ,S,T) ∧ Φ_C(ℓ,S,T)], restricted to the mark class of interest. Validity on a wall-order cell uses linear comparisons; same parity uses fixed-modulus congruences. Even an independent proof of a linear word bound would not turn the displayed gate determinants or variable moduli into a Presburger formula. The finite-flat result gives an existential description of a formula for integral darkness, without outputting its coefficients. These are two different sources of decidability: per-room orbit traversal terminates; emptiness over unbounded lengths still lacks an effective formula here. ## Number of turn skeletons

Set n=2r+4. A cyclic turn word has r reflex letters. Every such word is realizable: with r>0 there is a convex run of length at least two ending immediately before a reflex. Replace its last CCR by C and induct. To reinsert CCR at a convex corner with incoming east and outgoing north, extend the incoming edge slightly beyond the old turn, turn north, west back to the old outgoing line, then north. Use a sufficiently small rational neighborhood free of every other wall and rescale to the lattice. The base is a rectangle. Reflection of the room and traversal in the opposite order reverse the turn word while keeping convex/reflex labels. Thus binary bracelets of weight r give exactly the unmarked turn skeleton count.

For each n let [u^r] mean coefficient extraction. Burnside gives the count as the coefficient of u^r in

    ( Σ_{i=0}^{n−1}(1+u^{n/gcd(n,i)})^{gcd(n,i)}
       + (n/2)(1+u²)^{n/2}
       + (n/2)(1+u)²(1+u²)^{(n−2)/2} ) / (2n).

The last two terms are the two kinds of reflection axes. At r=0,…,8 the counts are 1, 1, 4, 8, 29, 79, 280, 912, 3260, totaling **4,574**. This formula counts no wall orders, placements of the marks, or mixed 45° types. No skeletons or rooms were enumerated. There is no justified numerical quantifier-elimination estimate per skeleton: the number of flats and bit lengths of their integral defining equations have no effective bounds here. A word-length estimate alone would supply neither.
