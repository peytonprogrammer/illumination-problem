# What rigidity would have to say about area

**Verdict.** Neither half of the proposed W≤20 dichotomy follows from the reviewed inputs. The word Rig in d39 denotes a particular per-class certificate over a quotient of the unit pillowcase. It does not denote local rigidity of a marked polygon. Below are a consequence for hypothetical mixed flats and an exact degree version of the area target. No room, ray, direction or reflection-word search was performed.

## (a) Which free directions yield long corridors?

A nontrivial kernel at a seed supplies a candidate infinitesimal motion only. It need not integrate to a two-sided dark line. On an *actual* dark flat a non-scaling wall motion does integrate inside the order cell, but in general it moves several supporting lines and both marks together. A nonradial plane can be given, for instance, by the positive gaps (u,v,u+v), where no single gap can be varied on its own. For mixed walls even the type equations link the two axes.

**Mixed-room progression.** Anchor a vertex at zero. Let a positive part of an actual dark flat contain a same-parity integer seed e whose wall-coordinate content (the gcd of all anchored vertex entries together) is one. Suppose its rational span of lattice points projects to a wall space of dimension ≥2. There are primitive same-parity dark rooms on the flat with area tending to infinity whose uniformly rescaled **marked shapes converge to a strictly simple interior point of the same cell**. In particular their local thickness tends to infinity and all nonzero gap-to-diameter ratios stay bounded below along this progression.

Indeed common scaling preserves the finite family of maximal flats and hence each one, so use a linear flat through the origin in anchored coordinates. Choose a rational direction arbitrarily close to e in that positive part with nonproportional wall vector. Clear denominators to obtain a positive integral v (positive refers to the oriented cell inequalities). Let J be the absolute value of a nonzero two-row minor of the two flattened wall vectors E,V. Take e+2Jt v with t a positive integer. Any prime dividing the content of E+2JtV must divide J by the minor, and reduction modulo that prime gives E, which is primitive. Parity of both marks and of every wall coordinate is unchanged. Linearity of the type equations and strict homogeneous cell inequalities keeps the whole ray physical. Division by 2Jt gives convergence to v with its marks, positive area and positive local thickness. This uses common dilation only, so diagonal walls are allowed. CONTENT ONE here is scalar normalization; separate axis normalization in the orthogonal case uses the minors on the varying axes as in primitive-dark-flats.md. If the real flat has only one rational wall ray, or only the marks move, the large primitive-area conclusion does not follow.

Thus an actual nonrigid arithmetic flat with these hypotheses would have large members with no emerging thin passage after rescaling. It could also have useful boundary degenerations on other rays. D124 requires specified mouths, chambers and a length threshold relative to those chambers. D128 requires a compatible placement; d129 preserves a supplied direction's signed phases. None of those hypotheses follows merely from the dimension of the flat. This argument neither constructs a dark member nor rules out a lighting route elsewhere on the hypothetical flat.

## (b) The invariant that a mixed-area proof must bound

For a mixed room form its eight-copy translation unfolding X. Take relative periods between physical **corner lifts** (including regular ones); leave S,T out of this lattice. Write Λ for their additive period lattice, in the original unit lattice coordinates. It is rank two, lies in ℤ², and is invariant under the room's D₄ reflections. More explicitly, it is generated by the D₄ images of oriented polygon edge vectors: these are relative edges upstairs, and the lifted polygon boundaries give a cellular one-skeleton containing all corner lifts and generating relative homology. Thus the gcd of all entries of Λ equals the scalar wall-coordinate content. Assume content one. Reflections and subtraction give (2a,0),(0,2b) from every (a,b) in Λ, and swapping axes lets all entries participate. Bézout gives 2ℤ²⊆Λ. Its image modulo two is a nonzero swap-invariant subspace, hence either the diagonal line or all of ℱ₂². Therefore its covolume c is precisely 2 or 1.

Integration modulo Λ gives a branched translation cover of ℝ²/Λ of degree d. The developing periods are single valued there, the corner lifts all map to a common point, and comparing flat areas gives the exact identity

    area(P) = c d / 8.

The degree is invariant under a common GL⁺ deformation of the unfolding and the period lattice. Hence physical area ≤20 would require d≤160 when c=1 and d≤80 when c=2. These are **required upper bounds**, not consequences of corner rank. Riemann–Hurwitz on this torus only counts total ramification. The cover degree multiplies the zero Euler characteristic of the torus, so it drops out of that count.

A named control makes the distinction concrete. In d135's chamfered-square parameter type take side 6q and chamfer leg 2q+1 for q=3j, j≥1 (the uniformly rescaled leg is 2+1/q in its side-six parameter). The two full axial sides and the sides of length 4q−1 give unit scalar content, since gcd(6q,4q−1)=1 for q divisible by 3. Their D₄ images already generate ℤ². The area is 36q²−(2q+1)²/2. Thus c=1 and d=288q²−4(2q+1)² is unbounded with the same five angles and the same two order-two zeros. The room is convex and its interior pairs are directly visible. This control excludes a degree bound from corner *types*; it says nothing against a degree bound using darkness and rigidity together. No marks or shots need be enumerated.

Nor does a corner's proximity to the marks bound its route coefficients. In the named R22 take a saved one-corner certificate with two nonzero holonomy components. On its square-tiled labelled unfolding a positive power of the horizontal integral shear lifts fixing every labelled corner and endpoint (finite marked origami orbit). Applying its k-th power to both legs preserves their corner labels, their corner-free interiors and their half-plane smoothing side when present. A nonhorizontal leg with vector (a,b) acquires vector (a+km b,b), m>0. Its travel is unbounded at identical physical corner and marks. This does not prove that a short independent basis is impossible. It shows that a separate basis-shortening or degree argument would be necessary. Tangent rank alone is invariant under common dilation, making a primitive normalization essential even to formulate an area bound.

**A qualitative bound for the rigid subproblem.** Grant the finite-flat theorem with its marked classifying-map input. There are finitely many order cells for fixed W and finitely many maximal dark flats on each. In a mixed cell, a genuinely isolated dark marked shape modulo dilation must lie on a dark ray: any higher-dimensional flat through an interior point supplies nearby nonhomothetic shapes. An integral ray contains at most one representative with scalar wall content one. Thus the rigid integral mixed subproblem has a finite maximum area if it is nonempty. In axial cells the analogous conclusion uses two independent axis scalings and separate wall primitivity. This does not identify the finite maximum or compare it to 20. The argument counts all relevant cells, including coordinate ties, and uses rigidity in the full physical family to imply rigidity on the containing cell.

## d39 and the finite checks

D43's reviewed implication assumes r≤8, corners in at least three parity classes, and a genuine essential per-class Rig certificate over the unit pillowcase (Par does not already certify that class). Its proof uses that canonical quotient's branching and seam structure. The tangent test instead concerns velocities that keep a set of corner alignments. No reviewed implication produces the specified quotient from tangent rigidity. In the orthogonal BBBB core the canonical class partitions are universal, so the nontrivial canonical quotient required by essential Rig is precisely missing. For mixed rooms the unit-pillowcase argument also requires a separate dictionary. Even assuming area ≤20 for canonical45 rooms, N=2 area can be as high as 40. Existing A/B coverage ends at N=32, unconditional C coverage at N=24, with additional C coverage in the A/B census to N=32. At W≤20 the extra ranges would therefore be A/B at N=33–40, C in previously uncovered rooms at N=25–32, and C at N=33–40, retaining only admissible types and pairs. Arbitrary diagonal intercept patterns and arbitrary real marks need a separate coverage or normalization proof; scaling to fit the tiling changes area. No extra finite range is justified today because the universal upper bound itself is missing.
