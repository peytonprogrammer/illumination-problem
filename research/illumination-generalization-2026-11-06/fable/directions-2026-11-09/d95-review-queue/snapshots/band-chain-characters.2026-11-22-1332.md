# Two endpoint phases for an arbitrary number of band gates

**Result for review.** A beam crossing the middle bands with clearance δ gives a two-dimensional winding test, independent of the number of middle bands. Failure requires a *short* integer relation involving the two endpoint depths. There is an exact scalar test when the relation group has rank one. These statements apply at W ≤ 20. They do not prove that one axis or cap always works, even at W = 10.

## Hypotheses and construction

Sweep a chain of rectangular runs in increasing longitudinal coordinate. Each open run has two solid transverse side walls. A gate is the common opening at the transition. Fix a chart for a straight developed line through the middle bands, of transverse slope s₀ per unit longitudinal travel. At every gate it admits a signed transverse perturbation of magnitude strictly less than δ>0, with the adjacent folds still matching in both position and outgoing velocity. Ordinary open doors provide such a window away from either end. A shared smooth wall also allows a signed window across zero under the common absolute-value fold. The incoming and outgoing source and target runs may be extended to the levels of the marks, using their respective side folds. Use only valid runs between these levels. Write w,V for the endpoint widths and a,b>0 for the distances from the marks to the first and last gate. The theorem concerns marks in the open endpoint bands.

For every full middle run j of width uⱼ and longitudinal thickness dⱼ choose a common P>0 with Pdⱼ in 2uⱼℤ. Integer rooms always admit P. Extra full side windings then preserve the signed exit at *each* middle run. Let A and B be the transverse coordinates at the source and target levels of the continued base line, in charts unfolded back from the first and forward from the last gate. Origins may be chosen independently at side walls. Choose any developed lifts X,Z of the physical endpoints in these charts. Each can be translated by its respective period 2w,2V.

Add kP to the slope, anchoring at the first gate and subtracting completed periods at later gates. The uncorrected endpoints now have coordinates A−kPa and B+kPb. Define on the unit two-torus

    α = (−Pa/(2w), Pb/(2V)),       θ = ((X−A)/(2w), (Z−B)/(2V)).

If kα lies in the open rectangle about θ with half-widths ε₁=δ/(2w), ε₂=δ/(2V), choose representatives so both endpoint errors have absolute value <δ. Add the linear interpolation of these two errors over the entire developed travel to the transverse coordinate. Its magnitude at every gate is <δ. It hits the chosen endpoint lifts exactly. Within each open run the only bounces are genuine side reflections. Arbitrarily large k of the same sign may be used whenever the open rectangle is hit, by recurrence of a rotation on a compact torus (or pigeonholing a sequence of powers near zero). This avoids zero-slope degeneracy at smooth common walls. The construction allows either sign of each endpoint lift. For a cap itinerary also enforce clearance at the cap, treating the open solid mirror as another window; include whole-period shifts there or verify separately that the cap contact is legal. An internal doorway cannot simply be sealed.

## Which resonances can prevent this construction?

Let L={m in ℤ² : m·α is integral}. For the above open rectangle the exact placement test is that it meet the closed subgroup annihilated by L. Indeed the averages of exp(2πi m·kα) tend to 1 for m∈L and 0 otherwise by summing the geometric progression. These are precisely the Fourier coefficients of uniform measure on that subgroup (integer diagonal reduction of L reduces it to circles and finite cyclic groups). Trigonometric polynomial approximation gives equality of measures and the asserted open-set test. This is an exact test for the rectangle construction. Other legal paths can exist when it fails.

* If L=0, every pair of transverse mark positions at these levels is lit.
* If L=ℤm has rank one, take its generator, even when its coordinates have a common factor. A chosen pair of lifts works exactly when dist(m·θ,ℤ)<|m₁|ε₁+|m₂|ε₂. This follows by taking the image of the open rectangle under the linear functional. A right side larger than 1/2 handles every pair of transverse positions. Equality can miss the antipodal phase.
* At **any rank**, failure requires a nonzero m∈L with |m₁|<4w/δ and |m₂|<4V/δ. Thus absence of these finitely many relations suffices for *every* pair of transverse mark positions at the specified depths. The condition allows exact relations of arbitrarily large order.

Here is a self-contained bound for the last assertion. Periodize, in coordinate i, the fourfold convolution of the normalized uniform density on [−εᵢ/4, εᵢ/4]. Take the product of the two periodized functions and center it at θ. Its mean is one; positivity implies membership in the open rectangle. At an integer frequency m, its Fourier magnitude is at most min(1, 2/(πt))⁴ where t=maxᵢ εᵢ|mᵢ|, by the transform of an interval. Orbit averaging keeps only m∈L. If all scaled nonzero frequencies have sup norm at least 2, they are pairwise at distance at least 2. In the shell 2j≤t<2j+2 there are at most (2j+3)² of them, by packing disjoint open squares of side 2. The sum of all surviving nonconstant coefficients in magnitude is at most 25 Σ(j≥1) j⁻² / π⁴ < 1. Absolute Fourier convergence also follows directly from the product of the one-dimensional fourth powers. The orbit average is therefore strictly positive for every θ. This proves the bound, including its strict endpoint.

For a fixed middle beam, δ is independent of a,b. A missed pair must therefore lie on one of finitely many explicitly specified lines in the bounded rectangle of possible depths: −m₁Pa/(2w)+m₂Pb/(2V)∈ℤ, with m in the bounds above. The union-of-lines conclusion is about failure of this shot family. One can intersect lists from valid beams, axes and cap charts after expressing the depths in physical coordinates. Identical or dependent equations must be retained; there is no independence assumption between itineraries.

## Named controls and the reflex budget

All four Ferrers controls have a common left side. A middle beam along its signed origin has δ=w (the perturbed shots have nonzero slope). The terminal floor-return tests already construct shots for all positions in U1 and U2, the endpoint rotation at equality does so for U2b, and transposing d170 does so for T1. These verifications survive exact middle resonance. In particular the two middle periods alone do not diagnose obstruction. [Length checks and transpose](deep-terminal-band.md)

The pyramid supplies a stronger negative test of the proposed all-length implication. In the reviewed-room-type setup of mask 761 take V=384, source (1/100,68), target (192,33). In the forward orientation w=2, u=4, middle thickness 2, so P=4. Direct travel has a=1 and b=32. Thus α=(−1,1/6); even the character (1,0) survives, and the second phase has just six values. After the terminal cap b=96, it has only two. The saved sign contradiction proves that *all* windings in this orientation fail, including the initial and terminal cap options. The perpendicular chart lights the same pair. [Gate contradiction and exact perpendicular shot](pyramid-sweep-obstruction.md)

The Ferrers grid (0,10,11,13,2860k) illustrates why the finite list is not uniform. The common signed beam has δ=10 and the far endpoint width V=2860k, so its second coefficient bound is 1144k. At integral endpoint depths with a a multiple of 10 and P=286, m=(1,0) already meets the resonance equation. Neither arbitrary area nor three reflexes eliminates it. This does not preclude another beam, and the relation alone does not imply that the phase rectangle is missed.

R22's known dark pair cannot pass any sufficient chart test above, since a passing test explicitly constructs a legal shot. This is a consistency implication only. It does not identify a short character for itineraries without a crossing beam or establish that R22 is characterized by endpoint resonance.

A W-wall orthogonal room has W/2 horizontal edges and hence at most W/2 distinct horizontal wall levels. A monotone sweep through consecutive open levels meets at most W/2−2 gates, at most eight for W≤20. The count does not bound w/δ or V/δ and does not force a second nonparallel beam. The unresolved step is geometric: for a pair on the residual phase lines, produce a valid alternate itinerary whose *translated* phase window meets its subgroup. Mask 761 rules out proving this by adding more windings to the same sweep. No claim of a dark ten-wall room follows.
