# First check on certificate completeness: what one site can distinguish

In the primitive branch with n≥49 the reviewed d99 result gives G containing Aₙ. Fix the pillowcase site x and its local permutation gₓ. A target-description invariant under the centralizer of gₓ in G cannot distinguish one ordered pair of distinct physical interior lattice positions over x from another if it retains just their local cycles. This remains true if the local description pairs the two regular lifts of every interior mark by the physical mirror and labels their two sides.

Indeed, on the pillowcase double each such position accounts for two disjoint 2-cycles of gₓ. To exchange two positions, exchange the corresponding cycles on each side, preserving the cyclic order on their letters. Exchanging two 2-cycles takes two letter transpositions, so the combined exchange is even. Extend by the identity everywhere else. It centralizes gₓ, respects the pairing and side labels, and belongs to Aₙ and hence G. Such exchanges generate all permutations of the physical positions in this fiber. The subgroup fixing a source can likewise exchange any two other targets. A predicate read only from these data and invariant under the indicated relabelings is constant on ordered distinct pairs in the fiber.

Consequently, if even one distinct pair in the fiber has a legal witness, a sound darkness certificate with only this invariance and information cannot single out a different pair. This is a limitation on certificate design, not a visibility theorem: an element of G centralizing one site need not preserve legal paths or the other site permutations. In particular no transitivity assumption about affine maps has been made.

The next certificate question is whether *three simultaneous exclusions* imply a relation respecting the four site permutations together. Cov4181's quotient exclusion uses the product relation with a permutation at a site the path never visits, a concrete reason to retain that information. The reviewed small-factor result does not eliminate primitive exclusions; no implication from three exclusions to a proper factor has been proved.
