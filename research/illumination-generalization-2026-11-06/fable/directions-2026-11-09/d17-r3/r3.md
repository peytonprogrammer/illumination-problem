# Rooms with 3 reflex corners: partial result and new lemmas

Theorem 3 isn't proved. This file gives two new lemmas with proofs and tests.
It also gives the exact gap that remains at r = 3 and the reason the gap
resists the certificate method. Notation follows
`../d15-small-r/theorems.md`.

## Results

- **Theorem 3 stays open.** The 578 open position-type pairs of
  `../d15-small-r/cert_r3.json` still have no metric-free certificate.
- **Lemma H** (door seen through a channel) is proved for every r. It handles
  a door with two reflex ends when the viewer sits between two parallel walls.
  It lights the pair unless L h / (w D) is a positive integer and D > h.
- **Lemma C+** (two doors on a hub with a cut corner) is proved for every r.
  It extends Lemma C, perp form and one par form, to a hub that isn't a
  rectangle. Its hypothesis is a condition on lengths.
- **No collinear lemma.** The 15 collinear pairs have no new lemma.
- **Coverage is small.** The sample has 7,000 rooms. At r = 3 the two lemmas
  certify 17,207 of 537,147 open instances (3.2%). At r = 4 they certify
  32,485 of 1,193,262 (2.7%).
- **Tests.** 60,389,695 Lemma H paths and 4,478,424 Lemma C+ sign pairs, exact
  arithmetic, 0 failures in the final run. An earlier run stopped on 94
  failures that came from a bug in the test code. See [Tests](#tests).
- **Evidence for the parity-free statement.** All 2,452,103 open-type pairs of
  rational points in 22,045 rooms with 10 walls are lit.

## Which statement is the right target

The right conjecture is the parity-free one: every two distinct real interior
points, real edge lengths. No test found a dark pair at r = 3, including
points with denominators 2, 3, and 4.

The right first milestone is narrower: rooms with integer edge lengths and
rational points. Two facts support this order.

- In Lemma H, the only failures are on a resonance set, where L h / (w D) is
  an integer. Rooms with integer edges and lattice points meet resonance sets
  often. Generic real rooms never do.
- A proof for all real rooms contains the integer case. The integer case is
  decidable room by room, and it's where residue classes enter.

This is a recommendation, not a proved reduction.

## Lemma H (door seen through a channel)

**Hypotheses.**

1. E = [p, q] x [d, e] is a corridor in P: its sides {p} x [d, e] and
   {q} x [d, e] lie in the boundary of P. T = (t1, t2) with p < t1 < q and
   d < t2 <= e.
2. S = (s1, s2) with s2 < d. There are a <= p and b >= q with a < s1 < b such
   that the channel Q = [a, b] x [s2, d] lies in P, and the open segments
   {a} x (s2, d) and {b} x (s2, d) lie in the boundary of P.

The door [p, q] x {d} needs no flat end. The top side of Q needs no wall.

Let L = b - a, w = q - p, tau = t1 - p, D = d - s2, h = t2 - d. For signs
eps1, eps2 and integers k, m let

    s' = a + eps1 (s1 - a) + 2 L k,   X = p + eps2 tau + 2 w m,
    xi = (h s' + D X) / (D + h).

**Statement.**

1. If p < xi < q, the segment from (s', s2) to (X, t2) is the unfolding of a
   valid path from S to T.
2. Some candidate has p < xi < q, unless L h / (w D) is a positive integer and
   D > h.

**Proof of 1.** The segment crosses the line y = d at (xi, d). Fold the lower
part into Q with the reflections in x = a and x = b. The folded path starts at
S, because a < s1 < b. It ends at (xi, d), because p < xi < q. It meets the
lines x = a and x = b at heights strictly between s2 and d. Those points lie
on the open wall segments of hypothesis 2. A wall has no vertex in its
relative interior, so they aren't vertices. Elsewhere the folded path is in the
interior of Q. The point (xi, d) is interior to P, with Q below and E above.
Fold the upper part into E with the reflections in x = p and x = q. As in
Lemma B, it meets the corridor walls at heights strictly between d and t2, and
it ends at T. The path isn't along a wall line, because it isn't horizontal
and it crosses the door at an interior point.

**Proof of 2.** The condition p < xi < q says that h s' + D X lies in an open
interval of length w (D + h). The values h s' + D X fill four cosets of the
group 2 L h Z + 2 w D Z, one for each sign pair.

- If L h / (w D) is irrational, the group is dense. Every coset meets the
  interval.
- If L h / (w D) = m / n in lowest terms, the group is g Z with
  g = 2 w D / n. If n >= 2, then g <= w D < w (D + h). An open interval longer
  than g meets every coset.
- If n = 1 and D < h, then g = 2 w D < w (D + h). Every coset meets the
  interval.
- If n = 1 and D = h, the interval has length g. A coset misses it only when
  the coset contains the endpoint. The cosets for eps2 = +1 and eps2 = -1
  differ by 2 D tau. This isn't a multiple of 2 w D, because 0 < tau < w. So
  at most one of them misses.

In the remaining case, n = 1 and D > h, exactly these sign pairs work: those
for which h (a + eps1 (s1 - a)) + D (p + eps2 tau) - p (D + h) has a residue
modulo 2 w D strictly between 0 and w (D + h).

**Relation to earlier lemmas.** Lemma B' is the case D <= h and needs no
channel walls. Lemma B needs one channel wall through a door end and no
arithmetic. Lemma H needs both channel walls. It uses the images of S in the
channel, which neither earlier lemma does.

**Status.** Proved. Tested: 15,222,626 configurations, 60,389,695 paths, 0
failures. In 61 configurations no sign pair had a candidate. All 61 had
L h / (w D) a positive integer and D > h.

## Lemma C+ (two doors on a hub with a cut corner)

Lemma C needs a rectangular hub G inside P. Lemma C+ needs only a convex part
K of G.

**Hypotheses, perp form.** G = [0, L] x [0, H]. Door 1 is {0} x [0, w1], with
flat end (0, 0) and corridor in x < 0. Door 2 is [L - w2, L] x {H}, with flat
end (L, H) and corridor in y > H. The floor [0, L] x {0} and the side
{L} x [0, H] lie in the boundary of P. S and T are arm points as in Lemma C.
Let K be the set of points of G on the closed side of the line through
E1 = (0, w1) and E2 = (L - w2, H) that contains (L, 0). Hypothesis: K lies in
P.

**Hypotheses, par-floor form.** G = [0, L] x [0, H]. Door 1 is {0} x [0, w1]
and door 2 is {L} x [0, w2]. Both flat ends are on the floor, and the floor
[0, L] x {0} lies in the boundary of P. Let K be the trapezoid with corners
(0, 0), (L, 0), (L, w2), (0, w1). Hypothesis: K lies in P.

**Statement.** The statement of Lemma C holds. For every sign pair there are
integers k1, k2 such that the segment between the images of S and T is the
unfolding of a valid path. The segment crosses each door line inside the
full-period window of its arm.

**Proof, perp form.** The existence part of the proof of Lemma C doesn't use
G. It gives a line through (0, a) and (b, H) with -w1 < a < w1 and
L - w2 < b < L + w2. Only validity inside the hub changes.

If w1 = H or w2 = L, then K = G and Lemma C applies. Otherwise let
c(z) = (L - w2)(z_y - w1) - (H - w1) z_x. K is the part of G with c <= 0.
Then c(0, a') = (L - w2)(a' - w1) < 0 for 0 <= a' < w1, and
c(b', H) = (H - w1)(L - w2 - b') < 0 for L - w2 < b' <= L. Also c < 0 at
every point (x, 0) with 0 <= x <= L and every point (L, y) with 0 <= y < H.

Lemma C shows that the segment crosses the floor line, if at all, at a point
(x°, 0) with x° < L, and never passes through (L, 0). Fold the segment into G
with the reflections in y = 0 and x = L. The folded path has at most three
pieces. Each piece joins two of these points: a point (0, |a|) of door 1, a
floor point, a point of the side x = L below H, and a point (b, H) or
(2L - b, H) of door 2. By the previous paragraph, c < 0 at every such point.
The function c is affine, so c < 0 on the whole folded path. The pieces lie in
G, so they lie in K and off the chord line. A point of K off the boundary of G
and off the chord line is interior to K, so interior to P. The folded path
meets the boundary of G only at its two ends and at its reflection points. The
reflection points are relative interior points of the floor or of the side
x = L, or the flat end (0, 0). None is a vertex.

**Proof, par-floor form.** Lemma C gives a segment from (0, a) to (L, b) with
|a| < w1 and |b| < w2. At x = t L the folded height is |(1 - t) a + t b|. It is
less than (1 - t) w1 + t w2, the height of the chord. So the folded path lies
in K, strictly below the chord. It meets the floor at most once, at a relative
interior point or at a flat end. The case a = b = 0 doesn't occur, as in
Lemma C.

**Status.** Proved. Tested on 540 perp and 1,119,066 par-floor configurations
where G isn't in P: 4,478,424 sign pairs, all with a valid path.

**How sharp the condition on K is.** For type 4991 the condition says that V2
is on or above the chord V1 V3. When a vertex is strictly inside K, a window
candidate can still be valid, but none need be. In 600 sampled rooms with
r = 3, 65,311 of 99,440 such perp configurations on open pairs had no valid
window candidate for any sign pair.

## What didn't work

This section records hand analysis. None of it is tested.

**An open-and-closed argument.** The lit set is open. The dark set isn't open
in any known case. By the finiteness theorems for translation surfaces
(Lelievre, Monteil, and Weiss; Wolecki), a point has finitely many dark
partners. I cite these from memory and didn't re-read them. So no local
argument shows that the lit set is closed. A proof must be global or
arithmetic.

**One-way jumps for chains.** Lemmas B, C, and C+ rest on one fact. Seen
through a door with a flat end, an arm point gives exactly one line per slope
in a full-period window. Its position moves continuously and jumps one way.
That fails after a second door. Take a corridor of width wc that narrows to a
corridor of width w2 < wc with a common wall. Transfer the lines of a point in
the narrow part through the wide part. The position in the wide window jumps
back by 2 w2, not by the full period 2 wc. For some slopes this is a jump in
the wrong direction. The intermediate value argument then has no monotone
structure, and I found no replacement.

**Two hubs joined by a corridor.** In the U shapes with a two-width leg, S is
in a wide part, T is beyond a narrow corridor and a second hub. One integer
(the image index in the corridor) must satisfy two window conditions at once.
The two windows have lengths 2 w / (1 - lambda2) and 2 w / lambda1 around
different centres. Each contains a solution, but they need not share one.

**Collinear pairs.** Every family I tried is valid on a part of the length
space only. Example: type 125, one bounce off the top of the bar is valid
exactly when the bounce point is a wall point.

## Where the proof uses interior points

The 10-wall staircase has a dark pair of boundary points, so a proof must use
interiority. Each lemma uses it at one step: an interior point has two
distinct mirror images across a wall line, and a wall point has one.

- **Lemma H.** The strict inequalities 0 < tau < w and a < s1 < b make the
  four cosets distinct. The case D = h uses 0 < tau < w directly. For a point
  on a corridor wall, tau is 0 or w, the cosets for eps2 = +1 and -1 merge,
  and the single coset can sit on the endpoint.
- **Lemmas C and C+.** The case a = b = 0, a path along the floor, is excluded
  because S isn't on the floor line. For a wall point on that line, this
  degenerate path is the only window candidate for both signs.
- **Lemmas B', B2, and D.** Each proves "at most one sign fails". That needs
  two different signs.

## Theorem 3, partial form

Let P have 3 reflex corners and let S, T be distinct real interior points. S
and T are joined by a billiard path in each of these cases.

1. Their position-type pair is one of the 13,030 certified pairs of
   `../d15-small-r/cert_r3.json`.
2. In one of the 8 frames and one of the two orders, Lemma H applies and
   statement 2 gives a candidate.
3. In one of the 8 frames and one of the two orders, Lemma C+ applies.

### The exact gap

The gap is the set of rooms and pairs of the 578 open position types where
neither case 2 nor case 3 holds. In the sample this is 96.8% of the open
instances. Two explicit members:

- **Resonance.** Type 122, grid lines x = 0, 3, 9, 12 and y = 0, 2, 5, 6. The
  bar is [0, 12] x [2, 5], the tab is [3, 9] x [0, 2], and the chimney is
  [0, 3] x [5, 6]. S = (1, 4), T = (4, 1). Here L = 12, w = 6, D = 2, h = 1,
  so L h / (w D) = 1 and D > h. The four residues are 0, 22, 20, and 18 modulo
  24. None is strictly between 0 and 18. The pair is lit by another path:
  direction (-1, 3), 5 primitive steps, 4 reflections, through the chimney.
- **Chain.** Type 4991, grid lines x = 0, 3, 4, 9, 12 and y = 0, 3, 5, 9, 12.
  V1 = (3, 9), V2 = (4, 5), V3 = (9, 3), and V2 is strictly below the chord.
  S = (1, 10), T = (10, 1). No window candidate is valid for any sign pair.
  The pair is lit: direction (-1, 1), 21 primitive steps, 8 reflections.

Neither example is an obstruction to Theorem 3. Each shows a family that
fails while a longer path succeeds. I found no dark pair and no candidate for
one.

## Route to general r

**Do the lemmas avoid enumerating order types?** No. Both lemmas are local and
hold for every r, but together they reach about 3% of the open instances.
Chains are 96% of the open pairs at r = 3 and at r = 4, and neither lemma
handles a chain through a hub that isn't convex in the sense of K.

**What the lemmas do show.**

- Metric-free certificates are close to their limit. Each open kind fails on
  a set of lengths that depends on real parameters, so a certificate must
  carry conditions on lengths.
- For doors, the failure set is arithmetic. Lemma H fails only when
  L h / (w D) is an integer, and then on explicit residues. This is the first
  place in the project where a lemma's exact failure set is known.
- For chains, the failure set isn't arithmetic alone. It includes open sets
  of lengths, as the staircase with V2 below the chord shows.

**Where parity must enter.** At r = 9 a 22-wall room has a dark pair, so a
proof for r <= 8 can't be purely geometric. Lemma H suggests the mechanism. A
family of paths fails only at a resonance, and then only for bad residues.
Rooms with integer edges and lattice points are resonant for every family at
once. A proof for r <= 8 has to show that the bad residues of all families
can't hold together with at most 8 reflex corners. That's a counting statement
about residue classes of image lattices, not about order types. I have no
proof of such a statement, and Lemma H gives only its one-door case.

**A route I consider plausible, unproved.** Split by arithmetic type. For
lengths with no rational relation, prove density statements like the first
bullet of the proof of Lemma H for chains. For integer rooms and rational
points, work on the square-tiled unfolding and count residue classes. The
mixed cases are the hard part, and nothing here addresses them.

## Weakest step

The weakest step is the claim that the gap can't be closed by more lemmas of
the present kind. It rests on hand analysis of four representatives (types
122, 125, 4991, and the U shapes) and on the 3% coverage, not on a proof. A
different family, for example one that controls two doors at once, could
still close chains.

Inside the proved material, the weakest step is the perp form of Lemma C+.
Its proof reuses the existence part of Lemma C unchanged, and its test has
540 configurations.

### Claim H (door seen through a channel)

Hypotheses.

1. E = [p, q] x [d, e] is a corridor. T = (t1, t2) with p < t1 < q and
   d < t2 <= e.
2. S = (s1, s2) with s2 < d. There are a <= p and b >= q with a < s1 < b such
   that the channel Q = [a, b] x [s2, d] lies in P, and the open segments
   {a} x (s2, d) and {b} x (s2, d) lie in the boundary of P.

Let L = b - a, w = q - p, tau = t1 - p, D = d - s2, h = t2 - d. For signs

    s' = a + eps1 (s1 - a) + 2 L k,   X = p + eps2 tau + 2 w m,
    xi = (h s' + D X) / (D + h).

Claim H: whenever p < xi < q, the segment from (s', s2) to (X, t2) is the
unfolding of a valid path from S to T. Consequence: S and T are lit unless

or D <= h, and no sign pair has a solution.

### Claim C+ (two doors on a hub with a cut corner)

Perp form. Take the frame of Lemma C, perp form: G = [0, L] x [0, H], door 1 =
{0} x [0, w1] with flat end (0, 0), door 2 = [L - w2, L] x {H} with flat end
(L, H). The floor [0, L] x {0} and the side {L} x [0, H] lie in the boundary of
P. Both corridors satisfy the corridor conditions, and S, T are arm points.
Replace "G lies in P" by: K lies in P, where K is the set of points of G on
the closed side of the line through (0, w1) and (L - w2, H) that contains
(L, 0).

Par-floor form. G = [0, L] x [0, H], door 1 = {0} x [0, w1], door 2 =
{L} x [0, w2], both flat ends on the floor, and the floor [0, L] x {0} lies in
the boundary of P. Replace "G lies in P" by: the trapezoid K with corners
(0, 0), (L, 0), (L, w2), (0, w1) lies in P.

Claim C+: in both forms the statement of Lemma C holds. For every sign pair
there are integers k1, k2 such that the segment between the images is the
unfolding of a valid path, and it crosses each door line inside the
full-period window.

Test: population G. For every ordered pair and each symmetry, enumerate every
configuration. For each sign pair, search k1 with |k1| <= 200 and every
compatible k2. Trace the direction with `rooms.py`. A sign pair with no valid
candidate counts as undecided and stops the run.
