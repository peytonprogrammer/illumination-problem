**Exact criterion.** A lattice pair is lit iff some marked orbit state has compatible lifts on either (I) the same interior integer level of one horizontal cylinder or (II) the same open horizontal saddle connection. In (I) the cylinder height is at least two. In (II) the two positions must lie between the same consecutive forbidden events. Membership in the closure of the same cylinder is insufficient.

Indeed, unfold a legal chord. Its nonzero integral displacement is m v for m>0 and v primitive. After straightening, follow the horizontal trajectory in X away from F. A square-tiled surface in a rational direction splits into periodic cylinders and their critical graph: a generic horizontal unit row closes by the finite right-neighbor permutation, and the row continues transversely until it meets a forbidden vertex. On an integer row the same finite permutation either returns the incidence or meets a cut. Thus the chord lies on a circle of type (I) or an open interval of type (II). Conversely, in (I) travel along the circle between the lifts; in (II) take the subinterval between them. Folding the segment proves lighting. The criterion concerns the same *level*. Two distinct levels in the same open cylinder also give a slanted chord in its developed strip, but that chord has a different direction.

### (b) An exact count in one state

There are 4n unit squares and 4n lower-left corner **incidences**, but only 4n−2r distinct lattice sites on X. Each reflex lift accounts for three incidences of the same site. Removing F leaves M=4n−4r−4 regular sites, including smooth wall lifts. Do not subtract the endpoint labels.

Write wᵢ,hᵢ for the positive integral circumferences and heights of the cylinders cut at F and L=Σwᵢ. The area identity is Σwᵢhᵢ=4n. The regular circles carry Σwᵢ(hᵢ−1)=4n−L lattice sites. There are E=3r+(r+4)=4r+4 oriented-right saddle connections: each outgoing right separatrix from F runs to the next forbidden point in this rational direction. Their lengths ℓⱼ are positive integers, and Σℓⱼ=L by counting the lower boundaries of the cylinders with multiplicity (each oriented saddle connection has one incident side above). Thus their open intervals carry L−E distinct regular sites. Including F itself gives L−2r sites on critical levels. The bounds E≤L≤4n follow. A corner count controls E; it does not determine L.

At n=22,r=8 there are 88 incidences, 72 distinct sites, 20 forbidden lifts and **52 regular sites**. Exactly 88−L are on regular circles and L−36 are inside critical intervals, with 36≤L≤88. These estimates alone give no positive lower bound on 88−L. Even positive circle occupancy need not put the two marked lifts on the same circle.

One can count *every* unordered distinct regular-site pair joined horizontally in that state, allowing either sign. The exact number is

    Σᵢ (hᵢ−1) binom(wᵢ,2) + Σⱼ binom(ℓⱼ−1,2).

This follows by partitioning the regular sites into disjoint circles and open intervals. The sum includes pairs of different lifts of the same physical point. Summing over states counts incidences for all pairs with multiplicity. To deduce lighting of a specified physical pair one needs a positive sum for its compatible lift pairs. The unmarked totals give no such lower bound. A horizontal shear (x,y)↦(x+ky,y) sends each horizontal leaf to a horizontal leaf and preserves the incidence verdict for transported marks. A returning power gives cylinder twists but never transfers a mark to a different level. Repeating shears within one horizontal direction therefore cannot supply the missing pair incidence. No inequality proving L1 at 22 follows from these counts.

### Area 22 on the primitive branch: a test for the specified pair

D220 confirms G=A₂₂ or S₂₂ when the original cover is primitive and r=8. The marked pair curve U therefore contains *every* ordered pair of distinct sheets. At any regular site over a pillowcase pole, the two sheets of its transposition give one diagonal branch on U. Both nontrivial local branches at each of the eight reflexes also belong to U. Thus 2d=42, J=16 and R=26 exactly. If gₐ has tₐ transpositions, the exact off-class count is 26−tₐ≥15. These counts live on the room double Q; the 52 regular sites counted above live on X. [d220 verdict](../../illumination-generalization-2026-11-06/fable/directions-2026-11-09/d220-review-large-monodromy/review.md) · [Coincidence identity](midpoint-necessity.md)

There is a direct way to ask which of these sites a geometric twist can use. In an adapted peripheral basis for the arc a→x, choose incident sheets s,t at compatible source and target lifts over a. A horizontal walk of exactly two primitive traversals joins those lifts iff (s t) is a 2-cycle at x for some such choices. Indeed its two edges must meet at the same *regular* x-vertex. Its two ends are already regular, so the walk is legal regardless of whether its horizontal continuation lies on a cylinder rim or an interior level.

Take an adjacent pair of peripheral entries gₓ,gᵧ away from a, in that order, and put k=gₓgᵧ. Twist about those two poles with support away from the a-tail. With the Hurwitz convention of the monodromy note, after j full twists the x-entry is k⁻ʲgₓkʲ, while the a-entry and its endpoint labels stay fixed. This follows by induction because the product k of the two entries stays fixed. Consequently a transposition (u v) of the original gₓ gives the desired two-edge walk somewhere in this twist family exactly when kʲs=u and kʲt=v for the **same** j, allowing the reverse assignment as well. This is the geometric braid convention; it requires no calibration of a composite code word to a physical slope.

This criterion can be checked without ranging over powers. Require u on the k-cycle of s and v on that of t. Let their forward offsets be iₛ,iₜ and let the cycle lengths be cₛ,cₜ. A simultaneous exponent exists iff gcd(cₛ,cₜ) divides iₛ−iₜ, by the two-congruence form of the Chinese remainder theorem. If s,t lie on one cycle, this says exactly that the ordered cyclic spacing is preserved. For distinct coprime-length cycles, the two memberships suffice. An exponent passing this test realizes the chosen transposition as an off-class midpoint and puts the lifts on one horizontal component after straightening. This twist changes the tested arc, unlike the horizontal shear that preserves it.

Giant monodromy supplies an element sending (s,t) to (u,v) in the full sheet group. It gives no reason for that element to belong to ⟨k⟩ for a geometric twist, or for any of the 26−tₐ sites and compatible incidences to satisfy these two congruences. Failure in one twist family would also leave longer walks and other states available. This is a sufficient test for L1 and an exact test only for the two-traversal witnesses in this family.
