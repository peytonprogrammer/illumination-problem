# Primitive BBBB rooms: sheet monodromy and a visible pair in the marked action

For the same-parity problem, the reviewed sheet classification already reaches area 49. The new observation below supplies the geometric input to a marked-orbit argument at a smaller threshold than d14's uniform 484. In a class with q regular lifts, **q > 2r+2 guarantees a legal segment between two distinct regular lifts of that class**. In particular this holds for every class at n > 8r+8, including n ≥ 73 for r ≤ 8. The lemma needs no primitive group. In the large primitive branch a dark pair would therefore yield both a lit unordered-pair orbit and a different, dark, unordered-pair orbit for the actual affine marked action. No transitivity theorem on marks is assumed here.

## Which group is already classified?

Write f_x,q_x,z_x for the numbers of fixed points, transpositions and 3-cycles of g_x on the n sheets. Then n=f_x+2q_x+3z_x, Σf_x=r+4 and Σz_x=r. It is **g_x squared**, at a class with z_x>0, that has small support, exactly 3z_x. The transpositions can give g_x itself support of order n.

For n ≥ 21, reviewed d81 Proposition P gives either G contains A_n or n ≤ 6z_x for *every* reflex-bearing class. In particular G is A_n or S_n for n ≥ 49, also for n ≥ 25 if at least two classes carry reflexes. In the natural action, two-transitivity makes the whole ordered off-diagonal sheet fiber one component U; its projection degree is n−1. Every regular transposition over every parity occurs there as one coincidence, by the local diagonal intersection calculation. Exactly 2(n−1)−2r−q_a of them are outside the source class a. This is at least 56 at n=49 and r≤8. It counts algebraic points on U, without providing a straight trajectory from the marked starts. ## Geometric lemma: q ≤ W−2 if the class has no visible pair of lifts

Fix a parity a. Its physical regular lattice positions consist of i interior positions and b smooth wall positions, so q=2i+b. Work on the room double: a straight segment along a smooth seam with no corner is legal there. Both endpoints may be wall lifts. We prove that if every distinct pair among the q lifts is hidden, then q≤W−2, where W=2r+4.

1. From each interior position draw its four cardinal rays up to their first boundary hits. All four hits must be reflex corners. A smooth hit would be transverse (the incoming open segment is interior and axis-parallel); bouncing there returns to the same physical position on the opposite half of the double, a legal segment to a **distinct** lift. At a convex orthogonal corner no cardinal ray can arrive with its preceding open segment in the interior.
2. These four segments per interior position give a noncrossing straight-line graph inside the polygon. For segments from distinct starts, a transverse interior crossing has the x-coordinate of one start and the y-coordinate of the other, hence parity a. The crossing is an interior lattice point. The subsegment from either distinct start to it gives a legal pair, contrary to the hypothesis. A start on another open segment gives the same contradiction. Collinear segments with overlapping interiors from two starts lie in the same open interval cut out of their common line by the room interior, so the starts are directly visible. Shared reflex endpoints are allowed. In particular, each reflex can receive at most two source rays: only two inward cardinal half-rays at a reflex enter the interior, and two starts on the same such half-ray would be mutually visible.
3. Triangulate the polygon with its W corners, the b smooth marked wall points and the i marked interior positions as vertices, keeping all drawn segments as edges. This is an ordinary constrained planar triangulation without extra vertices: the segments intersect only at their endpoints, and none passes through an intermediate vertex. Equivalently, split the boundary at its marks, complete the noncrossing edge set to a maximal straight-line triangulation, retaining the prescribed edges. There are W+b+2i−2 triangles by Euler's formula. Each has at most one marked regular vertex, since an edge between distinct such positions gives a legal segment on the double. Each marked interior vertex belongs to at least four triangles by the four prescribed rays. Each smooth boundary mark belongs to at least two nondegenerate triangles because its interior angle is π. Counting marked triangle incidences gives
     
       4i+2b ≤ W+b+2i−2,     hence q=2i+b ≤ W−2.

Consequently q_a>2r+2 is a sufficient visible-seed test. Via the sheet count it holds whenever n>4r+4+f_a+3z_a, hence for n>8r+8 uniformly. A separate obstruction under the same no-seed hypothesis is 4i≤2r by the endpoint count in step 2. The proof concerns absence of **every** visible pair in the class. Physical darkness of a particular pair gives no such absence.

## Exactly where affine transport is needed

Let M be the permutations of the q regular lifts induced by the pure orientation-preserving affine group (fixing the base poles). It preserves straight visibility as a relation on unordered pairs. If M is 2-homogeneous and q_a>2r+2, the visible seed above transports to every unordered pair, including each of the four source–target lift pairs. In fact it suffices for one seed and one of the four desired pairs to belong to the same unordered-pair orbit. An affine image with interior physical endpoints projects to a legal billiard path. This avoids an ineffective shadow constant and supplies d14's visible-seed input E in this range. [D14 definition of M and conditional theorem](../../illumination-generalization-2026-11-06/fable/directions-2026-11-09/d14-lemma-h/proof.md)

There is a precise algebraic trap. In S_n the centralizer of g_a permutes its q two-cycles as the full S_q. The same is true in A_n: perform a desired permutation of the two-element blocks identically on both entries, an even permutation. Thus when G contains A_n, **the centralizer inside G already supplies every formal relabeling of the marks**. These relabelings preserve inertia at a only. To obtain a permutation in M one needs a pure mapping-class return of the *whole four-permutation tuple*, with its transported labels; a passive sheet relabeling does not do this. For an independent check on the distinction, d14's archived degree-six tuple with G=S₆ has q=3 and actual M=C₃, strictly smaller than the inertia centralizer image S₃. This is an abstract small-degree control; its marked action is still 2-homogeneous and it supplies no dark room. In the other direction, for n≥5 there is no nontrivial homomorphism A_n→S_q when q≤n/2: simplicity and orders forbid it. The centralizer action is an action of a **subgroup** of A_n; it cannot simply be extended to A_n. These two facts identify the precise missing return condition.

A surviving large primitive dark pair must occupy four dark lift edges while its class also contains a lit orbit, whenever the visible-seed inequality holds. Both endpoints obey the reviewed mirror-suborbit bound and their mirrored lifts share the two hidden target lifts. All off-class regular points counted above occur on the same U. What still needs proof is a marked return taking one of the four edges to a lit edge, or a legal route to one coincidence. Full sheet monodromy alone has supplied neither. The narrower question about the four edges allows the rest of M to have multiple pair orbits.

## Finite group residue

Yes, the candidate **nonalternating permutation groups** are computable without a room search. In the open area range take degrees 21 through 48, use the primitive permutation group catalogue up to permutation isomorphism, omit groups containing A_n, and filter element conjugacy classes by cycle types 1^f 2^q 3^z. Four candidate classes must satisfy the degree equations above, q_x>0 for BBBB, q_a≥4, the two sum rules, and n≤6z_x for every positive z_x. For the algebraic branch-tuple list one can then impose product one and generation in each finite candidate group. Catalogue degrees below 21 as well if the object is the full group list irrespective of the already closed small areas. This procedure gives necessary algebraic possibilities; geometric room realization and illumination are separate tests. GAP's primitive library covers all these degrees.
