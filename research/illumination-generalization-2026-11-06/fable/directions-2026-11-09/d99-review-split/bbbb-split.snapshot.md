# The two sheet-monodromy branches of BBBB

**Scope correction.** At r≤8, after the Q-class closure and d64, BL is equivalent to illumination of every distinct same-class interior lattice pair in BBBB rooms. It does not reduce that problem. The finite BBBB slice at areas 21–36 has not been enumerated. Above 36 the primitive and imprimitive branches both remain open. This note treats the two branches separately. [D94 review](../../illumination-generalization-2026-11-06/fable/directions-2026-11-09/d94-review-transfer/review.md)

## Primitive sheets

D81 already gives A_n or S_n on sheets at n≥49. The small-support element is the square of a corner permutation at a reflex-bearing parity. Its support is three times the number of reflexes there. Every regular midpoint candidate then belongs to the same off-diagonal ordered-sheet component. The affine group on marked lifts has a different action. A physical dark pair would give four dark edges and the two shared targets used in d92. At n≥73 there is also a lit pair in the same parity by the visible-seed argument, submitted for review. The stabilizer of a chosen source cannot send either target to a point visible from that source. The presence of the lit orbit elsewhere yields no contradiction. Indeed all permutations of the source-parity transpositions can be realized formally inside the sheet centralizer of the one corner permutation; a marked affine return has to respect the whole cover. No such return to a lit edge has been proved. The nonalternating group candidates in degrees 21–48 are finite; the alternating case is unbounded. ## Imprimitive sheets: bounded suborbits pass to every quotient

Let f:Q→N be any proper intermediate pillowcase cover with inner degree k, and let z be the number of zeros on N. Both surfaces are spheres, and N has z+4 poles. Corrected normalization and local degrees give 1≤z≤floor(r/k), 2≤k≤r. The only possible branch values of f are poles of N: over a zero all upstairs local degrees over the pillowcase are already three, and over a regular point they permit no further branching. Write F_X for the full orientation-preserving affine group of the half-translation surface X. For points s,t on Q set u=f(s), v=f(t), allowing u or v to be singular or equal.

**Suborbit inequality.** For any such f,
    
    |(F_N)_u · v| ≤ k I |(F_Q)_s · t|,      I ≤ (k!)^(z+3).

Here I is the index in F_N of the subgroup admitting a lift through f. This inequality requires no uniqueness of the block partition, mirror descent, or complete fibers of hidden points.

Remove the z+4 poles downstairs and their full preimages upstairs. The resulting topological cover is connected and unbranched. The punctured base sphere has free fundamental group of rank z+3. Its connected k-sheet cover is recorded by a transitive monodromy representation to S_k up to sheet conjugacy, giving at most (k!)^(z+3) possibilities. The affine group permutes the poles and acts on these cover classes by pullback. The stabilizer H of this class is precisely the liftable subgroup. Lifts extend over the punctures by the local branched-cover model and are affine in the pulled-back flat charts. This proves the index bound, including when some of the removed poles were unbranched values.

Let L consist of the lifts of H and map L to H by descent. The preimage in L of H_u acts on the fiber over u, of cardinality at most k. Fixing s therefore costs index at most k there. The image J of this stabilizer in H_u has index at most k. Its orbit of v is contained in the projection of (F_Q)_s·t, while [(F_N)_u:H_u]≤I. The orbit of v under (F_N)_u is a union of at most kI translates of J·v, proving the inequality.

Apply this first to the four choices of s and t above the physical marks, in both orientations. If the pair is dark, each upstairs suborbit has size at most the ordinary shadow bound B(r). Thus all the corresponding full downstairs stabilizer suborbits have size at most k I B(r), independently of area. Applying it to s and its mirror gives the analogous bound k I e(S), and likewise for T. The projected points need not be hidden on N. At n≥120 the unique maximal proper sheet partition is preserved by the physical mirror by d80. For this choice f(ιs) is precisely the quotient mirror of f(s). In all degrees the comparison still applies to f(s),f(ιs) as two specified points. It says nothing by itself if those projections coincide. [D80 quotient uniqueness](high-valency-geometry.md) · [D92 mirror bound](mirror-suborbit.md)

This gives a way to disprove a *sequence* of hypothetical dark pairs by proving unbounded projected suborbit sizes, even when the quotient is not a polygon double. It gives no effective illumination cutoff because B(r) is unevaluated. Only a bounded number of physical marks can have a singular projection for a fixed quotient: any regular upstairs lift projecting to a pole uses one simple ramification, and there are at most 2k−2 such lifts by Riemann–Hurwitz. This statement counts lifts; without mirror descent one must not automatically divide the count by two.

## A sharper consequence for every degree-two factor

Here f has two distinct simple branch values, both poles, by Riemann–Hurwitz. The connected degree-two cover is determined by that unordered pair: the monodromy in C₂ is nontrivial precisely on those two peripheral loops. Thus I≤binomial(z+4,2)≤28 for r≤8, and the loss kI above is at most 56.

**At least one endpoint of a dark physical pair has two distinct regular images on N.** Let δ be the nonidentity deck involution of f. It acts over the identity of the original pillowcase, hence has projective derivative identity on Q. By d91 it commutes with the physical mirror and descends to a sign symmetry of the room. In particular the mirror preserves the δ-fibers even if there is another maximal block system. Call an endpoint collapsed when f(s)=f(ιs). For an interior mark this means δs=ιs, so δ exchanges halves and its physical symmetry is an axial reflection through the mark. If both endpoints collapse, they lie on the same axis of this one reflection and see each other directly by strict simplicity. If δ exchanges halves it has no interior fixed lift, hence the images of both endpoints are regular: an interior regular lift over a singular image would be ramified and δ-fixed. This proves the assertion in the exchange case. If δ preserves halves, neither endpoint collapses. Any interior lift with a singular image is δ-fixed, and its mirror is a second distinct fixed point. The involution has just two ramification points, so at most one physical interior mark can have a singular image. Thus the other endpoint again has two distinct regular images. [D91 kernel and axis argument](stabilizer-orbits.md)

Consequently in degree two the quotient mirror-orbit bound cannot be vacuous at both ends through collapse or singularity. At one endpoint u is regular and distinct from its quotient mirror u′, and |(F_N)_u·u′|≤56 e(S), with the notation S for that endpoint. A uniform growth theorem for these particular projected pairs could now be used upstairs. Neither group primitivity nor area growth downstairs gives that theorem automatically.

There is an effective geometric alternative for two **distinct regular** quotient images u=f(s), v=f(t). If two legal segments downstairs have relative loop winding oddly about one branch value in the sphere punctured at the two branch values (equivalently their relative transport is the deck swap), their lifts from s reach both preimages of v, including t. This follows by the same C₂ monodromy and uses no bound on B(r). It is sufficient; a legal upstairs path may project through a downstairs pole. Cases with a singular image or coincident u,v are outside this test. [D94 one-way transport review](../../illumination-generalization-2026-11-06/fable/directions-2026-11-09/d94-review-transfer/review.md)

**Verdict and next move.** Neither branch closes. In the imprimitive branch the transfer works for split mirror fibers and all degrees k, including k≥5, but a bounded suborbit is compatible with the lit cross. The degree-two case offers a geometric next question: for a seed having distinct regular quotient images, when do the two branch poles separate two legal quotient segments? A separation theorem with stated exceptions would give an actual lighting certificate without affine mixing or a numerical shadow budget. For primitive sheets the separate target remains an actual marked return taking one of the four edges to a lit edge. No discovery search was run.
