Advances: L1 — The first return from the shortened right cap obstructs inclusion at fixed entry and velocity. Endpoint inclusion requires comparing different histories.

Let P have the specified ten-wall chart, both line orders 3,4,2,0,1, and primitive horizontal wall gaps (n,n+1,n+2,n+3), n ≥ 3. Let P_s shorten only the terminal right rectangle of width w=n+3 and height h by s=1 or 2. Both moves preserve wall order, simplicity and axis normalization (the first two unchanged gaps are coprime). The vertical factor is arbitrary, including a line at its primitive representative. Assume 4 divides h. Take distinct interior lattice marks S,T strictly left of the mouth, with odd vertical coordinate difference. They remain fixed in P_s. Assume darkness in P conditionally.

### The inclusion needed for darkness

In the common part of the two rooms let R_Q(S) denote the lattice points reached from S by a finite legal ray in Q. Inclusion R_{P_s}(S) ⊆ R_P(S) would preserve the pair; for this pair alone only the implication at T is needed. Taking its contrapositive changes no quantifiers. A ray that stays in the common exterior of the cap works unchanged. Hence, conditional on darkness in P, any ray to T in P_s would have to use the altered part of the room.

### First failed step: one visit to the cap

Fix an absolute slope r=|p|/q>0 in lowest terms. At an interior point of the mouth encode height and vertical velocity sign by a phase θ modulo 2h, increasing in the direction of travel in the vertical unfolding. Height is the fold of θ into [0,h]. On the entering section the horizontal velocity points right; on the exiting section it points left. For a cap of width d the exit phase is θ+2dr, provided the excursion clears the far corners. The cap is deterministic, so each regular entry has at most one exit. The short and long exit graphs over the *same* entry are disjoint whenever 2sr is nonzero modulo 2h. If the long excursion hits a corner it has no legal exit at all. Even allowing different numbers of horizontal-wall hits does not repair this step.

For a putative S-to-T ray in P_s, unfolding at integral walls makes its vertical displacement an odd integer multiple of a unit lattice spacing, since reflection preserves each coordinate modulo 2. This displacement is an integer multiple of p, so p is odd. Equality of the exits would require hq to divide sp. Since 4 divides h and s=1 or 2, equality is impossible. Thus the first obstructing excursion is already a **single entry, reflection at the far vertical wall, and return to the mouth** at the target-compatible slope. No second visit or special sequence of wall hits is needed. For a regular mouth crossing the outgoing height or vertical velocity must differ. At a mouth endpoint one must separately check wall or vertex incidence; the regular-excursion obstruction does not rely on how such limiting paths are treated.

### Changing the entry history: exact one-visit test

For fixed r, let A_S(r) be the set of entering phases attainable from S up to the first regular cap entry, using only the common exterior before entry. Let B_T(r) be the set of exiting phases from which T is reachable in the common exterior without another cap entry, at the velocity encoded by the exit phase. These sets are independent of cap width. Restrict A_S to A_d by keeping entries with a legal regular excursion of width d. Then an S-to-T path with exactly one regular cap visit at this slope exists in width d if and only if

    (A_d + 2 d r) ∩ B_T(r) is nonempty   (mod 2h).

Necessity follows by cutting the path at the two mouth crossings; sufficiency follows by gluing the three pieces with matching position and velocity. Darkness in P forces the intersection for d=w to be empty. It does not, by the translation formula alone, force the intersection for d=w−s to be empty. To reproduce a given short exit using width w at this slope, the required long entry is θ−2sr. This value must itself belong to A_w. The formula for the cap gives no exterior path from S to that entry.

The phase shift is rational but nontrivial. Its additive order on the phase circle is hq/gcd(hq,s|p|), a multiple of four for s=1 and of two for s=2 under the parity assumptions. Thus even an approach that closes the first-entry set under this shift would have to account for several distinct reachable phases and their corner clearances. This order is an arithmetic requirement for closure, not evidence that the exterior supplies the phases. As a matter of set logic two distinct translates of a singleton can meet different exit sets. That explains the missing implication; it does not assert that such singleton sets occur in a dark room.

**First case still open.** A regular one-visit path in P_s connecting these marks, with no proven replacement entry in P. It remains possible for another entry, slope, or number of visits in P to reach T, or for another shrink to inherit a different dark pair. The first failure established here is of the local excursion-graph inclusion. Neither failure nor validity of inclusion of the actual endpoint sets follows. Consequently no inherited dark pair or general corner-budget statement is obtained.
