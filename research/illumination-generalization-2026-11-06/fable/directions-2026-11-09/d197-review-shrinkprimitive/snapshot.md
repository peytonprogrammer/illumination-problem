Advances: L1 — A one-gap shrink leaves the primitive horizontal factor for every n ≥ 3. The test is whether illumination of the shortened room supplies a path that survives restoring the gap.

Fix the chart with both wall orders 3,4,2,0,1. Work with distinct interior lattice marks S,T, including their coordinate lines in the marked chart. Project fine gaps to the four wall gaps by summing subdivisions. Assume the horizontal factor U projects onto the line spanned by v=(n,n+1,n+2,n+3), and take a positive integral marked realization projecting to v. The vertical factor V initially has no rank restriction. Its positive integral marked realization specifies all vertical wall and mark coordinates. Darkness of this product is a hypothesis under test.

**Admissible horizontal moves actually exist.** At most two of the four open horizontal coordinate gaps contain an x-coordinate of S or T. Choose either step s=1,2 in any gap containing neither. Keep all x-lines up to its left bank fixed, and translate the right bank and everything to its right by −s, marks included. All wall gaps stay positive since n ≥ 3. The order of the marked lines and cell occupancy are unchanged, so the marks remain distinct interior lattice points and the room stays strictly simple. Among the three unchanged horizontal wall gaps there are two consecutive integers, hence their gcd is one; the vertical gaps and their normalization are unchanged. Thus the fully-lit premise gives at least one legal path between these specific transported marks for each of these moves. This avoids assuming that arbitrary marks can be rounded or that some successor exists.

**What the product can decide.** For every j and s>0, v−s e_j is off the horizontal line: any unchanged coordinate forces the proportionality constant to be one. This holds independently of how marks are chosen in the successor. On the vertical side there is a positive result: if at fixed horizontal fine coordinates a different point of V realizes an admissible one-gap vertical shrink with interior integral marks, the successor inherits a dark pair under the retained-product hypothesis. This contradicts full illumination of all successors. But take V to have wall projection a line spanned by an arbitrary primitive positive four-vector b and realize b at its primitive representative. Any positive-gap vertical shrink leaves that line by the same calculation; a collapse changes charts and is not certified by this product either. This proves a limit on the use of product membership, for arbitrary allowed mark subdivisions. It says nothing about membership in other dark products.

**Geometric transfer test.** For one mark-free horizontal shrink, fix any finite legal itinerary between the transported marks in the shorter room. Restore the strip continuously by a parameter t from 0 to s. Unfold that itinerary. For every specified wall endpoint C and the unfolded target image T*, all x-coordinates are affine in t (reflections in vertical lines preserve affinity); all y-coordinates are fixed. Thus the signed side test
     
    det(T*−S,C−S) = F(0) + t B

is exactly affine. For an ordinary transverse crossing of the relative interior of a wall segment, its two endpoints must lie on opposite sides of the shot line. Strict legality at t=0 gives these signs there, but continuing that itinerary to t=s requires the signs there too. In particular whenever B has sign opposite F(0), retaining the strict sign requires |F(0)| > s |B|. Integrality of the data gives |F(0)| ≥ 1 for a nonzero test; it gives no such comparison to B. An itinerary avoiding the changed strip altogether lifts by translation on its unchanged side. Full illumination of the successor gives no avoidance or clearance guarantee for the prescribed pair. This endpoint test is a necessary check for transverse hits, not a characterization of all paths.

**Why combining shrinks needs another lemma.** Even in the favorable case of marks on wall-coordinate lines there is a precise compatibility condition. Then the horizontal wall coordinates determine the mark x-coordinates linearly, with fixed line assignments. For all four one-unit shrinks the marked order is unchanged. With Y fixed, the determinants above are homogeneous linear functions of the horizontal wall gaps (choose the left wall line as origin). For L=Σ v_j,
     
    Σ_j (v_j/L)(v−e_j) = (1−1/L)v.

Consequently any *common* list of strict linear sign requirements valid on the four shrinks is also valid at v. In particular this repairs the transverse endpoint-sign step if the same unfolded itinerary and orientations work on all four. The premise supplies a possibly different itinerary for each successor. Its quantifiers do not supply a common list of signs. For interior mark coordinates the same argument would require compatible changes to their subdivisions as well. The single-shrink calculation above already applies to those marks without this extra condition.

**Initial transfer test.** The first unsupported geometric implication is “a fully lit admissible shrink supplies, for the transported marks, an itinerary whose required signs survive restoration on at least one axis.” Neither the one-path extrapolation nor the four-shrink averaging establishes it. The rank-one product on each axis blocks the fallback of inheriting darkness on the same product. D152 (RESULTS §11) rules out a wall-blind uniform cutoff already at W=22 and explicitly leaves one-way descent open. The obstruction here is to transferring the proof; no dark instance or counterexample to descent is established. This ends the same-factor argument. The first open case is uniform geometric transfer or exclusion of this primitive marked product, already for a rank-one vertical wall projection. No general reflex-corner choice lemma follows.

## Approved continuation — restoring a terminal rectangle

There is a way to transfer a path without continuing the same sequence of wall hits. In the reviewed chart the rightmost column, from x0 to x1, is a rectangle of width w=n+3 and height h=y1−y0. Its whole left side, except its endpoints, opens into the top slab. Take any two distinct interior lattice marks strictly to the left of x0, in any vertical factor V. They keep exactly the same coordinates under either rightmost shrink. Consider a path between them in the room with that column shortened by s=1 or 2. If it avoids the column it already works in the original room. Otherwise use the following test on each excursion.

Measure height from the lower wall of the rectangle. Write the signed slope at entry, with horizontal velocity pointing right, as r. Unfold reflections at the two horizontal walls by using a vertical phase modulo 2h. The short excursion returns to the opening after horizontal travel 2(w−s); restoring the column adds 2s to that travel. The exit horizontal velocity points left in both rooms. For nonhorizontal motion, away from the endpoints, the phase records both folded height and the sign of vertical velocity. Thus the restored excursion has exactly the old exit state if and only if

    s r ∈ h Z.

A horizontal excursion satisfies this condition too. All excursions have the same absolute slope, so one divisibility condition serves the entire path. There is one additional legality test: the restored far-wall contact must miss both corners. If the entry height is z, its unfolded height at the restored wall is z+wr. Under the stated condition this differs from the old far-wall height z+(w−s)r by a multiple of h. Hence the old noncorner contact guarantees the new one. Horizontal reflections between entry, contact and exit are at smooth wall points. Splicing all restored excursions therefore gives a legal path with the original endpoints. Its horizontal-wall reflection count may change. This gives an exact positive transfer criterion uniform in n and independent of the factor height.

**Obstruction, uniform in n.** Write the nonzero slope in lowest terms as p/q, q>0. Returning to the same exit state requires q | s and h | (s/q)p. Now take any positive integral vertical realization with 4 | h and two marks as above whose y-coordinates differ by an odd number. This is a condition on the realization, compatible with a primitive rank-one vertical wall vector, for example any (1,b2,b3,4k) with positive entries. The other vertical coordinates and any integral mark subdivisions are unrestricted subject to the chart and interior conditions. No darkness assumption is needed for the obstruction.

In an unfolding across integral orthogonal walls, the two coordinates of each image of T are congruent to those of T modulo 2. The unfolded vertical displacement from S is therefore odd. For any connecting path entering the column it equals an integer multiple of p: the horizontal and vertical displacements are integral, and the direction is primitive. Thus p is odd. But for s ∈ {1,2}, the required divisibility 4 | (s/q)p is impossible. A horizontal path cannot connect these marks either. This proves that **no path using the shortened right cap can be transferred by preserving its entrance and exit states excursion by excursion**, for either allowed step in this parity class. This is a failure of a geometric transfer rule, even allowing a different number of reflections. It is not a failure of illumination. If the successor has a path avoiding the cap, that path still lifts. The calculation is for the right cap only; it gives no obstruction to a different gap, a different original slope, or changing an exit state and compensating later.

**Where the approved test stops.** The first missing implication can now be stated without a clearance estimate. For these marks, full illumination of both right-cap shrinks supplies a connecting path in each. Does it supply one avoiding that cap, or does a different admissible gap permit a transfer? In this parity class the same-exit alternative is arithmetically unavailable. The three reflex corners establish the terminal rectangle's shape but supply no choice between the remaining alternatives. With rank-one wall projections on both axes the same-product fallback is unavailable as proved above. This is the requested obstruction to the transfer attempt; the implication from *all* fully lit shrinks to illumination of the original pair remains open.
