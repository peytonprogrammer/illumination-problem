**Grid and moves.** Use a translated axis-aligned rectangular grid: divide each coordinate by the gcd of the vertex and mark differences on that axis, then use unit squares. Independent axial scaling preserves chords and reflections. “Coarsest lattice” needs this qualifier: an arbitrary lattice generated by all point differences can be oblique and does not give the specified rectangular cells. The theorem below in fact holds on any such adapted rectangular grid, including the unnormalized unit grid.

Let Aᵢ exchange cells across vertical edges at x≡i and Bⱼ across horizontal edges at y≡j, fixing a cell at a boundary edge. These four involutions act transitively by connectedness. Over one base tile of the doubled rectangle (pillowcase), take the front copy of even checkerboard cells and the back copy of odd cells. A positive pole loop advances by two quadrant sectors on the oriented double. At (i,j) it applies Aᵢ then Bⱼ for i=j, and the reverse order otherwise: from the NE quadrant positive rotation crosses west then south on the front, south then west on the back. Across a reflected boundary the second quadrant lies on the other copy. Thus an interior link gives two opposite-cell transpositions, a smooth wall one transposition, a convex corner a fixed point, and a reflex a 3-cycle. Reversing the global orientation inverts the cycles; choosing reflex orientations independently need not describe the cover. The group G generated by the four products is the even-word image of the edge group: any pair of unlike-axis edges is a generator or inverse, and a like-axis pair factors through an intervening involution of the other axis. G is transitive since the punctured double is connected (also a boundary involution fixes a cell, allowing adjustment of word parity).

**Exact meaning of separation.** Write Q for the doubled room, B for the pillowcase, and N for the intermediate branched cover defined by an invariant partition. A quotient point over a pole is a *cycle* of its permutation on blocks. A physical interior mark has two lifts in Q. Its two image cycles in N can coincide. The incident block set used by fp.py is their union. Disjoint block sets imply disjoint point sets immediately; mere inequality does not prove disjointness in the proposed quotient.

Here inequality still suffices. For each off-class x form the least G-invariant equivalence merging the endpoints of every x-transposition upstairs. Join these three equivalences to get Π. Any quotient without off-class 2-cycles coarsens Π, by cycle-length divisibility. Conversely Π kills every off-class 2-cycle, since upstairs lengths are 1,2,3. Each of the three equivalences is invariant under all four edge involutions: one edge factor of gₓ conjugates it to its inverse and preserves its transposition pairs. It normalizes G, hence preserves their G-closure. This edge and G generate the whole edge group. Their join Π therefore also respects the edge moves (the d64/R3.3 mechanism).

On Π the vertical edge involution at a conjugates gₐ to its inverse, so it permutes the quotient cycles above a. At each interior mark it exchanges the two diagonally opposite-cell cycles upstairs. Consequently the two marked images form one orbit of this same involution on quotient points, of size one or two. Two such orbits coincide or are disjoint. Unequal incident sets downstairs must remain unequal on refinement, since equal sets have equal images on coarsening. Thus fp.py's inequality produces disjoint fibers on Π. For the singleton partition the distinct physical interior stars of the same parity share no cell. No regular-image assumption is needed.

**FP implies dark.** A putative chord develops to a nonzero even-even integer displacement. Write it as an even number of primitive steps. On B the steps traverse one primitive arc between the marked pole a and one fixed off-class x, alternately in the two directions. This holds for axis directions too. A legal intermediate lattice hit is smooth upstairs, so its degree over B is two. The local degree of its image in N divides two. At x there is no quotient degree two, leaving degree one: the projected path returns along the same lifted arc to the same point above a. A quotient 3-cycle cannot be reached there on a legal path. At each next a point the outgoing lifted arc can change, but the next return still fixes that point. Induction puts the endpoint in the starting quotient point, contrary to disjointness. This argument applies to either physical lift.

**A′ count.** Write k=deg(Q→N), m=deg(N→B); the normalized room has km cells. If m=1 there is only one quotient point above a and separation is impossible. If k=1, every off-class site is a corner upstairs. Around each interior mark its four unit axial neighbors already have two adjacent incident cells and must therefore be reflex. A shared neighbor would put the same-parity marks two axial units apart, with four cells at that neighbor, a contradiction. Thus there are eight distinct reflexes. The third cell at the eastern neighbor of S contributes a second adjacent cell at one eastern diagonal neighbor of S. That off-class site must also be reflex, with parity different from the eight. Hence r≥9 (d14/d217).

Suppose k,m≥2 and r≤8. N has genus zero by Riemann–Hurwitz from the sphere Q. All its local degrees over the four poles divide upstairs lengths and are 1,2,3. Write z for the number of degree-three points and q for the degree-two points. Counting sheets and ramification over the four poles gives q=2m−2−2z. All q occur over a, whence 2q≤m and m≤4(z+1)/3. In particular z≥1. Each quotient degree-three point has k unramified preimages, each an upstairs reflex. Hence kz≤8, so z≤4. For z=1,2,3,4 respectively the bounds on (k,m) are (8,2),(4,4),(2,5),(2,6), componentwise. In all four cases km≤16. This step does not require q≥2 or either marked image to be regular. D9/d205/d206 cover these strictly simple rooms and every same-parity interior pair in them. FP would supply a dark pair, contradiction. Thus W=2r+4≥22, with equality realized by R22. This is an all-area implication with a finite verified base.
