# Physical axis midpoint: a pi-sector split at a reflex cannot connect distinct actual points

Hand proof, submitted for independent challenge. This is a physical reflection-quotient assertion at a genuine cone; it is not a rule allowing rays through that cone.

## 1. Prior scope and exact statement

A targeted read of existing axis/reflex/retracing assertions found the earlier failure of universal smooth first-axis returns (reflex-visibility-obligation.md), and the conditional opposite-sector convex-return construction. Neither proves nor refutes the statement here. Regular convex-corner erasure is a separate accepted dependency; the present midpoint is a genuine6pi cone, the direction is AXIAL and the two halves have equal length with ONE specified pi-sector between their germs. No claim for arbitrary cone continuation, nonaxial directions or unequal halves is made.

Let M be the connected four-copy reflection unfolding of a strictly simple orthogonal room, with physical quotient pi_P:M->P and physical Klein four action Gamma. Let Z be its genuine cones. Let z in Z. Let alpha and beta be length-L straight geodesic arcs emanating from z to regular actual interior lifts x and y, with L>0, and no Z point in their open parts. Their developed directions are opposite horizontal directions (or opposite vertical directions). Suppose their two germs bound a single flat pi sector in the cone star. Then pi_P(x)=pi_P(y). In particular disjoint actual interior endpoint orbits cannot be joined by this equal-half axial pi-sector split.

## 2. Physical cone-star classification

Rotate/reflect the coordinate frame so one original copy near z is the sector0<=t<=3pi/2. The completed cone has t modulo6pi. The two actual physical wall reflections fixing z act locally as

    h(t)=-t,
    v(t)=3pi-t.

Their derivatives are the horizontal-axis and vertical-axis reflections in developed coordinates. Their product is rotation by3pi, the intrinsic half-turn on the6pi cone. These formulas follow by reflecting the original3pi/2 sector across its boundary rays; they are actual Gamma isometries of the WHOLE unfolding, not locally invented maps.

First take alpha's outward developed direction to be west. Its three possible prongs have angles

    pi, 3pi, 5pi.

The prong3pi is fixed by h. A point on the corresponding outgoing germ is therefore h-fixed. Uniqueness of straight continuation in direction west, which h preserves, implies the whole Z-free arc from z along this prong is h-fixed. Its endpoint would be a fixed point of a nontrivial physical reflection, hence project to the physical boundary. Actual interior points have free Gamma orbits, so this prong is impossible for alpha.

For the prong at pi, the two east prongs at angular distance pi are0 and2pi. The east prong0 is h-fixed, so beta cannot have that prong if y is interior. The remaining allowed one is2pi, exactly v(pi). For the west prong5pi, its pi-adjacent east prongs are4pi and0; again0 is boundary-fixed, and4pi=v(5pi) modulo6pi is the only possible interior one.

Thus in every allowable case beta's germ is v applied to alpha's germ. If alpha initially points east instead, exchange the arcs (their lengths agree) and apply the just-proved west case. Vertical directions are the same classification after exchanging coordinate axes. The pi-sector hypothesis is essential: opposite developed directions may be represented by other prongs separated by3pi or5pi, which the argument does not identify with v(alpha).

## 3. Global equal-half endpoint identity

The actual global isometry v fixes z, maps genuine zeros to themselves and maps alpha's initial germ to beta's. Away from Z, the straight flow with given point and velocity is unique, including at regular physical corner lifts on M. Therefore v(alpha(s))=beta(s) for all0<s<=L. Equal lengths give y=v(x). Since v belongs to the physical Gamma action,

    pi_P(y)=pi_P(v(x))=pi_P(x).

The boundary-fixed alternatives in section2 similarly stay fixed throughout their Z-free arcs and cannot have interior endpoints. No legal passage through z has been assumed; this classifies two separate geodesic halves and their quotient endpoints. Regular physical corner contacts in the halves do not invalidate the surface uniqueness argument; they are ordinary flat points of M. The claim does not say those folded half-arcs are legal physical paths through such contacts.
