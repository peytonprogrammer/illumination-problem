# C2: global chord completion plus isolated corner completion

Hand proposal published before computational tests. No discovery search or
new clock is started. This is a geometric availability/complexity lemma
candidate; its optical transfer assertions remain unproved.

For any finite strictly simple orthogonal lattice disk D, define H(D) by
replacing each nonempty horizontal section of D by its full interval hull,
taking closure of the resulting interior. Define V(D) analogously with
vertical sections. Both keep the ORIGINAL distinct interior even-even S,T.

Proposed geometric lemma: H(D) and V(D) remain strictly simple orthogonal
lattice disks, and each strictly decreases r whenever it changes D. If
both leave D unchanged and r>0, an isolated C1 rectangle is available.
Consequently H, V, C1 together are globally available strict-r reductions
for every nonconvex actual target room, including protected endpoints.

Proposed checkable charge: for horizontal boundary walls, let M count those
with one convex and one reflex endpoint, R those with two reflex endpoints,
and C those with two convex endpoints. Then r(D)=M+2R and C=R+2. Every
reflex turn of H(D) should charge to a distinct mixed wall or an internal
convex-convex cap, with at least the global bottom and top caps uncharged.
This would give r(H(D))<=M+C-2=M+R, strict when R>0.

Main objections to challenge: multiple events at one height; whether one
convex cap can pay two new outer-envelope turns; strict simplicity at a
critical height; whether both-axis convexity really guarantees the FULL
C1 isolation condition, not merely an exterior open rectangle.

The optical obligation remains explicit: for EACH selected operation on an
original r<=8 room, existence of a legal actual S/T path afterward must
imply existence before, allowing rerouting. The geometry lemma by itself
does not prove any such implication or any illumination theorem.
