# B05 supplement: full-orbit calibration is stronger than individual inequalities

Frozen base: global-exchange-transition.md, SHA256 23476cda4d4eaba1a57704d22bd9040a98c11f4a470b353d004816de24960996. This supplement changes none of its scoped conclusions or evidence. No new orbit, state, control, or computation is used.

Suppose H >= (9-r)a + Delta_T Phi + Delta_U Psi holds on the ENTIRE finite labelled known22 orbit, where r=9 and H=0. Then D=Delta_T Phi+Delta_U Psi <=0 at every state. Since T and U are bijections of this finite orbit, sum D=0. Every individual D must therefore be zero. This assertion is about the labelled finite orbit; a real-structure-dependent potential must first satisfy the descent requirement in the separate real-structure note.

For the symmetric additive terminal cost Phi=F_v, Psi=F_h of section3, the actual A row gives 4Y-4P=0, hence P=Y. The actual B row gives 8P-2Y-6X=0, hence Y=X. Consequently

    f(periodic,4) = f(zero,3) = f(zero,1).

Thus threshold-wide calibration on this whole negative control excludes EVERY symmetric terminal-cost proposal distinguishing these three observed terminal classes, whether its zero-length cost increases or decreases. The two-state inequality proof in the base document remains useful because it needs no orbit-sum premise; it already excludes strictly increasing zero-length costs from the two prescribed states alone.

This is a constraint on a precisely named potential family. It does not prove such costs are constant at unobserved lengths, exclude direction-asymmetric or label-sensitive features, show anything impossible for r<=8-only coefficients, or prove a wall lower bound. An arbitrary pair of potentials can have a nontrivial divergence-free cancellation on the negative orbit, so nonconstant potentials in general remain possible.

Practical consequence: replacing the failed periodic count with an isotropic first-stop penalty does not supply the missing positive charge. To advance the original target, a candidate must use more information (for example which labelled separatrix meets which source/target fiber), prove its own transition inequality, and justify finite-orbit descent if it depends on the physical reflection. This is an unproved requirement, not an asserted successful replacement.
