# Axis-case correction for the beveled43-wall candidate

This supplement corrects the universal projection statement in Section5 of the frozen candidate. A physical trajectory can lie along an interior axial seam; its projection then glides along the base wall and cannot be called an ordinary nonsingular interior billiard path. The original text did not separately exclude this case. The following exact hand argument supplies the missing case for the stated room and actual S,T.

## Horizontal launches from S

The seam through S=(0,0) is precisely [-1,1]x{0}. Both endpoints (-1,0),(1,0) are SMOOTH physical points, each in the relative interior of an actual vertical boundary wall: the two reflected90-degree base sectors meet to give a straight vertical wall through the endpoint. No bevel or third copy reaches that seam neighborhood.

A horizontal ray from S therefore reaches x1 or x-1 at y0, reflects normally, and remains on the same horizontal interval. Repeating gives only normal reversals between those two endpoints. It never reaches T=(4,4). This excludes every finite S-to-T horizontal launch; it does not treat the folded seam glide as a legal base path.

## Vertical launches from S

At x0, the segment from S up to (0,1) lies in B1, and the segment from S down to (0,-1) lies in B0. Their endpoints (0,+/-1) are GENUINE270-degree physical corners, inherited from the staircase and untouched by the distant bevel. There is no prior wall contact on either open segment. A vertical launch therefore reaches a forbidden actual corner first and cannot be a legal S-to-T path.

If the initial horizontal and vertical velocity components are BOTH nonzero, every later velocity still has both components nonzero. Thus the path never runs along an axial seam; every seam crossing is transverse except possible discrete contacts at its endpoints, already the explicitly analyzed90-degree projected reversals.

A nonaxis physical ray also cannot graze an exterior diagonal wall in its relative interior: a tangent straight line there would already coincide locally with that wall, and could not approach from the interior without an earlier endpoint/corner. All genuine physical corners remain forbidden. Consequently the ordinary base-wall projection and the finite last-projected-a/first-projected-b reversal argument in Section5 apply to EVERY remaining legal candidate path.
