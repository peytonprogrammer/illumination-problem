# Unbounded-class mechanism screen

Status: target OPEN. This is a bounded source-applicability screen, not an illumination proof. The earlier similarity and current affine obstructions eliminate unconditional finite-k normalization; they do not eliminate a darkness-dependent reduction.

## Literature screen

Two focused queries for specular illumination in monotone/orthogonally convex polygons did not retrieve a theorem proving the needed class. This is a limited negative search outcome, not a claim that no such theorem exists. Art-gallery, floodlight and diffuse-reflection results do not address the same optical rule.

Apisa--Wright, Marked points on translation surfaces (author-hosted PDF), Theorem1.1(1) covers Gaussian, Eisenstein and rectangle-tiled polygons by finite blocking. That is not absence of dark pairs. Their Theorems1.3,3.6 and3.16 impose a non-torus-cover hypothesis on the relevant classification steps; Section3.4 explicitly begins with it. Our canonical45 rooms are Gaussian and hence lie on the torus-cover side of that distinction. Thus those non-torus classification conclusions do not directly replace k<=6.

Primary source: https://public.websites.umich.edu/~alexmw/ApisaWright.pdf . Locations read: pp1--4 and Section3, especially p22 Section3.4. No theorem about emptiness was inferred from finite blocking.

Apisa, Periodic Points in Genus Two (arXiv1710.05505v1), Theorem4 concerns primitive genus-two surfaces. Its application to six-sided L rooms distinguishes the torus-cover case through rational length ratios. Our arithmetic canonical subclass is precisely not supplied by that primitive hypothesis. The older arXiv version numbers the billiard-table theorem6; journal numbering differs. The publisher HTML could not be fetched, so no claim of fully reading the journal version is made.

Primary source: https://arxiv.org/html/1710.05505 . Locations read: Introduction, definition of primitive, Theorems4--6 and Figure1. This narrows applicability; it establishes no new low-wall illumination case here.

## Noncomputational next question

A viable scale-invariant proof must retain endpoint sheets and the absorbing corner data. On a torus cover, projecting a putative connecting geodesic to a smaller quotient is safe; lifting an arbitrary quotient connection need not arrive at the actual target sheet. Therefore a compression must either preserve a full target fiber or explicitly prove the necessary monodromy. The known22-wall dark example already warns against inferring illumination from its torus image.

Proposed next attempt: identify an illumination-relevant, bounded critical skeleton of the punctured unfolding, with regular strips kept as integer repetition parameters. Success requires an exact equivalence for the actual S/T sheet sets and a bound in W; a finite boundary word, finite genus, or a finite list of sampled directions alone does not suffice. Strongest objection: straight trajectories can traverse arbitrarily many regular strips, and their endpoint permutation can depend on the repetition counts. The earlier six-wall reflection-budget example already forbids a constant reflection cutoff.

Before any implementation, test that proposed equivalence by hand on a single rectangular band attached through two portals. Derive which data determine its full first-exit relation; then determine whether integer repetitions can be removed without losing endpoint phase. If the relation needs unrestricted phase data, keep that parameter symbolically rather than assert a finite-state quotient. This is a proof question, not authorization to expand a census.

## Realignment

Established baseline: all-support W<=21,k<=6, plus reviewed scale lemmas. Goal-wide frontier remains all W<=21,k>=7. Neither this literature screen nor the affine obstruction is a positive uncapped exclusion. The remaining exact gap is a darkness-preserving structural compression or a direct optical mechanism at unbounded degree.
