# Sector-to-convex return: bounded existence audit

A28 did not prove universal sector-return existence. No nonillumination or low-wall counterexample was produced. The positive fixed-shape collar from A27 is separately accepted; it does not supply a finite return word in a varying shape.

## Exact desired implication

At a specified physical reflex270 corner O, with missing NE quadrant, find a finite nonsingular corner-to-convex path whose initial physical direction lies strictly SW. Equivalently on the intrinsic half-turn quotient, find a zero-to-simple-pole geodesic in that prescribed local sector. On the unfolding this is an invariant saddle loop based at the PARTICULAR zero over O, with a REGULAR half-turn fixed midpoint and the required outgoing germ. This would provide the missing existence premise of A26. The existence of some invariant saddle connection somewhere is insufficient.

## Attempt1: invariant saddles and directional density

The primary-author survey at https://www-fourier.univ-grenoble-alpes.fr/~lanneau/articles/splitting_survey.pdf, section4.1 near PDFpages13-14, discusses symmetric saddles and centrally symmetric polygon models. Its density input is directions from a regular fixedpoint to SOME singularity. It does not select our zero O, nor a prescribed outgoing sector there. Its genus-two construction uses H(2),H(1,1). Our general orthogonal unfolding has r fixed zeros of order2 in genus r+1; for r>=2 this is not automatically one of the hyperelliptic COMPONENTS with the usual one-zero or conjugate-two-zero descriptions. The same survey explicitly distinguishes the fixed-two-zero genus-three locus. Therefore neither the density statement nor the component representation supplies the required full implication. No new external theorem is imported.

## Attempt2: symmetric shortest paths on the unfolding

For an order-two isometry centered at a regular fixedpoint in the simply connected nonpositively curved cone-surface cover, a symmetric minimizing path from a lift of O to its image passes through that midpoint. But it can pass through other cone zeros first. A cone of angle6pi can support a minimizing broken-through-cone path: both sides can have angle at least pi. Thus shortest-path minimization does not automatically remove the intermediate forbidden vertices. Also it supplies no chosen physical SW germ. A geodesic triangulation only ensures a connected graph of cone/marked points, not a single zero-free edge to the desired endpoint type. A chain through zeros is not an admissible A26 prefix.

## Result and next approach

Retire these two proposed shortcuts. Do not label the desired sector-return theorem false: it remains open, and no exact structural counterexample was found. Standard density, some invariant saddle, or connected triangulation cannot be substituted for the missing endpoint/germ constraints. No optical discovery, new room or worker was used.

Move to a genuinely different direct construction: protect a monotone spine crossing for arbitrary selected teeth in a comb when their separating gap is long relative to tooth heights. The accepted U finite-matching argument can be used with zero extra passage windings; the issue is then positive portal widths, not existence of a singular return.
