# Orthogonal convexity does not supply a visibility kernel

Independent prerequisite challenge for the author's orthogonally convex base proposal. This uses the EXISTING E5 four-chamber room, not a new geometric census or an asserted dark example. All earlier frozen sources remain unchanged.

Let R_j=[4j,4j+4]x[4j,4j+6], j=0,1,2,3, and let D be their union. Its exact16-wall/6-reflex boundary and complete geometry control are already in geometric-reduction-ear-conditions.md and its frozen check JSON. Its actual marked pair S=(2,2),T=(14,16) has the previously checked direct legal segment.

**Horizontal and vertical convexity.** At generic x in(4j,4j+4), the vertical section is the interval[4j,4j+6]. At a joining abscissa, the two adjacent intervals overlap by2, so their union remains an interval. At a given height, the active chamber indices are consecutive because4j<=y<=4j+6 is an interval condition on j. Their x-intervals are adjacent; their union is therefore an interval. This includes critical-height sections by closure. Hence D is BOTH horizontally and vertically convex.

**Empty visibility kernel.** For a point q to see every interior point of a simple polygon, it must lie in the interior-side closed half-plane of every genuine boundary wall. Here is the needed implication directly: if q lies strictly on the exterior side of a wall, choose an interior point sufficiently close to a smooth point of that wall. The segment to q crosses the same finite wall near that smooth point and then leaves the room, contradicting visibility. This argument needs no external kernel classification theorem.

In the displayed counterclockwise boundary, wall(4,0)->(4,4) points upward and has interior on its left; any kernel point must satisfy q_x<=4. Wall(12,18)->(12,14) points downward and has interior on its right; any kernel point must satisfy q_x>=12. Those conditions are incompatible. Therefore the kernel is empty.

Thus even within r<=8, an orthogonally convex disk need not be star-shaped, have a common visible anchor, or be representable by one global rectangular core visible from all points. The accepted geometric H/V reduction cannot assume any of these properties for its fixed points. This does NOT refute all-pairs billiard illumination of orthogonally convex rooms; the old actual pair is explicitly directly lit. It rules out only a proof that replaces the required optical theorem by a visibility-kernel assertion.

Likewise an axis-monotone path assembled from horizontal/vertical legs is not a billiard witness: turns at arbitrary interior joints are not specular wall reflections. A proof of the new base case must supply actual straight legs and real boundary contacts, including original marks at seams and arbitrarily narrow windows.

This finding is for the author's active base-case analysis, not a separate family expansion. The exact issue that would unlock positive progress is a coupled optical construction valid on every orthogonally convex input, with no star-kernel premise.
