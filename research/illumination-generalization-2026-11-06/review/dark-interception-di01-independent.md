## Cone adjacency relation

At an orthogonal reflex corner, the unfolded flat cone has total angle6pi. For every developed ray direction there are exactly three distinct prongs, separated by2pi in a local flat angular coordinate. For the triangle0,u,u+v with determinant+/-1, the interior angle alpha at u lies strictly between0 andpi. Its oriented boundary rays at that vertex are -u and v.

Fix an incoming u prong, or equivalently its outward -u ray at the cone. Turning through the triangle's prescribed side and angle alpha selects a UNIQUE outgoing v prong among the three: exactly one representative lies at that angular offset. Reversing this local rule recovers the incoming prong, so it is a bijection b between the three slots. The selected wedge has anglealpha<pi and develops one-to-one onto the triangle angle, despite the full cone's6pi angle.

If the incoming primitive-period leg starts at an actual regular x in X, the matching outgoing b-prong reaches an actual regular y in Y in one primitive period, then this wedge belongs to the same lifted empty triangle as the incoming edge. The triangle interior and open edges have no other lattice points. Complete its other two vertices at regular x,y. Its diagonal is a single straight (u+v) segment with exact endpoints x,y and no cone or other origin-fiber event in its open part. This construction never treats the turn at the cone as a legal optical trajectory.

Under actual darkness, therefore b(I_u(z)) and J_v(z) are disjoint. Since b is a bijection on three slots, |I_u(z)|+|J_v(z)|<=3. This is a valid abstract local necessary relation. It supplies no existence of occupied I or J, global cone encounter or physical-corner charge.

## Why this unit-period relation has no additional physical force here

For an orthogonal INTEGRAL room, reflection in x=c or y=c has position translation2c along that coordinate and a diagonal sign derivative. Thus every developed chart transition preserves coordinate parity. All lifts of actual even-even S and T have even-even developed coordinates. Every developed lift/image of a particular physical corner C has parity C modulo2; changing sheet or adding a period does not change that parity.

An incoming leg of positive integer duration a in primitive direction u from some X lift to the physical corner has

    C= a*u modulo2.

An outgoing positive integer duration b in direction v from that same cone to a Y lift similarly has

    C= b*v modulo2.

Hence a*u=b*v modulo2. Determinant+/-1 makes u,v an invertible basis over F2. It follows that a=0 modulo2 AND b=0 modulo2, and C=0 modulo2. The durations are integers because all these endpoints lie over0 and u,v are primitive for the specified UNIT lattice. These are congruences for actual corner images, not just a common torus projection.

In particular a=b=1 is impossible in EVERY room in this even-even class, irrespective of darkness. So either I_u(z) or J_v(z) is empty for each z (indeed only directions with the cone's nonzero parity can support a one-period arrival/departure). Its three-slot capacity inequality is then automatic: one occupied set alone has at most three prongs. It cannot yield a new dark-room ramification or nine-corner restriction.

The general shared-cone congruence is a genuine restriction: under these endpoint/lattice hypotheses, Farey-neighbor legs can share a corner only at EVEN times, and that corner must be even-even. But it does not prohibit sharing at even times or require different physical corners for different primitive directions. No independent counting is justified.
