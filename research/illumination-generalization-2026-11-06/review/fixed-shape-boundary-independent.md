# Independent review of fixed-shape finite-dark-pair deductions

Verdict: ACCEPT all stated deductions, with the qualitative, fixed-shape restrictions retained. This is an application of an imported published theorem, not an independent proof of that theorem.

Primary source checked directly: Amit Wolecki, Illumination in Rational Billiards, version3 (30 November2024), https://arxiv.org/html/1905.09358v3, Definition1.1, Theorem1.2(i), Lemma2.1 and Remark2.2. The theorem gives global finiteness of physical nonilluminating pairs for a rational-angle polygon with connected interior; no non-torus-cover restriction belongs to part(i). Its trajectories stop at vertices, and its lift formulation uses existence between full physical fibers. Thus the import matches our actual endpoint and corner conventions. The claim is not global finiteness for arbitrary translation surfaces.

For one fixed polygon P, each endpoint of every distinct dark ordered pair has positive boundary distance, and each pair has positive separation. Taking half the minima over the finite dark set gives positive boundary and diagonal collars, uniform over all actual interior pairs in P. If the set is empty any positive constants suffice. No limiting corner is used as an endpoint.

An invertible affine map satisfying the explicit reflection-conjugacy condition maps every actual legal finite trajectory to an actual legal finite trajectory. The inverse proves equality of dark sets. Corners, finite mirror interiors, internal legs and actual arrival are preserved. Similarities satisfy this in all orientations; independent positive axial scalings do so for orthogonal walls because diagonal reflections commute with their linear parts. The statement properly excludes general anisotropic changes of diagonal-wall rooms.

Along a normalized endpoint sequence, if the minimum of the two boundary distances and pair separation tends to zero, it eventually lies below the minimum of the three positive collar thresholds. At each index at least one applicable collar condition therefore holds, even if the smallest quantity alternates. Similarity scaling of bounded physical distance gives the advertised uniform result. This includes opposite-arm reflex coalescence without a convex return assumption, but only for the same normalized polygon.

A continuous map from a connected parameter space into the finite dark ordered-pair set is constant. This uses the ordinary topology on the actual interior product and never identifies source with target or sheets. It does not apply to a family with intervening illuminated parameters.

For rational one-parameter normalized coordinates, choose a globally nonconstant reduced coordinate p/q. Each dark pair imposes p-cq=0; nonconstancy excludes an identically zero polynomial for every real c. Each has at most max(deg p,deg q) roots. The finite union is finite, even with real coefficients or disconnected allowed pole-free domains. The all-constant and multi-parameter exclusions are necessary and explicitly retained.
