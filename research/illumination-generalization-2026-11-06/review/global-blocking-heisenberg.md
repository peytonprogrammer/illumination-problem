## 2. A connected27-square translation surface

```
(a,b,c)*(A,B,C)=(a+A,b+B,c+C+aB) mod3.
```

Put r=(1,0,0),u=(0,1,0),z=(0,0,1). Then z=[r,u] is nontrivial central of order3. Every element of G has cube identity; explicitly

```
(a,b,c)^n=(na,nb,nc+n(n-1)ab/2),

and at n3 all entries vanish modulo3. The quotient by the center is F3², with quotient coordinates(a,b). The elements r,u generate G, since their commutator supplies z and r^a u^b z^(c-ab) gives every triple.

Take one oriented unit square for every g in G. Glue the right edge of square g to the left edge of square g*r, and its top edge to the bottom edge of square g*u, by translations with matching unit-edge coordinates. These right/up maps are permutations, and the generated square adjacency is connected. This is a connected closed oriented translation surface M with area27 and a natural translation map

```

of degree27. All branching is over0, the square-vertex image.

There are therefore nine vertices, each with angle6pi and genuine zero order2. There are NO regular points over0. Euler gives V9,E54,F27, hence2-2g=-18 and g10. ## 3. Central deck translation

Left multiplication by z on square labels commutes with BOTH right multiplications r,u, so it defines a global affine automorphism tau of M. Its derivative is+I, it preserves p and it has order3. On M minus the nine zeros it acts freely, since the unbranched regular covering has a free deck action. Thus x and tau(x) are distinct regular points for every regular x. It permutes the nine vertex cycles trivially as sets: left z equals right z and stays in each length-three cycle. Hence tau fixes every genuine zero. No extra removable corner marks are added.

## 4. ALL straight directions are excluded for x to tau(x)

Assume there were a finite positive-length straight segment from regular x to tau(x) avoiding genuine zeros. Project under p. Because p(x)=p(tau(x)), its nonzero holonomy is an integer vector(a,b). The projected trajectory is a closed straight torus geodesic. It avoids0, because EVERY point over0 is a genuine zero and the original segment avoids them.

Write(a,b)=d(a0,b0), with d=gcd(|a|,|b|)>=1 and primitive integer(a0,b0). The projected trajectory is exactly d repetitions of the SAME simple primitive closed straight torus loop based at p(x), also avoiding0. Let h in G be that primitive loop's monodromy. Its abelianization is(a0,b0) modulo3, under the displayed r,u convention. This is nonzero: if both coordinates were divisible by3, the integer vector would not be primitive.

Consequently the monodromy of the full projected segment is h^d, with h having nonzero abelianization. Since EVERY h has order3:

- if3 divides d, h^d is identity;
- otherwise h^d has nonzero abelianization.

Neither possibility equals z or z², the two nontrivial central elements. But a lift from a chosen sheet g to tau's sheet z*g=g*z requires exactly right-monodromy z (or its inverse under an opposite convention). Contradiction. Thus no such zero-avoiding segment exists, for EVERY regular x and EVERY direction and finite length. The same proof excludes tau²(x).

This is an all-directions group argument, not failure of a finite ray search. Choosing a sheet at a basepoint on a square edge only conjugates the monodromy representation; z is central and the nonzero-abelianization property is unchanged. One may also choose a small unbranched chart to set the sheet convention. There is no hidden source-sheet/target-sheet replacement.

## 5. What this says about correspondences and monodromy

The graph {(x,tau(x)):x regular} is an embedded translation-surface graph with projection degree1 in both factors and scalar slope+1. It lies entirely in the TRUE-ZERO nonillumination locus. It cannot be covered by finitely many negative-slope graph patches and isolated points: their intersections with this open2D positive-slope graph are locally discrete. Thus generic reversing/negative-slope-only classification is not an available theorem.

Horizontal/vertical monodromy r,u generate the entire transitive27-sheet group. Nevertheless the central target sheet cannot be reached by a single straight trajectory from the same regular torus point. Concatenating horizontal and vertical paths changes direction and is not illumination. Algebraically the union of cyclic groups generated by elements of nonzero abelianization contains identity and the24 noncentral elements, and excludes z,z². This is an UPPER ENVELOPE of possible single-direction return monodromies; the proof does NOT claim that all25 are actually realized by primitive straight directions at every basepoint.

This gives a discriminating obstacle for an all-primitive return-map proof: group generation alone is insufficient. Compatibility of the one global straight direction matters, even though BOTH coordinate generators are transitive together and all true zeros have the mild angle6pi.
