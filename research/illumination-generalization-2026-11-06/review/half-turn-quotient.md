# Universal intrinsic half-turn darkness and its quotient

New exact hand corollary for independent review. It extends the peer's six-wall hostile control to every canonical45 room. It is not a physical dark-pair construction or a wall lower bound.

Let P be a finite strictly simple canonical45 polygon with genuine maximal walls, and use its minimal connected q=2N reflection unfolding M, where N=2 for an orthogonal wall frame and N=4 otherwise. Let Gamma be the full linear dihedral reflection group acting on M, let rho be its central half-turn with derivative -I, and retain every actual corner lift in Sigma, including regular corner lifts.

## 1. Exact fixed set

    Fix(rho)=Sigma.

A physical interior point lies in a free Gamma orbit, so no such lift is fixed by rho. At a smooth boundary point, its stabilizer is the order-two subgroup generated by the single physical wall reflection; rho is orientation preserving and is not that reflection. Thus it fixes no smooth-wall lift.

At a genuine corner, its local stabilizer is generated by the two incident wall reflections. In the orthogonal case, their product is the half-turn. In the N=4 case, write the interior angle m*pi/4. The allowed genuine m are1,2,3,5,6,7. The product of the two incident reflections is a rotation through plus or minus m*pi/2. For odd m it generates all quarter-turns and contains rho; for m=2 or6 it is rho itself. Hence every corner lift is fixed by rho. The excluded straight angle m=4 would have parallel incident mirrors and does not satisfy this argument, which is why redundant straight vertices must not be included as corners.

This proof uses the actual local stabilizers, not a supposition that every corner is a cone singularity or has a unique lift. Even-angle corners in the N=4 unfolding have two lifts, both fixed by rho.

## 2. Optical corollary

For every x outside Sigma, the distinct points x and rho(x) are surface-dark when Sigma is forbidden. If a legal finite segment joined them, applying rho to its time reversal would give the same initial point and velocity. Uniqueness forces a fixed midpoint, contradicting section1. This is the same independently accepted reversal proof as in the peer's L control, now with its fixed-set premise proved for every room.

Every such surface pair is within one physical Gamma orbit and projects to S=T. It is irrelevant to the required distinct physical pair except as a universal correspondence that must be kept separate. Nonemptiness of the surface dark locus is automatic in this entire model, so it cannot by itself identify candidate dark rooms or distinguish the 22-wall construction from illuminated rooms.

## 3. Quotient genus

The order-two orientation-preserving affine map rho is holomorphic for the translation-surface complex structure and its fixed points are simple ramification points of the double quotient map. Riemann-Hurwitz therefore gives, with G the genus of M/<rho>,

    2g-2 = 2(2G-2)+|Sigma|,
    G = (2g+2-|Sigma|)/4.

Use the already independently accepted genus/count formulas. For N=2, g=(W-2)/2 and |Sigma|=W, giving G=0. For N=4, let e be the number of even m and r=W-e the number of odd-angle corners. The formulas 2g-2=3W-e-8 and |Sigma|=W+e give

    G=(W-e-2)/2=(r-2)/2.

The parity/turning identity makes r even, and minimal N=4 makes r>=2, so this is a nonnegative integer. Consequently the canonical rho quotient is a sphere for every orthogonal room and for every genuine N=4 room with exactly two odd-angle corners. In the latter case rho is the hyperelliptic involution and g=W-2. No classification of other periodic points is asserted.

For N=4 with r>=4, rho's quotient has positive genus. This is an exact restriction on the physical half-turn quotient; it does not rule out a different hyperelliptic involution or other translation coverings.

## Limits and review targets

The full OFF-orbit optical condition still requires two disjoint free physical Gamma orbits A,B with all of A x B surface-dark. The universal rho graph supplies no such pair. No claim is made that the half-turn graph exhausts surface nonillumination, that higher genus forces darkness, or that genus bounds primitive degree.
