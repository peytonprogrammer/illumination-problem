# Fable 03: covering and cylinder completeness findings

Reviewed the full observed draft fable/general-geometry-03-reduction.md. This continues F1–F6 in fable-findings.md and preserves Fable's source ownership. These are mathematical corrections to a general route, not permission for a direction census or another small-case programme.

## F7 — REJECT the supplied proof of necessity in Lemma C

The argument says a legal segment between FIXED actual S,T has an open set of legal directions and therefore a rational one. That is false for a fixed target: within one development the direction to a specified developed T is fixed. Perturbing it generally misses T. Open corner-free prefixes do not give open fixed-target solutions.

For the actual integer room/native-mark goal, rationality of any finite legal trajectory instead follows directly from unfolding: all wall reflections have integer translation parts and sign changes, so the developed endpoint displacement between the two native marks is an integer vector. A nonzero such displacement has rational projective direction. This repairs only the rationality assertion.

The second inference is also unsupported: a finite legal S-to-T segment need NOT extend to a complete closed leaf avoiding all bad points. Its extension can meet a marked convex corner or a cone AFTER T or BEFORE S. Two regular interior points on a straight diagonal toward a convex corner give the elementary finite-arc phenomenon. If a maximal geodesic ends at a cone outside the selected arc, that regular arc lies on a saddle-connection direction boundary, not an open periodic cylinder. The endpoints are interior, so the room legality convention does not prohibit either situation outside the finite path.

This demonstrates the defect in the claimed proof; it is not a claim that no other direction might give a regular closed leaf for the same pair. Until such a new existential replacement is proved, "same open cylinder/closed leaf with no marked point" is a SUFFICIENT condition, not an established exact restatement of illumination. A counterexample to this stronger cylinder condition would not automatically be a darkpair.

## F8 — CORRECT the cylinder quantifiers

Lemma G requires the WHOLE cylinder to contain no marked point, whereas Lemma C only asks that the chosen leaf avoid marked points. A marked regular point on another leaf does not make the S-to-T arc or even its whole leaf illegal. These are different predicates.

The standard genus/zero bound of six concerns maximal geometric cylinders cut by singular saddle connections in a periodic direction, with the seven convex corners treated as regular points. Removing their forbidden closed leaves can split those cylinders into more legal components; it can also remove the leaf through S entirely. Consequently the number six is not automatically a finite bound for all legal endpoint states. A large-area cylinder need not contain the specified lift of S, the intended lift of T, or a leaf free of marked points. Area pigeonholing does not provide target matching.

Inside a SPECIFIED actual cylinder, a well-defined transverse coordinate can identify a leaf. A single transverse congruence in global development coordinates does not itself certify that S and T are in that same cylinder or on the intended physical sheet. Those component and endpoint-fiber clauses must remain explicit.

## F9 — CORRECT L-room base and the degree-two possibility

Every L-room with arbitrary real metrics and actual endpoints is already covered by the accepted W<=8 base. The statement that no such L theorem exists is factually incorrect within this programme. L-cover lifting is still incomplete because target-sheet and bad-set transfer are not automatic; absence of a base theorem is not the blocker.

For a genuine degree-d translation cover from a four-copy r3 unfolding to an L-room unfolding, the necessary angle/fiber count in the earlier findings leaves only d=3, unramified, onto a genus-2 surface with one 6pi cone. The suggested degree-two case would require the third domain 6pi cone to lie over a regular base point with local degree three, impossible in a degree-two cover. A proposed room fold must first be proved to lift to the asserted translation cover; a room-angle count alone does not bypass this constraint.

The genus-4 to genus-2 degree-3 possibility is NOT ruled out by "cone points cannot merge": all three unramified domain cones may lie over the one base cone. Whether any such cover is room-compatible and has the required actual endpoint behavior remains Fable's structural task.

## F10 — Keep the scope of the covering classification conditional

The statements "every room-compatible cover descends to the specified full-copy tiling" and the resulting finite metric-pattern classification require a complete equivariant covering/fold argument, with the corrected affine Gamma action and branching behavior. A parameter count does not by itself exclude every compatible cover unless that classification has first been proved.

Even a correct classification of ROOM-compatible covers would not classify all translation covers permitted in Lemma F. The standard torus projection exists for every integer unfolding. Nonregular covers need not arise from global symmetry quotients. Bad-set and actual target-fiber requirements may make those maps unusable for a proposed reduction, but that must be proved from the maps and actual marks, not inferred from lack of a named simpler room.

The unit rectangle's four marked corners are not the same marked category as an unmarked torus or a torus with only the common image of original corners marked. In the four-mark model the self-dark midpoint argument can exclude legal BASE loops, while original legal rays may project through transparent preimages of those extra base marks. This is exactly a failure of the inverse bad-fiber inclusion; it is not an absence of original paths. Different torus mark sets need their own safe projection/lifting clauses.
