# F10 qualification: when the full-copy fold statement does follow

This is an independent hand completion of the specific tiling implication in Fable file 03 §1, under genuine finite room-fold hypotheses. It does not classify every translation covering, select actual T, or prove illumination. It supplements the open F10 qualification in `three-reflex-nonemptiness-fable-v2-review.md` without changing that pinned review.

## Precise fold hypotheses

Let compact strictly simple polygonal disks D,D0 be related by a continuous finite-degree surjection f. Require:

- f maps the actual boundary of D into the actual boundary of D0.
- On f^-1(Int D0), f is a Euclidean local isometry.
- At an interior seam over a smooth wall of D0, the two local pieces of f agree on that seam and differ by reflection in the target wall.
- At vertices, f has the stipulated finite mirror-fold angle model. For applications to the accepted cone/fiber count, require the asserted degree-d translation covering of four-copy unfoldings, or prove that implication from these fold data separately.

These are genuine room-fold assumptions. They are not properties of an arbitrary translation cover without reflection compatibility. In particular an unproved converse from an arbitrary cover is not supplied here.

## Every interior sheet is a full isometric copy

Restrict f to f^-1(Int D0). It is proper over Int D0: the inverse image of a compact subset K of Int D0 is compact in D, and is disjoint from the actual boundary by the first hypothesis. A proper local homeomorphism with finite fibers is a covering map. Since Int D0 is simply connected, each connected component maps homeomorphically onto the ENTIRE Int D0.

This homeomorphism is a Euclidean local isometry. On a connected open planar component its orthogonal derivative is constant: overlapping local rigid motions that agree on an open set are identical. Consequently the inverse on this component is a single global rigid motion e_i. The component is exactly e_i(Int D0), not a truncated copy. There are d such components, with disjoint interiors. Taking closures covers D, since the open-sheet complement consists only of the finitely modelled wall/vertex preimages. Thus D is tiled by d full rigid copies of D0.

The adjacency graph through positive-length shared seams is connected. Any path through Int D connecting two open-sheet points can be perturbed to avoid the finitely many seam vertices, so it crosses a sequence of smooth seams. At an adjacency, e_i^-1 and e_j^-1 agree on the shared positive-length segment because f is continuous. Their relative rigid motion fixes that line pointwise. It cannot be the identity, since tile interiors are disjoint; it is reflection in that wall. This fixes the neighboring tile's placement with NO free translation along the seam.

For fixed d and a fixed finite-wall base shape, there are finitely many adjacency constructions: place one tile, choose its wall for a neighbor, and continue along a spanning tree. Every next placement is fixed by a chosen wall reflection. Invalid overlaps and nonsimple unions may be discarded. This proves a finite union of metric patterns without a new room census.

## What this justifies for the r3-to-L statement

Conditional on the genuine degree-d unfolded cover and its local angle model, the accepted F5/F9 calculation excludes degree two onto a genus-two one-cone base. A nontrivial nonrectangular room fold from r=3 therefore has d=3 onto an L-room. The full-copy argument then gives three mirror L tiles. An L shape has four positive metric parameters up to rigid motion; the finitely many three-tile placements add no continuous parameters. Thus the claimed finite union of at-most-four-parameter fold families is justified UNDER THESE FOLD HYPOTHESES.

Most importantly, even a valid L-fold still lifts a base ray to SOME endpoint fiber member. It does not establish original S-to-T transfer. No universal optical gap is closed by the tiling count.
