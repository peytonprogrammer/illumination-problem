# Review: the ten-wall residue ladder and the uniform selection lemma

Date: 2026-11-07. Author: Claude (Fable 5.1), own folder. Hand theory only. No scans, no checker runs, no edits to Codex files.

Scope: the accepted equal-arm packet LJ03 (`illumination-generalization-2026-11-06/construction/three-reflex-lower-equal-arm-proof.md`) and the coprime thin-cap packet (`codex-proof-verification/specialist-ideas/terminal-resumption-2026-11-07/coprime-proof.md`), both for the ordered ten-wall spiral of turn type (1,1,8) after the TR01/TR04/TR05 reduction.

## 1. Verdict

1. Both packets are internally sound as stated. Their arithmetic hypotheses (N odd, L a power of 2, c odd, h even, gcd(G,N)=1, H=h+1) are proof artifacts, not boundaries of the truth. Section 5 gives a hand instance with gcd(G,N)=2 and N even where the same itinerary works.
2. Both packets prove one itinerary: up the source arm, through the cap with one roof contact, down the right arm, down the bottom chamber, no floor contact. Section 2 writes that itinerary as a lattice-coset problem in R^3. Everything the packets do is a special case of hitting an open box with a coset of a rank-3 lattice.
3. The lemma the route needs, in uniform form, is Lemma U in section 4. It holds for generic real metrics by density (Kronecker), so the real-metric claim of TR03 is the expected shape. For commensurable metrics (every canonical45 instance) it is a finite combinatorial claim per instance, and nothing in the packets proves it uniformly.
4. The LJ03 box argument works exactly when a = R (equal arms). Section 3 proves this. For unequal arms the two-index fundamental domain does not fit the box in one coordinate, so the packet's main lemma cannot be extended by changing the rounding convention. A third index is required.
5. A failure of Lemma U for an instance is not a dark pair. Other itineraries (floor contact, ledge contact, source-arm re-entry) remain. So the residue ladder can at best prove the W10 conjecture for the families where Lemma U is verified; it cannot refute it.

## 2. The itinerary as a lattice problem

Normal form. Vertices (a,b),(a,h),(w,h),(w,c),(l,c),(l,0),(A,0),(A,H),(0,H),(0,b) with 0<a<l<w<A and 0<b<c<h<H. Widths: source arm a, cap A, right arm R=A−w, bottom chamber B=A−l. Residual pair: S=(s,u) with 0<s<a, b<u<h and T=(t,v) with l<t<A, 0<v<c. Put z=A−t, so 0<z<B.

Developed height. Fold the vertical coordinate once at y=H. The ray runs from developed height u to 2H−v. Junction times from the start: t1=h−u, t2=2H−h−u, t3=2H−c−u, D=2H−u−v. Equivalent packet names: d0=t1, G=2(H−h)=t2−t1, g1=h−c=t3−t2, d2=c−v=D−t3.

Charts. Let F_L(x)=dist(x,2LZ). The ray's abscissa in each chamber is a fold of an affine function of the developed height. The phase that enters the next chamber is the previous phase minus an even multiple of the previous width (plus the odd multiple (2m+1)A at the cap, which also carries the velocity sign). Write the index choices as i (source), m (cap), k (right), J (bottom). With slope tau the residuals are

    z0 = s + t1 tau − 2a i,
    z1 = s + t2 tau − 2a i − (2m+1)A,
    z2 = s + t3 tau − 2a i − (2m+1)A − 2R k,
    E  = s + D tau  − 2a i − (2m+1)A − 2R k − 2B J.

Conditions for a legal path on this itinerary:

- (C1) |z0| < a. Excludes corner (a,h). z0=0 is a smooth contact with x=0.
- (C2) |z1| < R. The cap exit lies in the right-arm mouth (w,A]. z1 = ±R would be corner (w,h) or a ledge contact.
- (C3) |z2| < R. Excludes corner (w,c).
- (C4) E = ±z. Then the final abscissa is A − F_B(E) = t.

C4 fixes tau as an affine function of the indices:

    tau = (±z − s + 2a i + (2m+1)A + 2R k + 2B J)/D.

Substituting back, the residual vector r=(z0,z1,z2) is r0(±) + i v_i + m v_m + k v_k + J v_J with

    v_i = 2a (t1/D − 1, t2/D − 1, t3/D − 1),
    v_m = 2A (t1/D,     t2/D − 1, t3/D − 1),
    v_k = 2R (t1/D,     t2/D,     t3/D − 1),
    v_J = 2B (t1/D,     t2/D,     t3/D),
    r0(±) = (s + t1 tau0, s + t2 tau0 − A, s + t3 tau0 − A),  tau0 = (±z − s + A)/D.

Let Lambda be the Z-span of the four vectors and Box = (−a,a)×(−R,R)×(−R,R). The itinerary succeeds for (S,T) if and only if some point of r0(+)+Lambda or r0(−)+Lambda lies in Box with the corresponding tau>0. Positivity is free: adding v_J enough times increases tau without leaving a coset.

Check against LJ03. With a=R=Pe, A=Ne, B=Le and LJ03's alpha=t1/2, beta=G/2, f=(g1+d2)/2, d=D/2, the first two coordinates of v_i are (2eP/d)(−(beta+f), −f) and of v_k are (2eP/d)(alpha, alpha+beta). These are LJ03's vi and vj. So LJ03 section 2 is the statement that the two-index sublattice span{v_i, v_k} has a half-open fundamental domain inside (−a,a)×(−R,R).

Check against the coprime packet. With a=R, A=Na, B=2a the packet uses the index combination q and the rotation tau → tau+2aM. In the lattice picture that is the sublattice generated by v_m − N v_i-type combinations; the packet's gcd(G,N)=1 makes one specific one-parameter subgroup hit the cap window.

Determinant. Row reduction gives det(v_i, v_m, v_k) = −8aAR·d2/D. So the three-index sublattice has covolume 8aAR·d2/D, and vol(Box)=8aR^2. The fourth generator v_J refines this by a finite index that depends on B.

## 3. The LJ03 box argument needs a = R

LJ03 proves |z0|<a and |z1|<R from the two-index sublattice span{v_i, v_k} by the half-open rounding in section 2 of the packet. The proof uses that, in each coordinate, the two basis vectors have opposite signs and absolute values summing to exactly the window width. Repeat the computation for unequal arms.

First coordinate: |v_i,1| + v_k,1 = (2a(D − t1) + 2R t1)/D. This is at most 2a if and only if R t1 ≤ a t1, that is R ≤ a.

Second coordinate: |v_i,2| + v_k,2 = (2a(D − t2) + 2R t2)/D. This is at most 2R if and only if a(D − t2) ≤ R(D − t2), that is a ≤ R.

So the two-index fundamental domain fits inside the box in both coordinates exactly when a=R. For a≠R one coordinate overflows by 2|a−R|·(fraction), and no rounding convention repairs it. Extending LJ03 to unequal arms needs the cap index m as a third free variable, which changes the covering question from a parallelogram in R^2 to a parallelepiped in R^3 with the right-corner condition folded in. Nothing in the packet addresses that.

## 4. The uniform selection lemma

Lemma U (needed). For every normal-form metric (a,A,R,B,b,c,h,H), every u in (b,h) and v in (0,c), and every s in (0,a) and z in (0,B), at least one of the cosets r0(±)+Lambda meets Box.

Equivalently: the two affine patches {r0(±)(s,z)} over (s,z) in (0,a)×(0,B) lie inside Lambda + Box.

What is known about Lemma U.

- Generic real metrics. If the ratios among a, A, R, B are not all rational, the Z-span of four vectors in R^3 is dense in a subspace of dimension at least 1, and for generic metrics it is dense in R^3. Then Lemma U holds by density. This is the honest reason to expect a real-metric theorem for the ten-wall spiral. It also says the hard case is the commensurable one, which is exactly canonical45.
- Commensurable metrics. Lambda is a rank-3 lattice. A necessary condition for Lambda + Box = R^3 is covol(Lambda) ≤ vol(Box). From section 2, covol(Lambda) ≤ 8aAR·d2/D, so A·d2 ≤ R·D is sufficient for the necessary condition to be satisfiable, but the patch condition is weaker than full covering and covolume does not decide it.
- Tall cap. As H grows with everything else fixed, t1/D, t2/D, t3/D all tend to limits with t2/D → 1 and D → ∞, so v_i, v_m, v_k shrink toward the coordinate axes and the lattice becomes fine relative to the box. This is TR03's threshold H ≥ c + 2A/delta in lattice form. TR03's "spacing 2A/(2H−u−v)" is |v_m| to first order.
- Equal-arm thin cap, cap window only. Take a=R, A=Na, B=2a with integer N≥2 and the packet's slope grid tau_q = (A+z−s+2aq)/D. Define the block index beta(q) = floor((X(q)+a)/(2a)) where X(q) is the cap phase. Condition C2 is beta(q) ≡ m* (mod N) for one residue m*. The increments of beta(q) are in {−1,0,1} because the cap phase gains t2/D·2a per step while the source fold gains 0 or 2a. Also beta(q+D) = beta(q)+G exactly. So over one period beta visits every integer between beta(0) and beta(0)+G, and over all q it visits every integer in [beta(0),beta(0)+G] + G·Z. That set meets every residue class mod N, since gcd(G,N) ≤ G. Conclusion: C2 alone is always satisfiable on the slope grid, with no hypothesis on gcd(G,N). The packet's gcd(G,N)=1 is used only to make a single one-parameter rotation suffice.
- What remains open in that family. Write Qbar = {q mod D : beta(q) ∈ m* + gcd(G,N)·Z}. The packet's three forbidden residue classes F1, F2, F3 live in Z/D with moduli m_i ≥ 2 dividing D. Lemma U for this family is the statement Qbar is not contained in F1 ∪ F2 ∪ F3. When gcd(G,N)=1, Qbar is all of Z/D and the packet's covering fact (three classes with moduli ≥2 cannot cover Z unless the moduli are (2,2,·), (3,3,3), or (2,4,4)) finishes it. When gcd(G,N)>1, Qbar is a proper subset, of size at least G/gcd(G,N), and three residue classes can in principle cover it. No argument in either packet addresses this case. Note that N even forces gcd(G,N) ≥ 2 because G is even.

So the exact missing statement for the equal-arm thin-cap family is:

Lemma U' (equal arms, a=R, A=Na, B=2a). For all integers D>G≥2, N≥2, t1≥0, g1≥1, d2≥1 with t1+G+g1+d2=D, and all residues, Qbar ⊄ F1 ∪ F2 ∪ F3.

This is a finite statement per (D,N,G,t1,g1,d2) and the forbidden residues. It does not follow from density. A counterexample to U' would show the monotone itinerary fails for some rational pair in that family, which would force the W10 programme to add a second itinerary. Deciding U' for small D is an exact finite enumeration; it needs Daniel's approval under the no-new-scan rule.

## 5. Hand instance outside the packet hypotheses

Metrics: a=R=2, A=8 (N=4), w=6, l=4 (B=4), b=1, c=2, h=4, H=5. So G=2 and gcd(G,N)=2, N even, c even. S=(1,2), T=(7,1). Then t1=2, t2=4, t3=6, D=7, z=1.

Slope grid tau_q = (8+1−1+4q)/7 = (8+4q)/7. With s=1 all three forbidden classes are empty (each congruence reduces to an even number being odd mod 28). Cap phases: q=0 gives X=11/7, q=1 gives X=27/7, q=2 gives X=43/7, which lies in the window (6,10). Take q=2, tau=16/7.

Residuals: z0 = 11/7, z1 = 43/7 − 8 = −13/7, z2 = −13/7 + 32/7 − 4 = −9/7, E = −9/7 + 16/7 = 1 = z. All strict.

Path (abscissa, height): start (1,2); hits x=2 at height 2+7/16; hits x=0 at height 53/16; reaches height 4 at x=11/7 moving right; roof contact at (27/7,5); returns to height 4 at x=43/7 in (6,8); hits x=8, then x=6, reaches height 2 at x=47/7; hits x=8; ends at (7,1). Six reflections, all on genuine walls: x=2 and x=0 on the source arm, the roof, x=8 and x=6 on the right arm, x=8 in the bottom chamber. No corner within distance 1/7 of any contact. Hand arithmetic only; not checker-verified.

This instance satisfies none of gcd(G,N)=1, N odd, c odd, or L a power of 2 (B/a=2 is a power of 2, but N=4 is even). It shows the packet hypotheses are where the proofs live, not where the mechanism stops.

## 6. What this changes

- The accepted equal-arm and thin-cap families stay accepted. Nothing here contradicts them.
- The route "add residue hypotheses one packet at a time" cannot reach arbitrary integer metrics because each packet proves Lemma U for a sublattice with a special shape. The unequal-arm case needs a genuinely three-index covering argument (section 3), and that argument does not reduce to the LJ03 rounding trick.
- The right next theorem for the W10 programme is Lemma U' for the equal-arm thin-cap family without the coprimality hypothesis, then its unequal-arm version with the third index. If U' fails for a small instance, the programme needs a second itinerary (one floor contact) and the same lattice analysis applies to it with D replaced by 2H+v−u and one more window.
- For the canonical45 conjecture the generic-metric density argument is unavailable, so the ten-wall spiral is an arithmetic problem in every instance. That matches the project's result 12 (darkness is a finite condition on the marked origami) and is the reason fold necessity (note A) is the structural statement to pursue.
