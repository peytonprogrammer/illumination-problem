## Read-only review of the proposed exact criterion

Input: `../claude-illumination-check/exact-dark/claude_origami_dark.py`.

For a correctly unfolded canonical room, all marked lifts and forbidden corner lifts are square vertices. Every source-to-target displacement is integral and hence has primitive integral direction. An integral determinant-one change of coordinates takes that oriented primitive direction to horizontal. The transformed square-tiled surface has finitely many squares, so its labelled origami orbit is finite. Exhausting the orbit and checking all horizontal source separatrices is consequently a complete mathematical decision scheme.

A crucial label issue can be checked algebraically. Write r and u for right and upper permutations and compose right to left. Lower-left vertex classes are generated by

    u r(i) ~ r u(i).

Under u_new = u r^-1 the new relation is

    u(i) ~ r u r^-1(i),

which is precisely the old relation with index r^-1(i). Under r_new = r u^-1 the new relation is

    u r u^-1(i) ~ r(i),

which is the old relation with index u^-1(i). Thus both elementary shear moves preserve the full partition of lower-left corners. Inverse moves do too. Carrying vertex labels unchanged through these moves is consistent. A connected origami is recoverable up to relabelling by the rooted r/u traversal used in canon; relabelling must move labels with squares, as the code does.

Horizontal motion along a square bottom edge visits successive lower-left corners under r. At a clear regular vertex it continues uniquely. A forbidden corner stops it, including a regular point fixed by the billiard half-turn. A target reached first gives illumination. If the finite r cycle returns to its source without a target, further laps cannot help. All source-labelled sectors are checked.

This supports the abstract criterion. It is not a complete independent implementation verification: the triangle-to-square chart/gluing builder and its correspondence with the physical room still need separately checked controls, including a known dark example and a transparent multi-event example. The reported agreement on 2,435 lit rooms establishes only positive-case evidence.

The implementation defaults to a one-million-state cap. Its `cap` result is inconclusive. A `dark` result is justified only after the entire reachable labelled orbit closes, not after a resource limit. Finite mathematical termination provides no useful practical bound: even the crude state bound (n!)² times 4^n grows extremely fast. This must not be presented as a cheap darkness oracle or run as a substitute direction search while Daniel’s pause remains in effect.

## Concrete construction question

Can four or more transparent B/C vertices save walls while leaving every source-to-target separatrix intercepted by an actual corner?

For J interior and s_N smooth-boundary B/C exceptions, the unfolded surface needs

    R >= 16 + 8J + 4s_N,

regular vertices outside the half-turn fixed set. The established <=3-exception exclusion implies R >= 32 for any sub-24 room in this family. The contact22 and simple24 controls have R = 16 and 20. A candidate in this unit presentation needs additional regular grid vertices. Dilation can create those vertices without improving walls; the separate fixed-period refolding proof is what excludes wall savings from the known controls. Euler gives R = 4(N-W+2), hence N >= W+6. These are necessary filters, not darkness certificates or bounds on total room size.

The next proof target is a sufficient invariant of the labelled permutations that prevents a source-labelled horizontal cycle from meeting a target before a blocker under every shear. Such an invariant, together with a simple planar quotient and a wall saving, would meet the theory-first gate. Without it, four exceptions are only an unexplored gap; they are not yet a promising construction mechanism.

A proof of universal illumination in that branch would also be useful, but it requires a structural bound or theorem. Enumerating a finite window cannot establish the arbitrary-size statement.
