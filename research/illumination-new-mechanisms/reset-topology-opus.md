**Verdict.** I don't have a positive surgery below 24 walls. I give two exact deductions that close the slit-thickening route, then one transfer lemma that covers what remains of your "different fundamental domain" idea.

## Lemma A: slits on a mirror axis are invisible (deduction)

Let R be a room with a reflection symmetry σ in line ℓ. Let H be one closed half, R ∩ {one side of ℓ}, with every segment of R ∩ ℓ treated as a wall. Write π: R → H for the fold. Assume S, T lie off ℓ.

Then the following are equivalent:

- No vertex-avoiding path in R joins S to T, and none joins S to σT.
- No vertex-avoiding path in H joins πS to πT.

The equivalence holds for every choice of which parts of R ∩ ℓ are slits and which are openings.

**Proof.** The fold π conjugates the billiard flow of R to the flow of H. At a point of ℓ, a ray either reflects off a slit or crosses an opening. The two outgoing rays are σ-images of each other, so they fold to the same ray in H. Each crossing toggles the endpoint between T and σT. Repeated channel visits are handled by parity. Slit endpoints and the points where ℓ meets the boundary are vertices in both rooms.

A ray along ℓ is σ-invariant and stays in ℓ. It hits walls perpendicular to ℓ or a vertex, so it never reaches T ∉ ℓ. A ray tangent to a slit is therefore harmless.

**Consequence (deduction).** The exact symmetric slit surgery is the same problem as darkness in a simple room H. That room has about (off-axis walls)/2 plus the axis segments as walls.

- If the known 20-wall slit room were σ-symmetric with both T and σT dark, it would yield a simple dark room with roughly 10–13 walls.
- Without symmetry, the fold gives no exact conjugacy.

The symmetric route therefore adds nothing beyond direct simple-room design.

## Lemma B: tile-compatible thickening stays in the exhausted family (deduction)

Suppose you thicken a slit into a channel whose new walls are unions of tile edges of the 45° tiling. The room stays a congruent 45° assembly, and the darkness argument has the same form.

Any thickening that keeps exactness through the tiling therefore falls in the class you have already excluded below 24 walls.

Any other thickening adds a tip face of width w > 0. A ray that hits the tip retroreflects with an offset. Its old counterpart passes on one side of the old slit endpoint p, and the old room places no constraint on it. No conjugacy maps this to an old path, because the old flow is continuous through a neighbourhood of p minus the slit. This is the sharp form of "a narrow slit approximation is invalid."

## Lemma C: re-quotient transfer (deduction)

Let X be the unfolding of a dark room P under its dihedral group G. Darkness of P means:

- No straight segment in X runs from s to G·t.
- That segment avoids Λ_P, the set of lifts of P-vertices. Λ_P includes regular points of X, such as lifts of π/2 corners.

Let G' be another finite group of isometries of X generated by orientation-reversing involutions, with X/G' a polygon P'. Darkness transfers to (π's, π't) in P' if all three conditions hold:

1. G'·s ⊆ G·s, which makes the source well defined.
2. G'·t ⊆ G·t.
3. Λ_P ⊆ Λ_{P'}.

Condition 3 is the step people tend to drop. A P'-path that avoids P'-vertices may pass through a regular lift of a P-vertex. Darkness of P does not rule out that path.

Lemma C covers every seam case at once. Paths tangent to the seam, repeated seam visits, and alternate reflected lifts are all straight segments in X between orbit points.

## Rigidity facts that answer your questions (deductions)

- **Conjugate actions.** If G' = φGφ⁻¹ for an isometry φ of X, then P' is congruent to P. You get the same wall count.
- **Affine conjugation.** An affine map with derivative A sends segments to segments, so darkness in X is affine-invariant. The conjugated generators are specular reflections only when A normalizes the set of mirror directions. For a dihedral group of order 6 or more, this forces A to be a similarity. Only D₂ (rectilinear) rooms admit genuine stretches, and stretches keep the wall count.
- **Antiholomorphic actions.** These must be flat isometries for X/G' to be a billiard table. So G' ⊆ Isom(X), which is finite for genus ≥ 2.
- **Collapse case.** If Isom(X) = G, then no other quotient exists. The extra isometries come from symmetries of P. For a σ-symmetric P, the subgroup ⟨G, σ⟩ reproduces Lemma A exactly. Lemma A is the basic instance of Lemma C.

## The route that remains (conjecture-level)

A genuinely new P' needs a slit or contact room P whose unfolding X has an isometry outside N(G). Here N(G) is G together with the lifts of the symmetries of P. Hidden symmetries of this kind occur for translation surfaces with large Veech groups, such as covers of the square torus. They are rare for asymmetric P.

The test is a single finite computation, not a search. Compute Isom(X) for the 20-wall slit room and the 22-wall contact room. Then list the reflection-generated subgroups G' that satisfy conditions 1–3.

Unit-area normalization constrains the result. P' has area area(X)/|G'|. Its corners are the corner-reflector points and the cone points on Fix(G'). So |G'| > |G| would shrink P', but each extra fixed curve adds walls.

**Corner budget (conjecture).** A gain below 24 needs Fix(G') to merge collinear segments across copies of P. That merge is the one mechanism that saves walls in this framework. Which situation you are in comes down to two outcomes:

- If Isom(X) = G for both control rooms, this whole topology-changing route is closed.
- If either room has an isometry outside G, that isometry is the only exact surgery candidate. Condition 3 is the first check to run on it.
