For the three reconstructed controls, consider a finite affine reflection action H on the SAME marked translation surface, with at least one free source orbit contained in the old eight source lifts and one free target orbit contained in the old eight target lifts. Require the quotient, in an H-invariant flat metric, to develop injectively to a simple planar polygon with ordinary mirror boundary and no interior cone singularities.

## 1. Correct group-order gate

A selected interior source in the new quotient has a free H orbit. Since that orbit must fit inside the old8-point source set, |H|<=8. **Divisibility by8 is not required unless every old source point remains interior/free.** This corrects the earlier overstrong divisibility claim in reset-findings.md.

The derivative kernel is trivial on these surfaces. The existing square-grid translation enumeration covers it: every genuine cone lies over the torus lattice origin, so a derivative-I automorphism taking cones to cones has integral translation offset and preserves the square grid. Finite affine actions therefore have faithful derivatives. After averaging a positive definite metric, an action generated by reflections is a finite plane dihedral group. With order<=8 the possibilities are2,4,6,8, including order6.

## 2. Local cone gate

Let p have cone angle2pi*m and suppose its stabilizer at a quotient boundary corner is dihedral of order2q. The corner angle is pi*m/q. Its local orientation-preserving generator rotates the cone through2pi*m/q. Derivative faithfulness requires gcd(m,q)=1; otherwise a nontrivial stabilizer power has derivative I. A genuine simple corner also requires0<m/q<2 and m/q!=1. Finally2q divides|H|.

An interior cone cannot disappear into a flat regular interior point under a faithful action: cancellation would require a cyclic stabilizer of order m whose generator has derivative I. Thus every genuine cone must descend to a boundary corner.

Every control has an m=3 cone. Orders2 and6 cannot accommodate it. Order4 cannot accommodate the m=5 or m=7 cones that occur in every control. Consequently only order8 remains. The20-wall slit surface has m=2 cones, which cannot satisfy the corner gate for q=1,2,4. This rules out that control entirely in the specified route.

This is an exact finite local-angle check; no optical trajectories enter it.

## 3. Every surviving action shares the SAME half-turn

For a dihedral action H of order8, let r generate its orientation-preserving order4 subgroup. Then D(r)^2=-I. Because the derivative kernel is trivial, r^2 equals the existing unique derivative-minus-I automorphism rho. This conclusion holds even for an affine action that is not an isometry of the original metric.

For a valid order8 simple-room quotient, the preimage of all genuine boundary corners is EXACTLY Fix(rho):

- At a simple polygon corner the reduced angle is m*pi/q with q in{2,4}; q=1 would give either a smooth pi angle or an invalid angle>=2pi. Its local stabilizer therefore contains rho.
- Conversely, a point fixed by rho cannot become a regular interior point under a faithful derivative action. Nor can it become a smooth boundary point: the local stabilizer contains a rotation and its reduced-angle denominator is at least2.

For the22/24 controls all cone indices are odd, so the local conditions apply without even-cone exceptions. In particular, the old regular absorbing corners cannot simply be discarded to save walls: their rho-fixed status forces them to remain genuine corners in any admissible new quotient.

## 4. Intrinsic wall-count formula

Write C=Fix(rho). For a corner orbit O with stabilizer size2q, its quotient angle is theta=2pi*m/(2q) and |O|=8/(2q). Hence

    theta = (1/8) sum_{p in O} 2pi*m(p).

Summing over all corner orbits and using the simple polygon angle sum gives

    (W-2)*pi = (2pi/8) sum_{p in C} m(p),
    W = 2 + (1/4) sum_{p in C} m(p).

On the24-wall control the fixed cone mass is9+23*3+2*5=88, forcing W=24. On the22-wall contact control it is10+21*3+7=80, forcing W=22. This rules out wall-count compression of the24-wall control without classifying all affine actions. It leaves topology repair of the22-wall control as a sharper target.

Equivalently, because all genuine cones are rho-fixed on these two controls, W=(F+2g+6)/4. Both independently computed genera and fixed-point counts reproduce the same values.

## 5. Explicit remaining algebraic target on the22-wall surface

Seek actual affine maps r,sigma such that:

    r^2=rho, sigma^2=1, sigma*r*sigma=r^-1.

Their generated order8 group must act transitively on the old eight source lifts and separately on the eight target lifts. This makes the two physical marks distinct interior points and transfers all64 old blocked lift pairs.

The orientation-preserving quotient X/<r> must be the topological double of a disk, hence a sphere. Riemann-Hurwitz gives

    2g-2 = -8 + F + 2*|Fix(r)|.

For g=25,F=32 this requires |Fix(r)|=12. Thus an arbitrary new quarter-turn is insufficient: it must have12 fixed points. On the finite set Fix(rho), the mirror must satisfy

    sigma(p) in {p,r(p)} for every p in Fix(rho).

This prevents rotation-only stabilizers from creating interior orbifold cone points; in particular sigma must fix each r-fixed point. Together with the finite group relations these are algebraic and finite marked-point conditions, independent of rays. They still do not ensure an injective planar development.

The action must not be affine-conjugate to the old action by a map preserving source, target and blocker markings. Such a conjugacy, after the invariant metric is chosen, produces the same developed contact geometry up to similarity. A new action would need a different boundary arrangement and an injective development. No such action has been found or enumerated here.

# A lattice obstruction to refolding the contact surface

## Conditional proposition

Under the SAME marked-surface, free source/target orbit, faithful affine reflection action, invariant metric, and simple injective planar development hypotheses in `refolding-bound.md`, the reconstructed 22-wall contact surface has no admissible simple quotient.

The final contradiction uses our previously established computer-assisted strict canonical-family minimum of 24. Thus this is conditional on that model and its verified finite exclusions, as well as the earlier local quotient lemmas. It is not a general 24-wall lower bound.

## Exact facts about the 22-wall surface

4. Every genuine cone and every old absorbing regular vertex belongs to C = Fix(rho), as established in `refolding-bound.md`. There are no additional fixed square centers or edge midpoints.

The argument is specific to the 22-wall surface.

## Reduction to the strict canonical family

Assume an admissible quotient exists.

**A. Normalize the full reflection group.** The previous cone gate forces an order-eight dihedral action H. Every affine automorphism preserves the absolute holonomy lattice 2Z², so its derivative is in GL(2,Z). Every order-eight dihedral subgroup of GL(2,Z) is integrally conjugate to the square symmetry group K = <J,F>, where J(x,y)=(-y,x), F(x,y)=(x,-y). See Jones–Pinto, *Hypermap operations of finite order*, Section 3, Remark 3. Apply the integral change of coordinates. An invariant positive definite metric for K is a scalar multiple of the Euclidean metric; thus this normalization describes the actual quotient geometry up to similarity.

**B. The torus action has no translation part.** In the normalized coordinates, the source and target sets still project to 0 modulo 2Z². Each h in H preserves each set, because a free eight-point orbit contained in an eight-point set equals that whole set. The induced affine torus map therefore fixes 0. Its translation part lies in 2Z².

**C. All corners lie on the canonical integer grid.** The earlier corner correspondence gives corner preimage = Fix(rho), with rho = r². The induced torus map of rho is x ↦ −x, so its fixed points lie over Z²/2Z². Consequently all developed polygon corners have integer coordinates, choosing a source representative as origin.

**D. Every side lies on a canonical grid edge line.** Its stabilizing reflection has linear part one of K's four reflections and translation in 2Z². The fixed lines are therefore x = integer, y = integer, x−y = even integer, or x+y = even integer. These are exactly the lines supporting edges in our canonical 45–45–90 triangle grid. Integer corner endpoints ensure that the polygon is a finite union of whole canonical triangles. The source and target have even-even coordinates and, by hypothesis, are distinct interior A points.

**E. No B/C exception is possible.** Lift a canonical B or C vertex that is interior or smooth on a side. It lies over one of the three NONZERO points of Z²/2Z². It is regular on X; smooth boundary doubling introduces no cone. It is not fixed by rho, since the corner correspondence forces every rho-fixed point to be a genuine polygon corner. But exact fact 3 says every regular square vertex outside Fix(rho) is a source or target lift, both over 0. Contradiction.

Thus the quotient is a strict canonical simple polygon, with no transparent B/C vertices. The previously verified strict-family result requires at least 24 walls. The fixed-corner mass formula in `refolding-bound.md` forces 22 walls on this surface. Contradiction.

## Why the lattice vertices in step E are the audited vertices

The original square vertices are the full inverse image of Z²/2Z² under the absolute-period torus map. The coordinate change is integral and invertible, so it preserves that four-point torus set and its full inverse image. No arbitrary finer grid is substituted. This is the essential extra content beyond merely citing that torus-cover billiards are Gaussian after some subdivision.
