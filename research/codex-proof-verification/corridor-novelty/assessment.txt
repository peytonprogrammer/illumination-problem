THE EXACT CANDIDATE PROPOSITION
For d,g,h_minus,h_plus>0 define
 P=([0,2d] x [-h_minus,0]) union ([d,3d] x [0,g]) union ([0,2d] x [g,g+h_plus]).
For every S=(s,-a), T=(t,g+b) with 0<s,t<2d, 0<a<h_minus and 0<b<h_plus, there is a finite actual S-to-T billiard trajectory satisfying equal-angle specular reflection on smooth walls and avoiding every polygon corner.
No rationality or generic-position restriction is imposed. No uniform reflection bound is asserted. Only the stated lower/upper chamber pairs are quantified, not all pairs in the polygon.

The widths and offsets are constrained: all three rectangles have width2d, the middle is shifted by d, and both outer chambers have the same horizontal span. This is not a theorem for three arbitrary rectangles or every twelve-sided polygon.
All its angles are rational multiples of pi, even when its lengths are irrational. Thus arbitrary real dimensions do NOT place it outside the established theory of rational polygonal billiards. Here “rational polygon” concerns angles, while the proof's commensurability splits concern lengths.

LITERATURE COMPARISON

1. Lelievre, Monteil and Weiss, Everything is illuminated, Geometry & Topology20(2016),1737–1762; Theorem2.
https://arxiv.org/html/1407.2975
For each point on a translation surface, only finitely many other points fail to be illuminated; further structure describes the nonillumination locus. This is a foundational general result. It does not, from its statement alone, eliminate every possible exception among our prescribed endpoint pairs. Our all-pair assertion for this particular pair of chambers therefore does not follow merely by citing it. A proof applying its structural classification to this family could still give an alternative derivation; this audit has not ruled that out.

2. Amit Wolecki, Illumination in Rational Billiards, arXiv1905.09358v3,30 November2024; Theorem1.2(i).
https://arxiv.org/html/1905.09358v3
A rational polygon with connected interior has only finitely many nonilluminating pairs. This covers our polygons but permits those pairs to exist. It does not identify or exclude them for this corridor. Dense billiard orbits or almost-everywhere illumination likewise cannot establish arrival at each specified target. Wolecki's wider geometric scope and our stronger pairwise conclusion for a narrow family should not be confused with a general strengthening of his theorem.

3. Paul Apisa and Alex Wright, Marked points on translation surfaces, Geometry & Topology25(2021),2913–2961; Theorems1.1,3.6,3.16.
https://arxiv.org/html/1708.03411v3
Their results classify finite blocking through periodic points and covering constructions. In particular the rectangle-tiled case has finite blocking for every pair. Finite blocking does NOT assert that a connecting billiard path exists: an already dark pair is blocked by the empty set. These results are a serious possible implication route, but a family-specific analysis of the relevant coverings and exceptional points is still needed. I have not performed a complete orbit-closure or periodic-point classification of this corridor. It would be incorrect to claim this paper cannot imply our theorem by a further argument.

4. Hubert, Schmoll and Troubetzkoy, Modular Fibers and Illumination Problems, IMRN2008,rnn011.
https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0602394
The accessed abstract studies invariant subspaces and countability/finiteness of nonilluminated points on pre-lattice/Veech surfaces. It does not itself state the corridor proposition. Full-text access was not obtained in this audit, so this is an unresolved source rather than a full nonoverlap determination. The finiteness theme and use of affine symmetries are prior art.

5. Thierry Monteil, On the finite blocking property, Annales de l'Institut Fourier55(2005),1195–1217.
https://www.numdam.org/item/AIF_2005__55_4_1195_0/
The publisher abstract gives classification results for finite blocking, including L-shaped translation surfaces. That is not the same assertion as every outer endpoint pair illuminating in our three-band room. Only the abstract and bibliographic record were inspected; no exhaustive comparison with its full proofs is claimed.

6. Diana Davis, Math424: Geometry, Surfaces and Billiards, Williams College, Fall2016, printed page21, problem6(b) (PDF page25).
https://math.williams.edu/files/2016/05/424-problems-all.pdf
An exercise asks for illumination of the C-shaped domain made from five squares and asks whether every source position works. This is genuine nearby elementary prior art and should be acknowledged when presenting a corridor result. It does not state arbitrary dimensions or our exact room. For d=g=h_minus=h_plus=1 our room has area6 and12 maximal boundary edges; the five-square example has area5. A homothety scales area, so area alone is not a shape invariant; the stronger distinction is the middle rectangle's protrusion beyond the outer chambers and the resulting12-edge boundary, unlike the standard C pentomino's8-edge boundary. The exercise text supplies no general parameter theorem or solution. Do not describe C-shaped illumination itself as new.

7. Mridul Aanjaneya, Arijit Bishnu and S.P.Pal, Directly Visible Pairs and Illumination by Reflections in Orthogonal Polygons, EuroCG2008.
https://orionquest.github.io/papers/DVPIROP/paper.html
The authors' abstract concerns direct visibility bounds, diffuse-reflection lower bounds, and combinatorial complexity after a fixed number of specular reflections. It does not state universal outer-to-outer connectivity in this family. This assessment is abstract-level; the linked Stanford PDF was unavailable, and the full paper was not inspected.

8. Marilyn Breen, A staircase illumination theorem for orthogonal polygons, Contributions to Algebra and Geometry48(2007),309–320.
https://ftp.gwdg.de/pub/misc/EMIS/journals/BAG/vol.48/no.2/1.html
The publisher abstract uses staircase visibility and common translated segments. Staircase paths allow freely chosen turns, so its assertion cannot be substituted for specular billiard trajectories. Similar search terms are not mathematical equivalence. Floodlight, modem and diffuse-reflection literature also uses different optical rules; those titles do not by themselves anticipate this proposition.

9. J.C.F.de Winter and George W.Tokarsky, Unilluminable rooms: minimal examples, classifications and exceptions, GitHub manuscript dated31 October2026.
https://github.com/JoostdeWinter/unilluminable-rooms/blob/main/unilluminable_rooms.tex
The manuscript concerns lattice blocking constructions, minimality/classification under corner conditions, and selected relaxations. Its square-tiling lower bounds apply to the defined lattice-room class, where specified tile vertices must be corners. Our arbitrary real dimensions and allowed noncorner crossings do not satisfy that hypothesis in general. Those bounds therefore do not establish universal illumination of our twelve-wall family. No matching corridor proposition was located in the text. This is a manuscript comparison, not a statement of peer-reviewed publication status.

WHICH PARTS COULD BE NEW
- Statement candidate: every permitted lower-to-upper pair in this exact family, uniformly across all positive real dimensions, including the formerly exceptional rational cases.
- Proof candidate: the family-specific combination of one-/three-pass folding, endpoint-preserving cap transfer, a proved finite parameter reduction, and exact continuous endpoint covers.
- Reproducibility contribution: independently derived rational guard certificates, including equality edges and isolated endpoint cells. Their quantity does not itself establish mathematical novelty.
- Established tools: unfolding, axis scaling, modular arithmetic, irrational rotations, finite Fourier averages, affine shears and exact polygon clipping. Do not claim invention of these methods.

The accepted proof reduces its residual rational data to22,538 configurations, with770,912 saved continuous-cover clauses;490 need three-pass clauses. These counts come from local proof/review artifacts, not external literature or a new rerun by this audit. Every configuration still has a continuum of horizontal endpoint placements. “Finite computer search found many successful rays” would misdescribe the accepted certificate claim.

WHY A QUICK GENERAL-THEOREM COROLLARY HAS NOT BEEN ESTABLISHED
Finiteness of dark pairs is not emptiness. Existence at nearby dimensions or for dense/generic parameter sets does not pass automatically to a limiting exact target: reflection counts can grow or a limiting ray can hit a corner. Enlarging a known illuminated subroom can remove a wall used by its path; an artificial-wall reflection is not automatically legal in the larger room. A covering or shear argument must preserve actual endpoint fibers and every forbidden corner preimage, including regular lifts of convex vertices.

For orientation only, the standard four-copy unfolding of this12-edge orthogonal polygon has four3pi/2 corners and eightpi/2 corners. Each reflex corner produces a6pi cone (zero order2); convex corners give regular lifts. The total zero order is8, hence genus5. A theorem restricted to genus2 surfaces therefore does not apply directly to this unfolding. This does NOT exclude a covering or quotient to a lower-genus surface; that would need a separate construction and endpoint analysis.
