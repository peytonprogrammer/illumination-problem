Exact middle returns and actual endpoint coupling
4 November 2026 — bounded follow-up, same-agent hand argument and exact checks

[done] The middle chamber admits an exact open-rotation formula, but summing its returns alone does not ensure the actual target. In the existing 12-wall fixture, every legal initially upward source ray that reaches the top chamber on its first visit to the middle misses the top chamber's center on its first upward target-height pass. A hand-derived 11-reflection trajectory reaches that SAME target after one top-ceiling reflection. This is a limitation of the stated itinerary class, not a dark pair or an impossibility theorem for general return maps.

1. Exact map, with its stopping rule
For the family defined in proof.txt, enter the middle rectangle at (q,2h), 0<q<delta, with upward velocity and signed horizontal/vertical ratio p. At the nth subsequent horizontal-level crossing, provided no earlier exit or corner has occurred,
  x_n = F(q+n*h*p),  n=1,2,...,
  F(z)=1-|((z mod 2)-1)|.
For odd n the level is 3h: exit upward iff 1-eta<x_n<1. For even n the level is 2h: exit downward iff 0<x_n<delta. Otherwise the relevant horizontal mirror reverses the vertical velocity. With entry vertical speed normalized to 1, the horizontal velocity at crossing n is p*F'(q+n*h*p); the arrival vertical velocity is (-1)^(n-1). The ratio to CURRENT vertical velocity therefore also has an alternating sign. A crossing with x_n=0 or 1, or at the appropriate portal endpoint, hits a corner and is illegal.
Proof: unfold side reflections into a straight horizontal phase with vertical travel nh. Fold x back to [0,1]; the derivative records the side-reflection parity. Horizontal reflections only change the direction of vertical travel. The formula stops at the first opening or corner. Continuing it through an opening would trace a fictitious rectangular billiard.
Thus arbitrarily many side reflections and level returns have a concise exact representation. The first-exit inequalities and source/target labels are still required. Rotation density does not specify which opening is reached first or hit a prescribed target.

2. Fixed fixture and complete source branches

  R0=[-9/10,1/10]x[0,2], R1=[0,1]x[2,3],
  R2=[4/5,9/5]x[3,4], S=(-2/5,1).
Set the NEW target to the center of R2:
  T*=(13/10,7/2).
Restrict only this test to initially upward rays that enter the middle before ANY horizontal reflection in R0, stay there until their first upper exit, then reach height7/2 before ANY horizontal reflection in R2. All vertical-wall bounce counts and all middle retries are allowed.
Every source ray in this class has first portal q=1/10-e, 0<e<1/10. The source unfolding from proof.txt gives exactly two possible incoming-middle ratios:

These retain both source-fold orientations and every initial signed slope. The integer 2m disappears at all integer-height middle crossings but reappears in top transport. No bounded-slope assumption is made.

4. Why every such ray misses actual T* on its first upward pass
Let a=4/5, x_N be the physical upper exit, and s the sign from the table. For the following upward distance1/2, unfolding within R2 gives local target-height coordinate
  u=x_N-a+(s*p)/2,
and physical horizontal position a+F(u). The actual center T* requires
  F(u)=1/2, equivalently u in Z+1/2.
In each table row, u=c+d*e+s*m. Removing the integer s*m gives these exact open ranges:

 row    | c+d*e                    | open range
 A7 -   | -13/20+(17/2)e          | (-9/40,-1/80)
 A7 +   |  21/20-(17/2)e          | (1/5,33/80)
 A11 -  | -13/20+(25/2)e          | (-3/20,-1/40)
 B5 -   | -3/20+(13/2)e           | (17/60,1/2)
 B9 -   | -3/20+(21/2)e           | (27/100,12/25)
 B9 +   | 11/20-(21/2)e           | (-3/20,-2/25)

None contains a half-integer. In B5 the limiting value1/2 occurs only at e=1/10, where q=0 is an illegal portal endpoint. Therefore NO ray in the stated entire itinerary class hits T*. This includes every integer m and every legal number of middle level returns, not a finite direction test.
This does NOT cover a source ray beginning downward, a horizontal reflection/return in the bottom chamber, an exit from the middle into R0 followed by reentry, or a horizontal reflection/return in R2. Those excluded behaviors are precisely why the result is NOT nonillumination.

6. Research decision and limits
The exact return formula is useful bookkeeping. However, even a COMPLETE treatment of middle retries, all source-fold labels and arbitrary side bounce counts misses the actual endpoint unless the target chamber's return behavior is included. A rule proving mere access to the upper opening cannot fill that gap.
This bounded test does not prove a general three-chamber theorem, a global finite-state reduction, or the proposed20-wall illumination theorem. The11-reflection path is one analytic fixture, not general coverage. The strongest new exclusion is the full itinerary-class statement in sections2–4. The method has not earned an unrestricted campaign; a useful next theory step would have to include returns across multiple chambers and preserve the actual target equation.
