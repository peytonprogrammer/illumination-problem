2. A fixed bounded room and actual endpoint pair
Let
  P=([0,2]x[0,1]) union ([0,1]x[0,2]),
  S=(3/2,1/2), T=(1/2,3/2), V=(1,1).
The boundary is (0,0),(2,0),(2,1),(1,1),(1,2),(0,2).
This is a simple orthogonal six-wall room of area3, with ONE reflex corner V. Both marks are strictly interior.
A singular target-aimed candidate means a folded straight development that connects these actual marks, obeys ordinary specular reflection at every smooth contact, and hits a specified corner. Under the corner-stopping convention this is NOT a legal billiard connection: the physical ray stops at its first corner. Any continuation after that event below is a prescribed formal candidate, not an asserted dynamical continuation through a corner.

3. Infinitely many candidates fail at the SAME single reflex corner
For every integer k>=0, put a=8k+1 and launch with velocity(-a,1). Write F_b(z)=b-|((z mod 2b)-b)|.
For 1/2<=y<=1, define
  x(y)=F_2(3/2-a(y-1/2)).
For 1<=y<=3/2, define
  x(y)=F_1(1-a(y-1)).
At y=1, the lower unfolded coordinate is1-4k, so F_2=1 and its derivative is+1. The upper starts at x=1 and immediately moves left. Thus both pieces arrive at V and leave V with the SAME velocity(-a,1); there is no arbitrary bend at V.
At y=3/2, the upper unfolded coordinate is1/2-4k and F_1=1/2. Hence it ends at the actual T. Except at y=1, the lower piece stays inside the lower rectangle with only smooth vertical-wall contacts, and the upper piece stays inside the upper rectangle with only smooth vertical-wall contacts. All contacts occur at strictly intermediate heights, away from every polygon corner.
The exact contact lists are:
  lower: z=-2j, y=1/2+(3/2+2j)/a, j=0,...,2k-1,
         x=0 for even j, x=2 for odd j;
  upper: z=-j, y=1+(1+j)/a, j=0,...,4k-1,
         x=0 for even j, x=1 for odd j.
The empty lists at k=0 are intentional. There are2k+4k=6k ordinary reflections and exactly ONE corner contact, namely V.
Both lower and upper smooth-reflection counts are even. The target development is therefore T_k=(1/2-8k,3/2), the developed corner is V_k=(1-4k,1), and V_k=(S+T_k)/2. These are actual sheet images for the displayed folded itinerary, not an arbitrary reflection-group word without geometry.
Because y increases strictly, each candidate has no physical self-intersection or repeated point. Its only arrival at T is its endpoint. Its initial direction differs for every k. All the infinitely many candidates, despite retaining the actual S and T and being loop-free/first-arrival, are obstructed by the same one physical reflex corner.
This disproves any universal constant bound on the number of such failed candidate directions charged to one physical reflex corner. It does not disprove a different invariant with cancellations, a specially selected finite independent family, or a theorem that uses the assumption of darkness in an essential way. These candidates all belong to one relative reflection frame, so this is NOT a four-frame independence counterexample.

4. Fixed-word endpoint rigidity
For a prescribed finite wall-reflection word in a fixed polygon, reflect the target backward through those supporting lines to get one developed target T_w. If T_w!=S, the initial direction is forced to be the direction of T_w-S. Any ordinary finite trajectory realizing that word must unfold to that unique straight segment. If T_w=S, a positive-length straight developed segment with those endpoints is impossible.
Therefore a small angular change while keeping the word fixed cannot both avoid a corner of that segment and still hit the same target. At a singular word boundary, one may switch itinerary, but the new developed target generally changes. Its endpoint-matching equation must be solved again; there is no automatic left/right pair of connecting rays.
The following exact length gap makes this failure concrete in the bounded room, even though the pair is lit.

5. Exact shortest legal length: sqrt(5), versus singular sqrt(2)
All wall coordinates are integers. Reflections in x=c or y=c, c integer, send the corresponding coordinate to2c-x or2c-y. Every developed target therefore has coordinates
  (epsilon_x/2+2m, epsilon_y*3/2+2n),
  epsilon_x,epsilon_y in{+1,-1}, m,n integers.
Subtracting S gives two integer displacement components. For any legal S-to-T connection both must be nonzero: orthogonal reflections preserve absolute component velocities, and an axial path cannot change both physical coordinates between these marks.
The squared length is consequently a positive sum of two nonzero integer squares. Below5 its only possible value is2. Equality2 forces one of the four initial directions (+/-1,+/-1), and developed travel time1 when normalized to |v_x|=|v_y|=1.
Three of those rays hit a corner after time1/2:
  NE ->(2,1) convex corner;
  NW ->V=(1,1) reflex corner;
  SE ->(2,0) convex corner.
The fourth, SW, first reflects at the smooth bottom point(1,0). At time1 its position is(1/2,1/2), not T. Hence length sqrt(2) is impossible for a legal connecting path. This tests only the four directions analytically forced by the possible shorter length; it is not a discovery search or a finite-ray completeness claim for arbitrary lengths.
A legal connection of length sqrt(5) is
  S -> Z=(0,5/4) -> T,
with initial direction(-2,1). It crosses y=1 at x=1/2, strictly inside the passage; Z is a smooth point of the left wall. Reflection reverses the horizontal component and reaches T with no corner contact. The two segment lengths are(3/4)sqrt(5) and(1/4)sqrt(5). Thus sqrt(5) is the exact minimum legal length.
At k=0 the singular candidate is the direct segment S->V->T with length sqrt(2). It is also a shortest path in the polygon closure, since its length equals the Euclidean endpoint distance. So even selecting a globally shortest corner-bending path does not supply a nearby legal fixed-endpoint path. Every legal connection is separated from its length by at least sqrt(5)-sqrt(2)>0.
This does NOT assert that a true globally shortest legal connection reuses corners: legal connections cannot hit them at all. It distinguishes the singular minimizing object from a legal connecting path.

6. What a viable distinct-corner theorem would still need
The proposed lower bound would require selecting obstructions whose independence is PROVED from simultaneous darkness of all actual endpoint lift pairs, with a controlled charge per physical reflex corner. Counting paths, winding, reflection words, first arrivals or singular target images is insufficient by section3. Local left/right resolution does not preserve the endpoint automatically by section4. Globally minimizing singular length is insufficient by section5. A wavefront index, four-label rank or noncancellation theorem would be additional new input; none is supplied by these controls or the prior notes.
In a simple orthogonal polygon W=2r+4, from convex-minus-reflex=4. Therefore r>=9 would indeed imply W>=22, but this identity is only the final conversion and supplies none of the missing nine independent charges.
Decision: retire the proposed unqualified local distinct-corner count. Do not label it a new successful strategy or begin a broader search. The potentially different darkness-dependent independence theorem remains open, with no concrete invariant presently established. No extension of the restricted lattice theorem, universal22 theorem follows.
