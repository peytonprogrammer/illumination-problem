Comparison with the referenced paper, for editorial context only

Theorem 7.4 assumes a single global reflection/half-turn sigma of R preserving disjoint endpoint sets U,V. At an exception its path identity is alpha(t+u)=sigma(alpha(t-u)); the proof removes an excursion using a shortest connecting ray, whose existence follows from discrete lattice lengths.

Our statement is on the folded base P. Its local identity is eta(t+u)=eta(t-u), with no sigma. The physical path in Q may retrace a different copy; equality is asserted only after applying f. First arrival at b and last visit to a replace shortest-ray selection, so no discreteness/minimum-length argument is needed for this step. Truncation at arbitrary preimages of a,b is essential.

Both proofs use deterministic reversibility to remove an exceptional excursion. Theorem 7.4 is related precedent and useful explanatory context. It does not apply directly to Q, and the local folded identity needs its own verification, supplied in clarification.txt. No novelty follows from this comparison.
