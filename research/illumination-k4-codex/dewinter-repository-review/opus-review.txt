# Verdict: accept, with wording changes

I found no logical gap in the proof. The comparison is accurate, with one omission. The mathematical comparison needs the clarifications below.

## 1. The proof

I checked the geometry by hand.

**Setup**
- P has 12 integer points, and 10 of them are corners.
- a=(0,0) lies inside the top side, and b=(2,-2) lies inside the side x=2.
- The maps f agree on both seams.
- P0 and P2 don't meet, so the only overlaps are the two seams. No pinch points add corners to Q.

**Exceptional points**
- E1 to E4 are smooth wall points of Q: walls x=-1, x=1, y=1, and y=3.
- At each one, two copies share a corner. 30 − 8 = 22 corners of Q is correct.
- I checked the velocity at E3. Incoming (p,q) in P1 folds to (p,−q). Outgoing (p,−q) in P2 folds to (−p,q). That's an exact reversal, and the same holds at E1 and E2.

**Endpoint preimages**
- a comes from S and from (4,0), a smooth point on the bottom wall of P2.
- b comes from T and from (2,−2), a smooth point on the wall of P0.
- None of these four points is a corner. The truncated path η can start physically at (4,0) or end at (2,−2), and the argument still holds because every contradiction happens in P.
- State these four preimages explicitly in section 3, since a reviewer will want to see them.

**Time intervals**
- Before time t, η(0,t) meets no corner of P. Any corner hit is either a reversal at a preimage of E1–E4 or a forbidden Q-corner.
- So no second reversal can occur at t+u, because η(t−u) is never a corner. The prefix also contains no a or b in its interior.
- If L<2t: 0 < 2t−L < t, so b is visited at an interior time.
- If L=2t: a=b, which is false.
- If L>2t: 2t lies in (0,L), so a is revisited at an interior time.
- All three cases are sound. First arrival and last visit exist because the path has finitely many legs.

**One missing justification.** "After t it must retrace that prefix" needs its reason stated. Add: "At every noncorner point of P, the folded motion is straight travel or specular reflection, whichever copy of Q the physical point is in. So the folded dynamics depend only on folded position and velocity." This sentence is what makes an identity without σ legitimate. It's true, but the note currently assumes it.

**Parity step**
- Unfolding is valid because η has no reversals, so it meets no corner.
- B is even-even and nonzero, and z = B/2^r lies strictly inside the segment because 2^r ≥ 2.
- z has an odd coordinate, folds to a point other than a or b, and is therefore a corner. That gives the contradiction.
- Add that a corner hit with no reversal is impossible by section 2. That closes the loop explicitly.

**Minor points**
- "Convex" is accurate but not used anywhere. You can drop it.
- Define "nonsingular" in the last line, or replace it with "corner-avoiding."

Add one fact: **Theorem 7.4 doesn't apply to P either.**
