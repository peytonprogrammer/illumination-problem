Main comparison
1. Definition 2.1 requires every B/C point to be an actual corner. Theorem 5.1 gives 24 sides for hole-free triangular lattice rooms; Theorem 5.2 gives 34 for square lattice rooms. Our Q has four smooth C points: (-1,0),(1,0),(2,1),(2,3). Thus neither strict minimum applies. No contradiction or refutation of their results follows from Q.
2. Their blocking lemma is the same lattice-parity obstruction used on our base P. This mechanism is published context, not the contribution to emphasize.
3. Theorem 7.4 allows transparent exceptions at fixed points of ONE GLOBAL reflection/half-turn preserving two disjoint endpoint sets. Its proof uses shortest-path symmetry and retracing. This is close conceptual published context for our reversal step but not a direct proof of Q: our map is a piecewise folding onto P with several seam endpoints, rather than the stated global symmetry of the room. Their Section 7 also contains 27-wall and nonagon examples, so the paper as a whole is not strict-only.
5. Our k<=6 theorem permits transparent non-A points and proves a lower bound in a broader class than their strict corner condition. Their inspected statements do not give this result. Do not repeat an unqualified claim that no restricted wall-count lower bounds exist: this preprint supplies several.
