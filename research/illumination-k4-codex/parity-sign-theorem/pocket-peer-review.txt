# Pocket-detection lemma: reductions, a parity criterion, and the remaining gap

**Verdict:** I can't prove the lemma, and I can't refute it. What I can do rigorously:

- reduce admissible events to a finite monodromy problem,
- give an exact local formula for Q(η) and a mod-2 detection criterion,
- give an exact iff criterion for failure, which isolates the missing hypothesis,
- rule out two natural families of counterexample (torus-level fibers and torus double covers).

What remains open is a non-abelian existence and parity question, stated in §7.

---

## 1. Parity rigidity: every event has primitive w and a reflex B/C corner

**Lemma 1.** Every admissible event has primitive w. Its corner c is a B or C corner, and c is the only integer point in the interior of s→c→t.

*Proof.*

1. Every developing chart is an element of the affine reflection group generated by the lines x=k, y=k and x±y ∈ 2ℤ. That group preserves ℤ² and the A/B/C types.
2. So a point of X whose developed image is an integer point is a lift of an integer point of P, and that point has the same type.
3. dev(s) is an A point. If w = k·w₀ with w₀ primitive and k ≥ 2, the interior point s+w₀ has type w₀ mod 2, which is not (0,0). That makes it a B/C point, hence a corner lift, which is forbidden.
4. So k = 1. Then c = s+w has type B or C, and t = s+2w is an A point. ∎

**Lemma 1′.** Corners of angle π/4 or π/2 (cone angle 2π in X) never carry an event with S ≠ T.

*Proof.*

1. Every corner's wall reflections generate D₂ or D₄, and both contain −I. So the deck involution ρ = −I fixes every corner lift.
2. If the cone angle at c is 2π, ρ maps the reversed first half onto the second half.
3. Then t = ρ(s) is a lift of S, which contradicts S ≠ T. ∎

C corners can only have angle π/2 or 3π/2, because no diagonal wall passes through a C point. So only **reflex** B/C corners matter. The pocket supplies two of them: B at 5π/4 and C at 3π/2.

## 2. Events are rigid: the problem is one monodromy question

From s, the ray in a primitive direction w meets no integer point before s+w. All cone points sit at integer points, so the first half always exists. Choosing a side σ then fixes the second half.

So each pair (w, σ) gives exactly one candidate endpoint E(w,σ), which lies over 0 in 𝕋 = ℝ²/2ℤ². Equivalently, E(w,σ) is the monodromy, applied to s, of the loop ℓ_{w,σ}: the closed geodesic 0 → w → 2w on 𝕋 pushed off the half-period w to side σ. The two sides differ by one extra 2π turn at c.

So option (a) has no freedom left. The question is exactly whether **T lies in R(s) := {E(w,σ)}**.

## 3. Finiteness

Let Γ₀ be a finite-index subgroup of Aff⁺(X) that fixes every point of X lying over ℤ². For ψ ∈ Γ₀:

- ψ fixes s, c and t,
- ψ maps straight segments to straight segments,
- ψ preserves sides and π-sectors.

Hence E(Dψ·w, σ) = E(w,σ).

Consequences:

- E(S,T) is a finite union of Γ₀-orbits.
- Whether any event exists can be decided by checking finitely many representatives of the Γ₀-orbits of primitive vectors.
- Every such orbit contains non-wall directions, which justifies the rule that wall-parallel directions can be excluded.

## 4. A pocket ledger formula and a parity lemma

Only two mirrors move, each with velocity (0,−1): D_t and H_t. The walls x=1 above and below the portal are invariant under a vertical slide.

Write the billiard realization of s→c→t as reflections r₁,…,r_k, counting the reflections made during the π-turn at c as part of the second half. Differentiating the composite of reflections gives:

  **Q(η) = Σ_{second half} L_{<j} u_j − Σ_{first half} L_{<j} u_j − 2·𝟙[c ∈ pocket]·L_c(0,−1)**

where:

- the sums run over reflections in pocket walls,
- L_{<j} is the linear part of the reflections before r_j,
- u_H = (0,−2) and u_D = (1,−1).

Every term except the D-terms is in 2ℤ², and L(1,−1) ≡ (1,1) mod 2 for every L ∈ D₄. This gives:

**Parity Lemma.** Q(η) ≡ N_D(η)·(1,1) (mod 2), where N_D counts reflections in the sliding hypotenuse along the whole event, including the corner turn. **If N_D is odd, the event is detecting.**

The frozen example's Qη = (−1,−1) is consistent with this: it requires N_D to be odd. That is a sanity check only.

**Topological form.** Let 𝔇 be the union of the lifts of the hypotenuse.

- Each B-lift and each A-lift meets 4 hypotenuse arcs, so 𝔇 is a mod-2 cycle.
- So N_D(η) ≡ η_σ·𝔇, the intersection number of η pushed to side σ with 𝔇. For closed cores, Q(C) ≡ (C·𝔇)(1,1).
- If P has no diagonal or antidiagonal wall besides the hypotenuse, then 𝔇 = ∂(K_ax-copies), where K_ax = {±I, axis reflections}. In that case **Q(η) is odd exactly when the lift t sits on a coordinate-swapped copy**, i.e. when the total linear part lies outside K_ax.
- Otherwise [𝔇] ≠ 0, and the criterion becomes: in the unbranched double cover X̂ defined by 𝔇, the lifted event from ŝ ends on the odd sheet.

## 5. Twist transport and the exact criterion

Take any cylinder direction v ∦ w. It has cores C_i and a multitwist ψ_v with weights k_i, and some power of ψ_v lies in Γ₀. The Dehn-twist formula for relative classes gives:

  **Q(ψ_vⁿ η) = Q(η) + n·Θ_v(η)**,  where Θ_v(η) = Σ_i k_i μ_i(η) Q(C_i)

and μ_i(η) = μ_i(γ₂) − μ_i(γ₁) is the signed transverse height through C_i, normalized by the height of C_i. Note that Σ_i h_i μ_i(η) = 0 because hol(η) = 0.

Your axis-twist invariance is the special case Q(C_i) = 0.

**Proposition.** If some event η exists and some direction v has Θ_v(η) ≠ 0, the lemma holds for that (P, S, T, pocket).

*Why:* Q(ψ_vⁿ η) is affine in n with nonzero slope, so it vanishes for at most one n. The direction Dψ_vⁿ·w is wall-parallel for only finitely many n. Choose an n that avoids both.

**Exact criterion (c).** The lemma fails for (P, S, T, pocket) **if and only if**:

- (i) E(S,T) = ∅, or
- (ii) every event η has Q(η) = 0 **and** Θ_v(η) = 0 for every cylinder direction v.

Both directions follow from the affine formula above. Condition (ii) is the missing global hypothesis. It says the crossing profile (μ_i(η)) is orthogonal to the Q-weighted twist vector in every cusp direction, beyond the automatic orthogonality to the heights h_i. The real mechanism is therefore non-axis cusp multitwists, not direction density.

## 6. What cannot produce a counterexample

- **Torus-level fibers or "nonadjacent regular fibers."** The pocket forces the translation lattice T_P = 2ℤ²: reflection in x=1 composed with DHD = V₂ gives (2,0), and conjugating by D gives (0,2). So every lift of every A point lies over the same point 0 ∈ ℝ²/2ℤ². No lattice or fiber invariant can separate S from T.
- **Intermediate double covers Y → 𝕋.** The source s is regular, so Y cannot be branched over 0.
  - If Y is unbranched, its label changes by e·[w] mod 2. That takes both values as [w] runs over B, C₁, C₂.
  - If Y is branched at two of {B, C₁, C₂}, switching side σ at a branched type flips the label.

  Either way both labels are reachable. I expect the same holds for all abelian covers, but I have only checked ℤ/2.
- **Symmetries of P.** No symmetry of P preserves the pocket together with its portal. So the slide cannot be killed by an equivariance argument.

## 7. Where a counterexample would have to live

Any failure must be either:

- (i) **non-abelian unreachability**: T ∉ R(S) under the full monodromy of X → 𝕋, restricted to the loops ℓ_{w,σ} through reflex corners; or
- (ii) **exact cancellation**: in every reachable Γ₀-orbit, N_D is even (equivalently, by §4, every reachable lift of T is on the even sheet), the H-terms, D-terms and endpoint terms cancel exactly, and Θ_v ≡ 0 in every direction.

I have not found a configuration of either kind inside the class, and I am not offering one outside it.

A route to a proof would be to show that some event through the pocket's own reflex corners (B or C) reaches a swapped lift of T, or the odd sheet of X̂. Events through a pocket corner whose path has no other pocket interaction have Q(η) = −2L_c(0,−1) ≠ 0 automatically.
