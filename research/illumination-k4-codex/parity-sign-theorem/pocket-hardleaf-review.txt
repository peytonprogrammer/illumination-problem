- **Tdown (H1):** detected when N's portal is on the right, by the event w=(5,3) at the moving B. Two natural families provably fail when N's portal is on the left.

## 1. Counting observation: confirmed

**P_A=9 is impossible globally.**
- Q≤12 and Q≥2P_A−6 force Q=12, with every partial charge equal to its degree.
- The only types allowed are m1 (in=1), m2 with q=0 (in=2) and m4 with q=0 (in=2, out=1).
- So Z=0 and there is exactly one merger. The other 8 partials are m1 leaves.
- The degree sum is then at most 8+8+3=19, but the tree has 10 edges, so it must be 20.

**P_A=7 with connected H is impossible.**
- The tree equals H. M's outgoing neighbor at (2,2) is not a cardinal distance-2 neighbor of S or T, so it is not in H.

**P_A=8 with connected H.**
- The leaves are the 7 listed fans. A degree-1 neighbor of a full fan has in=1 and out=0, so h=0, which forces (m,q)=(1,1) or (2,0). Each leaf is therefore m1 or m2.
- Q=10+r. Disconnected H gives Q=11+r. Both bounds on r follow.

## 2. Codex's Tup detection: confirmed, with one refinement

I checked every computation:
- the corridor leg S→(4,1);
- the intrinsic π arcs for both B sides (the arc crosses the W flank when B=(5,1) and the E flank when B=(3,1));
- the composition r_d r_y r_d = reflection in x=6;
- the wall-x=5 variant via (5,1/3).

**Refinement:** a literal Tup with B=(5,1) needs a 90° SE quadrant at (5,1). That forces Tright to be m2 upper. So the m1 triangular-cap subcase only occurs with B=(3,1). This is harmless.

## 3. Uniform principle

**Sealed-core lemma.** Seal the pocket portal, so that B and C become smooth points of a straight wall. Suppose a core billiard ray from S, in primitive direction w:
- has c∈{B,C} as its first lattice hit and T as its second;
- meets neither moving pocket mirror.

Then it is an admissible event. The π arc at c is the one that crosses the flank ray, and specular reflection at c is exactly that flank continuation. Its slide defect is

  Q = −2·g₁u ≠ 0,

where g₁ is the linear part of the first-half development. This holds because S\* and T\* are developed only through fixed mirrors, and the flank reflection fixes u. Section 26.6 and Codex's construction are both instances.

The lemma has a useful equivalent form. Viewed from c, the event is a pair of core rays mirror-symmetric about the flank normal, of length |w| each, ending at S and at T respectively.

## 4. New result for H1 (literal Tdown, B=(5,−1), Tright m2 lower), N portal on the right

Use corner B and w=(5,3). The physical path is:

> S → (5/3, 1) [corridor ceiling, left half] → (3, 1/5) → **B(5,−1)** → (6, −2/5) [x=6] → (16/3, 0) [Tright top] → (5, −1/5) → (11/3, −1) [C-flank] → (3, −3/5) [x=3 lower] → **T(4,0)**

**Checks.**
- Horizontal travel on each half is 5 and vertical travel is 3, so each half has developed displacement (5,3).
- No bounce point is a lattice point.
- The path never goes below y=−1, so it avoids both moving mirrors.
- It never reaches the T top, the N fan or any S leaf.
- The only non-forced wall it uses is the ceiling segment (1,1)–(2,1). That segment is a wall exactly when N's portal is (2,1)–(3,1).

**Sector at B.** The physical link is [0°, 225°]. The S germ is at 149°. The arc runs clockwise across the E flank to an intrinsic −31°, which is physical (5,3).

**Defect.** g₁ = diag(1,−1), so Q = −2u. Then det(w,Q) = 6 ≠ 0.

## 5. H1 with N portal on the left: in-class obstructions

**Corridor-straight S-leg (slope s<1/3).** Track the first-contact map over the four sub-intervals (0,1/7], (1/7,1/5), (1/5,2/7] and (2/7,1/3). In every case the ray re-enters the corridor before it touches y=−1 at x=4 or x=5. Its only bottom contacts are in the open interval x∈(5,6) inside Tright. This holds for every leaf configuration.

**One ceiling bounce on the right half (s∈(1/3,1/2)).** Only two candidates reach c:
- (8,3) at C;
- (7,3) at B, approaching from the Tright side.

Their second halves land on A corners, at (6,0) and (2,0) respectively.

So any H1 event with N on the left must use the S-square walls, the N or Tup leaves, or several corridor passes.

## 6. H2a (literal Tright, B=(5,1), Tup m2 right box): partial closure

Both events use corner C(5,0), and the flank is x=5 below C.

**w=(5,2), valid when N's portal is on the left and Tdown's portal is on the right.**

> S → (5/2, 1) → (3, 4/5) → **C** → (3, −4/5) → (7/2, −1) → (5, −2/5) → **T**

Q = 2u and det(w,Q) = 10.

**w=(7,2) (developed (−7,2)), valid when N's portal is on the right, Tdown's portal is on the left, and Sleft's portal is lower.**

> S → (−1, 2/7) → (1, 6/7) → (3/2, 1) → (3, 4/7) → **C** → (3, −4/7) → (9/2, −1) → (5, −6/7) → (3, −2/7) → **T**

g₁ = −I and det(w,Q) = −14.

**Still open in H2a:**
- N left with Tdown left;
- N right with Tdown right;
- N right with Tdown left and Sleft upper.

**H2b.** The analogous (5,2) T-half would cross x=5 below C, which is the pocket portal in that case, so it fails there. H2b is open.

## 7. A stronger lemma to target

The T-side is not the obstruction. In the bare T square, any primitive (even, odd) vector from C, or (odd, even) vector from C(5,0), reaches T at parameter 1, provided each fold crossing lands on a wall half. Every failure above happens on the S side.

So the lemma to prove is a **corridor-mouth transfer**: describe, symbolically in the S-leaf and N orientations, which mouth heights and slopes at x=3 the core ray carries to S at a prescribed developed length. Combined with the mirror-pair form in §3, that would decide each remaining subcase uniformly, instead of one closed-form event per subcase.
