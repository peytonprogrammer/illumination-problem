# A diagonal double packages the odd-count seed obligation

[in progress] Focused review2665 ACCEPTS the conditional transfer with
conditions below. It is an EXACT REFORMULATION of odd-count seed existence,
not a proof or a reduction in difficulty. Existence remains unproved. This is not a new room, ray, word, direction or orbit search.

## 1. The abstract reflected double

Let P be the specified raw simple canonical room and let D be its literal
pocket's moving diagonal wall, with endpoint angles A=pi/4 and B=5pi/4.
Take two copies P0,P1 and identify their D edges by the mirror reflection.
Equivalently, develop P0 as P and P1 as its reflected image in D, retaining
both as separate sheets if their planar developments overlap. Call the
result P_D. Gluing two topological disks along a full boundary interval
gives a disk. Its metric is flat away from the usual boundary vertices.

All remaining boundary angles are those of P, appearing twice, except

    angle(A_D)=pi/2,       angle(B_D)=5pi/2=450degrees.

There is thus exactly one boundary angle exceeding2pi. P_D is an abstract
flat disk with boundary; it cannot be an embedded simple Euclidean polygon
near B_D. In particular, a theorem requiring a simple planar room cannot
be applied to it by ignoring its overlapping planar development.

The diagonal edge has no integer point in its relative interior. All
other non-A integer points remain nonsmooth boundary vertices; the seam
endpoints remain corners. The two marked points we select are actual S
in P0 and actual reflected T in P1. They remain regular interior points.
In the reflected development the latter coordinate is r_D(T). The selected
D line has even diagonal offset because its A endpoint has both coordinates
even; its reflection therefore preserves the even-even class. We never replace T by another physical label.

## 2. Its eight-copy unfolding is the diagonal-bit cover

Describe the eight-copy unfolding X of P by copy labels g in D4. Define
X_D from sixteen copies (g,b), b in F2, using the original gluings, but
change b by1 exactly when the crossed physical wall is D. All other
walls leave b fixed. Forgetting b gives a degree-two covering of X away
from its corner lifts.

At either endpoint of D, the two adjacent reflections generate D4. A
small loop around its full lifted cone crosses the D seam four times,
an even number. There is therefore no local branch at either endpoint.
At all other corners there is no toggled seam. The map X_D->X extends
as an UNRAMIFIED degree-two local translation covering, possibly with
two connected components.

In the unfolding of P_D, its second half in a frame g has original room
frame g*r_D. Set its bit to1 and the first half's bit to0. The internal
D seam then changes (h,b) to(h*r_D,b+1), while every external original
wall E changes h to h*r_E and keeps the bit. This is precisely the
sixteen-copy construction above. It is an intrinsic flat construction,
not an embedding of P_D into the plane.

If P has another diagonal wall, Section8's prescribed odd closed crossing
loop makes X_D connected. If D is the only diagonal wall, the bit equals
the endpoint copy-colour difference and X_D splits into two copies of X.
Then P_D has only axis wall directions and its minimal unfolding uses
four copies; the eight-copy construction is the corresponding redundant
two-component unfolding. This exceptional case must not be silently
called a connected sixteen-copy surface. In either case the component
containing an initial source lift has appropriate lifts of the selected
actual target in P1, by connectedness of the abstract disk P_D.

## 3. A cross-copy seed would detect the original slide

Suppose P_D admits a legal one-corner midpoint event from actual S in P0
to actual reflected T in P1. Require equal primitive integer developed
halves w, no other PHYSICAL corner (including the unfolded-regular A_D),
and an embedded intrinsic pi-sector
in its minimal unfolding. Require w to avoid ALL FOUR original canonical
direction lines, even if removing D eliminates one exposed wall direction
from P_D. Avoiding only the doubled disk's exposed wall directions is
not silently substituted for this stronger requirement. Use the bit0 initial source lift.

The selected corner cannot be unfolded-regular. The halfturn tau=-I fixes
every regular corner lift and preserves physical labels. At such a corner,
the unique straight pi continuation of the incoming half is its reversed
tau image: it ends at tau(S), still a physical S lift. This cannot be the
specified different physical target T. Thus an actual distinct-label seed
must select a cone corner of angle>2pi, for which the reviewed opening
proposition applies. This also explains why an argument that simply forgets
regular physical corners would be invalid: A_D remains forbidden at every
nonselected encounter, despite having a regular unfolded metric.

Its target lies in a bit1 original-P copy. Hence its projected original
P itinerary crosses D an odd number of times. At a corner on a seam,
count the crossings on a push through the selected labeled pi-sector,
exactly as in the accepted Section8 bit convention.

The covering X_D->X is locally an isometry also at the lifted corners.
It preserves the cone angle and the distinct angular germs of every
embedded pi-sector. Projection therefore gives a legal one-corner event
between ACTUAL S and ACTUAL T on X, retaining the same primitive halves.
A sector that passes through the internal D seam merely projects to the
corresponding fixed-flank or moving-flank continuation in the original
room; it is not permission to substitute a3pi continuation.

By accepted Section4's parity identity, the full original pocket-slide
defect satisfies, in the integer source-copy frame,

    Q_x(eta) = (# selected D factors) mod2 =1mod2.

Thus Q(eta)!=0. Ordinary moving-mirror contributions and actual corner
motion are included in that identity. It is not just a corner-velocity
calculation. Section5's axis sign transfer then supplies an admissible
actual event with nonzero opening determinant if the first event's
nonzero vector happens to be parallel to w; it preserves Q and actual
labels and avoids the finitely many wall directions. This last step is
needed: an odd defect alone does not assert det(w,Q)!=0 for that first
representative.

## 4. Scope and value of the reduction

The covering transfer is reversible: lift any supplied odd-D event in P
from the bit0 source. It ends in a bit1 copy of the actual T and therefore
is a cross-copy event in P_D, componentwise in the sole-diagonal case.
Accordingly the doubled seed assertion is EXACTLY the old odd-word
existence assertion. It is not equivalent to full vector detection, since
even-word events can detect as well. No simplification is established.

A seed theorem for these particular doubled abstract disks would imply
universal detection for the original literal pockets, rather than seed
existence alone. It would supply the required endpoint
and an odd diagonal count, but establishing that theorem is itself the
missing work; the cover construction has not supplied it. This is a sufficient extension, not a claim
that every detecting event is odd or arises this way.

The open obligation is substantial: extend actual seed existence to a
flat topological disk whose one boundary corner has angle450degrees.
Neither planarity of the original room, the nonzero bit character,
ordinary topological connectivity, nor gonality supplies that seed.
The redundant unfolding in the sole-diagonal case also needs the minimal
component convention. No existing simple-room theorem has been extended
here, and no in-class counterexample to the original goal is asserted.
