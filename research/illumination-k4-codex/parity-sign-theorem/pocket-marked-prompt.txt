## 25. Candidate marked-period argument via an auxiliary branched cover

**Status: candidate under independent review.** This addresses the marked
field-of-definition prerequisite in Section23. It does not classify
periodic points or construct the required event.

Take two distinct labeled actual regular lifts s(t),t(t) on X_t, over a
small complex period disk about the raw room. All actual zero orders of
X_t are even. Topologically choose a connected double cover branched at
exactly s(t),t(t). A character of the punctured surface group sends each
of their meridians to1 in Z/2, and arbitrary handle generators to0.
The surface relation holds because the two meridian values sum to0.
Transport this choice on the disk. The pulled-back differential defines
Y_t of genus2g(X_t). It has two simple zeros at the branch points; every
other zero is an unramified lift of an even-order zero. Thus these two
new zeros are distinguished among its actual zeros by their odd order.
No artificial order0 markings are used upstairs.

In a fixed topological chart the relative periods of Y_t are rational
linear functions of the periods of X_t marked at Sigma union {s,t}:
project a relative path and integrate downstairs. Conversely transfer
and division by2 recover downstairs periods. In particular a downstairs
path a from s to t lifts to a closed cycle obtained by traversing one
lift of a and returning on the other, with period2int_a omega. Paths
from old zeros to marks lift to relative paths between actual upstairs
zeros. The local covering locus and the quotient period map therefore
have rational homogeneous linear descriptions. Along the original slide
all upstairs coordinates are vhat_0+t*vhat_1 with coefficients in Q[i].

The local covering construction is finite over the marked base stratum:
there are2^(2g) characters with the prescribed meridians. Include the
covering involution as finite discrete data and, where necessary, pass
to a local labeled/level cover. On a smooth upstairs limit, the
involution persists as a nonidentity holomorphic involution. One can
see nonidentity at a distinguished simple zero: its local multiplier
is-1. Its quotient retains the two distinct regular branch marks and
the downstairs zero orders; fixed points cannot collide in the smooth
upstairs family. The marked covering locus is consequently closed in
the relevant smooth upstairs stratum. Conversely finite covers over a
compact subset of the smooth marked base cannot degenerate: after local
trivialization there are finitely many topological covers, whose lifted
flat metrics vary continuously. Thus the covering map is proper over
the smooth marked base.

Let N_tau be the GL orbit closure of Y_tau at a transcendental real tau.
The actual-zero-stratum theorem applies to N_tau. Wright Theorem1.1
supplies homogeneous local equations over a number field. Coefficient
separation and the disk-continuation argument of Section21 imply that
N_tau contains the whole small cover disk. By symmetry the orbit closure
N_* is common to all small transcendental parameters. It contains the
square-tiled Y_0, so Wright Example1.2 gives fieldQ.

Keep the finite choice of involution and branch labels while taking
orbit closure; if needed work with its finite decorated lift, whose
local tangent branches agree with those of N_*. Taking the quotient
sends this decorated orbit closure onto the orbit closure of the actual
marked pair (X_tau,s(tau),t(tau)). Inclusion in one direction follows
from continuity. For the other direction, any sequence of marked-base
orbit points converging to a smooth marked base has a convergent lift
by the preceding properness statement; a subsequence retains the finite
cover decoration. Thus no unsupported marked orbit-closure theorem is
being invoked. In local period charts the image branches are rational
linear images of the upstairs branches, hence are defined overQ.

**Candidate consequence.** For the two selected actual lifts, all small
transcendental pocket slides have the same marked orbit closure M'_*,
containing the actual marked slice and every small rational parameter.
Its local tangent is defined overQ; forgetting marks projects it onto
M_*. At each rational X_r, a finite-index stabilizer of the two rational
marks and the local branch acts on that tangent. The physical marked
slide tangent is therefore available to a multi-direction affine-module
argument with those ACTUAL labels. Repeat with each desired pair of
lifts, or use a cover branched at all16actual lifts if simultaneous

The last conclusion supplies only a valid domain for the proposed marked
