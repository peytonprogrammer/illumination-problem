# The transcendental-slide route: what holds, what it gives, and where it stops

## Verdict

- **Your premise is true, and it extends further than you stated (L1).** The tangent space contains the slide class at every rational parameter of the slide, not only at the base. It is invariant under all the Veech groups along the slide at once.
- **Unconditional consequence (L2): rank M ≥ 2.** This holds with no fold-compactness and no genericity assumption.
- **Main negative findings (L5, L7).** No single affine multitwist, in any direction and at any rational parameter, can produce a free-mark direction. At the base X₀, in every direction (p,q) with p+q odd, each S-lift behaves exactly like a C-corner. Freeness can only come from mixing several directions, and the base point is close to the worst place to look.
- **The bridge to the event is genuinely missing (L8).** Every actual midpoint event on the literal slide occurs at a rational parameter, i.e. on a square-tiled X_{t*}. Freeness in the orbit closure neither implies nor is implied by such an event.

## Notation and conventions

- Σ is the set of all corner lifts, including the regular ones (pocket A, B, C are all regular: angles π/4, π/4, π/2). m is the set of mark lifts.
- Per(X_t) = w₀ + t·w₁ in H¹(X, Σ∪m; ℂ). Both w₀ and w₁ lie in ℤ[i]ⁿ, because the D4 matrices have entries in {0, ±1}.
- u₁′ := w₁ (with marks) and u₁ := forget(u₁′) ∈ H¹(X, Σ). The absolute part is δ := p(u₁), which is your slide cohomology.
- M and M′ are the GL₂ orbit closures of X_t and (X_t, m) respectively.
- At a point of M′, the tangent is V′⊗ℂ with V′ ⊂ H¹(X, Σ∪m; ℝ); similarly V for M.

## L1. Transcendental separation and whole-slice containment

**Statement.** Let t be transcendental and let I be the open interval of parameters on which the combinatorics is constant (I contains 0). Then:

- (a) X_s ∈ M′ for every s ∈ Ī. One linear branch L = V′⊗ℂ of M′ contains the whole segment in the fixed chart. In particular Re w₀, Im w₀, Re w₁, Im w₁ all lie in V′.
- (b) For every rational r ∈ I, a finite-index subgroup Γ_r′ of Aff(X_r, Σ∪m) preserves V′, acting through Gauss–Manin transport to the common chart. Write G := ⟨Γ_r′ : r ∈ ℚ∩I⟩.
- (c) M′ (and M) is the same for all transcendental t ∈ I.

**Proof.**

1. By Wright (field of definition), each local branch of M′ at X_t is cut out by homogeneous linear equations ℓ with coefficients in a number field K. The equations are homogeneous because of the ℝ₊ and rotation invariance.
2. Each such equation gives ℓ(w₀) + t·ℓ(w₁) = 0, with both values in K(i). Since t is transcendental over K(i), ℓ(w₀) = ℓ(w₁) = 0. So the line w₀ + s·w₁ lies in the branch near t.
3. Let S = {s ∈ Ī : X_s ∈ M′}; it is closed. Let J be the maximal open subinterval of S containing t, and suppose it had an endpoint e ∈ I. Near X_e, M′ is a finite union of linear branches (EMM: proper immersion). Infinitely many points of the segment then lie in one branch. A linear subspace containing two points of a line contains the line, so e is interior to S, a contradiction. Hence S ⊇ Ī, and a single branch L carries the whole segment. This proves (a).
4. For (b): at rational r, an element γ ∈ Aff(X_r) with derivative A acts on the chart by v ↦ A·γ*(v), and permutes the branches at X_r. A finite-index subgroup fixes L. Since L = V′⊗ℝ² is A-stable, it follows that γ*V′ = V′.
5. For (c): two transcendental parameters in I give orbit closures that each contain the other's base surface, so they coincide. ∎

This strengthens your proposal. The module is G·{w₀, w₁}, where G is generated by the Veech groups of all the torus covers X_r, not just Γ₀.

## L2. rank(M) ≥ 2, unconditionally

**Proof.**

1. p(V) contains Re ω₀ and Im ω₀ (the tautological plane), and also Re δ and Im δ (by L1).
2. Suppose a·Re ω₀ + b·Im ω₀ ∈ {Re δ, Im δ}. δ vanishes on horizontal cores, where Im ω₀ = 0 and Re ω₀ is a nonzero circumference, so a = 0. δ vanishes on vertical cores, so b = 0. Hence Re δ = Im δ = 0, contradicting δ ≠ 0 on H₁.
3. Therefore dim p(V) ≥ 3. By Avila–Eskin–Möller, p(V) is symplectic, so dim p(V) ≥ 4. ∎

This makes the Apisa–Wright rank-≥2 marked-point classification applicable without your conditional fold-compactness. Please check the exact hypotheses of that theorem against the published version before relying on it.

## L3. The halfturn imposes no rigidity on the marks

**Statement.** The halfturn J has derivative −I, which is central in GL₂. So M′ lies in the J-symmetric locus, and V′ ⊂ H¹₋(J).

The mark-only directions in H¹₋(J) are the vectors Σ v_s e_s with v_{Js} = −v_s. Since J fixes no mark lift, each J-orbit {s, Js} contributes one free complex parameter. Compare a J-fixed mark lift, where v_s = −v_s would force v_s = 0, i.e. periodicity.

The rotation ρ by π/2 does not survive in M: its derivative is not central. ρ also fixes no mark lift.

**Consequence.** Any periodicity of S must come from a hidden structure. Conditionally on Apisa–Wright, this would be a persistent half-translation map φ: X → (Q, q), not the J-quotient, with an S-lift being a simple critical point over a pole of q. Because the condition is closed, φ would persist on all of Ī, including X₀ and every X_r. Excluding this is exactly the "proper covering locus" case. Nothing in the pocket data excludes it locally (see the obstruction section).

## L4. Twist formula

**Statement.** Let T ∈ Γ_r′ be an affine multitwist in direction θ. Its cylinders C_i are taken relative to Σ only (marks ignored), with twist numbers k_i, and the power is chosen so that T fixes m. Let σ′_C ∈ H¹(X, Σ∪m) be the class of the closed 1-form dh_C/h_C supported on C, so that forget(σ′_C) = PD[C]. Then

  (T − 1)·u₁′ = −Σᵢ kᵢ · δ(Cᵢ) · σ′_{Cᵢ}.

**Proof.** For any path π between points of Σ∪m, T⁻¹π − π is the cycle Σᵢ kᵢ · σ′_{Cᵢ}(π) · [Cᵢ]. If π ends at a mark inside C_i, the last crossing counts with fractional weight equal to the relative height θ_s; the product k_i θ_s is an integer because T fixes s. Evaluating u₁ on this cycle gives the formula. ∎

This agrees with your multitwist increment formula. For a mark s and any path π_s from a corner to s, σ′_C(π_s) is the integer crossing count plus the fractional height θ_s(C).

## L5. No single multitwist detects freeness

**Statement.** (T − 1)u₁′ is a nonzero pure-mark vector for no multitwist T, in any direction and at any rational r.

**Proof.**

1. A pure-mark vector must satisfy forget((T − 1)u₁′) = −Σ kᵢ δ(Cᵢ) PD[Cᵢ] = 0.
2. Each C_i has a cross-curve π_i ∈ H₁(X, Σ) running from a Σ-point on its bottom boundary to one on its top. These satisfy π_i · C_j = δ_ij.
3. Hence the classes PD[C_i] are linearly independent. Since k_i ≠ 0, this forces δ(C_i) = 0 for every i.
4. Then (T − 1)u₁′ = 0 by L4. ∎

**Corollary.** Your seed-increment formula, applied one direction at a time, can never by itself produce a free-mark direction.

## L6. Exact criterion for G-detected freeness

**Setup.** Take finitely many multitwists T_j, in directions θ_j, at rational parameters r_j, with coefficients a_j. For each direction set

  Ξ_θ := Σ_{C ∈ θ} b_C · δ(C) · PD[C], with b_C := a_j·k_C.

The combination ξ := Σ_j a_j (T_j − 1)u₁′ is pure-mark exactly when Σ_θ Ξ_θ = 0 in H¹(X, Σ). In that case its s-component is

  ξ(π_s) = −Σ_C b_C · δ(C) · σ′_C(π_s).

This value is independent of the choice of π_s, precisely because Σ_θ Ξ_θ = 0. Any nonzero value proves that S is free in M′, by L1(b).

**Necessary condition for this route.** Some cylinder C with b_C ≠ 0 must satisfy δ(C) ≠ 0. By your Π-passage rule, this means its core enters and exits the pocket square Π through adjacent sides, or through opposite sides in different halves.

## L7. Parity rigidity at the base X₀

**Statement.** On X₀, fix a primitive direction (p, q) with p + q odd. Every S-lift s is joined, by a saddle connection rel Σ of holonomy (p, q), to a C-corner z_θ(s).

**Proof.**

1. The straight segment from s of holonomy (p, q) contains no interior lattice points, and every corner is a lattice point. So it reaches its endpoint without meeting any corner.
2. Fold the segment into P. Every reflection used is in a line x = c, y = c, or y = x + c with c an integer, so each preserves ℤ².
3. The parity classes behave as follows under these reflections:
   - C stays C;
   - {A, B} maps into {A, B}.
4. The endpoint before folding is s + (p, q), which is of type C because p + q is odd. So the folded endpoint is a C-type integer point of P.
5. By hypothesis every non-corner integer point of P is A. So the endpoint is a corner. ∎

**Consequences.**

- In such a direction, s always lies on a cylinder boundary, so θ_s ∈ {0, 1}.
- The derivative of T fixes (p, q), and the chosen power fixes Σ∪m, so T fixes the saddle connection [z_θ(s) → s] pointwise. Hence T acts on the class of s exactly as on the corner z_θ(s).
- If only such directions are used, L6 reduces to

  ξ(π_s) = Σ_θ Ξ_θ([z_* → z_θ(s)]), for any fixed corner z_*.

  Freeness at X₀ therefore requires that the corner which s imitates changes with the direction, in a way δ can detect.
- The argument fails in odd–odd directions, where the endpoint can be of type A.
- It also fails at rational r ∉ ℤ. There (1,1) becomes an interior B-type point on the portal, so non-corner B/C points exist. These parameters are the natural place to look, and L1(b) makes them available.

## L8. Every event occurs at a rational parameter

**Statement.** Fix a corner c and the relative classes of the paths [S → c] and [c → T]. The event condition hol[S → c] = hol[c → T] reads e₀ + t·e₁ = 0, with e₀, e₁ ∈ ℤ[i] by Gauss–Manin.

- A nonzero vector slide defect means exactly e₁ ≠ 0.
- In that case the event can occur only at t* = −e₀/e₁ ∈ ℚ, which requires e₀ ∈ ℝ·e₁.
- Then X_{t*} is a torus cover.

The proof is immediate from the affine dependence of periods on t. ∎

## The obstruction, stated sharply

1. **Locality.** The pocket determines only δ, which is local: it is supported on passages through Π. The mark data σ′_C(π_s), equivalently the corners z_θ(s), are global: they depend on where S sits relative to the whole surface.

   The G-criterion pairs these two pieces of data bilinearly. So no argument that uses only the leaf pocket can force a free direction. One needs a global input: two directions, at some r_j, with Π-crossing cylinders whose imitated corners for s differ in a way detected by Σ Ξ_θ.

2. **One-sidedness.** L1 gives only lower bounds on V′. That is enough to prove freeness, but it can never exclude periodicity by itself. Excluding the covering-locus case needs the conditional Apisa–Wright step (L3) together with an argument ruling out the hidden map φ on the torus covers X_r.

## The missing bridge to the event

Even granting that S is free in M′:

- That is a statement about the ambient orbit closure.
- The event (L8) lives at one rational point X_{t*} of the real slice.
- It also needs a path class with e₀ ∥ e₁, a geometric realisation, and no other corners on the trajectory.

Freeness does not produce such a class. Conversely, periodicity does not block events: periodic points are often midpoints.

What freeness *would* rule out is the trapping scenario. In that scenario, for every path class, e₁ and e₀ would be K-linear functions of the corner periods: e₁ = Λ(w₁) and e₀ = Λ(w₀). That is the form your covering-type obstructions take on the slice.

A real bridge needs a mechanism that turns a nonzero s-component ξ(π_s) at some X_r into a path class whose defect e₁ is nonzero and parallel to e₀ at a rational t*.
