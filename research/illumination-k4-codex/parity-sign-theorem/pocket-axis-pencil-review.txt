# Review of §28: axis-pencil obstruction

**Verdict:** The argument is correct, conditional on two inherited premises (P1, P2 below) and two small precision fixes. The determinant is right, and the conclusion holds as you scoped it: the module generated by the axis pencil cannot contain a nonzero marked-only vector.

## Inherited premises

**P1. Pocket flux at every parameter.** For every parameter r in the chart, the complex value δ(γ) must be 0 for every cylinder core γ of the horizontal and vertical decompositions at r. This is only a dependency on §16, but two things need care:
- **"Generic" is too narrow.** You need the core of every cylinder in the decomposition at r. That includes thin cylinders or cores that might not cross the pocket square. If any cylinder can avoid the pocket, it needs its own argument that δ vanishes on its core.
- **Both parts must vanish.** You need x₁ and y₁ both zero on each core. The cylinder simply staying horizontal only gives y₁ = 0, which is not enough.

**P2. Quarter-turn symmetry along the slice.** R must be an actual symmetry of each slice surface, and it must vary continuously in t. Then R·C at t is the Gauss–Manin transport of R·C at 0, which gives period(R·C) = i·period(C) throughout. You state this. It is used only on the slice, not on the GL-orbit closure, which is fine.

## Precision fixes

**F1. Use pure multitwist powers.** "Label-fixing" should mean the power fixes every cone point, every marked point, every horizontal saddle connection, and every cylinder. The point is that the power must not permute cylinders or translate their boundaries. Then the twist acts on each cylinder as a power of a Dehn twist with boundaries fixed pointwise, and for every relative path:

  φ₊γ = γ + Σᵢ nᵢ·coreᵢ, with nᵢ ∈ ℤ.

- **Endpoints inside cylinders:** this is fine. A fixed interior mark at fractional height h has shear displacement equal to an integer multiple of the circumference, so it adds only integer winding.
- **Marks on boundary saddle connections:** these are fixed pointwise, so they add nothing.

**F2. Drop the dependence on a ∈ ℚ.** (28.1) puts a·y₀ and r·a·y₁ in W_ℚ only if a and r are rational. You can either:
- argue a ∈ ℚ (the periods lie in ℚ(i) at rational r, so cylinder moduli are rational), or
- replace W_ℚ by W_ℝ.

The second is cleaner. The determinant argument works over ℝ, so nothing is lost, and the restriction to rational r becomes irrelevant to the obstruction.

## Points checked and fine

**Point-push terms.** Twisting on one side of an interior mark versus the other differs by a small loop around the mark. That loop bounds a disk, so it is zero in H₁(S, Σ∪P). It is invisible in relative cohomology. It would only matter in H¹(S∖P), which is not the period coordinate space.

**Action convention.** The pullback φ\*ω(γ) = ω(φ₊γ) = D·ω(γ) gives φ\*ω_r = H_a·ω_r.
- Since δ vanishes on all cores, the twist formula gives φ\*δ = δ.
- φ\* is real (it comes from an integral action), so φ\*x₁ = x₁ and φ\*y₁ = y₁.
- Subtracting r·x₁ from the real part then gives (28.1) exactly. (28.2) follows the same way.
- The pushforward convention only replaces a with −a, so invariance is unaffected.
- W is finite-dimensional and each φ\* is invertible on it, so inverses and arbitrary products are covered. That includes mixing horizontal twists at r₁ with vertical twists at r₂.

**Gauss–Manin comparison.** One simply connected chart identifies the integral relative cohomology lattices for all r in it. Each φ_r\* is conjugated into the base, and the core classes at r are transported classes. This is valid provided no marks collide or leave the chart within the interval. A small interval still contains infinitely many rational r.

**Determinant.** The matrix is block lower-triangular (the upper-right 2×2 block is zero):

  det = det[[−2,0],[0,−2]] · det[[0,2],[−2,0]] = 4 · 4 = 16.

This is independent of p and q. It is also robust to the rotation sign: using −i instead of i gives −16. The evaluation matrix is real and nonsingular, so no complex combination of x₀, y₀, x₁, y₁ vanishes on A, B, C, R·C. Therefore W_ℂ meets the kernel of H¹(S, Σ∪P) → H¹(S) only in 0.

**Scope.** The module generated by ω₀ and δ lies in W_ℂ, so it contains no nonzero marked-only vector. That is exactly the claimed obstruction. Your disclaimers are accurate: the argument says nothing about V′ versus W, about non-axis affine elements, about periodicity of marks, or about a supplied event pairing with δ.

## Suggested edits to §28

- Replace "every generic horizontal/vertical closed core" with "the core of every cylinder in the horizontal and vertical decompositions at every parameter in the chart." Add a citation or argument for any cylinder that avoids the pocket.
- Define "label-fixing power" as a pure multitwist power, as in F1.
- Work with W_ℝ, or add one line justifying a ∈ ℚ.
- Add one sentence noting that point-push loops are zero in H₁(S, Σ∪P).
