## 2. Missing optical assumption (needs a correction)

The e=0 positive path meets C₀ only as a graze through a 225° reflex vertex. It never leaves the closed room and is locally straight. Under a "pass-through at reflex grazes" convention, that path is a valid connection, and **e=0 would not be dark.** The parity argument therefore needs an explicit rule: any trajectory that meets any vertex is absorbed, including tangential grazes of reflex corners. This is Tokarsky's convention. K is a head-on vertex hit, so it is dark under any convention.

State this rule in the claim. The darkness at e=0 depends on it, and so does the statement that e=0 is a jump.

## 3. Continuity argument

The argument is sound. The open conditions are strict bounces, strict segment clearances, and nonparallel directions. They persist because S′ and T′ move continuously. The single non-strict contact is handled locally:

- Near C₀, only walls 4 and 5 are within reach.
- Near K, only walls 15 and 16 are within reach.

One sentence is missing: name these as the only walls near the limiting vertex. The other inequalities are then uniformly strict on a neighbourhood. The hand proof gives an inexplicit radius. Only the checker gives 1/1000.

## 4. General q > 2p rule

The rule is correct as stated. I re-derived e(q−2p+2e)/(p−e). Two corrections:

- **The symmetric hypothesis is unnecessary.** Take source c − (p₁,q₁) and target c + (p₂,q₂) with q₁/p₁ = q₂/p₂. The clearance is 2e(q₁ − p₁ − p₂ + 2e)/(p₁ + p₂ − 2e). The condition is **q₁ > p₁ + p₂**, and q > 2p is the case p₁ = p₂.
- **The rule covers one sign only.** It also relies on the specific deformation: corner moving (0,2e), top vertex moving (−e,e), and the moving pair H, D used exactly once, first in the suffix, with no moving walls in the prefix. For e<0 the same chord hits wall4. The negative side uses a different mechanism, a 270° corner turned into a reflection, for which there is no general rule.

## 6. Contribution

This is a rigorous two-sided opening of one dark parameter in one one-parameter family. It adds a conditional local lemma for one sign. For a general sign theorem it does not provide:

- existence of a return itinerary meeting q₁ > p₁ + p₂ for an arbitrary ear;
- any general mechanism for the opposite sign;
- handling of limiting paths through several vertices;
- multi-parameter or non-ear perturbations.

Since both signs open here, the family shows no sign selection. That is evidence about what a sign theorem must exclude, not progress toward proving one. The write-up's own scope statement is accurate.
