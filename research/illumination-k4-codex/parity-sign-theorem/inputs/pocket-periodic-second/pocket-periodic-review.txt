## Question A: is the exact-degree fold locus closed?

### Definition

Let H\* be the smooth pointed stratum, where the mark is regular. For fixed even D, let L_D be the set of (X′, x′) in H\* such that there exist:
- a halftranslation cover f : X′ → Q of degree exactly D, meaning f\*q = ω²,
- with f(x′+u) = f(x′−u) for all small u.

No minimality is imposed on f.

### Claim: L_D is closed in H\*

Take (X_n, x_n) → (X, x) in H\*, with covers f_n : X_n → Q_n.

**Step 1. Finitely many target strata.** This is proven.
- Zeros of Q_n of order k ≥ 1 only have preimages with cone angle e(k+2)π ≥ 3π. These are zeros of X_n.
- So the number and orders of the zeros of Q_n are bounded by the stratum of X and by D.
- Poles are then bounded by the relation Σk = 4g_Q − 4, and g_Q ≤ g_X.
- Pass to a subsequence with a fixed target stratum.
- area(Q_n) = area(X_n)/D, which converges.

**Step 2. Targets do not degenerate.** This is the crux. I believe it is proven, and it is the step to audit.

Let γ be a saddle connection on Q_n of length ε.
- Preimages of zeros of Q_n are zeros of X_n.
- A preimage of a pole is either a zero (e ≥ 4) or a regular point with e = 2.
- At a regular point with e = 2, exactly two lifts of γ meet, and they meet in opposite directions. So they continue as one straight line.
- f_n^{-1}(γ) is therefore a union of D lifts that chain into straight trajectories.
- Each trajectory is either a closed geodesic or a saddle connection on X_n, of length ≤ Dε.

Since X_n → X in the unpointed stratum, the shortest saddle connection or cylinder circumference on X_n is bounded below by some δ > 0. Hence every saddle connection on Q_n has length ≥ δ/D.

By Masur compactness for quadratic differentials, a subsequence Q_n → Q in the same stratum. This is exactly where a degree jump would have to come from, and it is excluded.

Branch values that are regular points of Q may collide with each other or with poles. That is harmless here, because nothing below marks them.

**Step 3. Equicontinuity.** This is proven, citing standard period-coordinate facts.
- Choose piecewise-affine maps φ_n : X → X_n with φ_n(x) = x_n, and ψ_n : Q_n → Q, with derivatives tending to I uniformly in charts. These come from shared triangulations.
- Each f_n is a local isometry, so it is 1-Lipschitz for the flat path metrics.
- So g_n = ψ_n ∘ f_n ∘ φ_n is (1+o(1))-Lipschitz.
- Arzelà–Ascoli gives a subsequence g_n → f uniformly.
- f is surjective, being a uniform limit of surjections.

**Step 4. f is holomorphic of degree D.**
- g_n is K_n-quasiregular with K_n → 1. Uniform limits of such maps are 1-quasiregular, i.e. holomorphic (Reshetnyak / Lehto–Virtanen). f is nonconstant.
- For large n, g_n is uniformly close to f, hence homotopic to it. So deg f = deg f_n = D.
- **This is why degree cannot jump** once Step 2 holds.

**Step 5. f\*q = ω_X².**
- Pick a regular point a with f(a) regular, and a small disk U around a.
- For large n, f_n is u ↦ ±u + c_n on φ_n(U). The sign is constant because U is connected and its image lies in a regular disk.
- The limit on U is therefore z ↦ ±z + c, so f\*q = ω² on U.
- By the identity theorem, f\*q = ω² on all of X.

**Step 6. The fold persists, with a uniform radius.**
- Let R_n = dist(x_n, Z(X_n)). Then u ↦ f_n(exp(u)) and u ↦ f_n(exp(−u)) are holomorphic on B(0, R_n).
- They agree near 0, so by the identity theorem they agree on all of B(0, R_n).
- R_n → R = dist(x, Z(X)) > 0.
- Pass to the limit using the uniform convergence of φ_n⁻¹ ∘ exp_{x_n} to exp_x. This gives f(x+u) = f(x−u) for |u| < R/2.

**Why this sidesteps Section 20's listed gaps.** "Uniform disk, bounded exceptional set, transitivity of the limit relation, extension across zeros" only arise if one takes limits of covering relations. With maps:
- the uniform disk is Step 6;
- the exceptional set and extension across zeros are handled by holomorphy (Step 4);
- transitivity is automatic.

The (2d+1)-point nonmerging trick is locally valid, but it does not address the actual danger, which is target degeneration (Step 2).

### Finite fiber over X, with D = 2d and d = deg π

This step was already essentially correct in Section 20.
- A fold forces a pole, so q_Q is not a square and Q̂ is connected.
- The fiber product with X splits because f\*q = ω_X². So f lifts to a translation cover X → Q̂ of degree d.
- Every translation cover factors as X → Z → Y. With degree equal to d, this forces Q̂ ≅ Y.
- Y has trivial translation-automorphism group; otherwise Y/G would be a smaller quotient. So Y has at most one negating involution, and τ = j.
- Hence f ≅ q ∘ π, and its regular folds are exactly the regular points of π⁻¹(Fix j).

### Dimension step

This is proven, with standard inputs.
- L_{2d} is closed and invariant under GL₂R, since −I is central.
- So the orbit closure N of (X, x), which is affine by EMM with marked points, satisfies N ⊂ L_{2d}.
- dim_ℂ N ∈ {dim M, dim M + 1}.
- If dim N = dim M + 1, then N is open and closed in the connected full pointed space over M, so N is all of it. Its fiber over X is then X minus its zeros, which is infinite. This contradicts the finite fiber.
- So x is periodic.

### Fallback via degree ≤ D and de Franchis

This is not needed, but it also works.
- L_{≤2d} is closed, being a finite union of closed sets.
- Its fiber over X is finite:
  - intermediate covers X → Z → Y have g_Z ≥ 2, so there are finitely many Z and finitely many maps (de Franchis–Severi);
  - each Z has a finite automorphism group, so finitely many negating involutions;
  - each involution has finitely many fixed points.
- So exact-degree closedness is not load-bearing.

### Status of Q-A

- **Proven, given the cited standard results:** Steps 1, 3, 4, 5, 6 and the dimension step.
- **Needs a careful written audit before Section 20 is promoted:** Step 2, specifically the chaining of lifts at regular double points over poles, and that closed geodesics on X_n are uniformly long.

Also worth checking: Apisa–Wright or Möller may already state "regular points of π⁻¹(Fix J) are periodic." If so, cite it rather than reprove it.

## Question B: Section 19 with no compactness input

I checked every step.

**r, j and uniqueness.** All correct.
- π∘ρ is a translation cover onto (Y, −iω_Y), so minimality gives r with r\*ω_Y = iω_Y.
- From r⁴π = πρ⁴ = π and surjectivity of π, r⁴ = 1. The derivative i gives order exactly 4.
- Uniqueness of the negating involution follows from Aut_trans(Y) being trivial.

**Q_min = Y/j.** Section 19 asserts this; it needs a one-line proof or a citation of Remark 3.4.
- X → Y/j factors through Q_min, so deg(X → Q_min) ≤ 2d.
- X → Q_min lifts to a translation cover X → Q̂_min of degree at most d, so deg(X → Q_min) ≥ ... let me state it precisely: since X → Y/j → Q_min, we have deg(X → Q_min) ≥ ... no, Y/j → Q_min only lowers the target, so deg(X → Q_min) ≥ deg(X → Y/j) = 2d.
- Combining, the degree is exactly 2d, so Y/j → Q_min has degree 1 and they are equal.
- If Q_min were a translation surface, the pullback to Y/j would be a square, contradicting the connectedness of Y.

Without this, the step "a q-fiber has at most 2 points" is unjustified.

**Fiber argument.** Correct.
- r·π(s₀) ∈ {π(s₀), jπ(s₀)} = {π(s₀), r²π(s₀)}. Either case forces r to fix π(s₀).
- So all 16 lifts go to a single regular, unramified point p, and deg π ≥ 16.

**Theorem 3.6 hypotheses.** You must verify that each deformed surface X is *not a torus cover*. This is the same condition as g_Y ≥ 2, which Section 19 also uses. "Non-torus" must mean exactly that.

**Darkness implies all-to-all finitely blocked.** This holds trivially: the blocking set is empty, then apply the deck symmetry.

**Zero orders.** Correct.
- A corner of type m has gcd(m,4) lifts, each of order m/gcd(m,4) − 1.
- This gives orders 0, 0, 2, 4, 2, 6 for m = 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 7. All even.
- A target zero of odd order would force an odd source order, since ord_x + 1 = e(ord_y + 1).

**Genus.**
- g_X = 1 + n₃ + 2n₅ + 2n₆ + 3n₇.
- If g_Y ≥ 3, then 2g_X − 2 ≥ 16·4 = 64, i.e. g_X ≥ 33. So g_X ≤ 32 forces g_Y = 2, hence Y ∈ H(2).
- **Check that `pocket-planar` verifies g_X ≤ 32 per pattern, not just inequality (10).** Patterns with g_X ≥ 33 are not covered by Section 19.

**Fix(r).** Correct, and independent of X/ρ.
- The hyperelliptic involution acts as −1 on all 1-forms, so it equals j.
- Riemann–Hurwitz for Y → Y/r, with a points of stabilizer order 4 and 6 − a points of stabilizer order 2:
  2 = 8g′ − 8 + 3a + (6 − a), which gives g′ = 0 and a = 2.
- So Fix(r) = {z, p}: the unique zero (fixed by any automorphism) and p.
- The hypothesis "X/ρ is a sphere" is not needed.

**Corners.**
- m = 1, 5, 7 have a single D₄-fixed lift, so it is ρ-fixed and maps into {z, p}.
  - Mapping to z would need m = 3e, which is impossible.
  - So each maps to p with e = m. **(8) is correct.**
- m = 2 and m = 6 lifts are swapped by ρ and fixed by ρ². They land in Fix(j) but not necessarily at p, so omitting them is correct.
- m = 3 corners go either to p with e = 3 or to z with e = 1.
  - Tracking them does not sharpen anything: with c₃ of them over p, the condition becomes 16 + n₁ + 5n₅ + 7n₇ + 4c₃ ≤ n₃ + 2n₆, which is weakest at c₃ = 0. That is (10) exactly.

**Over z.** Correct.
- A source point over z needs ord_x + 1 = 3e with ord_x + 1 ∈ {1, 3, 5, 7}.
- So only order-2 zeros with e = 1 lie over z. **(9) is correct.**

**Verdict on Q-B.** Section 19 is a valid fixed-surface proof once three things are written in: Q_min = Y/j, the torus-cover hypothesis, and the per-pattern genus check.

It excludes only the case where all 16 lifts are nonperiodic, on patterns with g_X ≤ 32 that satisfy (10). It says nothing about dark periodic pairs, illumination, or the existence of the first-order event. The 33/34 count is arithmetic about a necessary condition, as the section already says.
