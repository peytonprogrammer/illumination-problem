# Does darkness preserve the covering structure?

[in progress] Candidate route. The central preservation statement is unproved.

## What can now be proved conditionally

The wall-reflection argument in [holonomy-rigidity.md](holonomy-rigidity.md) proves: if all absolute loop displacements change by one common linear map, the fixed D4 reflection symmetry forces that map to be scalar. Finite-group averaging then shows that every wall moves by the same translation and scale. Fixed relative profiles measured from a reference corner make the marked points follow as well. The reference corner's profile must also be retained if profiles are instead described on a covering torus. This is independent of optical event enumeration.

Thus a structural preservation theorem could establish local isolation without first constructing a signed positive balance of corner-event gradients. That is an alternative to the first-order sign theorem, not a proof of it.

## What the primary literature supplies

Apisa–Wright classify finitely blocked pairs on non-torus covers using periodic points and minimal half-translation quotients (Theorem 3.6). Their Proposition 3.11 characterizes rational polygons whose unfoldings cover tori as Gaussian, Eisenstein, or rectangle-tiled. Its proof relates relative and absolute periods through rotational symmetry. These results do not say that a moving dark pair preserves a fixed torus-cover diagram. [Primary paper](https://arxiv.org/html/1708.03411v3).

Lelièvre–Monteil–Weiss characterize torus covers by blocking of all pairs, or an open set of pairs, on a fixed surface. They explicitly describe non-torus surfaces with infinitely many dark pairs. One dark pair is therefore insufficient to invoke their torus-cover characterization. Their fixed-surface illumination theorem also does not classify changes of room shape. [Theorem 1, Theorem 2 and introduction](https://msp.org/gt/2016/20-3/gt-v20-n3-p11-s.pdf).

These are the relevant primary results checked in this pass. Neither provides the missing preservation theorem.

## Necessary distinctions

1. A single tracked dark pair is weaker than an open set of finitely blocked pairs on one surface.
2. Regular lifts of convex polygon corners must be retained as marked forbidden points. They cannot silently be replaced by zeros of the differential.
3. A continuously identified covering diagram and fixed marked rational profiles are stronger data than the existence of an unspecified cover for each shape.
4. Actual isolation of dark configurations is weaker than strict first-order separation by one-corner event gradients.
5. A fixed-polygon finiteness theorem for dark pairs does not establish isolation while the polygon changes. Finiteness alone also does not imply a nondegenerate first-order certificate.

## The concrete missing theorem

For a neighborhood of a chosen parity room, show that every dark marked configuration has the same continuously marked torus-cover period diagram and the same rational relative profiles for the reference corner and both marks, up to one common linear map. The exact rigidity theorem would then force similarity.

No proof currently rules out nearby dark configurations on non-torus covers, or on different covering diagrams. The periodic-point and quotient alternatives in Apisa–Wright must be handled, rather than omitted.

## Consequence for research priorities

The period theorem is a useful endpoint for a classification argument. It is not a replacement name for the missing sign argument. The next theoretical work must either derive preservation from darkness, or identify a geometric family that defeats it. More affine rank checks cannot decide that issue.

## Why arbitrary cover preservation is not enough

Even at fixed wall count, nonsimilar Gaussian rooms can converge to a Gaussian room. For n=1,2,... take the convex pentagon

    (0,0), (3+1/n,0), (3+1/n,2), (1,2), (0,1).

Its five wall directions and angles are fixed. All coordinates are rational, so it is Gaussian after a grid refinement. The polygons are nonsimilar and converge to the analogous pentagon with width3. They have no dark pair: they are convex. Their role is to show that the existence of some torus cover does not isolate a room shape.

On the other hand, normalized Gaussian wall coordinates lie in Q², so any connected continuous family of normalized Gaussian shapes is constant. This path-rigidity observation does not exclude sequences such as the one above. It cannot be promoted to local isolation without an additional local-structure or bounded-diagram argument.

## A scale-invariant degree, rather than raw tile count

Let Lambda be the full lattice generated by absolute periods of a torus-cover unfolding X. The canonical map to R²/Lambda has degree

    d=Area(X)/covol(Lambda).

This follows by integrating area over the covering; branch points have area zero. The quantity is an integer and is unchanged by common linear changes of coordinates, since numerator and denominator both acquire the factor |det A|. In particular, dilation does not change d.

The finite return generators in holonomy-rigidity.md compute Lambda. For integral generators in R², its covolume is the gcd of their two-by-two determinants; clear a common denominator first in the rational case.

This intrinsic degree avoids the earlier raw-tile-count scaling flaw. It does NOT give a wall-count bound: neither a bound d<=f(W) for dark rooms nor preservation of d under a dark limiting sequence has been proved. Any proposed finite reduction using degree must supply that missing bound or local rigidity of the covering diagram.
