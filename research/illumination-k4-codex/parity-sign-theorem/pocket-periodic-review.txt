# Audit: §19 fixed-surface obstruction, §20.1 fold-persistence lemma, periodicity corollary

**Verdict.**

- **§20.1 lemma:** correct, given the standard inputs listed in section E.
- **§19:** correct, given the imported Apisa–Wright inputs.
- **Corollary:** the step "every lift of a dark pair is periodic" has one gap. It needs a form of the blocked-pair theorem that covers the mixed case, where some lifts are periodic and others are not (see C).
- **Scope:** none of this touches the open universal S/T one-corner π-sector event.

## A. §20.1 lemma (bounded-degree fold persistence)

**Step 1: finitely many target strata. Correct.**

- The pullback relation 2a+2 = e(k+2) is right.
- If k ≥ 1, then a ≥ 1. So each target zero has only source zeros above it. That bounds the number and orders of target zeros.
- If k = 0, then a = e−1. So regular branch values are images of source zeros, and there are boundedly many.
- Riemann–Hurwitz gives genus(Q_n) ≤ genus(X_n). Then #poles = Σk₊ − 4g + 4 is bounded.
- At a fold, a = 0 and e even. So e = 2 and k = −1. The target therefore has a pole and is not a global square.
- Area(Q_n) = area(X_n)/D.

**Step 2: a short target saddle connection lifts to a short source saddle connection or closed geodesic. Correct.** I checked each case.

- **Interior points of γ.** These are regular target points.
  - An unramified preimage there is a regular source point of valence 2. The two edges are collinear.
  - A ramified preimage has a = e−1 > 0, so it is a source zero, and the chain is cut there.
  - This covers regular branch values in the interior of γ.
- **Endpoint at a target zero.** Every preimage is a source zero, so the chain stops.
- **Endpoint at a simple pole.** Here e = 2a+2.
  - If a > 0, the chain stops at a zero.
  - If a = 0, the two preimage rays of the radial segment are opposite in the source flat chart, since angle π doubles to 2π. The chain continues straight.
- **No backtracking.** At every valence-2 vertex the two directions are opposite.
- **Pole-to-pole and loop cases.** A pole→pole connection gives a zig-zag lift, and a loop gives a chain that may close. Either way the result is a straight trajectory: a saddle connection, or a closed regular geodesic, possibly a multiple traversal. Its length is at most Dε.
- **Source systole bound.** A closed regular geodesic has length at least the circumference of its maximal cylinder. That circumference is at least the length of any boundary saddle connection. A genus ≥ 2 surface has zeros, so the boundary is nonempty.
- **δ along the sequence.** The source systole is bounded below along a convergent sequence. So (12) holds.
- **Target compactness.** Masur's compactness criterion for a quadratic stratum with simple poles is the right import. It must be applied with saddle connections that include pole endpoints and loops, and the lift argument covers exactly those.

**Step 3: limiting the maps. Correct.**

- The maps f_n are 1-Lipschitz, being local isometries off a finite set.
- Equicontinuity, Arzelà–Ascoli, and the surjectivity argument are all fine.
- Degree is preserved because maps that are uniformly closer than the injectivity radius are homotopic, and holomorphic degree equals topological degree.
- The local ±z+c argument and identity continuation are fine.

The quasiregular normal-family import can be removed. An elementary route:

1. The maps g_n have uniform path lifting: geodesics lift with the same length, and φ_n, ψ_n are near-isometric.
2. Hence each point of f⁻¹(p) is a limit of points of g_n⁻¹(p). So |f⁻¹(p)| ≤ D, and f⁻¹(Sing Q) is finite.
3. Off that finite set, together with the source zeros, f is locally ±z+c.
4. f is continuous, so the singularities are removable. Thus f is holomorphic.

**Step 4: the marked fold persists. Correct.**

- The identity theorem applies to holomorphic maps between Riemann surfaces on a connected disk of fixed radius.
- φ_n(x) = x_n, because x is a vertex of the triangulation.
- φ_n(x+u) = x_n + u + o(1), and f_n is 1-Lipschitz. So the limit gives f(x+u) = f(x−u) exactly.
- Evenness at a regular point forces e = 2. So x is a genuine fold over a simple pole.

## B. Fixed-fiber and dimension checks (done independently)

**The fiber over X is finite.**

1. Let f be any degree-2d fold cover of X. Its target has a pole, so the orientation double cover Q̂ is connected.
2. f*√q = ±ω selects a branch, so f lifts to X→Q̂. This lift is a translation cover of degree d.
3. X covers no torus, so Q̂ is not a torus. Möller's factorization then gives Q̂→Y of degree 1, so Q̂ = Y.
4. The negating involution is unique. If two existed, their composition would be a nontrivial translation automorphism, contradicting minimality. So the deck involution is j, and f = q∘π.
5. For regular x, π is unramified at x. Then f folds at x exactly when π(x) ∈ Fix j.

**The locus is GL⁺(2,R)-invariant**, because −I is central and the fold relation is preserved.

**Dimension.** Let N be the orbit closure of (X,x).

- N ⊂ F ∩ p⁻¹(M), which is closed by the lemma.
- By Eskin–Mirzakhani–Mohammadi, N is a linear immersed submanifold.
- Its projection is dense in M, so dim N ≥ dim M.
- Suppose dim N = dim M + 1. Then some local sheet of N is open in the corresponding sheet of p⁻¹(M). It would contain (X, x+u) for all small u, giving an infinite fiber. Contradiction.
- So dim N = dim M, which is periodicity in the Apisa–Wright sense. This local argument is enough; the "full free-point marking" phrasing is stronger than needed.

## C. §19 checks, and the precise gap in the corollary

**Arithmetic and structure: correct.**

- Source zero orders are m/gcd(m,4) − 1, giving 0, 0, 2, 4, 2, 6 for m = 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 7.
- Lift counts are gcd(m,4). So m = 1, 5, 7 each have one ρ-fixed lift, and m = 6 has two order-2 zeros.
- Target zeros must have even order.
- With genus(X) ≤ 32 and deg π ≥ 16: 62 ≥ 16(2g_Y − 2), so g_Y = 2. Even order then forces H(2).
- j is the hyperelliptic involution.
- Riemann–Hurwitz for Y→Y/⟨r⟩: 2 = 4(2g′−2) + 3a + (6−a). This gives a = 2 − 4g′, hence a = 2.
- Corners with m = 1, 5, 7 cannot map to the zero, since 3 does not divide 1, 5, or 7. So they lie over p.
- Over the zero, a+1 = 3e forces a = 2 and e = 1. This gives (9).
- I did not recheck the frozen genus values (27–29) or the counts for the 34 patterns.

**Gap.** §19 assumes *none* of the 16 lifts is periodic. Combined with the lemma, it only yields "some lift is periodic". Periodicity is preserved by the D4 action, including the reflections: X is reflection-symmetric, so M is too, and conjugation preserves orbit-closure dimension. So the strongest conclusion is "all S lifts are periodic, or all T lifts are periodic". It does not follow that all 16 are periodic.

To get "every lift is periodic", the imported Apisa–Wright blocked-pair theorem must state: if x, y are finitely blocked on a non-torus-cover surface and are *not both* periodic, then qπ(x) = qπ(y). With that form, the §19 argument runs unchanged for a non-periodic T lift and lands it in π⁻¹(Fix j), which is periodic by the lemma. That contradicts the assumption. Verify the exact hypothesis in the theorem statement. If it requires both points to be non-periodic, the mixed case stays open.

## D. Physical darkness versus empty blocking

- On X, lifts of convex corners with m = 1 (45°) and m = 2 (90°) are regular points, with cone angle 2π.
- A billiard path through any corner is undefined. So physical darkness of S, T means: every segment s_g→t_h meets the zeros or B = {regular corner lifts}.
- Since zeros terminate trajectories, this is *finite blocking with a nonempty finite B*. It is not non-illumination on X, which would mean B = ∅.
- The implication runs one way: physical darkness ⇒ finite blocking. The converse fails, but the corollary only uses the forward direction.
- All 8×8 pairs are blocked. Unfolding handles one fixed s-lift, and the D4 action carries B to itself.
- Consequently the cited theorem must be the *finite-blocking* classification. An illumination-only (B = ∅) result would not cover physical darkness.

## E. Standard inputs required

1. Möller, Theorem 2.6: the minimal translation quotient, with factorization and equivariance. Its uniqueness gives the uniqueness of j.
2. Apisa–Wright, §3: the quotient Y/j = Q_min (Lemma 3.3). Also the finite-blocking pair classification, in the "not both periodic" form for the full corollary.
3. Masur's compactness criterion for quadratic strata with simple poles, with saddle connections that include poles.
4. Eskin–Mirzakhani–Mohammadi linearity of orbit closures, or Apisa–Wright Lemma 2.1, for the dimension dichotomy.
5. Comparison maps from persistent triangulations. This is the standard meaning of convergence in period coordinates.
6. Optional: quasiregular normal families. The elementary route in A avoids it.

## Scope

Granting C, the corollary says only this: a dark physical interior pair on a non-torus-cover D4 unfolding has all lifts M-periodic. It does not classify the periodic pairs. It does not show they illuminate. It does not construct the one-corner midpoint event. The universal actual S/T one-corner π-sector event remains **OPEN**.
