1. Exact hypotheses
One simple orthogonal parity base B has w maximal walls, r reflex corners,
and two distinct smooth boundary lattice marks a,b. All noncorner lattice
points of B lie in a+2Z^2. Finitely many whole congruent copies g_i(B) have
pairwise disjoint interiors; their union Q is a strictly simple polygonal
disk. The inverse-copy fold g_i^{-1} agrees pointwise wherever pieces meet.
An actual interior preimage of each of the two distinct base marks is required.
Copy maps are specified isometries, not arbitrary identifications allowed
only modulo a symmetry of B. No slits, holes, overlaps or pinches are allowed.

The corner-count identities themselves apply more generally to orthogonal
bases without the parity assumption. Parity and marked interiority enter
only the last numerical inequality.

2. Independent check of full-wall rigidity
At a positive-length contact segment I, equality g_i^{-1}(x)=g_j^{-1}(x)
for x in I implies h(x)=x for h=g_j composed with g_i^{-1}. A plane isometry
fixing two distinct points has identity or reflection as its linear part
relative to their line. Identity makes the whole copies identical, contrary
to disjoint interiors. Therefore h is reflection across that line.
It follows that the entire maximal wall W of g_i(B) containing I is mapped
to itself, as a maximal wall of g_j(B). Both entire polygons are reflected
images, so ALL of W, not merely I, is a contact. This verifies the direction
of the composition and does not assume a common placement grid.
Thus partial seams cannot occur with this pointwise whole-copy fold.
A shifted reflected copy can have partial geometric contact, but then its
inverse fold disagrees along the seam. The prescribed half-unit control
checks precisely this failure; it does not exclude every other fold mechanism.

3. Independent local angle exhaustion
A270-degree copy corner cannot lie on a reflected seam endpoint: reflection
of its270-degree occupied sector in either boundary ray overlaps that sector
on an open set. No other copy can fill its missing90-degree sector without
making a positive-length contact along an incident ray; section2 then forces
the same impossible reflected seam. Two270-degree incidences cannot coincide,
since their interiors overlap locally. Thus every one of the m*r reflex
incidences survives as a distinct reflex boundary corner of Q.

At a convex copy corner, an incident smooth neighbor would produce a seam
whose endpoint is a corner for one copy but smooth for the other. This
contradicts the full-wall reflection established above. An interior neighbor
overlaps, and a reflex neighbor was just excluded. Hence every incident copy
there supplies one convex90-degree quadrant. One/two adjacent/three/four
quadrants produce respectively a convex/smooth/reflex/interior point.
Two opposite quadrants violate strict simplicity. These cases exhaust new
reflex corners. Therefore R(Q)=m*r+t3, where t3 counts three-copy convex
junctions. Since w=2r+4 and W=2R+4 for orthogonal disks,
 W=m*(w-4)+4+2*t3.
In particular a shared three-corner endpoint adds TWO walls; a four-corner
junction contributes zero new reflex corners but cannot erase old ones.

4. Independent cycle count
Let t_j count convex junctions with j incident copy corners. Every reflex
incidence already survives separately, so convex-incidence and boundary-turn
counts give
 m*(r+4)=t1+2*t2+3*t3+4*t4,
 t1-(m*r+t3)=4.
Subtracting yields t2+2*t3+2*t4=2m-2. A seam endpoint has respectively
one/two/four incident internal seam rays at a two/three/fourfold junction,
so 2E=t2+2*t3+4*t4. Thus E=m-1+t4.
Here E counts seam segments, not merely distinct adjacent copy pairs. Treat
the seam graph as a multigraph if necessary. It is connected: a strictly
simple polygonal disk has connected interior, which cannot connect clusters
solely through boundary point contacts. Its cycle rank is E-m+1=t4.
This supplies the needed cycle control without assuming a tree initially.

5. Independent parity-base lower bound
Translate a to0. Every unit square has exactly three vertices outside2Z^2.
Each occupied vertex outside2Z^2 must be a genuine corner, contributing one
square if convex and three if reflex. Hence 3A=c_o+3r_o<=c+3r=4r+4.
If w<10, orthogonal parity gives w=2r+4<=8, so r<=2 and A<=4.
The nine explicitly given polyominoes of size at most four are exhaustive:
four strips, L triomino, and square/T/L/S tetrominoes. The finite shape list
follows from connected unit-square adjacency: cycles require the four-cell
square; an acyclic degree-three vertex is T; otherwise a path gives strip,
L or S (two same-sense turns instead close the square).
The independent checker inspects precisely those supplied shapes, without

6. Why both interior marks require three copies
Every maximal base wall has integer length at most2: length>=3 would contain
two consecutive smooth integer points in different parity classes. Hence
one wall contains at most one smooth lattice point, in particular at most
one of a,b.
An image of a smooth base mark can become interior only along a seam
INTERIOR. It cannot be a seam endpoint, since section3 shows that every
seam endpoint is a convex corner in each participating copy. One copy cannot
interiorize a boundary mark. For two copies, t4=0, since four quadrants at
that kind of junction require four distinct copy corners and no copy can
place two of its corners at the same point. Equation E=m-1+t4 gives E=1.
That unique seam contains at most one base mark, so it cannot interiorize
preimages of both distinct marks. Therefore m>=3.
Consequently W=m(w-4)+4+2t3>=3*(10-4)+4=22.
No darkness premise is used: this is a geometric cost of the construction
and the two marked interior preimages.

distinct-endpoint class. This does not classify all ten-wall parity bases
