## 1. Why labels survive the two shears

Write permutations as functions, with AB meaning A after B. Lower-left square-corner equivalence is generated by

    kappa = u r u^-1 r^-1.

Walking counterclockwise around the corner crosses left, bottom, right, top edges and sends i to kappa(i). Labels must be constant on its cycles.

For T:(r,u)->(r,u r^-1),

    kappa_T = (u r^-1) r (r u^-1) r^-1 = kappa.

For L:(r,u)->(r u^-1,u),

    kappa_L = u (r u^-1) u^-1 (u r^-1) = kappa.

Thus the vertex partition is preserved exactly on square indices, not merely up to an unrecorded conjugacy. This is the concrete algebra missing from the brief correctness explanation. Geometrically each recut square is anchored at its original lower-left vertex; the new upward or rightward edge reaches the neighbor represented by the stated composition.

Both shears are invertible and generate the integer special-linear action. Their positive semigroup explores the full group orbit because, on a finite state space, inverses are positive powers of the corresponding bijections. Labeling and rooting make the state space larger but still finite. For an unlimited completed traversal, every primitive lattice direction is represented by a horizontal check; target images at nonprimitive multiples are reached by subsequent r steps.

## 2. Canonicalization and root

The BFS relabeling uses outgoing r and u edges in a fixed order, preserving labels and rooting the start square at0. Both are permutations. Their positive paths cover the connected origami component, so inverses add no missing connectivity. The shears preserve the subgroup generated by r,u, hence the source component stays the same.

The8-copy construction of an orthogonal room can be disconnected; dropping components not containing the chosen source is legitimate because every physical billiard connection lifts from that source, and its direction is varied over all primitive vectors. A nonsingular source vertex has one northeast sector on the surface. The chosen square is that sector for a selected physical source lift. Dihedral symmetry relates the other physical source lifts. A corner source would require additional sector treatment and is outside this argument.

2421 rooted relabeling/covariance checks PASS. Five fixed builder fixtures (square, diagonal triangle, and L, with interior/smooth-boundary marks) give valid permutations, consistent vertex labels and regular S/T vertices. This supports, but does not substitute for, the geometric construction argument.

## 3. Required endpoint restrictions

The builder gives `block` precedence over `T`. Therefore it never labels a room-corner target as T. In the square with vertices(0,0),(2,0),(2,2),(0,2), source(1,1), target(0,0), the target is directly visible as an allowed terminal corner, but `build` returns zero T labels. No orbit state can ever report LIT. A completed traversal would report DARK for that unsupported convention.

Fix: explicitly reject actual-corner endpoints under the present API, or implement a separate terminal-corner/sector convention. Do not treat this procedure as an oracle for the third star in the earlier diagram under its allowed-corner-target convention. The interior/interior marked-A application is unaffected.

For two smooth boundary marks, a surface geodesic can project along a physical wall. The existing horizontal check does not distinguish that from an ordinary billiard connection. Keeping integer vertex labels alone does not remember tangent directions relative to physical boundary germs. If at least one endpoint is interior, wall gliding before the target is impossible without passing a corner or starting along that wall, so this issue does not arise. For two boundary endpoints, either admit wall-gliding in the stated problem or preserve/filter the required physical sector data. In particular, a LIT verdict for a boundary-mark base does not by itself establish the ordinary base connection needed by a folding argument; this matches the caveat already accepted in the folded-base work.

Inputs must also be genuine simple canonical tile unions, with redundant collinear vertices removed before treating V as the room-corner set. The builder currently assumes rather than validates these preconditions.

## 4. Two mathematical cautions about the surrounding memo

The sign-theorem-plus-degeneration program is a sufficient route to the finite-lattice reduction. Compactness alone does not establish equivalence. A nonisolated real family need not contain a rational/lattice family; an arithmetic-density theorem would be needed for that converse. The peer memo already calls the converse “in spirit”; do not promote that to a proved equivalence on the board.

The proposed optimization “stop at the first orbit element where corners precede targets on all relevant horizontal cycles” needs a new invariant theorem before returning DARK. A blocked horizontal check at one transformed surface is not a certificate about its remaining orbit. Existing code correctly checks the entire orbit before DARK; preserve that behavior. No counterexample to the current full-orbit loop was found.

## Verdict and next action

Accept the core finite-orbit reasoning under the stated domain and physical path convention. Request endpoint validation and explicit two-boundary/gliding semantics before broad use. No recommendation to rerun all old rooms or launch another search. The existing source remains peer-owned and unchanged.
