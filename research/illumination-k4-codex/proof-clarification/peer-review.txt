## (1) Base parity lemma: ACCEPT

**Census.** I checked all ten corners cell by cell.
- **Convex (one occupied cell):** (-1,-1), (-1,0), (1,0), (2,-1), (2,-3), (1,-3), (0,-2).
- **Concave (three occupied cells):** (0,-1), (1,-1), (1,-2).
- **Smooth:** (0,0) is the midpoint of the top wall y=0, x∈[-1,1]. (2,-2) is the midpoint of the right wall x=2, y∈[-3,-1].
- **Interior integer points:** none.

**Logic.**
- Every unfolding map is a composition of reflections x↦2c−x and y↦2d−y with integer c and d. So each map has the form (x,y)↦(±x+2m, ±y+2n).
- Such maps preserve the lattice and both coordinate parities. The same holds for their inverses, which is what the fold-back step needs.
- V = g(b) is even-even. V is nonzero because the path has positive length.
- z = V/2^k sits at parameter 2^(−k), which lies strictly inside (0,1).
- The fold of z is the path's actual position at that parameter. It is an integer point of P with an odd coordinate. It cannot be a or b, and P has no interior integer points, so it is a corner. That contradicts corner avoidance.
- The "join" case is fine. Adjacent unfolded copies agree on their shared wall, so the fold of z is still well defined.

**Correction (wording only).** Say explicitly that the inverse unfolding maps also preserve parity. The text uses this fact but does not state it.

## (2) Event classification and census: ACCEPT

**Fold continuity on the seams.**
- On y=0, the identity and (x,−y) agree.
- On x=2, (x,−y) and (4−x,−y) both give (2,−y).

**Seam crossings.**
- At y=0, folded velocity (u,v) becomes (u,−v). This is a reflection off P's top wall.
- At x=2, folded velocity (u,−v) becomes (−u,−v). This is a reflection off P's right wall.
- Neither seam endpoint is included, because the seams are open. So a crossing always folds to a reflection at a smooth point of P (possibly at a or b).

**Endpoints.** I checked each one cell by cell. Each is a smooth point of Q and folds to a convex corner of P:

| Endpoint in Q | Occupied cells | Local wall | Fold in P |
|---|---|---|---|
| (−1,0) | (−1,−1), (−1,0) | vertical | (−1,0) |
| (1,0) | (0,−1), (0,0) | vertical | (1,0) |
| (2,1) | (1,1), (2,1) | horizontal | (2,−1) |
| (2,3) | (1,2), (2,2) | horizontal | (2,−3) |

I computed the before/after folded velocities at all four. Each gives an exact reversal (factor −1,−1), and by time reversal this holds from either side.

**Census.**
- 30 corner incidences = 8 at the four smooth endpoints + 13 convex corners of Q + 9 concave corners of Q. The total of 22 matches the boundary list.
- Every integer point of Q is accounted for. S and T are the only interior integer points.
- Every point that folds to a corner of P is an integer point. So the only non-corner physical points with abnormal folded dynamics are the four endpoints.

**Correction (expository).** "The other three by symmetry or interchanging x and y" should be replaced by the direct per-endpoint check in the table. Also add one sentence: nonaxiality is preserved, because |u| and |v| are invariant. That rules out legs running along a seam or a wall.

## (3) First-b / last-a argument: ACCEPT

**Stopping at the first b and starting at the last a.**
- Folding is purely a statement about the folded curve f:[0,L]→P.
- t_b > 0 exists because f(L) = b.
- t_a is the last visit to a in [0,t_b], so t_a < t_b.
- It does not matter which physical point produced each visit: S or (4,0) for a, (2,−2) or T for b. Step C only needs some a-to-b ordinary path in P.

**Step B: does a reversal force a return to a before the first b?** Yes. The text needs this lemma made explicit:

> At any physical point whose fold is an interior point or a smooth wall point of P, the folded motion locally *is* the billiard flow of P. This holds regardless of which copy the physical point is in, and regardless of whether the event is a physical wall reflection or a seam crossing.

Given that lemma:
1. Let t_r be the first reversal on γ. It falls strictly inside (t_a, t_b), because a and b are not corners.
2. The forward route on [t_a, t_r) touches only interior and smooth points of P.
3. So the reversed route is a valid P-billiard trajectory, and determinism forces f(t_r+s) = f(t_r−s) for s ∈ [0, t_r−t_a].
4. That retraced segment contains no b, since t_r < t_b.
5. f(t_b) = b, so t_b > 2t_r − t_a.
6. Therefore f(2t_r−t_a) = a is an interior visit to a in (t_a, t_b). This contradicts the choice of t_a.

The retrace also cannot reach an actual corner or a seam endpoint, because it passes only through folds of smooth or interior points.

**Step C.** With no reversals and no actual-corner hits, γ avoids every corner of P. Lemma (1) then applies.

**Finiteness.** The argument is valid. As a side note, finite length already forces finitely many reflections here: consecutive reflections on parallel walls are at least 1 apart in that coordinate.

**Correction.** Replace "retracing visits no point that was not already on the preceding route" with steps 3–6 above, including the explicit inequality t_b > 2t_r − t_a.

## (4) Four-copy extension: ACCEPT

**New copy and seam.**
- P3 = (−2−x, y) maps the base cells to (−2,−1), (−3,−1), (−3,−2), (−4,−2), (−4,−3).
- The seam x=−1, y∈(−1,0) folds continuously; both maps give (−1,y).
- A crossing folds to a reflection off P's left wall x=−1, which runs between corners (−1,−1) and (−1,0).

**Endpoint (−1,−1).**
- The occupied cells are (−2,−1) and (−1,−1), so it is a smooth point on a horizontal wall.
- Folded velocity: (−u,−v) before, (u,v) after. This is a reversal at the convex corner (−1,−1) of P.

**Endpoint (−1,0).**
- Three cells are occupied: (−2,−1) in P3, (−1,−1) in P0, and (−1,0) in P1. So it is an actual concave corner of Q4 and corner avoidance excludes it.
- All three copies send it to (−1,0), so the fold is consistent there.
- The P1–P3 contact is only this excluded point. The seam graph is the tree 2–1–0–3, so there is no monodromy.

**Other endpoints.** (1,0), (2,1) and (2,3) are unchanged. The four reversal points are (−1,−1), (1,0), (2,1), (2,3), folding to (−1,−1), (1,0), (2,−1), (2,−3). This matches your statement.

**Remaining checks.**
- S and T stay interior.
- The extra mark preimages (−2,0) and (−4,−2) are smooth wall points. Step A already permits visits to them.
- Finiteness carries over with three seam lines.

**Corrections required.** Section 2's text is specific to the three-copy room and must be restated for the four-copy room:
- **Reversal list:** (−1,0) is replaced by (−1,−1).
- **Census:** 40 incidences = 8 at the four smooth endpoints + 3 at (−1,0), which together form one concave corner + 17 convex corners + 12 concave corners from base concave corners. That gives 17 convex + 13 concave = 30 walls.
- **Wording:** the sentence "three concave base corners give nine" does not apply here.

- The reversal test (componentwise g_j·r·g_i = (−1,−1)) is the correct algebra.

- The parity and halving "controls" are finite sanity checks, not proofs. The text correctly says so.
