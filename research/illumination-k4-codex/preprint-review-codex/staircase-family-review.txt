Hand review of the supplied staircase family

[done] Codex, 2026-11-02. This is a general hand derivation, not a parameter sweep, new optical search, or external referee verdict. No manuscript was changed.

Definition
For an integer m>=2 let C_m be the unit-square lower-left corners
 {(-1,-1)} union {(j,-j-1),(j,-j-2):0<=j<m}.
Let P be their closed union, a=(0,0), b=(m,-m).
Set P0=P, P1={(x,-y):(x,y) in P}, P2={(2m-x,-y):(x,y) in P}.
Let Q=P0 union P1 union P2, S=(0,0), T=(m,m).

1. Base geometry for every integer m>=2
There are2m+1 squares. Its square-adjacency graph is a chain: one initial edge, m vertical within-column edges, and m-1 edges between consecutive two-square columns, totaling2m. Its vertical columns are one initial square at x in[-1,0] and, for j=0,...,m-1, two stacked squares spanning y in[-j-2,-j]. Consecutive columns overlap in exactly one unit-height interval.

At an integer vertex there cannot be four adjacent squares: neighboring two-square columns are shifted vertically by one, so their intersection has height1, rather than2. No diagonal-only pair occurs at a boundary point; the turns of the square chain have three incident squares. Thus there are no interior integer points or boundary pinches. The boundary is a simple staircase cycle, with no hole.

At each internal interface x=j,1<=j<m, the integer vertices (j,-j) and (j,-j-1) each have three incident squares and are reflex corners. There is one more reflex corner at (0,-1). Hence there are2m-1 reflex corners and2m+3 convex corners, totaling4m+2 maximal sides. Equivalently its lattice perimeter is4m+4 and it has precisely two smooth integer boundary points.

Those smooth points are a, the midpoint of the top edge [-1,1]x{0}, and b, the midpoint of the final vertical edge {m}x[-m-1,-m+1]. Every other integer point belonging to P is a vertex.

2. Three-copy assembly
The copy interiors are disjoint. P0 and P1 meet only along [-1,1]x{0}. P1 and P2 meet only along {m}x[m-1,m+1]. P0 and P2 have no contact: their only possible common y is0, where the respective x intervals are[-1,1] and[2m-1,2m+1], disjoint for m>=2. Gluing these disks along two disjoint complete boundary intervals produces a strictly simple disk with no hole or interior partition.

The seam endpoints are(-1,0),(1,0),(m,m-1),(m,m+1). Each is a smooth Q-wall point formed from two convex right-angle base corners. All other base-corner incidences remain distinct actual Q corners. Therefore
  W(Q)=3(4m+2)-8=12m-2.
The convex/reflex counts are6m+1 and6m-3, respectively, and area(Q)=3(2m+1)=6m+3. S and T are the interior seam midpoints.

3. Darkness for even m
The copywise folding map is id, (x,-y), (2m-x,-y), respectively. It agrees on both seams and sends S to a and T to b. Seam endpoints fold only to convex base corners, producing exact velocity reversal by the same local matrix calculation as the22-wall case. Smooth seam-interior crossings give ordinary base reflections.

For even m, both a and b have even coordinates. A hypothetical ordinary finite base path unfolds to a positive-length straight segment from0 to an even-even image B!=0. Dividing B by its maximal common power of2 gives a strictly intermediate integer point with an odd coordinate. Folding this point preserves each coordinate parity; it cannot be a or b, so it is a base vertex. Contradiction.

Any finite folded path with convex-corner reversals is excluded by taking its last folded a before its first folded b. The first reversal would force retracing to a before reaching b. This truncation must occur in the base because a and b have multiple preimages in Q. Axis-parallel physical paths cannot join S to T since their coordinates both differ.

Conclusion: for every even m>=2, the construction has the claimed12m-2walls and two non-illuminating interior marks. m=4 gives46walls and area27. No non-illumination claim for odd m follows from this argument: b would be odd-odd, so the endpoint-parity step would fail. This does not assert odd-m rooms are lit.

4. Scope


The m=4 construction is a further instance of the folded-parity family. The restricted lower-bound work is a separate result with its own hypotheses.
