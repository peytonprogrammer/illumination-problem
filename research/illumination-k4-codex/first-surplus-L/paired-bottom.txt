1. Two-reflection return map, including its branch conditions
The centered bottom pair is the triangle with vertices(-1,-1),(0,-2),(1,-1).
Its mouth is y=-1, -1<x<1. Its two walls are perpendicular diagonals.
Let a ray enter at(u,-1) with velocity(d,-1). If

 -1<u<1,  -1<u+2d<1,  u+d != 0,                                  (3)

it hits both diagonal walls, without a corner collision, and returns to
the mouth at(-u-2d,-1) with velocity(-d,1).
These strict conditions imply -1<d<1. The sign of u+d determines the first
wall; zero means a collision with the apex, which is excluded.

For u+d<0 the first wall is x+y=-2. In local coordinates X=x,Y=y+2,
the first hit is
 X1=(u+d)/(1-d), Y1=-X1.
Reflection changes(d,-1) to(1,-d). The second wall is Y=X, with hit
 X2=Y2=-(u+d)/(1+d).
The entry condition puts0<Y1<1. The inequality u+2d>-1 puts0<Y2<1,
so the second collision occurs before reaching the mouth. The second
reflection gives(-d,1). Its mouth intersection is Xexit=-u-2d, which is
strictly inside the mouth by(3). Each open leg is inside this convex
triangle. Reflect x to -x for the u+d>0 branch; this gives the same map.

There is also a direct unfolding derivation. The composition of the two
wall reflections is the half-turn about(0,-2). The unfolded image of the
exit(-u-2d,-1) is(u+2d,-3). The entering straight ray reaches this image
after time2. This explains the map, while(3) supplies the missing validity
conditions; the formula alone does not prove that a ray hits both walls.

For the saved witness take u=d=-r, with0<r<1/3. Then the pair returns the
ray at(3r,-1) directed(r,1). Both collisions are legal. A single B+ cap
in the peer paired-left proof takes three reflections to reach the SAME
