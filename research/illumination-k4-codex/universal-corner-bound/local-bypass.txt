Attempt 1 — local replacement of a corner-bending path
3 November 2026. Hand argument, independent review pending.

Gap attacked
Can failure to replace a shortest corner-bending path by a legal billiard path force several distinct physical corners? Success criterion: a valid local replacement theorem, or a counterexample to its local premise. Strongest objection: a single reflex wedge may already prevent the replacement.

Local obstruction
Let V be the closed planar wedge 0<=arg(z)<=theta, pi<theta<2pi. Choose epsilon with 0<epsilon<(theta-pi)/2 and marks on the unit circle at arguments epsilon and theta-epsilon.
There is no corner-avoiding finite specular connection between these marks staying in V and reflecting only on its two bounding rays.

Proof
A straight segment between positive points of the two different bounding rays lies in their smaller sector, which is the excluded sector of angle 2pi-theta<pi. Thus a legal trajectory cannot have consecutive reflections on different rays. Two consecutive reflections on the same line are also impossible for a transverse straight segment. A tangent path following a wall cannot originate at an interior mark under these rules. Therefore there is at most one reflection.
The direct chord crosses the excluded sector: its angular separation through the allowed wedge is theta-2epsilon>pi, so the chord takes the shorter sector crossing the missing angular gap.
For a reflection on ray0, reflect the second mark in its supporting line. The first mark and this image both have positive y-coordinate, since theta-epsilon lies strictly between pi and2pi. Their segment cannot intersect that line. Reflection in the wedge bisector exchanges the two marks and the two walls, so a reflection on the other ray is impossible as well.
This proves the local claim.

Scope and realignment This is an unbounded wedge, not a bounded-room dark-pair counterexample. A sufficiently small neighborhood of any reflex corner has this geometry, so local smoothing can fail already at one physical corner. Remote walls can still supply a global connection, as in the previously proved L-room theorem. The premise that each locally failed bypass costs further distinct corners is therefore unavailable. Retire this local-bypass counting route. No 22-wall bound follows.
