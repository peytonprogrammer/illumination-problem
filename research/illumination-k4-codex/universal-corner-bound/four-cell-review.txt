1. Expansion: independently checked without compactness assumptions
For each occupied row of S, shifting its rightmost point one step right adds a new point. These additions are distinct across rows and are outside S. Therefore H=S union(S+e1) has at least m+r points.
The occupied columns of H include every occupied column of S and an extra column beyond their maximum: at least c+1. In each such column, shifting its uppermost point one step up adds a point outside H. The additions are distinct. Thus |H union(H+e2)|>=m+r+c+1.
This union is precisely S+{0,1}^2. Gaps in rows/columns and disconnected S do not invalidate either injection. Since m<=rc, the minimum r+c values for m=1,2,3,4 are2,3,4,4, giving union bounds4,6,8,9. All entries checked; notably the m3 bound is8, not7.

2. Checkerboard-coordinate bijections
For even x+y, (u,v)=((x+y)/2,(x-y)/2) is integral, with inverse (x,y)=(u+v,u-v).
For odd x+y, (u',v')=((x+y-1)/2,(x-y-1)/2) is integral, with inverse (x,y)=(u'+v'+1,u'-v').
Applying the odd-coordinate map to the four neighbors of an even cell gives, respectively for +x,-x,+y,-y:
 (u,v), (u-1,v-1), (u,v-1), (u-1,v).
Their union over S is S+{-1,0}^2, a translate of S+{0,1}^2. Therefore the expansion bounds apply to the distinct neighbors, with no counting multiplicity. Translating the original grid exchanges colors and proves the corresponding odd-color claim.

3. Subtraction of full cells: key double-counting check
Neighbors of even full cells are odd; neighbors of odd full cells are even. Their unions are disjoint. Hence they can be added before removing full cells. For color splits3+1 and2+2 the lower bound for the union is12; removing F deletes at most its4 points, leaving at least8 external neighbors. For4+0, every neighbor has opposite color from F and none is deleted; the bound is9. These cases include disconnected F and exhaust all four-point sets. No equality classification or finite census is used.

4. Physical charging: key geometric check
Each nonempty partial cell corresponds to a distinct boundary A point. A proper fan is contiguous because a disconnected fan would create a forbidden boundary self-contact/pinch. Its Q contribution is positive: a corner contributes its positive incidence; a smooth A point contributes3. Thus each occupied external neighbor supplies a distinct unit of Q.
At the midpoint C of the side between a full and an empty cell, the full cell supplies precisely the occupied half-plane of the local four-quadrant star. The empty cell supplies none of the opposite half-plane. This is a smooth physical boundary point, not a corner or an interior exception, and contributes to s_N. Other A cells cannot alter these quadrants. A chosen midpoint determines exactly its full/empty cell pair, so different empty cells cannot be assigned the same midpoint. The A charges and C midpoint charges are disjoint. Therefore Q+s_N>=b(F).
This does not incorrectly charge one smooth midpoint for each incident full cell: exactly one full neighbor is chosen per empty cell. Some empty cells could supply more smooth midpoints, but these extra contributions are simply unused.

5. Exact ledger and arithmetic
Euler for the disk triangulation gives N=2V_int+V_boundary-2, with V_int=I+J and V_boundary=W+s_A+s_N. All triangle vertices are integer points and W counts maximal walls/genuine corners.
A-incidence gives N=8I+4s_A+sum_Acorners(m)=8I+Q+s_A. Subtracting yields W=2+6I+Q-2J-s_N. Because k=J+s_N, adding2k yields W+2k=2+6I+Q+s_N.
For I4, b(F)>=8 and Q+s_N>=b(F) imply W+2k>=26+8=34. k<=6 therefore gives W>=22. No darkness premise is used or needed.
