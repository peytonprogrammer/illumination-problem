2. Why one square gives the complete finite map

Unfold across the two nonportal mirrors V and H, which meet at angle45
at apex A=(0,1). Their eight dihedral images tile exactly the square
 [-1,1] x [0,2], with A at its center. Each outer half-edge is an image
of the original portal. A folded triangular trajectory is therefore the
straight unfolded segment
 (u+a*t,t), t>=0,
until its FIRST exit from that square. No reflected path needs to be
guessed: its exit time is exactly
 tau=min(2, (1-u)/a if a>0, (1+u)/(-a) if a<0).
For a=0 the side terms are omitted and tau=2.

The ray crosses the fixed radial tile edges. Folding across them yields
the prescribed H/V reflection word. Any exact passage through A is a
true apex collision, not a mirror reflection; it occurs at a=-u,t=1.
No other interior tile vertex exists. Strict outer half-edge interiors
exclude the remaining portal-endpoint collisions. Thus the resulting
branch partition is exhaustive and its corner exclusions complete for
this LOCAL cap return. It does not establish global segment clearance
outside the cap.
