## Priority issues from the supplied memo

1. The symmetry group of the square has eight elements. Axis reflections generate four, and adding a 45-degree diagonal reflection gives eight. The usual canonical 45-degree unfolding therefore uses eight reflected copies. In that connected unfolding the corner-lift counts for a=1,2,3,5,6,7 are respectively 1,2,1,1,2,1, with cone angles 2pi,2pi,6pi,10pi,6pi,14pi. A proposed 16-copy construction needs its additional gluing specified; the table currently doubles the usual counts.
2. An arbitrary orthogonal polygon need not be square-tiled. The square-tiling assertion in Lemma1 needs the fixed integral-grid hypothesis. The corner-lift and genus computation can hold without that hypothesis.
3. Uniform scaling changes the translation metric. The correct relationship is a homothety (or the same normalized surface after division by the scale), with a finer subdivision. Independent axis scaling gives affine equivalence in the orthogonal case, not metric equality.
4. Fixed-word wall-intersection conditions are expressed using determinants of vectors depending linearly on variable edge lengths and mark coordinates. Those determinants are generally quadratic in the joint parameters. Linearity of unfolded endpoint coordinates does not make visibility inequalities linear. A polyhedral-cone claim needs an additional argument; semialgebraic conditions are the natural starting point for a fixed word.
5. A rational linear subspace does not have a unique primitive integral representative up to scaling. For example, the rational subspace z=0 contains the infinitely many pairwise nonproportional primitive positive vectors (1,n,0), n>=1. Finite containment in subspaces alone cannot yield the proposed finite check. The stronger assertion that darkness is constant on each feasible slice would need its own precise hypotheses and proof.
6. The established strip example disproved a naive straight-path-transfer map. It did not prove that the modified room becomes lit. The memo's evidence paragraph currently claims the stronger, unverified conclusion.
