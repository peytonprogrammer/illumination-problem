Darkness-dependent constraints for the remaining two-/three-A branches
3 November 2026

Outcome
Two bounded hand approaches yield a sharper general boundary-charge bound
and explicit optical necessary conditions. They do NOT yield a new wall
minimum or replace the remaining I2/I3 certificates. A fixed strictly simple
16-wall, k=6, I=3 completion satisfies all optical conditions derived here,
so those conditions together are insufficient for the desired contradiction.
This completion is not claimed dark. The existing restricted theorem would
imply that it is illuminated; no new connecting path search was performed.

Scope
Exactly the canonical 45-degree triangle disk class and notation of
../restricted-theorem-note/theorem.txt. I counts all interior A points,
J interior B/C points, s_N smooth boundary B/C points, k=J+s_N. Q charges
3 at smooth boundary A points and m at genuine A corners with m triangles.
Use physical coordinates for explicit paths; use A/2 cell coordinates for F.
Existing Euler identity:
  W+2k = 2+6I+Q+s_N.                            (1)
The current task is a bounded hand investigation. All geometry below is
specified explicitly; no room, direction, reflection-word or ray-discovery
run, solver, census expansion or worker was used.

1. Approach one: charge full-cell SIDES rather than neighboring cells
Let p(F) be the number of cell sides with a full A cell on precisely one
side. If e(F) counts shared sides between full cells, then
  p(F)=4I-2e(F).
Claim:
  Q+s_N >= p(F),
  W+2k >= 2+10I-2e(F).                         (2)
Proof. For each exposed full-cell side, inspect the adjacent nonfull cell.
If at least one triangle in that cell touches the common side, assign one
charge from its A center. A proper m-triangle fan touches at most m cell
sides; a smooth four-triangle fan touches at most three sides and has Q
charge three. Hence each partial cell can pay for every touched side leading
to a full cell. If neither triangle touches the common side, its midpoint C
is smooth boundary and contributes one to s_N. Different sides have distinct
midpoints, and A-center charges and C-midpoint charges are disjoint.
This proves (2), including disconnected full-cell supports.

For I=3 at W<=21,k<=6, equation (1) gives Q+s_N<=13. Thus:
  e(F)=0: p=12, at most one surplus unit above (2);
  e(F)=1: p=10, at most three surplus units;
  e(F)=2: p=8, at most five surplus units.
These are geometric restrictions, not consequences of darkness. They leave
feasible rooms. They do not supply a new optical contradiction.
For a dark I2 pair, e(F)=0 (adjacent full cells already give direct light),
but its bound Q+s_N>=8 is far below the possible budget Q+s_N<=19.

2. A physical corner forced by a dark DIAGONAL pair
Normalize a diagonal pair to S=(0,0), T=(2,2). Their full stars are the
squares [-1,1]^2 and [1,3]^2. The entire segment ST is in their union and
its only potentially noninterior point is B=(1,1).
If B is interior, ST is a legal direct path. Darkness therefore forces B
to be a genuine corner. The two full stars force opposite 90-degree sectors
at B. Strict simplicity requires a single contiguous fan. A proper fan
containing those sectors has six or seven canonical triangles, hence angle
270 or 315 degrees. Eight would be interior and four cannot contain both
opposite sectors contiguously. Thus a dark diagonal pair forces an ACTUAL
reflex corner at its midpoint, with one of these two angles.
This does not justify charging different unfolded copies as distinct
physical corners. It concerns this one fixed physical point only.

3. Smooth bridge centers give one-reflection connections
For the same pair, consider A_NW=(0,2). Suppose its occupied fan is exactly
the half-star on the side y<=x+2. It is smooth boundary with mirror y=x+2.
Then
  (0,0) -> (0,2) -> (2,2)
is a legal one-reflection connection: vertical velocity reflects to
horizontal velocity. Except for the bounce, the first leg is inside the
S star and this half-star; the second is inside the half-star and T star.
At their shared side midpoints both sides of the path are occupied, so
these crossings are interior points, not corner hits.
Likewise the southeast bridge A_SE=(2,0), if smooth with occupied half-star
y>=x-2, gives
  (0,0) -> (2,0) -> (2,2).
A dark diagonal pair forbids both specified smooth bridge fans. Merely
saying that a bridge center is smooth without specifying its orientation
and occupied side is insufficient.

4. Approach two: two explicit reflection guards for the L support
Assume all three stars centered at
  S=(0,0), U=(2,0), T=(2,2)
are full. U is an interior A point, not a reflection point.

Bottom route:
  S -> q=(1/2,-1) -> T.
Incoming velocity (1/2,-1) reflects in y=-1 to (1/2,1), and T-q is three
times that reflected velocity. The outgoing leg y=2x-2 passes successively
through the S, U and T stars, crossing at (1,0) and (3/2,1). Those crossings
are interior to the union. The only needed mirror is the open half-side
between C=(0,-1) and B=(1,-1), containing q.
There is exactly one canonical triangle across that half-side:
  tau_bottom = conv{(0,-2),(0,-1),(1,-1)}.
If tau_bottom is absent, q is a smooth physical boundary point, so this
route illuminates T. Therefore darkness forces tau_bottom to be present.
Other external triangles cannot cover an interior point of this edge.

East route:
  S -> r=(3,3/2) -> T.
Incoming velocity (3,3/2) reflects in x=3 to (-3,3/2), and T-r is one
third of that reflected velocity. The first leg y=x/2 passes through the
S, U and T stars at (1,1/2) and (2,1); the last leg is in T's full star.
The needed mirror is the open half-side between B=(3,1) and C=(3,2).
Its unique opposite triangle is
  tau_east = conv{(4,2),(3,2),(3,1)}.
Darkness forces tau_east to be present as well.

These implications are exact universal completion conditions. They need
no restriction on triangles elsewhere in the room. They give a compact
hand explanation of two optical guard clauses, not an assertion that such
guards were absent from the historical machine-generated library.

5. Fixed countermodel to sufficiency / surplus charging
Start with the three full stars in section 4 and add precisely four
triangles, all in distinct external A cells:
  tau_bottom = {(0,-2),(0,-1),(1,-1)},
  tau_east   = {(4,2),(3,2),(3,1)},
  tau_top    = {(2,4),(2,3),(1,3)},
  tau_left   = {(-2,0),(-1,0),(-1,1)}.
Each is a single triangular attachment along an exposed half-side. The
four attachments are disjoint, with distinct attachment endpoints; the
boundary remains a strictly simple single cycle. All are canonical.

Exact ledgers from the independent, standalone fixed-geometry script:
                         N   W   I  J  s_A  s_N   Q   k
  Three full stars      24   6   3  2   0    10   0  12
  Plus required caps    26  12   3  2   0     6   2   8
  Plus all four caps    28  16   3  2   0     4   4   6

The final completion satisfies W<=21,k<=6 and ALL optical necessary
conditions obtained above: midpoint B has six triangles (270-degree
reflex corner); the northwest bridge cell is empty; the southeast bridge
is the full U star; both required cap triangles are present.
Moreover e(F)=2, p(F)=8 and Q+s_N=4+4=8. Thus it SATURATES the sharpened
perimeter inequality. Interrupting the two explicit short paths does not
force any surplus in (2).

Why the counts change: the bottom/east caps each turn a smooth C and a
smooth B into genuine corners, adding an A corner. The top/left caps each
turn a smooth C into a corner, add an A corner, and modify an existing B
corner without changing its corner status. Thus the exception budget can
fall while the boundary charge remains at its minimum. Calling each cap
an additional independent wall obstruction would count the wrong cost.

For clarity, in this fixed completion the old bottom ray, continued into
its cap instead of reflected at q, hits (2/3,-4/3) on the diagonal cap wall
and then reaches the actual C corner (0,-1). The old east ray continues
to the cap's A corner (4,2). This explains how those particular paths fail.
Neither calculation blocks every other trajectory.

Verification
Ran darkness-fixed-check.py on exactly the three specified triangle unions.
It independently builds canonical triangles, cancels oriented edges,
checks one simple boundary cycle, matches exact polygon area to tile area,
classifies all interior/smooth/corner lattice points, checks the full A
stars, checks both Euler/incidence identities and the midpoint fan, and
checks exact reflection laws plus the prescribed square containment of
the two hand-derived paths. No peer implementation is imported.
The smooth-bounce and physical-corner implications are hand arguments
above, not a claim that the script proves optical completeness.
This is author verification, not a fresh independent peer review.
Receipts: darkness-fixed-check.json. Diagram: darkness-local-paths.png/svg.

Decision / next useful target
The bounded local-charge approach has reached a concrete limit: even the
combined corner, bridge and two-path constraints admit the W16/k6 model
at zero perimeter surplus. Do not turn these necessary conditions into a
22-wall claim or launch a larger search on their strength.
The next meaningful proof would control the complete light path after it
enters an added cap, or another global coupling that all directions must
obey. No such global implication is established here. Existing I2/I3
certificates remain necessary to the current proof.
