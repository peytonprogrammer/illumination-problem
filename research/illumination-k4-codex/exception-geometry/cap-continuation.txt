Following rays through the triangular caps
3 November 2026

Result
The fixed W=16,k=6 room is explicitly illuminated from S=(0,0) to T=(2,2)
by a legal ten-reflection trajectory with initial velocity (-3,-10).
A saved certificate supplied the path. Exact independent replay and a
separate actual-boundary intersection check both pass. A hand derivation
then explains the complete itinerary and an open interval of related rays.
Two triangular-cap response lemmas, with precise entry hypotheses, describe
the mechanism. No new wall-count lower bound or complete I2/I3 exclusion
follows. The result is a concrete constructive case, not merely a sampled
failure to find darkness.

2. Cap response A: a two-reflection quarter-turn
Translate the left cap to its actual position. Its mouth is x=-1, 0<y<1;
its mirrors are y=0 and y=x+2. A ray enters at (-1,h) with velocity (-r,-1).
Assume
  0<h<1, r>0, r(1+h)<1.
These imply r<1. The ray hits the horizontal mirror first at
  (-1-rh,0),
then the diagonal mirror, then exits through the same mouth with
  exit height h'=1-r(1+h),
  exit velocity (1,-r).
There are exactly TWO cap reflections. Reflection in y=0 changes (-r,-1)
to (-r,1); reflection in y=x+2 swaps components to (1,-r).

Derivation. After the first hit, the time to the diagonal is
  t=(1-rh)/(1+r).
Its coordinates are (-1-rh-rt,t). Travel next with (1,-r) until x=-1.
The exit height simplifies to 1-r-rh. The hypotheses make the first and
second hits interior to their mirror segments and the exit height strictly
between zero and one, so no third reflection or corner collision occurs.
For the actual connecting ray r=3/10 and h=2/3, giving h'=1/2.
This cap turns a steep downward-left ray into a shallow downward-right ray.

3. Cap response B: three reflections equivalent to a farther mirror
Use local east-cap coordinates X=x-3, Z=y-1. The cap is
  0<=X<=Z<=1.
Its mouth is X=0; its mirrors are Z=X and Z=1. A ray enters at (0,h) with
velocity (1,r). Assume
  h>0, r>0, h+2r<1.
Then it makes exactly THREE reflections: diagonal, horizontal, diagonal.
The successive local hit points are
  (d1,d1),               d1=h/(1-r),
  (h+r,1),
  (d2,d2),               d2=(h+2r)/(1+r).
It exits the mouth at
  (0,h+2r), with velocity (-1,r).

Derivation. Swap velocity components at each diagonal reflection and negate
the vertical component at the horizontal reflection:
  (1,r) -> (r,1) -> (r,-1) -> (-1,r).
Solving each linear intersection gives the points above. The strict
inequality h+2r<1 ensures they lie in the open mirror segments and that the
ray exits before a fourth hit. The output matches what a VIRTUAL vertical
mirror at X=1 would do: an outward-and-back horizontal displacement of two
raises the height by 2r. No physical vertical wall at X=1 is claimed.
For the actual connecting ray h=1/10,r=3/10, the exit height is 7/10,
so the physical exit point is (3,17/10), directed toward T.

These two response formulas are local lemmas for triangular mirror caps
with their two mirror walls intact. They are not assertions about arbitrary
partial fans or a cap whose external walls have been extended by more tiles.

4. A hand construction of the complete beam
Launch S with velocity (-r,-1), where
  5/17 < r < 4/13.                              (A)
This is an OPEN interval, with 3/10 strictly inside it.
Two horizontal reflections in the S star, first on y=-1 and then y=1,
give cap entry at x=-1 with height
  h_L=4-1/r.
Condition (A) places h_L strictly between zero and one and satisfies the
two-reflection cap condition. The left cap returns the ray at
  (-1,2-5r), velocity (1,-r).

It next reflects on the elbow's right wall at
  (3,2-9r),
and its bottom wall at
  (12-3/r,-1),
and the source star's left wall at
  (-1,13r-4).
The ray now travels with velocity (1,r). At the east-cap mouth x=3 its
height above y=1 is
  h_E=17r-5.
Condition (A) gives h_E>0 and h_E+2r<1. The three-reflection response
therefore returns it at
  (3,19r-4), velocity (-1,r).
At the vertical line x=2 it arrives at
  (2,20r-4).                                   (B)
Setting r=3/10 makes this point exactly T=(2,2). This gives the witness
without treating a saved successful simulation as the proof.

Path validity for the WHOLE interval (hand check)
- The initial bottom/top hits have x=-r and x=-3r, in the open uncapped
  source-wall portions. The source-to-cap entry height h_L is in (0,1).
- Left-cap exit has height 2-5r in (0,1). On the way to x=3, the ray crosses
  x=1 at height 2-7r in (-1,1), so it stays inside the source/elbow stars.
  The right-wall hit 2-9r is in (-1,1).
- The bottom hit x=12-3/r is in (1,3), in the elbow bottom wall, away from
  the optional bottom cap. On returning across x=1, the height is 11r-4,
  also in (-1,1).
- The next source-left hit has -1<13r-4<0, below the left-cap mouth.
- After this reflection, the ray crosses x=1 at height 15r-4 in (-1,1).
  It crosses y=1 at x=5/r-14 in (1,3), so it passes from the elbow star
  into the target star before entering the east cap.
- The two local cap hypotheses are strict. All five cap bounces are
  consequently away from corners and all cap legs are interior.
- East-cap exit has 1<19r-4<3. Both this point and the target in (B) are
  in the target star, with the intervening open segment in its interior.
These inequalities follow directly from (A). Source and target stars are
full, and all stated portals join occupied full sides. No other physical
boundary point occurs on an open leg.

The exact formula for every bounce is implemented in family(r) in the
checker. Its r=3/10 value equals the unchanged saved path exactly. The
checker evaluates only that supplied ray; the interval proof is the hand
inequality argument above, not a numerical sampling claim.

5. What generalizes and what does not
As r varies in (A), the same ten-wall itinerary illuminates every point
  (2,Y), 32/17<Y<28/13.
Thus T is inside an explicit beam, not at a singular limiting direction.
Since the required/forbidden conditions do not involve the optional bottom
and top triangles, the SAME path remains legal if either or both are
removed. More generally, it works in any canonical completion containing
its required triangle set and avoiding the nine forbidden triangles, with
interior endpoint stars. This is a local universal guard with an explicit
human-readable derivation.

However, adding other tiles can remove one of the mirrors this itinerary
uses. The response windows also require the incoming ray to arrive with
specific direction and position. Neither the wall count nor the charge
identities proved so far force these conditions in every I2/I3 completion.
These gaps prevent a conclusion about all low-wall rooms or a universal
22-wall minimum. No claim that every triangular addition improves
illumination, or that all caps have one constant response, is made.

Decision
The fixed 16-wall example is now explained constructively. The reusable
output is a pair of cap response lemmas and a complete beam calculation.
The next genuinely stronger target would force some admissible cap-entry
window and a compatible return to the actual target throughout a class
of remaining completions. That global condition has not been proved.
Do not restart a broad room/direction search or the paused universal
corner-bound goal on the strength of this one solved example.
