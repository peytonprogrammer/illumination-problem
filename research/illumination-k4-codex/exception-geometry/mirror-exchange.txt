A structural two-path cover at zero boundary-charge surplus

Result
Every strictly simple canonical room with exactly the three full A stars
S=(0,0), U=(2,0), T=(2,2), containing all four caps of the fixed16-wall
reference room R, and satisfying Q+s_N=8, illuminates S and T.

General positive-surplus I2/I3 rooms remain outside this result.

Definitions
Use exactly the canonical45-degree triangle disk model in
../restricted-theorem-note/theorem.txt. I counts all full interior A stars;
J interior non-A points; s_A/s_N smooth boundary A/non-A points; k=J+s_N.
Q assigns3 to a smooth boundary A fan and m to an A corner with m triangles.
The three full cells have8 exposed sides, p(F)=4I-2e(F)=8.
The reference R contains, beyond these stars, the four single triangles
 L+ = {(-2,0),(-1,0),(-1,1)},
 B+ = {(0,-2),(0,-1),(1,-1)},
 E- = {(4,2),(3,2),(3,1)},
 T- = {(2,4),(2,3),(1,3)}.
The assumption Q+s_N=p(F)=8 is called zero surplus below. It is essential,
and is stronger than merely W<=21,k<=6.

1. The equality rule is structural
For each occupied partial A cell v, let d(v) count its sides leading to a
full cell that have at least one occupied triangle. Let G be the set of
exposed full-cell sides having neither opposite triangle occupied. Their
midpoints are distinct smooth C points. Thus
 p(F)=sum_v d(v)+|G|.
For each partial fan q(v)>=d(v), by the already proved side-charge lemma.
Every point counted by G is counted by s_N, so
 Q+s_N-p(F)=sum_v(q(v)-d(v))+(s_N-|G|).          (1)
All terms are nonnegative. Equality forces them all to vanish.

Consequences, independently checked:
- There are no occupied partial cells outside the full-cell neighborhood:
  they would have d=0 but q>0.
- The neighborhood has seven external A centers:
  (-2,0),(0,-2),(2,-2),(4,0),(4,2),(2,4),(0,2).
  Only the northwest cell centered at(0,2) has two full neighbors; all
  others have one. Hence q=d<=1 outside that cell and q=d<=2 inside it.
- A nonempty partial fan has q=1 only when it is a single triangle; q=2
  only when it has two triangles. All larger fans, including smooth
  half-stars with q=3, are excluded here.
- The unique two-triangle fan allowed in the northwest cell is its
  southeast quarter: one triangle touches the bottom full side and the
  other touches the right full side. Other two-triangle contiguous fans
  there either touch just one full side or violate contiguity.
- Equality also prohibits EVERY smooth B point, because G contains only C.

Thus, except for a directly illuminating bridge handled below, every room
has at most one cap triangle in each of seven specified cells. The two
half-side choices at six cells and four choices at the northwest cell give
sixteen possible individual triangles. This is a necessary and exhaustive
reduction, not a list of sampled rooms.

If both northwest bridge triangles are occupied, B_mid=(1,1) gains the
missing90-degree quadrant. The other three quadrants are in the full
stars already. The whole segment ST is then interior, including B_mid,
so direct illumination follows immediately.

2. Four reference caps protect the mirror walls of two fixed paths
Take the previously verified13-reflection path p from the saved guard
line60, with initial direction(-2,-11). Its detailed hand construction
and parameter interval are in ../cap-return-review/findings.txt.
Outside the full stars it requires exactly B+ and T-.
Among the16 possible cap triangles, its forbidden ones are
 B-, L-, Nb-, Nb+, T+.
Its other two forbidden triangles cannot occur in the equality reduction.
Here the four northwest options are
 Nb- = {(-1,1),(0,1),(0,2)},
 Nb+ = {(0,1),(0,2),(1,1)},
 Nr- = {(0,2),(1,1),(1,2)},
 Nr+ = {(0,2),(1,2),(1,3)}.

Because R is contained in the room, L+,B+,E-,T- are already present.
Putting B-,L-,T+ in their respective occupied cells would require at
least two triangles in a cell with only one full neighbor; this violates
q=d. In addition, L+ together with Nb- would make B=(-1,1) smooth, which
violates equation(1). Therefore p can be spoiled within this family only
by the single triangle Nb+.

Now reflect p by
 sigma(x,y)=(2-y,2-x),
and reverse its direction. The resulting path p_sigma still starts at
actual S and ends at actual T. The map preserves the canonical tiling and
permutes the full stars and four reference caps. Outside the full stars,
p_sigma requires exactly E- and L+, which are present.
Its forbidden cap options are E+,L-,Nr+,Nr-,T+. The first, second and last
are excluded by the occupied-cell condition. Nr+ is excluded because with
T- it would make the B point(1,3) smooth. Consequently p_sigma can be
spoiled only by Nr-.

This does NOT assume that the extended room is symmetric. The two fixed
paths have separately established occupied/absent tile implications;
R supplies their required tiles. Symmetry is used to construct the second
fixed path and its implication, not to identify the entire unknown room.

3. Blocking both makes a direct path
The only remaining blockers Nb+ and Nr- are in the SAME northwest cell.
A potentially dark equality room can have at most one triangle in that
cell, unless it has the unique two-triangle southeast bridge.
There are therefore only these possibilities for an eligible superroom:
 - no northwest cap: both reflected paths remain legal;
 - Nb+ alone: p_sigma remains legal;
 - Nr- alone: p remains legal;
 - both: the direct segment ST is legal.
The other northwest single caps are excluded by the smooth-B condition
with the already present reference caps. Thus every room in the stated
family illuminates S,T. No room enumeration or direction search is needed.

This is the useful exchange rule in this scope: removing both specified
reflection routes requires an addition that opens a direct route.
It is stronger than a claim that an unspecified new smooth point must help.
The proof keeps the actual source and target throughout.

4. Review of the wider geometry classification
I also checked the other session's full zero-surplus geometric reduction,
without assuming containment of R. The two initially smooth B points at
(1,-1),(3,1) require (B+ OR D-) and (R+ OR E-). At the five initially
convex B corners, the forbidden smooth-making pairs are
 (L-,B-),(D+,R-),(E+,T+),(T-,Nr+),(L+,Nb-).
The cap definitions and corner fans give exactly these conditions.
Single-cap attachments meeting these rules are strictly simple disks:
each attaches along a full exposed half-edge; different cells' caps meet
only at already occupied B endpoints; their fans extend the old fan
contiguously. Triangle collapse preserves the core's topology, with no
hole or pinch. No new interior B/C points arise in the single-cap branch.
If n caps are occupied, the exact ledger is
 Q=n, J=2, s_N=8-n, k=10-n, W=8+2n.
Under W<=21,k<=6 this gives n=4,5,6 and (W,k)=(16,6),(18,5),(20,4).
The previously proved one-reflection paths force B+ and E- for darkness.
None of this forces L+ and T-, so it does not extend the theorem here to
all the other zero-surplus cap patterns. That optical gap remains real.

5. Why the general mirror-exchange ledger alone does not prove darkness
For completeness, adding a single canonical triangle to a disk, when the
result is again a strictly simple disk, obeys
 Delta W = 1-2Delta I-2Delta J-Delta s_A-Delta s_N,
 Delta(W+2k)=1-2Delta I-Delta s_A+Delta s_N.      (2)
These follow from the existing Euler identity with Delta N=1. They are
exact local bookkeeping, not a new optical invariant.
At B, the corner-to-smooth and corner-to-interior transitions increase k;
at C the same is true. Smooth-to-corner transitions decrease k. The old
examples of cheap shared extensions obey(2). These point counts do not
say which source/target lifts become joined. A legal reflection point can
support only a return toward the source; the known dark22room already
contains reachable smooth seam endpoints. Thus a bare count of new legal
points is insufficient. Section3 works because the geometric classification
forces the specific midpoint B_mid to become interior and exposes ST itself.
