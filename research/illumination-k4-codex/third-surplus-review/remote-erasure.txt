3. Quarter parent plus one remote triangle: whole-family illumination
Suppose the only positive e cells are a d1/e1 quarter parent and a d0/e1
single-triangle remote leaf. Their shared C has two adjacent occupied right-angle
quadrants, hence is smooth and lies outside M. Therefore b>=1. Delta3 forces
b=1 and no other positive e cell. All other partial cells are ordinary caps.

Erase the remote triangle. The shared C changes from180 to90 degrees, losing
one smooth non-A vertex. The remote A vertex disappears, reducing Q by1.
Only one other vertex can affect s_N: the B endpoint of the attached BC edge.
The A endpoint disappears; its status is accounted for in Q. At B, removal
subtracts45 degrees from an end of its occupied angular fan, and can create
at most one smooth vertex. Write delta_B for the change in its smooth indicator;
delta_B<=1. Thus
 Delta_after = 3 - 1 - 1 + delta_B <= 2.
It is not legitimate to assert Delta_after=1 without checking B.

Erasure preserves a strictly simple disk: the triangle has just one shared
edge, and at its C and B endpoints the removed angular sector is at an end
of the contiguous occupied fan. Removing it cannot split that fan; the tip
is unique to the leaf. The boundary replaces a two-edge detour with the shared
edge. No holes or new boundary contacts arise. The full S/U/T stars survive,
and no new interior A can be created by deleting triangles.

The resulting room is in the fixed-support Delta<=2 theorem. Its illumination
path is one of section2's fixed paths. Reinsert the remote triangle: its A cell
is outside K, so the path remains legal. This proves illumination of every
valid room in this remote family, with at most13reflections, independently
of W/k. The actual endpoints remain S and T.

Whether each local pattern can be extended to a valid W<=21/k<=6 room is a
