## Statement

For the established three-band corridor and arbitrary permitted s,t,a,b,
illumination holds if at least one of a/g,b/g is irrational. The exterior
heights need only satisfy the original interior hypotheses. Together with
the accepted centered theorem, this leaves only commensurable depth pairs
with independent horizontal coordinates in the left portions of both chambers.

As before normalize d=g=1. The actual-mirror L reduction handles either
horizontal coordinate>1. Reflection in y=1/2 and time reversal let us order
0<t<=s<=1; depths are exchanged as well. Set H=a+1+b,alpha=a/H,
beta=(a+1)/H. Both phases are rational if and only if both a,b are rational,
by the inverse map a=alpha/(beta-alpha),b=(1-beta)/(beta-alpha).
Thus at least one phase is irrational under this theorem's hypothesis.

If no integer relation m alpha+n beta=r exists with(m,n)!=(0,0),
the independently accepted finite-Fourier foundation immediately supplies
a common-chart connection. We only need the case of an integer relation.

## 1. Complete closure of the one-parameter irrational orbit

Because at least one phase is irrational, two linearly independent rational
relations would force both phases rational, and are impossible. All integer
relations form one cyclic group generated by(m,n,r), chosen primitive with
gcd(m,n,r)=1. To justify the generator statement, the rational relation
space is a one-dimensional rational subspace of Q^3; its integral vectors
are exactly the integer multiples of its primitive generator. Change sign
so m>0, or m=0,n>0.

The closure of M(alpha,beta) modulo1, for M integer, is exactly

    K={(x,y) mod1: m x+n y is an integer}.                (1)

Here K can be DISCONNECTED; its components must not be discarded.
An elementary proof avoids assuming a general torus-density theorem.
If n!=0, alpha is irrational (otherwise beta would be rational too).
Restrict M to each residue j modulo|n| and put z=M alpha/n. Then

    M alpha = n z,
    M beta ≡ j r/n - m z   (mod1).

As M runs through that residue class, z is dense modulo1 by irrational
circle density. Hence the closure is the union of the curves
(nz,jr/n-mz), for j=0,...,|n|-1. Every such curve lies in K. Conversely,
given(x,y) in K, let Z=m x+n y be an integer using real representatives.
Choose integers j,k solving Z≡jr-mk(mod n), possible because
 gcd(m,r,n)=1. Then z=(x+k)/n gives the desired point on that curve.
This proves equality, including every component. If n=0, alpha=r/m,
gcd(m,r)=1 and beta is irrational. Restricting M modulo m gives all m
allowed x coordinates and dense y in each, again exactly K.

After a fixed initial phase vector v0 is added, the closure is v0+K.
Every relatively open nonempty part of that closure is visited by some
finite M, simply by the definition of closure and the density just proved.
Integers of either sign are permitted in the folding formulas.

    m E1+n E2 ≡ C (mod4),

    lo < C+4k < hi   for some integer k.               (3)

The strict image interval follows by taking the signed extrema on the two
intervals. Every point of this image is attained inside the open rectangle.
