# Targeted torus-cover literature check

Question: can an existing theorem exclude every remaining rational endpoint pair merely because its horizontal coordinates avoid original vertex fibers?

Outcome: no applicable theorem verified. Retire this shortcut; this is not a proof that no such theorem exists.

Lelièvre–Monteil–Weiss, *Everything is illuminated*, https://arxiv.org/pdf/1407.2975, Lemma 15 and Proposition 16: torus covers can have dark pairs over nontrivial affine correspondences. These statements do not give the desired branch-fiber-only criterion. Proposition 16 uses branching over a finite division-point set; it does not by itself refute a stronger single-branch primitive-cover conjecture, whose hypotheses and conclusion were not verified here.

Wolecki, *Illumination in Rational Billiards*, v3, https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.09358, Theorem 1.2(i): finitely many dark pairs for each fixed rational polygon. Finiteness does not exclude isolated fully rational pairs in this corridor and supplies no uniform bound across denominators.

Remark 2.2 supplies a relevant independent conceptual check: convex corner continuation retraces in the polygon, and cannot create a connection between distinct points. Our accepted cap lemma gives an explicit legal subcurve with actual endpoints; no continued corner is traversed.

Searches for orthogonal staircase illumination mostly concern art-gallery visibility, staircase paths, floodlights, diffuse reflection or wall transmission. Those results do not establish specular connectivity in this problem.

No universal illumination, darkness, primitive-cover classification follows. Return to finite-cyclic arithmetic with endpoint-loop trimming already accepted.
