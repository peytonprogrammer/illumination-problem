1. **Tokarsky (1995), *Amer. Math. Monthly* 102**, "Polygonal rooms not illuminable from every point." I recall a 26-sided room built from right-isosceles triangles. It contains a point pair with no billiard path between them. I recall a construction and an upper bound only, with no lower bound on the number of sides.
2. **D. Castro (1997), *Quantum***. I recall a 24-sided version of the same kind of room. Again, this is a construction, not a minimality proof.
3. **Lelièvre, Monteil and Weiss (2016), "Everything is illuminated," *Geometry & Topology* 20**. I recall this result: in a rational polygon, each point illuminates all but finitely many points. This is a finiteness result and gives no wall-count bounds.

## How this relates to your restricted lower bound

Your result needs four restrictions together:
- right-isosceles lattice rooms with the canonical A–B diagonal;
- a strictly simple disk boundary;
- marks at two distinct interior A points;
- at most 6 occupied non-A points (k ≤ 6) and at most 21 maximal walls.

None of the works I recall states an exclusion with this many conditions. All of them, as I remember them, give constructions, finiteness results, or illumination results for whole classes of surfaces.

An upper-bound construction such as Wolecki's 22-wall room doesn't imply that rooms with 21 or fewer walls illuminate. If Wolecki §3 claims minimality within its seed tiling, check two things:
- whether that seed class contains every room with k ≤ 6;
- whether it uses the same wall count, meaning maximal straight walls.
