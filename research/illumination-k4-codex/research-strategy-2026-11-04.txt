Decision
The main new-theorem target should be arbitrary simple ORTHOGONAL rooms with at most20 walls, arbitrary real side lengths and arbitrary distinct interior marks. This would remove the grid/mark and six-exception restrictions on this whole class. It would not settle nonorthogonal rooms with21 or fewer walls. Treat22 as a conjectured threshold, not a constraint that a proof must confirm. The present request does not activate a universal campaign or cancel the other session's Delta3 goal.

Why change targets
The accepted W<=21,k<=6 theorem already excludes all lower-surplus cases in the requested restricted class. The recent Delta<=2 theorem gives a human proof without those caps for one fixed support, but does not establish a broader minimum. Continuing layers is useful closure work, not our strongest route to a new lower bound. k changes under scaling; independent axis scaling is already known and does not remove all relative lengths.

Bet1: symbolic transport with arbitrary bounce counts
The useful unit should be a geometric chamber together with exact position-and-direction transport. A tree of chambers by itself is not an illumination proof. Keep physical source/target labels, portal position, direction, exact legality intervals, and true reflex-corner cost.

A concrete elementary transport formula is available. Consider a rectangular cap [0,a] x [0,h], whose full bottom side is an open portal, other three sides mirrors. Enter at(x,0),0<x<a with velocity(u,v),v>0. Unfold the side-wall reflections and the single top reflection. At the first return to the portal,

 x_out = F_a(x+2h*u/v),
 (u_out,v_out)=(F_a'(x+2h*u/v)*u,-v),
 F_a(z)=a-|((z mod 2a)-a)|.

This treats every finite number of side bounces at once. Exclude x+h*u/v in aZ (a top corner), and x+2h*u/v in aZ (portal endpoint); take the derivative only off its breakpoints. These are the only possible cap corner encounters. If u=0, the same formula holds with interior x. Proof: the unfolded x-coordinate changes uniformly for total time2h/v; folding at multiples of a recovers the horizontal coordinate and velocity sign. This is standard rectangular unfolding, not a claimed novel result.

The proposal is to use such exact maps to prove an overlap theorem for the source's reachable portal states and reversed target's states, allowing integer repetition counts. Our existing arbitrary-real L-room theorem succeeds by rounding a target image to land inside an open portal. The next method test is TWO successive portals with one common slope, arbitrary lengths and actual endpoints. Independent rounding at each portal is invalid: one slope must satisfy all conditions simultaneously. Success means a whole parameter-family theorem, not a supplied lit fixture. Failure means an exact geometric incompatibility or a counterexample to the proposed overlap criterion.

Hard global gap
The map for a single cap is not a finite-state reduction for a whole room. Multiple portals and repeated returns can create infinitely many branches; a valid proof needs a summation/composition theorem with endpoint coupling and corner avoidance. We have not supplied it. Interval-exchange recurrence or density alone does not hit a specified target. This proposal differs from retired strip deletion because it retains the lost phase displacement and arbitrary repetitions; it differs from retired sealed portals only if that global composition step is actually proved.

Important fixed control: do not count rectangles alone
For a simple orthogonal polygon, W=2r+4, where r is the number of reflex corners. Thus the target is r<=8. A vertical decomposition uses at most r cuts and at most r+1 rectangles, but fewer rectangles can already support darkness.
I checked the supplied known22-wall boundary Q in tokarsky-followup-2026-11-02/seam-endpoints.py. Its nine reflex corners are
 (0,-1),(1,-2),(1,-1),(1,1),(3,1),(4,1),(3,2),(1,2),(0,1).
The five distinct interior vertical cuts are
 x=0,y in[-1,1]; x=1,y in[-2,-1]; x=1,y in[1,2];
 x=3,y in[1,2]; x=4,y in[0,1].
They partition Q into SIX rectangles. Four cuts join two reflex corners, the last joins a reflex corner to a smooth wall point. Therefore an assertion that all trees of at most9 rectangles illuminate is already false. Any transport argument must retain physical reflex-corner costs and the actual portal geometry. This fixed-artifact check uses the published workspace boundary only, no room discovery.

Bet2: finish angle-changing rigidity through actual endpoint cylinders
This is an existing hard route, not a new discovery. The147 saved corner events all miss one exact nonsymmetry angle deformation. More rank or higher derivatives of those same functions cannot fix that failure. The repeated-cylinder conditional theorem and the peer's winding formula supply an explicit alternative: for an ACTUAL source/target cylinder, angular response is2*sum_v(n_v*alpha_v_prime). Appropriate cylinder-boundary families must give both opening signs across every deformation outside the known family symmetries.
The first decisive question is whether the known strict-escape deformation is detected by an actual source/target cylinder with the required opening sign. A formal closed wall word or unrelated periodic core is insufficient. This remains unproved; no cylinder discovery is authorized here. Even full local rigidity would concern a neighborhood of the known22-wall room and would not prove global minimality. This route is strategically useful for explaining why the known construction is special, but has less direct leverage on the wall minimum than Bet1.
