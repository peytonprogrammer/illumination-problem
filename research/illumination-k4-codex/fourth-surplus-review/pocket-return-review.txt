1. Rectangle
Assume w,h,b>0, 0<x<w. Horizontal unfolding gives X(t)=x+at.
The vertical flight has its single top reflection at h/b and its first
bottom return at tau=2h/b. Fold X by the triangular wave of period2w.
Since x is in its initial open strip, the final strip index floor(X(tau)/w)
counts signed vertical-side crossings. Its parity gives the horizontal
velocity sign and its absolute value gives the number of side hits.
Thus exit=(F_w(x+2ha/b),0), outgoing=((-1)^floor((x+2ha/b)/w)*a,-b).
Only top/bottom corners can be vertices: reject x+ha/b in wZ or
x+2ha/b in wZ. There is no intermediate bottom visit. For a0 neither
condition holds and the one top bounce returns vertically.

For a nonzero and h'=h+n*w*b/|a|, the exit phase shifts by
2n*w*sign(a) and the top phase by n*w*sign(a). Exit position, velocity,
and the truth of both singularity exclusions are unchanged. The strip
index keeps its sign or is zero before the shift, since a and X(t)-x
have the same sign. The absolute crossing count therefore increases by
exactly2n. This proves the periodicity assertion without finite sampling.

2. Triangle: why the reflected square really represents the same billiard
D is x>=0,y>=0,x+y<=w; only its bottom leg is a portal.
Reflect D across x+y=w. The copy covers the other half of [0,w]^2.
Its left wall maps to the square's top, and its portal to the right.
Therefore left/top reflect and bottom/right exit. Crossing the diagonal
changes the coordinate chart by the physical hypotenuse reflection.
All four square vertices correspond to physical triangle vertices.

In the square, X(t)=|x+at| until the first right exit: a<0 allows exactly
one left reflection; a>=0 allows none. Its first right-exit time is
tx=(w-x)/a for a>0, (w+x)/(-a) for a<0, infinity for a0.
Likewise Y(t)=bt up to w/b, then2w-bt; the first bottom exit is
ty=2w/b. Hence the first portal exit is at min(tx,ty), always finite.

If ty<tx, |x+2wa/b|<w and folding back leaves bottom coordinates unchanged:
exit=(|x+2wa/b|,0), outgoing=(sign(x+2wa/b)*a,-b).

If tx<ty, set z=w-b*tx. At the square right exit, y=w-|z| and
velocity=(|a|,sign(z)*b). Inverting the diagonal chart sends (w,y) to
(w-y,0) and velocity(vx,vy) to(-vy,-vx). Thus the physical exit is
(|z|,0), outgoing=(-sign(z)*b,-|a|), as stated by the peer.

3. Exact singularities and the five-hit bound
The square corners are exhausted by: bottom-left (bottom-branch z0),
top-right (right-branch z0), bottom-right (tx=ty), and top-left
(simultaneous left/top hit). The last is equivalently a<0 and
x+a*w/b=0; this event indeed precedes the exit, since then tx>ty.
There is no other physical vertex in the square or on its diagonal.
Thus the peer's exclusions are necessary and sufficient, for strict
triangle-vertex avoidance. A smooth endpoint in a larger room might admit
more inputs; no such relaxation is used by this lemma.

At most one left and one top reflection occur. The square path has at
most three straight legs, each meeting the folding diagonal at most once.
A leg lying along that diagonal would have to start at its endpoint on
the square boundary, already excluded as a vertex. Thus at most two square
wall hits plus three diagonal hits give five physical reflections.
This bound is sufficient; no sharpness assertion is needed.
