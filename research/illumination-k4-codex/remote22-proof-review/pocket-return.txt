Reusable pocket return maps
4 November 2026 — Codex01a1a00f

Purpose
Use a pocket's exact returning position and velocity as one optical operation.
This handles a deleted mirror by following the excursion, instead of assuming
that deleting/reinserting a pocket preserves illumination. The maps below are
proved directly. They do not force a source ray to enter the pocket, avoid
corners, or reach the target after returning; that global compatibility remains
the next question. No is intended.

1. Rectangle with one open bottom side
Let 0<=x<=w,0<=y<=h be a rectangle, open only at y0. A ray enters at(x,0),
0<x<w, with velocity(a,b), b>0. All other sides reflect.
Unfold vertical-wall reflections. The vertical coordinate reaches h once,
then returns to0 after time tau=2h/b. Its unfolded horizontal coordinate is
 z=x+2ha/b.
Define F_w(z) by period2w: F_w(z)=z on[0,w], and2w-z on[w,2w].
The exit is(F_w(z),0). If n=floor(z/w), the exit velocity is
 ((-1)^n a,-b).
Reject corner cases: x+ha/b or z an integer multiple of w. The former hits
a roof corner; the latter exits at a portal endpoint. These are precisely
the possible corners because vertical motion has only one roof contact.
For a=0, every positive height returns at the same x with velocity(0,-b).

Proof: fold the single straight unfolded horizontal motion into the interval.
Every integer-w crossing flips horizontal velocity; the one roof reflection
flips vertical velocity. The ray cannot hit the bottom earlier. This proves
finite return, with1+|floor(z/w)| reflections, for every nonsingular input.

Height periodicity, a nonzero:
Replacing h by h+n*w*b/|a| for any integer n>=0 changes z by sign(a)*2nw.
The return position and velocity are therefore EXACTLY unchanged. Roof phase
changes by sign(a)*nw, so corner avoidance is unchanged too. The excursion
adds exactly2n side-wall reflections. This is a class-wide replacement rule
for any outside path whose only contacts with the pocket occur at its portal.

Its four-contact return has z=4-5/d in(-3,-2), so its physical exit at y1 is
 x_exit=5/d-8, velocity(1,-d).
This is the compact optical operation underlying the new14-reflection path.

3. Single right-isosceles triangle, open at one leg
This is the remote leaf in BOTH remaining parent2 rows. Choose coordinates
D={(x,y):x>=0,y>=0,x+y<=w}, with portal y0 and reflecting x0/hypotenuse.
Entry(x,0),0<x<w, velocity(a,b),b>0.
Reflect D across its hypotenuse into the square[0,w]^2. The hypotenuse becomes
a fold, while square LEFT and TOP sides reflect and BOTTOM and RIGHT sides
are exits. Crossing the fold represents an actual hypotenuse reflection.

Define ty=2w/b. Define tx=(w-x)/a for a>0; tx=(w+x)/|a| for a<0; tx=infinity
for a0. The first square exit is at tau=min(tx,ty).

BOTTOM exit, ty<tx:
 z=x+2wa/b;
 original exit=(|z|,0), velocity(sign(z)*a,-b).
Here |z|<w; z0 is a corner and is excluded. The square horizontal motion
can have at most one reflection at the left edge before this exit.

RIGHT exit, tx<ty:
 z=w-b*tx;
 original exit=(|z|,0), velocity(-sign(z)*b,-|a|).
The square's right edge belongs to the reflected triangle; folding sends
(w,y) to(w-y,0), and(vx,vy) to(-vy,-vx). This gives the displayed formula.
The square vertical motion has at most one top reflection. Reject tx=ty,
z0, or a simultaneous left/top collision: these hit triangle vertices.
The local lemma excludes ALL triangle-vertex events. An attached room might
make a portal endpoint smooth, but that more permissive case is not used here.

Uniform local return bound: before the first square exit there is at most
one left and one top reflection, hence at most three straight square legs.
Each leg crosses the folding diagonal at most once. There are thus at most
five physical reflections (two square-boundary plus three diagonal contacts).
A collinear folding-diagonal leg would require entry at its endpoint, already
a forbidden corner. The bound need not be sharp. Every corner-avoiding input
returns, with the explicit exit state above. Five prescribed exact controls
exercise right exits, bottom exits, left/top reflections, and a0; all pass.

4. Composition principle and precise remaining work
If a room is split at a portal and an excursion returns through that portal
at a specified point/velocity, the outside trajectory depends only on that
exit state. Replacing the pocket by another with the same return state
preserves the connecting path, provided the outside path and endpoints stay
outside the changed region. This follows by uniqueness of straight flight
and specular reflection at each subsequent smooth wall contact. No assertion
of monotonic illumination under arbitrary enlargement is needed or true here.

Use the SAME triangle map in remaining rows(2,1,0,1) and(2,1,1,0), rather
than erasing the leaf and exposing a new smooth C/B point. The parent2 fan
can present a full C pair with only one half attached to the triangle; that
half is a unit-leg portal. Rotate/reflect the displayed coordinates to it.
Compose the local return with the parent and core flights; then solve the
actual S/T endpoint equation and prove that its parameter lies in an allowed
branch. In the second row, the extra local unit fan must be handled explicitly
as a possible changed exterior mirror. The return map alone does not remove it.

Success criterion: one common implication closes both charge rows, with every
branch obstruction paid by the budget or resolved by another explicitly
compatible return. Failure criterion: a legal arrangement blocks the proposed
external itinerary; then report the obstruction rather than scan more rooms.
This is the next bounded theoretical task; no global existence theorem or
new case closure is claimed by these local maps.
