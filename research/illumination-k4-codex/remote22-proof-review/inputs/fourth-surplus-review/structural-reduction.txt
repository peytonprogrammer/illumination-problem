2. Root and transmission facts
Remove the directly illuminating N bridge sectors6/7. Write H for the full
three-star core and K for H plus its seven neighboring A cells. A positive
cell has e=q-d>0. Zero cells are ordinary single H-facing caps and cannot
transmit beyond H: their sole BC edge already faces H.
A root touches H (d>0); a d0 cell has e=q. At these budgets a root with a
remote descendant has d1. Indeed d2 occurs only at N; q3/e1 and q5/e3 fans
with both H sides contain the bridge, q4 does not occur, and q>=6/e>=4
leaves no charge for a remote descendant. A root of e1 is a two-sector fan:
a centered pair has no outward side, and a quarter has exactly one outward
side, contributing90 degrees at that side's C midpoint.
A d0/e1 cell is a single triangle and has exactly one BC edge; it has graph
degree at most one. A d0/e2 transmitter must be a quarter pair (a centered
pair only faces one neighboring cell); it contributes90 degrees at each
of its two different C sides.
At any outside-H C only the two adjacent A cells can supply triangles.
Two attached90-degree contributions give a smooth180-degree C and force
one unit of b. No third cell can repair that C.

3. At most one remote cell at Delta4
Use the edge-connected occupied A-cell graph, with H contracted. Every
connection to a remote cell must pass through positive cells; ordinary caps
cannot transmit. There are at most four positive cells since sum(e)<=4.
A d0 cell of e1 cannot be an internal vertex of a connecting graph.

If there are at least two remote cells, choose a connecting tree spanning H
and two of them; do not assume that tree has no other occupied adjacencies.
With three positive cells and sum(e)<=4, the only potentially connecting
charge patterns are:
(a) root2 with two remote1 leaves;
(b) root1, remote2 transmitter, remote1 leaf.
A root1 with two remote1 leaves cannot branch. In (a), the root has q3 and
three or four sectors and must meet THREE distinct sides: H plus two remote
sides. Three sectors meet at most two sides. A four-sector fan meeting three
sides has contributions90,180,90. At most one side faces H, so at least one
remote leaf joins a90-degree contribution and gives a smooth C. Thus
sum(e)+b>=4+1=5. In (b), the quarter transmitter joins a quarter root at one
C and a single leaf at another, giving TWO distinct smooth C points and
sum(e)+b>=6.

With four positive cells, each e=1. All d0 cells are graph leaves. Roots1
have at most one non-H neighbor, so two remote leaves require two distinct
roots; each pair creates a smooth C, giving sum(e)+b>=6. Three remote
leaves with only one root are impossible. Extra adjacencies cannot heal
these C points: both incident A fans are already fixed. Additional positive
cells or larger charges exceed four. This exhausts every connecting tree.
Therefore at most ONE remote cell exists.

4. A sole remote cell has one direct K parent
An intervening d0 cell inside K must transmit. The minimum chain is then
root1, transmitter2, remote1, exactly the quarter-chain case of section3,
with two smooth C points: impossible. Thus the remote cell is directly
adjacent to a root in K.
If it joined two roots, it would need q>=2. The only charge-four possibility
is two roots1 and a remote2 quarter. Again both C joins are90+90 and force
b>=2. A single remote triangle cannot join two roots; q>=3 costs at least
five with two roots. Thus the parent is unique.
