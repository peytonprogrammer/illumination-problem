# The ten-wall spiral puzzle

## Three turns. One exact target.

You are standing in the left arm of a mirrored spiral. Your target is around three corners, in the bottom arm. You can choose the launch direction once. After that, every turn must come from a real wall. Can you guarantee an exact hit?

## The cap is only one unit

Keep your eye on the thin passage across the top. Its height is only one unit in our worked example. A ray must enter it from the left, emerge on the right, and eventually reach the actual target.

## Ten walls, three inward corners

The manuscript studies one specified orthogonal spiral, formed from a left column, an upper cap, a right arm, and a bottom arm. The three highlighted inward corners are dangerous: a path cannot reflect there. Dashed portal lines merely divide the proof into regions. They are open passages, so a ray must cross them without changing velocity.

## Two theorems, two sets of hypotheses

There are two different guarantees. A sufficiently tall or sufficiently wide room illuminates every distinct pair of real interior points. A separate arithmetic theorem works in an infinite family with a unit-height cap, but only for actual even-even lattice points.

## First, reuse the paths already proved

Before solving the hard pair, remove the cases already controlled by L-room and U-room constructions. The established L-room and U-room constructions have specific orientations: the chosen paths avoid the artificial cuts introduced when smaller rooms are drawn inside the spiral. Enlarging a room does not automatically preserve a billiard path, because an old reflecting wall may become an open passage.

## Keep the difficult pair in view

After those protected constructions, the residual pair has a bottom-arm target and either a left-column source or an upper-cap source. Our worked example uses the left column. The general proof retains the actual coordinates throughout.

## Read the route backwards

Start at the target and reason backwards through the bottom arm. Write z for its horizontal distance from the far right wall, and d for its depth below the bottom portal. For a positive horizontal slope tau per unit developed height, the reverse portal phase is z minus d tau. Requiring its absolute value to be smaller than the right-arm width gives an open interval of slopes.

## Corners remove isolated slopes

Inside a smaller slope interval, the left-arm and right-arm corner events occur only at finitely many isolated slopes. Remove those events. The remaining components are open intervals, and on each component the source and right-arm unfolding indices stay fixed. Counting the removed events guarantees a component of length at least delta.

## Make target images close enough

Once those indices are fixed, exact target hits occur on an arithmetic progression of slopes. Consecutive roots are separated by twice the full cap width A, divided by D, where D equals two H minus the source and target heights. If it is smaller than the safe interval, a genuine target root must fit inside.

## The inequality earns the hit

The tall condition is H at least c plus two A over delta. Since the actual endpoint heights are strict interior heights, D is strictly greater than H minus c. Consequently two A over D is strictly smaller than delta. Taking the first progression root after the interval’s left endpoint puts it strictly before the right endpoint. This is an exact hit for every real endpoint pair under the stated condition.

## Fold four bands into one ray

Now fold the developed curve through four bands. Each horizontal coordinate is a triangular wave: straight travel in the unfolding becomes equal-angle reflection in the room. At each portal, strict residual inequalities make the neighboring formulas agree in both position and signed velocity. The curve does not turn at a dashed portal. Every reflecting surface used is an actual exterior wall.

## The roof corner cannot be ignored

For a left-column source, a roof-corner hit would force the two cap-portal abscissae to be equal by triangular-wave symmetry. But the first lies in the left opening and the second in the disjoint right opening. Impossible. For an upper-cap source, the singular candidate has a later occurrence of the same actual source; the proof keeps the legal descending suffix after that occurrence.

## Turn the room sideways

Swap horizontal and vertical coordinates and reverse the boundary traversal. This is an isometry, so it preserves the reflection law and every physical corner. The room becomes the same normal form with transposed parameters. Applying the tall construction there gives the wide condition here.

## But this room is nowhere near tall

Return to the thin-cap room. Its parameters are a equals four, ell twelve, w sixteen, A twenty, and heights one, five, seven, eight. The tall test would require H at least five hundred five; the wide test would require A at least six hundred forty-four. Our room is eight high and twenty wide. Failure of those sufficient tests does not imply darkness. It calls for another proof.

## Let arithmetic replace extra space

The second theorem chooses equal terminal arm widths a, bottom width two a, and full cap width N a, where N is odd and at least five. The dimensions are integers, c is odd, and the cap height is one. It guarantees illumination of every distinct actual even-even interior pair.

## Freeze one actual pair

Use the actual source two, six and target fourteen, two. The developed travel height D is eight. The three critical portal times are one, three, and five. These numbers will let us inspect the finite arithmetic construction without launching a cloud of guessed rays.

## Choose from target-compatible slopes

Begin with the target-compatible slope grid. Here tau sub q is three plus q. Certain values would hit a portal corner, so they must be removed. The source, cap-return, and right-arm exclusions become residue classes of integers q. In this example, the forbidden residues modulo eight are seven, seven, and three.

## Why three exclusions cannot win

The general proof must show that the three forbidden residue classes cannot cover all integers. A density and intersection argument leaves only three possible covering patterns: two moduli equal to two, three moduli equal to three, or moduli two, four, four. Distinct portal times exclude the first two. In the last pattern, the cap travel and final depth would have to be equal, contradicting their even and odd parities.

## A safe residue is only the start

Take q equal to six, the archived checker’s selected safe residue. The initial slope is nine. Its source residual is three, strictly inside the left-arm width four. Avoiding corner congruences is necessary, but we must still arrange the cap return. A slope that avoids some corners is not automatically a connecting path.

## Five phases on one clock

Replace the slope by nine plus eight M. Integral portal times preserve the strict source and right-arm residue information. In the cap, the phase advances by sixteen M modulo forty. Because two and five are coprime, M from zero through four visits all five points of its coset. The clock shows phases twenty-one, thirty-seven, thirteen, twenty-nine, and five.

## One clock point enters the window

The cap return window runs strictly from sixteen to twenty-four on that forty-unit clock. Its length is eight, exactly the spacing between coset points. The corner exclusion prevents a point from landing on either endpoint. Therefore exactly one point lies inside. Here it is phase twenty-one, already at M equal to zero.

## All portals line up. Almost.

The three strict residuals are three, one, and three, each smaller in absolute value than the arm width four. Positions and velocities now splice correctly through the portals. Yet one issue remains. The bottom arm is twice as wide, so its triangular fold has period sixteen, not eight. Matching only modulo eight can still give the wrong physical endpoint.

## The wrong image looks convincing

Watch the provisional slope-nine path. It obeys the portal guards, but the final bottom phase is thirty. Folding in a width-eight bottom arm sends that phase to two, so the physical endpoint is eighteen, two. Our actual target is fourteen, two. This is exactly why a proof must track the target’s full reflection period instead of accepting a convenient image.

## One parity switch fixes the target

Add two a N, which is forty, to the slope. All three portal residuals stay exactly three, one, three. But the final phase increases by one hundred twenty, an odd number of eight-unit steps. That flips the parity obstruction. The new phase is one hundred fifty, congruent to six modulo sixteen. Folding gives six, so the physical endpoint is twenty minus six: exactly fourteen.

## Now follow the guaranteed ray

Now follow the corrected ray all the way. This archived exact path has sixty smooth reflections. It is a constructive witness, not a claim of shortest travel. Every leg stays in the original room, every bounce avoids vertices, and the final point is the actual target fourteen, two. The arithmetic did not bend the light. It selected a launch direction whose real reflections do all the work.

## Why the lattice hypothesis matters

To finish the whole-family theorem, return to the endpoint reduction. An integer-height mark cannot lie strictly between h and h plus one, so there are no native marks inside the cap band. Every remaining difficult even-even pair satisfies the terminal arithmetic lemma: the cap travel is two, coprime to odd N, and c minus the target height is odd. Protected paths and reversal cover the other placements.

## Two mechanisms. One exact standard.

The two results earn different conclusions. The geometric proof fits an exact target root into a strict interval. The thin-cap proof avoids residue classes, rotates a finite cap orbit, and fixes the full target period. Neither result settles every ten-wall room or the universal twenty-two-wall minimum. ## A tiny opening can be enough

The puzzle’s answer is more interesting than making the passage bigger. Sometimes extra height guarantees that a safe exact slope exists. In the thin-cap lattice family, arithmetic supplies that slope instead. The opening can stay one unit high. What matters is that all the strict portal guards and the actual target equation succeed together.
