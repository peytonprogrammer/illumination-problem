\begin{abstract}
Consider strictly simple orthogonal polygons with integer vertices, and
call a parity class of $\Z^2$ \emph{corner-only} in such a room when every
point of that class in the closed room is a polygon corner. We give two
computer-assisted results. First, a room with a corner-only class and a dark
pair of distinct interior lattice points, of any parity, has at least 22
walls. A known 22-wall room with a corner-only class attains the bound. A
counting identity bounds the area of every such room below 22 walls by 36,
so a finite enumeration of 780{,}565 rooms and 3{,}933{,}192 pairs suffices.
Two further exact tracers, which share no code with the search, replay
every witness.
Second, every 15-cell room with a corner-only class and fewer than 22 walls
lights every pair of distinct interior points, with real coordinates. The
finite reduction has a complete proof. The computations have independent
AI review and independent reimplementation of key steps, but no human
referee.
\end{abstract}

\section{Statement and conventions}
\paragraph{Rooms.}
A \emph{room} $P$ is a finite union of closed unit lattice squares (cells)
whose boundary is one Jordan polygonal curve. Equivalently, the interior is
connected, there is no hole, and there is no \emph{pinch}: a lattice point
whose only cells are two diagonally opposite ones. We call such rooms
\emph{strictly simple orthogonal lattice rooms}. The grid $\Z^2$ is fixed.
Write $n$ for the number of cells (the \emph{area}). A \emph{wall} is a
maximal straight segment of the boundary, and $W$ is the number of walls.

For a lattice point $p$, let $k(p)$ be the number of cells of $P$ that
contain $p$. A point with $k=1$ is a \emph{convex corner}, $k=3$ a
\emph{reflex corner}, $k=2$ with edge-adjacent cells a \emph{smooth wall
point}, and $k=4$ an \emph{interior lattice point}. The corners are exactly
the polygon vertices. Write $v$ and $r$ for the numbers of convex and
reflex corners.

\paragraph{Light.}
A \emph{lighting path} from $S$ to $T$ is a billiard path with finitely
many straight legs. It reflects specularly from walls and never meets a
corner, convex corners included. Passing through a smooth wall point is a
legal reflection, and passing through an interior lattice point is legal.
Distinct points $S,T\in\interior(P)$ are \emph{lit} if a lighting path
joins them, and \emph{dark} otherwise. The relation is symmetric. Marks
are always interior points; boundary endpoints are out of scope.

\paragraph{Parity classes.}
The four \emph{parity classes} are the cosets of $2\Z^2$ in $\Z^2$.
Fix a class $y$. Write $\cvx_y$, $\smooth_y$, $\rfx_y$, $\intr_y$ for the
numbers of class-$y$ lattice points of $P$ that are convex corners, smooth
wall points, reflex corners, and interior lattice points.

\begin{definition}
A parity class $y$ is \emph{corner-only} in $P$ if every class-$y$ lattice
point of the closed room is a corner of $P$. Equivalently,
$\smooth_y=\intr_y=0$.
\end{definition}

In the project's research record a corner-only class is a class of
\emph{type Q}: its canonical factor is the whole double cover
\cite[\S2.5 and \S3.12]{results}. This manuscript doesn't use factors
except in Proposition~\ref{prop:proper}.

\paragraph{The 22-wall room.}
Let $R_{22}$ be the room of \cite{q22}. It has 15 cells and 22 walls, so
$r=9$. Translated by $(1,3)$, its cells have lower-left corners
\[
(0,2),(0,3),(1,1),(1,2),(1,3),(1,4),(2,0),(2,1),(2,4),(2,5),(3,4),(3,5),(4,3),(4,4),(5,3).
\]
In the coordinates of \cite{q22}, the class of odd--odd points is
corner-only, with six convex and three reflex corners. The main theorem
of \cite{q22} says that the only dark pair of distinct interior points of
$R_{22}$ is $\{(0,0),(2,2)\}$. Both are interior lattice points.

\paragraph{Background.}
Tokarsky constructed polygonal rooms containing points that don't
illuminate each other \cite{Tokarsky1995}. Illumination on translation
surfaces is studied by Leli\`evre, Monteil and Weiss \cite{LMW}, and
Wolecki gives general finiteness results for rational polygons
\cite[Theorem~1.2]{Wolecki}. The four-copy unfolding used in
Section~\ref{sec:area15} is the usual correspondence between billiard
paths and straight segments \cite[Lemma~2.1 and Remark~2.2]{Wolecki}.
This manuscript asks for the fewest walls of a strictly simple orthogonal
lattice room with a dark pair, and answers it in a restricted class.

\subsection{Main results}
\begin{theorem}[Corner-only class closure; computer-assisted]\label{thm:closure}
Let $P$ be a strictly simple orthogonal lattice room with a corner-only
parity class. If $P$ has a dark pair of distinct interior lattice points,
of the same or of different parity classes, then $P$ has at least 22
walls. The room $R_{22}$ satisfies the hypotheses with exactly 22 walls,
so 22 is the minimum for this class of rooms.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Area-15 off-grid theorem; computer-assisted]\label{thm:area15}
Let $P$ be a strictly simple orthogonal lattice room with exactly 15 cells,
a corner-only parity class, and fewer than 22 walls. Then every pair of
distinct points of $\interior(P)$ is lit, with arbitrary real coordinates.
Since $R_{22}$ has 15 cells, 22 is the minimum wall count among 15-cell
rooms with a corner-only class and a dark pair of distinct interior points.
\end{theorem}

The theorems allow every area in Theorem~\ref{thm:closure}, and no bound on
path length or reflection count in either theorem. Theorem~\ref{thm:closure}
concerns lattice marks only. Section~\ref{sec:scope} lists what the results
don't cover.

Both proofs have a finite computational part. Section~\ref{sec:reduction}
proves the finite reduction for Theorem~\ref{thm:closure} without a
computer. Section~\ref{sec:computation} describes the computation and its
independent checks. Section~\ref{sec:area15} gives the argument for
Theorem~\ref{thm:area15}.

\section{The finite reduction}\label{sec:reduction}
The reduction is the counting identity of \cite[\S1]{d68}, with the
corner-only bound reviewed in \cite{d97}.

\begin{lemma}[Corner count]\label{lem:corners}
In a strictly simple orthogonal lattice room, $v=r+4$ and $W=v+r=2r+4$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Traverse the boundary counterclockwise. It turns by $+\pi/2$ at each convex
corner and by $-\pi/2$ at each reflex corner. Its turning number is 1, so
$(v-r)\pi/2=2\pi$. Each wall ends at two corners and each corner ends two
walls, so $W$ equals the number of corners, $v+r$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Local patterns]\label{lem:patterns}
In a strictly simple orthogonal lattice room, every lattice point $p$ with
$k(p)\ge1$ is a convex corner, a smooth wall point, a reflex corner, or an
interior lattice point.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The cell patterns around $p$ are: one cell; two edge-adjacent cells; two
diagonal cells; three cells; four cells. Two diagonal cells form a pinch,
which strict simplicity excludes. With four cells, no slit can run between
them, since a slit makes the boundary non-Jordan. So $p$ is interior.
This is the table in \cite[\S1]{d97}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Counting identity]\label{lem:count}
Let $P$ be a strictly simple orthogonal lattice room and $y$ a parity class.
Then
\[
n=\cvx_y+2\,\smooth_y+3\,\rfx_y+4\,\intr_y.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Each cell $[i,i+1]\times[j,j+1]$ has four corners, one in each parity
class. So the number of pairs (cell, class-$y$ corner of that cell) is $n$.
Count the same pairs by the class-$y$ point $p$ instead: $p$ contributes
$k(p)$. Lemma~\ref{lem:patterns} lists the possible values $1,2,3,4$ with
their names.
\end{proof}

For a general orthogonal lattice polygon the same count gives an extra term
$2\,\pinch_y$ \cite[\S1]{d97}. The bound below needs strict simplicity.
A control in \cite[\S2]{d97}, two $2\times2$ squares meeting at one point,
has $n=8$ while $4r+4=4$ under the convention that a pinch isn't a corner.

\begin{proposition}[Area bound]\label{prop:area}
Let $P$ be a strictly simple orthogonal lattice room with a corner-only
class $y$. Then $n\le 4r+4$. If $W\le 20$, then $r\le8$ and $n\le36$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Since $\smooth_y=\intr_y=0$, Lemma~\ref{lem:count} gives
$n=\cvx_y+3\,\rfx_y$. Class-$y$ corners are a subset of all corners, so
$\cvx_y\le v=r+4$ and $\rfx_y\le r$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:corners}. Hence
$n\le (r+4)+3r=4r+4$. If $W\le20$ then $2r+4\le20$, so $r\le8$ and
$n\le36$.
\end{proof}

The bound is attained: the enumerated room with census index 780{,}546 has
$n=36$, $r=8$, and all 8 reflex and all 12 convex corners in its
corner-only class \cite[\S2--3]{d97}. $R_{22}$ has $r=9$, outside this
range, and satisfies $15=3\cdot3+6$.

\begin{corollary}[Finite reduction]\label{cor:finite}
Let $\mathcal C$ be the set of strictly simple orthogonal lattice rooms
with a corner-only class, $r\le 8$, $n\le36$, and at least two interior
lattice points, taken up to translation by $\Z^2$ and the eight symmetries
of the square. If every pair of distinct interior lattice points is lit in
every room of $\mathcal C$, then the lower bound of
Theorem~\ref{thm:closure} holds.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Let $P$ satisfy the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:closure} with $W<22$.
By Lemma~\ref{lem:corners}, $W$ is even, so $W\le20$, and
Proposition~\ref{prop:area} gives $r\le8$ and $n\le36$. A dark pair needs
two distinct interior lattice points. Translations by $\Z^2$ and the
symmetries of the square map lattice rooms to lattice rooms, map lighting
paths to lighting paths, and permute the parity classes. So they preserve
the corner-only condition and the dark pairs. Hence $P$ is congruent to a
member of $\mathcal C$, which has no dark pair.
\end{proof}

\section{The computation}\label{sec:computation}
This section describes the computation that verifies the hypothesis of
Corollary~\ref{cor:finite}. The sources are \cite{qreadme} and the review
\cite{d102}. All counts in this section are copied from the cited logs.

\subsection{Enumerator}
Fix the corner-only class $y$ and translate so that the lowest row's
leftmost cell is an anchor cell. Each cell has exactly one class-$y$ vertex,
so the $2\times2$ blocks of cells centred at class-$y$ points partition the
cell grid. By Lemma~\ref{lem:patterns}, class $y$ is corner-only exactly
when every block meets the room in 0, 1, or 3 cells. The block masks
offered are the four single cells and the four L-shaped triples; the
diagonal masks are pinches and are excluded \cite[\S1, item 1]{d102}.

The enumerator \texttt{enumerate.cpp} \cite{qreadme} tries the four
alignments of $y$ relative to the anchor. Starting from the anchor, it
queries the edge-neighbour cells of the current connected set in a fixed
order. Each query branches on ``absent'' or ``present, through one allowed
block mask containing it''. It prunes only impossible vertex stars,
inevitable excess corner counts, and $n>36$. At completion it rejects
pinches and holes (it requires $v-r=4$), requires at least two interior
lattice points, and keeps the lexicographically least of the eight square
orientations. The four alignment runs emitted 250{,}590, 82{,}228,
283{,}071 and 164{,}941 records; after removing rooms emitted by two
alignments, the list has \textbf{780{,}565} rooms.

\paragraph{Completeness.}
The review \cite[\S1]{d102} reads the argument against the code and finds
it complete. In outline: for a target room, the branch that follows that
room's own blocks is always consistent; connectivity ensures every cell of
the room is queried; the pruning tests use a superset of the true
completions, so they never cut a real room; and the least orientation of
a qualifying room is itself qualifying, so some alignment emits it.
Completeness is checked by reading only. Section~\ref{sec:checks} lists
the count comparisons that support it.

\subsection{Tracer and witness format}
The first tracer is the shooter \texttt{shoot.c} of \cite{d9shoot}, patched
to pair all interior lattice points of the room regardless of class
\cite{qreadme}. It works in the four-copy unfolding of $P$, a square-tiled
surface whose squares are (cell, sheet) pairs. From each lift of $S$ it
follows every primitive direction $(p,q)$ with $p,q\ge0$ and $p+q\le B$,
lattice step by lattice step. It stops at a lift of a corner or on return
to the start. A lift of $T$ reached first gives a witness.

Each witness is re-checked by \texttt{verify\_plane.c} \cite{d9shoot}. It
traces in the room itself, not in an unfolding, and shares no code with the
shooter. Within one lattice step it orders grid-line crossings by exact
integer event times. It rejects any path through a corner, crosses interior
lattice points straight, and reflects at smooth wall points.

A witness record has the form
\[
\texttt{P}\ S_x\ S_y\ T_x\ T_y\ \texttt{L}\ \textit{sep}\ d_x\ d_y\ m\ \textit{k}\ \textit{nblk},
\]
where $(d_x,d_y)$ is the signed primitive launch direction and $m$ is the
number of lattice steps from $S$ to $T$. The fields \textit{sep}, \textit{k}
and \textit{nblk} are statistics of the original shooter and play no role.
Each room is preceded by a line \texttt{R} giving its index, area, wall
count, reflex count and cell list. An unresolved pair is recorded with
\texttt{U} in place of \texttt{L}.

\subsection{Independent checks}\label{sec:checks}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Audit.} \texttt{audit.py} \cite{qreadme} reads the compressed
witness files independently of the shooter. For each room it recomputes
the lattice points from the cell list, checks $W\le20$ and the corner-only
condition, and checks that the recorded pairs are exactly all unordered
pairs of interior lattice points. Every first-pass miss has a lit record in
the retry file. Its output reproduces the counts in the table.
\item \emph{Independent census.} \texttt{independent.c} adds single cells,
never blocks, and checks topology at the end by Euler characteristic. Its
counts agree with the enumeration for every area 1 to 36 and every
alignment, after expanding isometric copies: 144 comparisons
\cite{qreadme}.
\item \emph{Polyomino counts.} A patched copy of the polyomino enumerator
\texttt{polyo.c} of \cite{d67} counts fixed in-scope polyominoes through
area 15. The counts match the oriented census at every area; at areas 8 to
15 they are 14, 112, 474, 1{,}828, 7{,}107, 25{,}984, 82{,}890 and
205{,}548. The review \cite{d102} adds a separate Redelmeier enumeration in
Python, sharing no code with the other three. It matches the room list at
every area 1 to 13, with 3, 16, 63, 234, 907 and 3{,}262 rooms at areas 8
to 13.
\item \emph{Full independent replay.} The review \cite[\S2]{d102} replays
all 3{,}933{,}192 witnesses in all 780{,}565 rooms with the integer
billiard of \cite{d67}, which imports nothing from the shooter or from
\texttt{verify\_plane.c}. It checks the hypotheses of each room again. It
reports 0 hypothesis failures, 0 replay failures, 0 step-count mismatches,
and 0 missing, extra or duplicate pairs; all 4{,}655 first-pass misses
resolve from the retry file. The recorded run took 72.5 CPU seconds.
\item \emph{Sampled independent search.} An earlier coordinator check
using the same independent billiard, with its own directions, lit all
141{,}882 pairs in 20{,}200 sampled rooms with $\max(p,q)\le20$
\cite[\S11]{results}.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Controls}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Corruption control} \cite[\S2]{d102}. On 2{,}000 rooms the
replay rejects all 9{,}587 wrong-target corruptions and all 4{,}877
off-by-one step counts. It accepts 28 of 10{,}268 sign flips. These are
expected: in a mirror-symmetric room a reflected direction can reach $T$
in the same number of steps.
\item \emph{Dark-room control.} The shooter, run on $R_{22}$ at $B=60$
(1{,}103 directions), finds no witness for its dark pair, as it must
\cite{qreadme}. The independent billiard finds none with
$\max(p,q)\le40$ \cite[\S11]{results}. These controls test the programs;
the darkness of the pair rests on the proof in \cite{q22}.
\end{itemize}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:closure}]
By Corollary~\ref{cor:finite}, the lower bound follows once every pair of
distinct interior lattice points is lit in every room of $\mathcal C$. The
enumeration lists $\mathcal C$, and the computation supplies an exact
lighting path for every such pair, each replayed by two tracers. Each
replayed witness is itself a proof that its pair is lit. Sharpness is the
main theorem of \cite{q22}, together with the corner-only class of
$R_{22}$ recorded in \cite[\S3]{d97}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Rooms with a proper canonical factor}
A side computation in \cite{qreadme} extends an earlier finite result to
pairs of different classes. We state it in the language of
\cite[\S2.3--2.5]{results}. Let $DP$ be two copies of $P$ glued
along the boundary, an $n$-sheeted cover of the unit pillowcase branched
over its four corners. For a class $x$, let $g_x$ be the monodromy at the
corner of class $x$, let $B_x$ be the finest block system merging every
2-cycle of $g_x$, and let $N_x=DP/B_x$. The room is \emph{normalized} when
the horizontal edge lengths have gcd 1 and so do the vertical ones.

\begin{proposition}[Proper-factor branch; computed]\label{prop:proper}
Let $P$ be a normalized strictly simple orthogonal lattice room with
$W\le20$ in which some class $x$ has $1<\deg N_x<n$. Then every pair of
distinct interior lattice points of $P$ is lit, of the same or different
classes.
\end{proposition}

The source d64 lists every such room by enumerating quotients and their
covers, with a written completeness argument \cite[\S2]{d64}. It found
4{,}776 rooms, 4{,}581 normalized and 2{,}800 in scope, and lit all
6{,}680 same-class pairs. The review d67 finds the completeness argument
correct step by step, and reproduces the counts and the lighting with its
own billiard and its own polyomino count through $n\le16$ \cite{d67}. The
one evidence gap d67 recorded, the $z=4$ quotients, was closed by an
independent enumerator that matches all 3{,}663 cases object by object
\cite{d98}. For pairs of different classes, \cite{qreadme} runs the
patched shooter ($B=45$, 629 directions) on all 3{,}889 saved d64 rooms
with at least two interior lattice points. All 43{,}524 pairs have
witnesses, with 0 \texttt{verify\_plane} failures. These rooms overlap
$\mathcal C$, so the counts must not be added. The covering-room witnesses
were not part of the full replay in \cite{d102}.

\section{The area-15 off-grid theorem}\label{sec:area15}
The sources are \cite{offgrid} for the computation, \cite{d13} and its
review \cite{d96} for the cylinder lemma, and \cite{d74} for the
independent check.

\subsection{The cylinder certificate}
Let $M$ be the four-copy unfolding of $P$ and $\Sigma\subset M$ the set of
lifts of corners, convex corners included. For a rational direction
$\theta=(p,q)$ with $p,q\ge1$, the leaves through $\Sigma$ are finitely
many saddle connections, and their complement is a finite union of open
maximal cylinders. Let $\tau$ be the sheet flip $e\mapsto -e$. A
\emph{DP cylinder} $D$ is the image of $C\cup\tau C$ for an $M$-cylinder
$C$. For $S\in\interior(P)$, let $\Lambda_\theta(S)$ be the set of DP
cylinders whose open $M$-cylinders contain a lift of $S$, and let $K_D$ be
the projection to $P$ of the closures of the $M$-cylinders of $D$.

\begin{lemma}[Lemma A$'$ of \cite{d13}]\label{lem:A}
Let $S\ne T$ be points of $\interior(P)$, $\theta$ a rational direction,
$D\in\Lambda_\theta(S)$ and $T\in K_D$. Then $S$ and $T$ are lit.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch from \cite{d13}]
Choose a lift $\tilde s$ in an open cylinder $C$ of $D$ and a lift
$\tilde t$ in the closure of $C$, replacing it by $\tau\tilde t$ if needed.
Develop $C$ as a strip $\mathbb R\times(0,h)$ modulo a translation. Join
the preimages of $\tilde s$ and $\tilde t$ by a straight segment, using a
translate that makes the endpoints differ. Its relative interior lies in
the open strip, whose image avoids $\Sigma$. So it projects to a lighting
path.
\end{proof}

For a finite set $\Theta$ of directions, put
$\operatorname{Ill}_\Theta(S)=\bigcup_{\theta\in\Theta,\,D\in\Lambda_\theta(S)}K_D$.

\begin{theorem}[Theorem C of \cite{d13}]\label{thm:C}
(1) $S$ lights every $T\ne S$ in $\operatorname{Ill}_\Theta(S)\cap\interior(P)$.
(2) If $(S,T)$ is dark, then $T\notin\operatorname{Ill}_\Theta(S)$ and
$S\notin\operatorname{Ill}_\Theta(T)$.
\end{theorem}
Part (1) is Lemma~\ref{lem:A}; part (2) is its contrapositive with the
symmetry of light. The review \cite{d96} finds Lemma A$'$, Theorem C, and
the soundness argument of the exact cylinder program \texttt{cover.c}
sound, with four wording points that change no result.

\subsection{The computation}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Rooms.} The block enumerator, with the two-interior-point
filter removed, lists the 15-cell rooms with a corner-only class and
$W\le20$, including rooms with zero or one interior lattice point. A
separate cell-by-cell enumeration, filtered by area, topology, wall count
and parity, gives the same set of rooms after canonicalizing under
translation and the eight square symmetries \cite{offgrid}. The counts by
wall number are below; other wall counts below 22 contribute zero.

\begin{tabular}{lrrrrrr}
\toprule
$W$ & 12 & 14 & 16 & 18 & 20 & total\\
\midrule
Rooms & 13 & 327 & 3{,}413 & 18{,}966 & 55{,}006 & 77{,}725\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}

\item \emph{Covering.} For each direction, the program partitions
$\interior(P)$ into relatively open faces, edges and points. Each source
piece carries closed polygons with exact rational vertices that contain
every target not yet certified by Lemma~\ref{lem:A}.
\item \emph{Reciprocity.} By Theorem~\ref{thm:C}(2), a dark pair $(S,T)$
needs $T$ uncovered for $S$ and $S$ uncovered for $T$. If a source bucket
$A$ has possible targets in $B$, but no surviving source in $B$ has a
possible target in $A$, then the bucket pair contains no dark pair. The
implementation bounds each polygon by all half-grid buckets meeting its
bounding box. It can discard an expensive refinement and restore the
previous state, which only forgoes a certificate \cite[step 3]{offgrid}.
\item \emph{Tile pairs.} Six rooms were run separately on each unordered
pair of cells (tiles), both orders included. Each interior source belongs
to one tile by a consistent assignment of shared open edges and vertices,
and each target ranges over its whole closed tile. A point of a tile
outside a finite union of closed lit sets has an open neighbourhood of
positive area in that tile, so the positive-area test survives the
restriction \cite[step 4]{offgrid}.
\item \emph{Acceptance.} A run is accepted only when its remaining source
pieces are isolated points and every mutually uncovered pair consists of
lattice points. Such pairs are settled by the lattice census of
Section~\ref{sec:computation}.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Independent check of the six hardest rooms}
The check d74 \cite{d74} reimplements the certifier from the prose of
\cite{d13} and \cite{offgrid} only, without reading their code. It uses
exact rational arithmetic, in Python and in a C++ port that stops on
overflow. Its soundness argument maintains the invariant that every
uncovered target of a source lies in that source's polygons, and drops a
piece only when it has no live mutual partner. It runs on whole rooms, so
it doesn't need the tile-pair restriction.
\begin{itemize}
\item On the six rooms that needed tile pairs, at $B=16$ it clears five
rooms completely. Room 1 keeps the single lattice pair $(2,1)$--$(4,3)$ at
$B=10$ and $B=16$. Room 5 keeps $(1,2)$--$(4,5)$ at $B=10$ only. Exact
paths in directions $(-2,3)$ and $(1,-3)$ light these two pairs.
\item On $R_{22}$ at $B=8$, the Python and C++ versions both leave exactly
the known pair.
\item A validation samples 987 certificates and finds an explicit path for
each, with 0 failures. This tests for false merges of cylinder labels.
\end{itemize}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:area15}]
Let $P$ satisfy the hypotheses. Its area is 15 and $W\le20$, so $P$ is
congruent to one of the 77{,}725 enumerated rooms. Every room is accepted
at some stage. In an accepted room, Theorem~\ref{thm:C} lights every pair
except possibly mutually uncovered lattice pairs. Those are lit by the
replayed lattice witnesses. For the six rooms of the last stage, d74
independently confirms the result on whole rooms. Sharpness follows from
\cite{q22}.
\end{proof}

\section{Scope and limitations}\label{sec:scope}
\paragraph{Status.}
Both theorems are computer-assisted. Each source step has independent AI
review: the counting bound in \cite{d97}, completeness and the full replay
in \cite{d102}, the cylinder lemma in \cite{d96}, and the six hard rooms in
\cite{d74}. Each referee was one AI agent, usually from the same model
family as the author. No human mathematician has refereed any step, and
nothing is formally verified. The upper bound rests on \cite{q22}, which
has the same review status.

\paragraph{Stated gaps.}
\begin{itemize}
\item Completeness of the block enumerator is checked by reading the code
\cite[\S1]{d102}. The independent enumerations agree in counts per area
and alignment, not room by room, except at area 15 in
Section~\ref{sec:area15}, where the sets agree shape by shape.
\item For Theorem~\ref{thm:area15}, only the six hardest rooms have an
independent recomputation. The other 77{,}719 rooms rest on one
implementation. Pass 1 uses \texttt{cover.c}, whose soundness argument
\cite{d96} reviewed. Passes 2 and 3 use patched versions with reverse
pruning and a refinement skip. The review \cite{d96} states that these
patches are outside its scope, and no source records a separate review of
them for area 15. Their soundness argument is \cite[step 3]{offgrid}, and
d74 proves the same pruning rule for its own code.
\item A second tile-pair run with the unmodified cylinder predicates and
undefined-behaviour checks completed 708 of 720 tile pairs within its
resource limit \cite{offgrid}. The source itself calls a separate
end-to-end implementation of the continuum proof desirable.
\item The witnesses for Proposition~\ref{prop:proper} have one tracer
re-check (\texttt{verify\_plane.c}); the full replay of \cite{d102} didn't
include them.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{What is not covered.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{BBBB rooms.} Rooms in which every parity class has an interior
lattice point or a smooth wall point. Theorem~\ref{thm:closure} shows that
a room below 22 walls with a dark lattice pair must have a regular point
in every class. It doesn't exclude such a room. This set is infinite.
\item \emph{Non-lattice marks} in Theorem~\ref{thm:closure}, except at
area 15 by Theorem~\ref{thm:area15}.
\item \emph{Non-lattice rooms}: real side lengths, and rooms whose vertices
lie off the fixed grid.
\item \emph{45-degree walls}, slit rooms, pinched rooms, rooms with holes,
and boundary endpoints.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Further work in the sources.}
The research record lists further corner-only results that this
manuscript doesn't claim. An 18-wall real-mark result is recorded as
computer-assisted with an independent check of its shear step, with stated
gaps \cite[\S11]{results}. A run over all 913{,}622 rooms at 20 walls is
recorded as proved but not yet reviewed, with partial independent
confirmation \cite[\S11]{results}.
